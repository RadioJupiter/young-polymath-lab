# Level 2 · Day 6 · Thousands, 10,000, and a rocket’s height

**Today's big question:** How do we write 4,036 in thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones, and how tall is the Saturn V?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: discs for place value, columns with carries, and the first part-whole and comparison bars. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. The Saturn V height used in this level is 111 meters (363 feet), from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?” Do not replace that figure with an invented classroom height, and do not average it with another official figure. Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes; measure a shadow, not the Sun. If the placement check started the student here, Level 1 greetings and numbers 1–10 may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later bars with larger numbers; today’s bars are a first look.</p>

## Math circle: why a 0 holds a place, up to 10,000 {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

In 4,036 the 0 is not “nothing.” It holds the hundreds so that 4 can mean thousands. 4,036 and 436 and 4,360 are three different numbers.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

A zero can hold a place. The place of a digit is what gives it its value.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Why is 4,036 not the same as 436, and not the same as 4,360?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>4,036 is 4 thousands and 36; 436 is 4 hundreds and 36, with no thousands.</li>
<li>4,360 is 4 thousands, 3 hundreds, and 6 tens, with 0 ones — the 3 is in a different place than in 4,036.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>A student says 10,000 is “a 1 and four 0s, so it is the same as 1,000 because both have zeros.” What is wrong?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

The number of zeros, and the place of the 1, are different.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

10,000 is ten thousands; 1,000 is ten hundreds, or one thousand.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These defend place value with a short argument.</p>

1. Why must you write a 0 in 4,036?
2. How many hundreds are in 10,000?

</div>

### Practice

1. Write a short argument that 4,036 + 1,000 is 5,036, not 4,136.
2. Why does this lesson not replace 111 m with 63 m for the Saturn V?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Without the 0, the 4 would not stay in the thousands place.
2. 100 hundreds (because 100 × 100 = 10,000), or 10 thousands, each of 10 hundreds.

1. 1,000 changes the thousands place, not the hundreds place. 4 thousands plus 1 thousand is 5 thousands; the 0 hundreds, 3 tens, and 6 ones stay. 4,136 would be 100 more, not 1,000 more.
2. 111 m is the NASA student-page figure. 63 m is an invented classroom height. Official and invented figures are not swapped.
