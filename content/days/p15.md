# Level 3 · Day 15 · Level 3 check

**Today's big question:** Can you use equal groups, arrays, and division, and keep four weeks distinct from the NASA synodic month?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: counters for equal groups, arrays, and sharing before the written fact. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. The Moon’s synodic period used in this level is 29.53 days from the NASA Moon fact sheet, taught as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html Do not replace that figure with 28 days (four weeks), and do not begin Moon phases as fractions or the sidereal month. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact, not a NASA orbital period. If the placement check started the student here, earlier foundation greetings and numbers may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later multiplication with larger numbers; today’s facts and arrays are a first look.</p>

### Unit check

Work alone. Use counters, an array, or a fact family when it helps. For the Moon’s month, use about 29½ days from 29.53 days on the NASA Moon fact sheet taught this level. Classroom bags, rocks, and arrays stay labeled invented. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: equal groups and multiplication**

1. Find 7 × 8. You may show equal groups or an array.
2. Why is 7 × 8 the same as 8 × 7?
3. How many are in 4 rows of 9?
4. Find 4 × 7. What can that count, if each 7 is a week?
5. A student writes 7 × 8 = 15. What was mixed?

**Part B: division and fact families**

6. Find 54 ÷ 6. Show a multiplication that checks it.
7. Find 54 ÷ 9.
8. Write the fact family for 6, 9, and 54.
9. Find 36 ÷ 4.
10. A student writes 54 ÷ 6 = 48 because 54 − 6 = 48. What was mixed?

**Part C: scaling equal groups, the week, and the Moon**

11. Three bags hold 24 rocks shared equally (invented). How many rocks are in each bag? How many rocks are in 5 such bags?
12. How many days are in four weeks?
13. What length does this level use for the Moon’s synodic month, and from which source?
14. Why is about 29½ days not replaced by 28 days?
15. Two invented boxes hold 18 cards equally. How many cards are in 5 such boxes?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Unit 1 (Level 4), if that is the next step, or before a review sitting. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 56; 36.
2. 9.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. **56**.
2. The same array can be 7 rows of 8 or 8 rows of 7; the marks are not added or taken away. (Or: seven 8s is the same total as eight 7s.)
3. **36**.
4. **28**. Four weeks (calendar days).
5. 15 is 7 + 8, the sum, not seven 8s. The product is 56.

**Part B.**
6. **9**. Check: 6 × 9 = 54 (or 9 × 6 = 54).
7. **6**.
8. 6 × 9 = 54, 9 × 6 = 54, 54 ÷ 6 = 9, 54 ÷ 9 = 6.
9. **9**.
10. 48 is subtracting 6 once, not sharing 54 into 6 equal groups. The share is 9 in each group.

**Part C.**
11. **8** in each bag; **40** in 5 bags. Invented.
12. **28** days.
13. **About 29½ days**, from **29.53 days** on the NASA Moon fact sheet.
14. 28 days is four calendar weeks. The NASA synodic period is 29.53 days, about 29½, a little more than 28. The official month is not replaced by the four-week count.
15. 18 ÷ 2 = 9 in each box; 5 × 9 = **45**, invented.
