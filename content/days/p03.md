# Level 1 · Day 3 · The other part of the bond

**Today's big question:** If 6 + 7 = 13, how do you know what 13 − 6 is?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete step in the core lesson, even if the student already seems quick: discs, a ten-frame, or a bond are the foundation. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. If the placement check started the student here, this is the first mathematics of the course, and Spanish and Latin also begin here. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that recap is for students who skipped these foundation levels.</p>

## Core lesson: subtraction facts, from the same bonds {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


**Subtraction** answers “how many are left?” and also “how many more to reach …?” Both can use a **number bond**. If two parts make a whole, taking one part away leaves the other part.

<div class="nbond" aria-hidden="true"><div class="whole">13</div><div class="arms"></div><div class="parts"><span>6</span><span>7</span></div></div>

Because 6 + 7 = 13:

- 13 − 7 = **6**
- 13 − 6 = **7**

For **13 − 6**, you may also make ten in reverse: 13 − 3 = 10, then 10 − 3 = 7, because 6 = 3 + 3. Either method is taught; the bond and making ten stay the tools.

A concrete step: 13 counters, take 6 away, 7 remain. The bond is the picture. 13 − 6 = 7 is the sentence.

Invented story, labeled invented: 13 classroom “stars” on a card, 6 covered. 7 remain visible. This is not an official sky count.


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Subtraction uses the same bond as addition. 13 − 6 = 7 because 6 + 7 = 13. You can also subtract to 10, then subtract the rest. “How many left?” and “how many more?” can use the same fact.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Show 13 − 6 with a bond, and give the related addition.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>The whole is 13; one part is 6; the other part is 7.</li>
<li>6 + 7 = 13, so 13 − 6 = 7.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>A student computes 13 − 6 by counting back 6 steps from 13 and lands on 8, having counted 13 as the first step. What went wrong, and what is the fact?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: The start</summary>

If you count back, you start at the number before 13, or you use objects; counting 13 itself as a jump is a miss.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The bond</summary>

6 + 7 = 13, so 13 − 6 = 7, not 8.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These use the bond or making ten, not a lucky count-back.</p>

1. Find 15 − 8, and write the related addition.
2. Find 14 − 9 by going back to 10 first.

</div>

### Practice

1. Find 13 − 6 and 16 − 9.
2. Invented (labeled): 12 cards, 5 taken away. How many remain? Write the subtraction and the addition bond.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 7, because 8 + 7 = 15.
2. 14 − 4 = 10, then 10 − 5 = 5, because 9 = 4 + 5. So 14 − 9 = 5. (And 9 + 5 = 14.)

1. 7 (6 + 7 = 13); 7 (9 + 7 = 16, or 16 − 6 = 10, then 10 − 3 = 7).
2. 12 − 5 = 7; 5 + 7 = 12. The 12 and 5 are invented classroom numbers.

## Challenge problems {#challenge}

### Practice

1. What is 10 less than 13, and what is 10 less than 300? Which job uses today’s facts, and which uses place value from Day 1?
2. Why is 13 − 6 not the same as 31 − 6, even though both have a 6?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 7, because 9 + 7 = 16 (or 16 − 9 = 7).
2. 6, because 8 + 6 = 14 (or 14 − 8 = 6).

1. 13 − 10 = 3, a subtraction fact from a bond of 13. 10 less than 300 is 290, from the hundreds and tens places (Day 1). Both use “10 less,” but 300 is a place-value job.
2. 13 − 6 = 7. 31 is 3 tens and 1 one; 31 − 6 needs the ones (or regrouping) and is 25, a different whole. The same 6 is not the same problem.

## Math circle: a missing part, and a reason {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

If you are told the whole and one part, the other part is fixed. There is not a second correct part for the same whole and the same given part (when we use whole numbers and one bond).

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

One whole and one part name the other part. That is why a subtraction fact has one answer.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>The whole is 13 and one part is 6. Why can the other part not be 8?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>6 + 8 = 14, not 13.</li>
<li>The part that makes 13 with 6 is 7, so 13 − 6 = 7, not 8.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>A student says 13 − 6 could be 7 or 8 “depending on how you count.” What would you say?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: The bond</summary>

The parts of 13 that use a 6 are 6 and 7 only.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: Counting</summary>

A count-back error can land on 8, but that is a miss, not a second answer.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These defend a single subtraction answer with a bond.</p>

1. If the whole is 10 and one part is 3, what is the other part, and how do you know you have them all?
2. Can 15 have two different partners for the part 8, if we stay with whole numbers?

</div>

### Practice

1. Write a short argument: why 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 give the same whole, but 15 − 8 and 15 − 7 do not give the same part.
2. Invent a whole and a part (labeled invented) and name the other part two ways: as a subtraction and as a missing-addend sentence.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 7, because 3 + 7 = 10. There is only one whole-number partner of 3 that makes 10.
2. No: 8 + 7 = 15, so the partner is 7 only.

1. 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 are the same two parts, so they make the same whole, 15. 15 − 8 takes the part 8 away and leaves 7; 15 − 7 takes the part 7 away and leaves 8. Those are the two parts, not two values for the same subtraction.
2. A model: whole 12, part 5; other part 7, because 12 − 5 = 7 and 5 + 7 = 12. Other honest invented bonds accepted.

Original captions:

- **Sōl est.** — It is the Sun. (pointing at a Sun drawing)
- **Sōl nōn est.** — It is not the Sun. (pointing at a Moon drawing, or a star)

The same pattern works with lūna and stella. Do not invent long stories. The job is to say when the picture matches the word, and when it does not.

<p class="note">All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; <em>c</em> is always like <em>k</em>; <em>v</em> is like English <em>w</em>; vowels are pure (<em>a</em> as in <em>father</em>, <em>e</em> as in <em>they</em>, <em>i</em> as in <em>machine</em>, <em>o</em> as in <em>note</em>, <em>u</em> as in <em>rude</em>).</p>


</div>

## Notebook: a subtraction bond, and “how many left” {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html)</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


Draw first: 13 counters or marks, then 6 taken away or covered, and a number bond of 13 with parts 6 and 7. Label whole, part, left. Then write from stems.

**Word bank:** whole, part, left, bond, subtract, thirteen, seven.

**Stems:**

1. 13 − 6 is 7 because …
2. I could count back from 13, but …
3. 6 and 7 make 13, so …

End with the two sentences 13 − 6 = 7 and 6 + 7 = 13, and one “what next” question.


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Draw the objects and the bond first. The because-clause should name the bond, not only the answer. A count-back can help, but the bond is the reason this level wants in writing.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Finish: “13 − 6 is 7 because …”</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>A model: “13 − 6 is 7 because 6 and 7 make 13.” Other complete finishes that name the bond are accepted.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>Finish the but-stem about counting back. What miss can that stem name?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2: A model</summary>

</details>

</div>
