# Level 2 · Day 8 · Taking from 1,000, and a comparison bar

**Today's big question:** How do we find 1,000 − 264, and how much shorter is an invented rocket that is 18 m shorter than 63 m?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: discs for place value, columns with carries, and the first part-whole and comparison bars. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. The Saturn V height used in this level is 111 meters (363 feet), from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?” Do not replace that figure with an invented classroom height, and do not average it with another official figure. Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes; measure a shadow, not the Sun. If the placement check started the student here, Level 1 greetings and numbers 1–10 may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later bars with larger numbers; today’s bars are a first look.</p>

## Mental math warm-up {#mental}

<p class="meta">10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

To subtract 1,000 − 200, take 2 hundreds from 10 hundreds: **800**. To find 1,000 − 264, you may **count up** from 264 to 1,000: 264 + 36 = 300, 300 + 700 = 1,000, so 36 + 700 = **736**.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Subtracting hundreds changes the hundreds. Counting up from the smaller number to the whole is often clear when the whole is 1,000.

## Core lesson: subtraction, counting up, and a comparison bar {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


A **comparison bar** is a new picture in this level. Draw two bars lined up at the left. The extra piece on the longer bar is the **difference**.

Invented classroom rockets, labeled invented: Rocket A is **63 m** tall. Rocket B is **18 m** shorter. How tall is Rocket B? How tall are they together if stacked?

<div class="barmodel">
<div class="row"><span class="lbl">Rocket A</span><span class="seg" style="width:45%">same as B</span><span class="seg u" style="width:18%">? 18 m</span></div>
<div class="brace">Rocket A’s whole bar = 63 m (invented)</div>
<div class="row"><span class="lbl">Rocket B</span><span class="seg" style="width:45%">45 m</span></div>
</div>


Rocket B is 63 − 18 = **45 m**. Together, 63 + 45 = **108 m**. Both heights, the 18 m, and the 108 m, are invented classroom numbers, not NASA figures.

**1,000 − 264.** Count up: 264 + 36 = 300, 300 + 700 = 1,000, so 1,000 − 264 = **736**. You may also trade in columns: you cannot take 4 ones from 0, so trade a thousand into hundreds, tens, and ones, then subtract, and check by adding 264 + 736 = 1,000.

The Saturn V’s 111 m stays the NASA student-page figure. Do not replace it with 63 m.

<p class="note">The Saturn V height used in this level is from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?”: the Saturn V rocket was 111 meters (363 feet) tall. https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-saturn-v/ Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA measurements. This level does not average 111 m with any other figure.</p>


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

A comparison bar: two bars lined up at the left; the extra piece is the difference. Invented rockets: A 63 m, B 18 m shorter, so B is 45 m; together 108 m. 1,000 − 264 = 736, by counting up or by trading, checked by addition.

## Math circle: which bar, and why a check addition? {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A comparison bar and a part-whole bar answer different questions. “How much shorter?” is a difference. “How tall together?” is a whole. Every subtraction can be checked by an addition: the part you took plus the part that remains makes the whole.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Choose the bar that matches the question. Check a subtraction by adding.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Why is “how much shorter is Rocket B?” not answered by 63 + 45?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>63 + 45 is the stacked whole, a part-whole sum.</li>
<li>The shorter question is the difference, 63 − 45 = 18, on a comparison bar.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>Why does 264 + 736 = 1,000 show that 1,000 − 264 = 736 is right?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

264 and 736 are the two parts of 1,000.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

If the parts make the whole, the subtraction of one part leaves the other.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These choose a bar or defend a check.</p>

1. When would you draw a part-whole bar for the invented rockets?
2. When would you draw a comparison bar?

</div>

### Practice

1. Write a short argument that 1,000 − 264 = 736, with a check.
2. Why are 63 m and 18 m labeled invented next to the Saturn V’s 111 m?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. When you want the stacked height, the total of the two heights.
2. When you want how much taller or shorter one is than the other.

1. Count up: 264 + 36 = 300, + 700 = 1,000, so 736. Check: 264 + 736 = 1,000.
2. 63 m and 18 m are classroom rockets and a classroom difference. 111 m is the NASA student-page figure for the Saturn V. The labels keep those facts from being mixed.

## Notebook: a comparison bar for two invented rockets {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html)</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Draw first: a comparison bar for invented Rocket A 63 m and Rocket B 18 m shorter (B = 45 m), and a part-whole bar for the stacked 108 m. Label invented, difference, whole. Then write from stems.

**Word bank:** comparison, difference, part, whole, stacked, invented, shorter.

**Stems:** 1. Rocket B is 45 m because …  2. I could have added 63 and 18 to find B, but …  3. They are 108 m stacked, so …

End with 63 − 18 = 45 and 63 + 45 = 108, both labeled invented, and one “what next” question.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Draw both bars first. The but-stem should refuse using the difference as if it were B’s height, or adding 63 and 18 to get B.

</div>

<h3>Watch me</h3>
