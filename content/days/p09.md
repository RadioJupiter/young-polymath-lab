## Core lesson: bars in two jobs, and shadows through the day {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · bars already taught; then a classroom shadow observation, labeled invented where a length is measured</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


Yesterday’s comparison bar and the part-whole bar are both used today. Choose the bar that matches the question.

Invented classroom rockets, still labeled invented: 63 m and 45 m. Difference: 18 m (comparison). Stacked whole: 108 m (part-whole).

**Shadows through the day.** A stick of fixed height casts a **longer** shadow when the Sun is **lower** in the sky (morning or evening) and a **shorter** shadow when the Sun is **higher** (near midday). That is an observation you can make with a stick and a shadow, not a NASA official length.

Invented classroom measurements, labeled invented: in the morning the shadow of a stick is **80 cm**; near midday the shadow of the **same** stick is **20 cm**. The shadow is 60 cm longer in the morning (comparison). The stick’s height did not change; the Sun’s height in the sky did. These 80 cm and 20 cm figures are not official NASA measurements, and they are not the Saturn V.

Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes. Measure a shadow, not the Sun.

<p class="note">The Saturn V height used in this level is from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?”: the Saturn V rocket was 111 meters (363 feet) tall. https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-saturn-v/ Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA measurements. This level does not average 111 m with any other figure.</p>

Part-whole for a total; comparison for a difference. A longer shadow goes with a lower Sun; a shorter shadow with a higher Sun. Classroom shadow lengths (80 cm and 20 cm) are invented and labeled invented. The stick’s height stays the same.

## Math circle: a shadow, a stick, and what we may claim {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

You may claim that a longer shadow goes with a lower Sun, from the observation. You may not claim that 80 cm is an official NASA morning shadow, or that every stick on Earth has those two lengths.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

</div>

<li>You must not claim that NASA measured 80 cm and 20 cm, or that the Saturn V’s shadow is 80 cm.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

</details>

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

</div>

### Practice

1. Write a short argument that a longer shadow goes with a lower Sun, without inventing an official length.
2. Name one NASA figure and one invented figure from this level, and say how you tell them apart.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. The Sun’s height in the sky (higher near midday). The stick’s height stays the same.
2. A comparison bar.

1. When the Sun is lower, light hits the stick more from the side, so the shadow stretches farther along the ground. When the Sun is higher, the shadow shrinks. No official length is needed for that reason.
2. NASA: Saturn V 111 m (363 feet) on the student page cited. Invented: 63 m and 18 m rockets, or 80 cm and 20 cm shadows. The lesson names the source or the invented label.

## Notebook: shadows through the day, with honest labels {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html)</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Draw first: the same stick at two times, a long morning shadow and a short midday shadow, with invented lengths 80 cm and 20 cm labeled invented. Label stick, Sun (higher / lower), shadow, morning, midday. Never look at the real Sun. Then write from stems.

**Word bank:** shadow, stick, morning, midday, longer, shorter, invented, higher, lower.

**Because / but / so:** 1. The midday shadow is shorter because …  2. The stick is the same height, but …  3. The morning shadow is 80 cm, so …

End with 80 − 20 = 60 cm (invented), the NASA-free observation about higher and lower Sun, and one “what next” question.

**Safety:** never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Draw both shadows first. Because: the Sun is higher. But: the shadows are different lengths. So: the morning one is 60 cm longer, invented. Do not call 80 cm a NASA length.
