### Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar model or a rate strip when it helps. Keep invented mixes, maps, and walks labeled. Keep NASA figures labeled as fact-sheet diameters, already-taught distances, the FAQ’s about-28,000 km/h, or NASA’s “about / roughly 400” wording. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: ratio and equivalent ratios**

1. Write the ratio of 6 to 8 in simplest form, and say how many parts there are in all.
2. 2 : 5 = 8 : □. Find the missing number.
3. A 3 : 4 mix has 21 in the first amount. What is the second amount? (Invented classroom mix, labeled invented.)
4. Earth’s fact-sheet diameter is 12,756 km and the Moon’s is 3,475 km. Why is “exactly 4 : 1” a little high?
5. Why is 2 : 3 not the same comparison as 3 : 2?

**Part B: scale and rate**

1. At the model scale 1 cm : 1,000 km, how many centimeters across is the Moon (3,475 km)? You may leave the exact value or round to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.
2. At the same scale, the already-taught mean Earth–Moon distance is 384,400 km. How many centimeters, and about how many meters, is that gap?
3. An invented walk covers 24 km in 4 hours, steadily. What is the speed, and how long for 18 km at that speed?
4. The ISS travels at about 28,000 km/h (NASA FAQ). How far, at that labeled figure, in 3 hours?
5. Why will this course not invent a “1 cm to 4 light-years” scale for a printed star chart?

**Part C: percent**

1. Write 25% as a fraction in lowest terms and as a decimal.
2. Find 10% of 60, and then 30% of 60.
3. Find 25% of 80.
4. An invented box holds 50 tiles, and 10 are blue. What percent are blue?
5. Why is “25% of 40 is 25” not sound?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 11. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Three headings, ready for the sitting.
2. A note that extra items are not requested; four out of five is the mastery rule, not a score.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. 3 : 4 (divide both by 2); 7 parts in all.
2. 20 (the factor is 4).
3. One part is 7, so the second amount is 28. Invented mix.
4. 4 × 3,475 = 13,900, which is 1,144 km more than 12,756. Earth is a little less than 4 times as wide as the Moon.
5. The order matters. 2 : 3 and 3 : 2 are different comparisons.

**Part B.**
1. 3,475 ÷ 1,000 = 3.475 cm, about 3.5 cm to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.
2. 384,400 ÷ 1,000 = 384.4 cm, which is 3.844 m, about 3.84 m.
3. 6 km/h; 18 ÷ 6 = 3 hours. Invented walk.
4. 28,000 × 3 = 84,000 km, using the FAQ figure.
5. A star chart is usually a map of directions, not a scale model of distances, and we did not measure such a scale. Inventing 4 light-years would be a homemade official number.

**Part C.**
1. 1/4; 0.25.
2. 6; 18 (three lots of 10%).
3. 20 (a quarter of 80).
4. 10/50 = 20/100 = 20%. Invented box.
5. 25% is 25 per 100 of the named whole, not 25 of every whole. 25% of 40 is 10, not 25.

## Math circle: a last short argument {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a short reason, then a check</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

The level has one honesty habit in three clothes: a ratio, a scale, and a percent all need a named job. An invented 2 : 3 mix is not a NASA fuel. A star chart is not automatically a distance scale. 25% is not the bare number 25. Sourced figures keep the wording they came with (about 28,000 km/h; about / roughly 400), and we do not blend them into a homemade official number.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Day 5: 1 cm : 70,000 km, to walk the Solar System around a 20 cm Sun. This unit: 1 cm : 1,000 km, to see Earth and the Moon as sizes.
2. A percent is a part per hundred *of a named whole*. 25% of 80 is 20; 25% of 40 is 10. Without the whole, 25% is only a comparison, not an amount.

1. No. They are two rounded NASA figures. Five miles per second is a coarser 18,000 mph. We compute with 28,000 km/h, labeled as the FAQ’s metric figure.
2. It is the product of two rounded FAQ figures (about 28,000 km/h and 90 minutes). It is not a surveyed NASA orbit length published as its own measurement in this unit.
3. A star chart is usually a map of directions (angles), not a scale model of how far the stars are.
4. They are the same amount: 25 per 100, 25 hundredths, and one quarter. “25” without “per 100 of this whole” drops the job of the number.
5. A note that the circle is a reason, not a score.
