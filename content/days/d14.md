# Unit 3 · Day 14 · Different parts, one bar

**Today's big question:** How do you add or subtract fractions when the parts are not yet the same size — for example 1/2 and 1/6?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> a 6-part bar is the concrete step; do not jump to a least-common-multiple trick before the bar is seen. Keep mass and pull distinct if a gravity example is used. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done.</p>

## Mental math warm-up: finding a common size of part {#mental}

<p class="meta">10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

To compare or combine 1/2 and 1/6, you need parts of the **same size**. Sixths work, because 1/2 = 3/6 (Day 12). Once the parts are the same size, you are back in yesterday’s rule: 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6. A number that both denominators divide is a **common denominator**. For 2 and 6, 6 works.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

To add unlike fractions, first rename them so the denominators match (a common denominator), then add as like fractions. 1/2 = 3/6.

## Core lesson: unlike denominators, and 1/6 again {#core}

<p class="meta">35–40 minutes · Method: a bar that can show both unit sizes, then the common-denominator rule</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Fractions with **unlike denominators** have parts of different sizes. You cannot add the numerators until the parts match.

**A 6-part bar** can show halves and sixths, because 1/2 is 3 of the 6 parts.

**1/2 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3.**  
**1/2 − 1/6 = 3/6 − 1/6 = 2/6 = 1/3.**  
**1/3 + 1/6 = 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.**

**Gravity.** Earth’s pull is 1, or 6/6 on the bar. The Moon’s pull is about 1/6. The **difference** is 1 − 1/6 = 5/6: Earth’s pull is 5/6 stronger than the Moon’s, in the sense that you add 5/6 of Earth’s pull to the Moon’s pull to recover Earth’s. The **ratio** remains about 1/6. Keep those two ideas separate.

**The rule.** Find a common denominator (often a multiple of both denominators). Rename each fraction. Then add or subtract as like fractions, and rename if you can.

**Concrete step.** On a 6-cube train, 3 cubes are 1/2, 1 cube is 1/6. Together, 4 cubes are 4/6 = 2/3 of the train.

</div>

## Math circle: the smallest common denominator, and a trap {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a diagram, then a short argument</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Any common multiple of the denominators will work, but the **least common multiple** keeps the parts larger and the numbers smaller. For 4 and 6, 24 works, and so does 12, which is smaller. 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12. If you used 24, 6/24 + 4/24 = 10/24 = 5/12 after renaming — the same amount.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Any common denominator works; the least one is often easiest. The trap is adding numerators and denominators (1/4 + 1/6 = 2/10), which is not addition of amounts.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<ol class="steps">

<li>5/12 compared with 1/5 = 12/60 against 12/60? 5/12 = 25/60, 1/5 = 12/60, so 5/12 is larger, and they are not equal.</li>
<li>Adding 1+1 and 4+6 invented a different amount.</li>
</ol>

</div>

## Latin · Puella lūnam spectat {#latin}
