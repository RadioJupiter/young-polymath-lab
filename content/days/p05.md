### Unit check

Work alone. Use discs, a bond, or a ten-frame when it helps. For day and night, use the NASA facts taught on Day 4. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: numbers to 1,000, and counting**

1. Write 247 in hundreds, tens, and ones.
2. What number is 6 hundreds, 0 tens, and 4 ones?
3. What is 10 less than 300?
4. An invented classroom sky picture (labeled invented) has 3 rows of 4 stars. How many stars? Why is this not an official count of the real sky?
5. Why is 305 not the same number as 35?

**Part B: addition, and making ten**

6. What is 6 + 4? What is 4 + 6? What do you call this?
7. Find 8 + 7 by making ten. Show the split.
8. Find 9 + 6 by making ten.
9. Find 46 + 30.
10. Find 28 + 5 by making ten with the ones.

**Part C: subtraction, and day and night**

11. Find 13 − 6, and write the related addition.
12. Find 15 − 8, and write the related addition.
13. How many more is 16 than 9?
14. In the NASA wording taught this level, why is there day and night? Use facing toward or away from the Sun, and Earth’s spin.
15. How long is the cycle of day and night for most places, in the figure this level uses? Is half of Earth in darkness at all times?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 2. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.


### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 76.
2. 290.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. 2 hundreds, 4 tens, 7 ones.
2. **604**.
3. **290**.
4. **12** stars. The picture is invented, not an official count of the real sky.
5. 305 is 3 hundreds and 5 ones (0 tens). 35 is 3 tens and 5 ones. The 0 holds the tens place.

**Part B.**
6. Both are **10**. It is a **bond of 10**.
7. 8 + 2 + 5 = **15**.
8. 9 + 1 + 5 = **15**.
9. **76**.
10. 28 + 2 + 3 = **33**.

**Part C.**
11. **7**, because 6 + 7 = 13.
12. **7**, because 8 + 7 = 15.
13. **7** (16 − 9 = 7, or 9 + 7 = 16).
14. Places facing toward the Sun have day; places facing away have night. As Earth spins, a place moves from one to the other.
15. **About 24 hours** for most places (one cycle). **Yes**, half of Earth is in darkness at all times; that is the night side, not “night everywhere.”

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 1,000; 990.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. They are the same two parts in the other order, so they make the same whole, 15.
