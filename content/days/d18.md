# Unit 4 · Day 18 · Covering a floor

**Today's big question:** How do we find the area of a rectangle, an L-shape, and a right triangle?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Unit squares, then L × W. Split or subtract for L-shapes. A right triangle is half the rectangle on its legs. Invented floors stay labeled. Keep cm² and m² on coverings.</p>

## Core lesson: area of rectangles, L-shapes, and a right triangle {#core}

<p class="meta">35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (unit squares), pictorial (an array or a bar), then L × W; a right triangle as half of a rectangle</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

**Area is covering, in square units.** A 5 by 3 rectangle holds 15 unit squares: 15 cm² if the side is in centimeters. We write cm² (square centimeters) and m² (square meters).

**An L-shape.** Split it into rectangles, or complete a rectangle and subtract the missing part. For the picture: an 8 cm by 3 cm rectangle (24 cm²) plus a 4 cm by 2 cm rectangle on the left (8 cm²) totals **32 cm²**. Or: an 8 cm by 5 cm rectangle (40 cm²) minus a 4 cm by 2 cm bite (8 cm²) is also 32 cm².

**A right triangle as half a rectangle.** A right triangle with legs 6 cm and 4 cm is half of a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle, so its area is ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². (That is also ½ × base × height, with those legs as base and height.) We are not doing non-right triangles today, except to note that the same ½ × base × height formula will wait for a careful height in a later lesson.

**Invented habitat floor (labeled invented).** A 6 m by 3 m module has floor area 18 m². That is a classroom number, not a NASA floor plan.


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Area of a rectangle: L × W, in cm² or m². L-shapes: add or subtract rectangles. A right triangle is half the rectangle on its legs: ½ × b × h. Perimeter and area are different jobs. Invented floors stay labeled.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Find the area of the L-shape in the picture in two ways (add two rectangles, or subtract a bite).</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>Add: 8 × 3 = 24, and 4 × 2 = 8, total 32 cm².</li>
<li>Subtract: 8 × 5 = 40, minus a 4 × 2 bite of 8, total 32 cm².</li>
<li>Both ways agree, which is a useful check.</li>
</ol>

</div>

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: Arithmetic</summary>

</details>

</div>

### Practice

1. A 7 cm by 5 cm rectangle: area and perimeter.
2. A right triangle with legs 5 cm and 8 cm: area.
3. An L made of a 10 by 6 rectangle minus a 3 by 3 corner bite: area.
4. A 2 m by 3 m rectangle beside a 2 m by 3 m rectangle, joined along a 3 m side: what is the combined area? (Does joining change area the way it can change perimeter?)
5. Draw a right triangle, mark the two legs, and write ½ × b × h beside it.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 36 m², invented.
2. 26 cm is a length (the walk); 32 cm² is a covering. They are different kinds of quantity, so “bigger” is the wrong comparison unless you name two lengths or two areas.

1. 35 cm²; 24 cm.
2. 20 cm².
3. 10×6 − 3×3 = 60 − 9 = 51 cm².
4. Each has area 6 m², total 12 m². Joining does not remove area the way it can remove outer edge from a perimeter; the covering stays the sum.
5. A labeled triangle with the half-rectangle formula.

## Math circle: why the units are square {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a short argument, not a new formula</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A centimeter is a length. A square centimeter is a little square, 1 cm on a side — a covering. That is why the unit is written cm², not cm, and why 15 cm and 15 cm² are different kinds of quantity. If you only multiply 5 × 3 and forget the unit, you have not yet named an area.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

cm is a length unit; cm² is an area unit (a 1-by-1 square). Name the unit, or the number is unfinished.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A student writes “the area is 15 cm.” What is missing, and how would you repair it?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>15 cm is a length, not a covering.</li>
<li>The rectangle 5 cm by 3 cm has area 15 cm².</li>
<li>The repair is the square in the unit, and the word area.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>Why is 1 m² not the same as 100 cm², even though 1 m = 100 cm?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: A square</summary>

A 1 m by 1 m square is 100 cm by 100 cm.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The product</summary>

100 × 100 = 10,000, so 1 m² = 10,000 cm², not 100 cm².

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These are about what the unit means.</p>

1. Draw 1 cm² and 1 cm, and write a caption for each.
2. A 1 m by 1 m invented tile: area in m² and in cm².

</div>

### Practice

1. Why can two shapes have the same area and different perimeters? Give one pair you can actually draw (for example, a 6 by 2 rectangle and a 4 by 3 rectangle).
2. Write one sentence a guide could post: length unit, area unit.
3. What would be wrong with calling Mars’s diameter 6,792 km²?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. A 1 cm segment and a 1 cm square, each captioned.
2. 1 m², which is 10,000 cm².

1. 6×2=12 and 4×3=12, both 12 cm², but perimeters 16 cm and 14 cm. Same covering, different walks. Other honest pairs are fine.
2. A clear posted sentence.
3. km² is an area unit; the diameter is a length, so km is the right kind of unit.
