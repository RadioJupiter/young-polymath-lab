# Level 3 · Day 14 · Five bags, four weeks, and about 29½ days

**Today's big question:** If three bags hold 24 rocks equally, how many are in 5 such bags, and how does about 29½ days compare with four weeks?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: counters for equal groups, arrays, and sharing before the written fact. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. The Moon’s synodic period used in this level is 29.53 days from the NASA Moon fact sheet, taught as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html Do not replace that figure with 28 days (four weeks), and do not begin Moon phases as fractions or the sidereal month. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact, not a NASA orbital period. If the placement check started the student here, earlier foundation greetings and numbers may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later multiplication with larger numbers; today’s facts and arrays are a first look.</p>

Do not jump from 24 to 40 by a guess. Find the equal group first (8), then the new number of groups (5). The 24, 8, and 40 are invented classroom counts, not official NASA rock counts.

**The week and the Moon.** A week has **7** days (calendar). Four weeks have 4 × 7 = **28** days. The NASA Moon fact sheet gives the Moon’s **synodic period** as **29.53 days**, taught here as **about 29½ days**. That is a little more than four weeks (28 days). 29½ is not 28, and it is not 4 × 7; it is a sourced length, rounded to a half-day for this level, not a finer invented official figure. This level does not teach Moon phases as fractions (for example “first quarter”). A sidereal month is not begun.

## Math circle: find the equal group first, and keep two lengths distinct {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">
