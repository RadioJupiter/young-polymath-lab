### Unit check

Work alone. Use the graphs and tables already taught when a question names them, or make a quick drawing when it helps. Keep invented classroom counts labeled invented. Keep SILSO yearly means labeled as SILSO figures from the SN_y_tot_V2.0 file. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: tallies and tables**

1. Write a tally for 14, and say the number in groups of five and leftovers.
2. An invented classroom watch has counts 5, 9, 4, and 7. What is the total, and which watch has the most?
3. A labeled invented table of three watches has a total of 20. Watch 1 is 6 and Watch 2 is 8. What is Watch 3?
4. Why do tallies group in fives?
5. Why must an invented classroom meteor total stay labeled invented, and not be called an official rate for a named shower?

**Part B: graphs and sourced change**

1. A bar graph of a labeled invented watch has an even scale from 0 to 12 in steps of 2. The bar for Watch 2 reaches 8. What count does that bar show?
2. A pictograph has the key “one star = 2 meteors.” A row shows 5 stars. What count does that row show?
3. In the SILSO yearly means used on Day 43, which year in 2019–2025 has the highest mean, and what is that mean?
4. The 2025 SILSO yearly mean is 123.2, and the 2024 mean is 154.7. How did the mean change, and why is “it will keep rising in 2026” a miss?
5. Why would a bar-graph scale marked 0, 2, 4, 10, 12 be a problem, and why is a pictograph without a key not ready to be read?

**Part C: chance and measuring with error**

1. A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the chance of a 4, as “out of” and as a fraction?
2. A fair coin is tossed 10 times. About how many heads would you expect, and must you get exactly that many?
3. A spinner is divided into four parts that are not equal, and the shaded part is the small one. Why is “1 out of 4” the wrong chance for that shaded part?
4. A rod ends a little past 8 cm and does not reach 9 cm on a centimeter ruler. Write an honest length, and say why 8.27 cm is a miss.
5. A fair coin comes up heads five times in a row. Why does that, by itself, not prove the coin is unfair?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before moving on. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Three headings, ready for the sitting.
2. A note that extra items are not requested; four out of five is the mastery rule, not a score.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. Two groups of five, and four leftover marks; 2 × 5 + 4 = 14.
2. The total is 25. The second watch, with 9, has the most. Invented.
3. 6 + 8 = 14, so Watch 3 is 20 − 14 = 6. Invented.
4. Groups of five can be skip-counted at a glance, and the leftover marks are easy to see. The total is unchanged.
5. The total is from an invented classroom watch, not from a sourced official rate for a named shower. Calling it official would invent a figure this course does not have.

**Part B.**
1. 8.
2. 5 × 2 = 10.
3. 2024, with a yearly mean of 154.7, from the SILSO SN_y_tot_V2.0 file.
4. The mean went down (154.7 to 123.2). A 2026 official yearly mean is not in the table we opened, so it is not invented, and a past rise is not a promise that the next year will rise too.
5. The steps are not even, so a bar’s height would no longer match a steady scale. Without a key, a reader cannot tell what one picture stands for.

**Part C.**
1. 1 out of 6, or 1/6.
2. About 5 heads. You must not get exactly 5 every time.
3. The four parts are not equal chances, so “1 out of 4” names a fair split the spinner does not have. The small part is less than 1 out of 4.
4. About 8 cm, or between 8 and 9 cm. 8.27 cm claims tenths and hundredths the ruler does not show.
5. A fair coin can make a short run of heads. Five tosses is a short record, not a proof that the coin is unfair.

## Math circle: a last short argument {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a last argument about labels, scales, and “about”</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

This sitting gathers three habits from the level: invented classroom counts stay labeled invented; sourced SILSO means stay labeled as SILSO; a graph needs an even scale or a key; and “about,” or a range, is the honest wording when a tool or a short run does not support a finer claim.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Invented and sourced figures stay labeled. A graph is only as honest as its scale or its key. “About” is not a dodge; it is a fair reading.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Why is it honest to say “the 2024 SILSO yearly mean is the highest in this 2019–2025 window,” but not honest to invent a 2026 official yearly mean from that rise?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>The 2024 high is something the sourced table already shows.</li>
<li>A 2026 official mean would be a new figure, and this course has not opened one.</li>
<li>The habit is to describe what the table shows, and to leave unsourced future figures unclaimed.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>How does “a pictograph needs a key” sit beside “a measurement needs the marks the ruler actually has”?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: The key</summary>

Without a key, a reader invents what one picture stands for.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The marks</summary>

Without finer marks, a reader who writes 8.27 cm invents a precision the ruler does not have.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These check the last argument.</p>

1. Give one reason an invented meteor total and a SILSO yearly mean must not be dropped into the same unlabeled graph.
2. Why is “about 5 heads in 10 fair tosses” a stronger sentence than “the coin is unfair, because I just got 7”?

</div>

### Practice

1. This argument is not a score or a badge.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. One set of numbers is invented, and the other is a sourced official series. Mixing them without labels would make a classroom watch look like a sunspot record, or the other way around.
2. The fair chance points to about 5, and a short run can differ (7 is still a short record). Calling the coin unfair is a new claim that short run does not prove.

1. It is a reason about honest labels and honest precision, not a prize.
