# Unit 1 · Day 3 · Adding up the worlds

**Today's big question:** If you lined up planets side by side, how wide would the row be? Adding big numbers accurately is the tool.

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> regrouping ("carrying") should be shown with the base-ten pieces first: ten ones are traded for one ten, ten tens for one hundred. The written method is a record of those trades. Introduce the bar model as a drawing of the problem's *structure*, before any calculation.</p>

## Core lesson: adding big numbers and the part-whole model {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes</p>

**1. Concrete.** With base-ten pieces, build 1,276 and 475 and push them together. Whenever you have ten of a piece, trade them for one of the next bigger piece. What do you end up with?

**2. Pictorial: the part-whole bar model.** When parts are put together to make a whole, draw one bar split into the parts, and a bracket for the whole:

<div class="row"><span class="lbl">diameters</span><span class="seg" style="width:62%">Earth 12,756 km</span><span class="seg u" style="width:17%">Moon 3,475</span></div>
<div class="brace">whole = ?</div>
</div>

**3. Abstract: column addition.** Line up the places. Add the ones first; if a column makes ten or more, write the ones digit and carry one to the next column.

```
   12,756
 +  3,475
 --------
   16,231
```

Ones: 6 + 5 = 11, write 1, carry 1. Tens: 5 + 7 + 1 = 13, write 3, carry 1. Hundreds: 7 + 4 + 1 = 12, write 2, carry 1. Thousands: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6. Ten thousands: 1. **16,231 km**: Earth and the Moon side by side.

**Check with an estimate:** 13,000 + 3,000 = 16,000, so 16,231 is reasonable.

### Practice

1. Mercury (4,879 km across) and Mars (6,792 km) side by side: how wide? Is that wider or narrower than Earth (12,756 km)?
2. A spacecraft flies to the Moon and back: 384,400 km each way. How far is the round trip?
3. A probe travelled 125,600 km on its first day and 98,750 km on its second. Draw the bar model and find the total.
4. Venus (12,104 km), Earth (12,756 km) and Mars (6,792 km) side by side: how wide?
5. Estimate first, then calculate: 47,385 + 28,946.

### Answer key

1. **11,671 km**: narrower than Earth, by 1,085 km.
2. **768,800 km**
3. One bar split into 125,600 and 98,750; total **224,350 km**
4. **31,652 km**
5. Estimate 47,000 + 29,000 = 76,000; exact **76,331**

## Challenge problems {#challenge}

<p class="meta">25 minutes</p>

1. A two-digit number AB plus its reverse BA equals 121. Find every possibility. (AB means the digits A and B, not A × B.)
2. Add up every whole number from 1 to 100, without adding them one at a time.
3. Mercury, Venus and Mars have diameters 4,879 km, 12,104 km and 6,792 km. Add them in the order that is easiest for you, and explain why you chose it.

### Answer key

1. AB + BA = (10A + B) + (10B + A) = 11A + 11B = 11 × (A + B). So A + B = 11: **29, 38, 47, 56, 65, 74, 83, 92** (each with its reverse).
2. **5,050.** Pair 1 with 100, 2 with 99, 3 with 98, …: each pair adds to 101, and there are 50 pairs. 50 × 101 = 5,050.
3. **23,775 km.** For example: 4,879 + 6,792 = 11,671 (the two smaller first), then + 12,104. Any order gives the same total; the point is choosing one that makes the work easier.

## Math circle: the magic square {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes</p>

Place the numbers 1 to 9 in a 3 × 3 grid so that every row, every column and both diagonals add to the same total.

Before you start guessing, think: what do all nine numbers add up to? There are three rows, each with the same total, and together the rows use every number once. So what must each row add to? Then: which number must go in the centre? (Hint: the middle row, middle column and both diagonals all pass through the centre.)

### Answer key

1 + 2 + … + 9 = 45, and the three rows share it equally, so each line adds to **15**. One solution:

| 2 | 7 | 6 |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | 5 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 | 8 |

The centre must be **5**. The four lines through the centre add to 4 × 15 = 60; they cover every square once, except the centre, which is counted four times. So 45 + 3 × centre = 60, and the centre is 5. Every other solution is a rotation or reflection of this one.

## Notebook: how big is the Sun? {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes</p>

Draw Earth as a circle **1 cm** across. Then, at the same scale:
- Jupiter (142,984 km) is about 11 times as wide as Earth (12,756 km): draw it **11.2 cm** across with a compass or a traced plate.
- The Sun (1,391,400 km) is about 109 times as wide as Earth: **109 cm**. It will not fit on the page; draw just a piece of its edge as a curve, and write how big the whole circle would be.

Then check those two "times as wide" numbers yourself: is 11 × 12,756 close to 142,984? Is 109 × 12,756 close to 1,391,400? (Use rounding to check, or ask for help with the multiplication; it is coming next week.)

### Answer key

11 × 12,756 = 140,316, close to 142,984 (the precise ratio is about 11.2). 109 × 12,756 = 1,390,404, very close to 1,391,400. A good page shows the drawings with labels and the check.
