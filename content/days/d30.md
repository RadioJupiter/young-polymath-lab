### Unit check

Work alone. Use a place-value chart or a number line when it helps. For planet temperatures, use the figures taught this unit, and keep means, ranges, and invented classroom numbers labeled. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: place value and comparing**

1. Write 0.07 and 0.70 in ones, tenths, and hundredths, and say which is greater.
2. Write 3 ones, 2 tenths, and 5 hundredths as a decimal.
3. Write 0.4 as hundredths, and as a fraction of a whole.
4. Order from least to greatest: 0.8, 0.08, 0.75.
5. Why is 0.40 the same amount as 0.4, and what can a written 0.40 still mark, if the number is a measurement?

**Part B: adding and subtracting decimals**

1. 0.6 + 0.25.
2. 1.36 + 0.4.
3. 2.05 − 0.7.
4. 3.14 − 0.04. (Use 3.14 as a decimal to compute with; it stays a labeled approximation of π, not a changed value of π.)
5. An invented rod is 1.25 m, and a second invented rod is 0.8 m. What is the total length? Label the total invented.

**Part C: negative numbers and temperatures**

1. Which is colder, the Moon’s fact-sheet mean (−20°C) or Mars’s fact-sheet mean (−65°C)?
2. Sketch a Celsius number line and mark −65, 0, and 15. Label −65 and 15 as fact-sheet means for Mars and Earth.
3. How many degrees warmer is Earth’s mean (15°C) than Mars’s mean (−65°C)?
4. NASA’s fact-sheet mean for Mercury is 167°C. NASA Science gives a sunlit high of 430°C and a night-side low of −180°C. Why is 167°C not “the temperature of a place on Mercury”?
5. How many degrees hotter is Venus’s fact-sheet mean (464°C) than Mercury’s fact-sheet mean (167°C)?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 10. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Three headings, ready for the sitting.
2. A note that extra items are not requested; four out of five is the mastery rule, not a score.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. 0.07 is 0 ones, 0 tenths, and 7 hundredths (7 hundredths). 0.70 is 0 ones, 7 tenths, and 0 hundredths (7 tenths). 0.70 is greater.
2. 3.25.
3. 0.40 (40 hundredths); 4/10, or an equivalent fraction such as 2/5 or 40/100.
4. 0.08, 0.75, 0.8.
5. 0.40 is 4 tenths and 0 hundredths, which is the same amount as 4 tenths. If the number is a measurement, a written 0.40 can still mark a reading to the nearest hundredth, while 0.4 may mark only the nearest tenth.

**Part B.**
1. 0.85.
2. 1.76.
3. 1.35.
4. 3.10, or 3.1; 3.14 remains a labeled approximation of π.
5. 2.05 m, invented.

**Part C.**
1. Mars’s mean is colder (−65 is farther left than −20).
2. A line with −65, then 0, then 15, and the two means labeled as fact-sheet means.
3. 80 degrees (65 up to 0, then 15 more). Both figures are fact-sheet means.
4. The fact-sheet mean averages the very hot sunlit side and the very cold dark side, and the sheet’s own note says that mean is not representative of a given region.
5. 297 degrees (464 − 167). Both figures are fact-sheet means.
