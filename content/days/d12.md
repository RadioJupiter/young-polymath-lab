# Unit 3 · Day 12 · One sixth as strong

**Today's big question:** The Moon's gravity is about 1/6 of Earth's. What does that 1/6 actually mean — and what does it not change?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> keep mass and pull distinct; do not let “weigh 1/6 as much” turn into “become 1/6 as many kilograms.” The 6-part bar is the concrete step. NASA’s figures (about 1.62 against 9.80 m/s², about 1/6) are on the sources page. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done.</p>

## Mental math warm-up: renaming a number as equal parts {#mental}

<p class="meta">10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A number can be written as equal parts without changing its value. 1/2 of 12 is 6, and 2/4 of 12 is also 6, because 2/4 is the same amount as 1/2. Half of 8 is 4; two quarters of 8 are also 4. You are not making a new number; you are naming the same amount in a different way.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Equal amounts can have different names: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6. Finding half, then checking that two quarters give the same answer, is a first test of that idea.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Is 2/4 of 20 the same as 1/2 of 20? Show both.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>1/2 of 20 is 10.</li>
<li>2/4 means two of four equal parts of 20. Four equal parts of 20 are 5 each, and two of them are 10.</li>
<li>The two answers match, so 2/4 of 20 = 1/2 of 20.</li>
</ol>

## Core lesson: equivalent fractions, and gravity as 1/6 {#core}

<p class="meta">35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (fold or cut equal parts), pictorial (bars), then the written rule</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Two fractions are **equivalent** when they name the **same amount** of the same whole. You can see this on a bar: a bar cut into 2 equal parts with 1 shaded is the same length shaded as a bar of the same size cut into 6 equal parts with 3 shaded. So **1/2 = 3/6**.

**The rule, after the picture.** Multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator by the same number, not zero, and the amount stays the same: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6. You are making finer equal parts, or grouping them, not changing the length.

**Gravity on the Moon (NASA).** The pull of gravity at the Moon’s surface is about **1.62 m/s²**, against about **9.80 m/s²** on Earth, a ratio of about **0.165**, which is **about 1/6** (NASA Moon fact sheet). We will use **about 1/6**.

**Mass and pull, carefully.** A person’s **mass** does not change on the Moon. The **pull** (weight) is about 1/6 as strong. If a bar of 6 equal units stands for the pull of a given mass on Earth, **1 unit** is the pull of that same mass on the Moon. Do not write “you become 1/6 as heavy in kilograms” as if the mass changed. Kilograms measure mass; the pull is what becomes about 1/6.

**Concrete step.** Take a 6-part bar (or six cubes). One part is 1/6. Two parts are 2/6, which is the same as 1/3 of the same bar.

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Equivalent fractions name the same amount: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number. Moon gravity is about 1/6 of Earth’s (NASA; 1.62 against 9.80 m/s²). Mass stays the same; the pull is about 1/6 as strong. A 6-part bar: 1 part is the Moon’s pull compared with Earth’s.

</div>

## Challenge problems: gravity on Mars, and a careful estimate {#challenge}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · same skills, a new number from NASA</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Mars’s mean surface gravity is about **3.73 m/s²**, against about **9.82 m/s²** on Earth, a ratio of about **0.380** (NASA Mars fact sheet). 0.380 is **38/100**, which simplifies to **19/50**. **2/5 = 0.4**, which is a little more than 0.380, so “about 2/5 of Earth’s gravity” is a convenient estimate, slightly high. When you use 2/5, say that it is an estimate.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Mars gravity ≈ 0.380 ≈ 19/50 of Earth’s. 2/5 = 0.4 is a slightly high estimate. Equivalent fractions must be exact; estimates must be labeled.

</div>

### Practice

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 380/1000 = 19/50 after dividing by 20.
2. 19 units.

## Math circle: proving two fractions are the same amount {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a diagram, then a short argument</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A **proof** here is a reason you can show, not a guess. To prove 2/4 = 1/2, you can: (1) draw two bars of equal length and show the shaded parts match; or (2) start with 1/2 and multiply top and bottom by 2; or (3) cut a halved bar each half into two, making fourths, and see that two fourths cover one half.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Equivalent fractions can be proved with a matching bar, or by multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number. A matching length is the test, not a similar-looking scribble.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A student shades 2/6 of a short bar and 1/3 of a longer bar and says they are equivalent because both look ‘about a third’. What is missing?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>The wholes are not the same size, so the shaded amounts are not being compared as parts of one whole.</li>
<li>Equivalent fractions compare parts of **equal wholes**.</li>
<li>Redraw both fractions on bars of the same length.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>Prove, in two ways, that 4/8 = 1/2.</p>

<details>
<summary>Hint 1: A picture</summary>

Two equal bars: one split into 2 with 1 shaded; one split into 8 with 4 shaded. The shaded lengths match.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The rule</summary>

Divide top and bottom of 4/8 by 4, or multiply 1/2 by 4/4.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>The skill is a reason, not a new fraction fact.</p>

1. Can 3/6 = 2/4? Prove it or show they both equal 1/2.
2. Why is multiplying top and bottom by 0 not allowed?

</div>

### Practice

1. Prove 2/6 = 1/3 with a 6-part bar and with the rule.
2. A friend says 2/5 = 3/6 because ‘you add 1 on the top and 1 on the bottom.’ Show that this is not a legal step.
3. Write a two-sentence argument: *These are equivalent because ________. They would not be equivalent if ________.*
4. On one 12-part bar, mark 1/2, 2/4, 3/6, and 6/12. What do you notice?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Yes: 3/6 = 1/2 and 2/4 = 1/2, so they are equivalent to each other.
2. Multiplying by 0/0 is meaningless, and multiplying only the top or only the bottom by 0 would change the amount (or make a zero denominator, which is not a fraction). The rule needs the same non-zero number on both.

1. A 6-part bar with 2 shaded is 1/3 of the bar; 2/6 ÷ 2/2 = 1/3.
2. Adding 1 to each gives 3/6 = 1/2, which is not 2/5 (2/5 = 4/10, 1/2 = 5/10). Adding the same number to top and bottom does not keep the amount.
3. A real because about the same amount or the same multiply/divide step, and a real if about unequal wholes or a wrong step.
4. All four marks reach the same place, the middle of the bar.
