## Unit check {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · alone, no calculator</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

This is a check of skills already taught in Units 1 and 2, not a new lesson. Use place-value discs or a chart, number bonds, rounding only when a table or model needs fewer places, comparison from the left, column methods, mental splits, and part-whole or comparison bars.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

×10 moves every digit one place left. A number bond splits a whole into parts. Round by looking at the next place. The difference of a whole and a part is the other part.

</div>

### Unit check

Work alone. Use the methods from these two units: place-value discs and charts, number bonds, rounding when a table or model needs it, comparison from the left, column methods, and bar models.

**Part A: number**

1. Jupiter is about 2,867 million km from the Sun (rounded). Write this in kilometers. What is 10 times that distance?
2. Show 1,632,450 on a place-value chart (or with discs). Write a number bond that has the hundred-thousands part as one piece.
3. Round 49,528 to the nearest thousand. In a table that only needs the nearest ten thousand, what do you write? Are both answers the same? Why?
4. Compare from the left: which is smaller, 51,118 or 49,528? Which is larger, 120,536 or 142,984? Then list all four from smallest to largest: 120,536; 51,118; 142,984; 49,528.
5. What number is 100,000 less than 1,042,000? Think of taking away one hundred-thousand disc.

**Part B: calculation**

6. 68,457 + 24,986
7. 90,000 − 37,465
8. 248 × 6
9. 35 × 24 (show your area model)
10. 952 ÷ 7

**Part C: problems** (draw a bar model for each)

11. The Moon is about 384,400 km away. A spacecraft has 129,850 km still to go. How far has it travelled?
12. A rover drives 125 m each day. How far does it drive in 4 weeks?
13. Radio messages take 500 seconds to come from a probe. How many minutes and seconds is that?
14. A planet's year lasts 225 Earth days (close to Venus's 224.7). How many full weeks is that, and how many days left over?
15. Two moons have diameters that add up to 5,000 km. One is 1,200 km wider than the other. How wide is each?

### Answer key

1. **2,867,000,000 km**; 10 times is **28,670,000,000 km**.
2. Chart: 1 million, 6 hundred-thousands, 3 ten-thousands, 2 thousands, 4 hundreds, 5 tens, 0 ones. Bond: 1,632,450 = 600,000 + **1,032,450**.
3. Nearest thousand: **50,000**. Nearest ten thousand: **50,000**. Yes, both are 50,000, because 49,528 sits 472 below 50,000, which is the rounding point for both cuts.
4. 49,528 is smaller than 51,118 (same ten-thousands, then 9 thousands vs 1). 142,984 is larger than 120,536. Order: **49,528; 51,118; 120,536; 142,984**.
5. **942,000**
6. **93,443** · 7. **52,535** · 8. **1,488** · 9. 30 × 24 + 5 × 24 = 720 + 120 = **840** · 10. **136**
11. 384,400 − 129,850 = **254,550 km** · 12. 125 × 28 = **3,500 m** · 13. **8 min 20 s** · 14. 225 = 32 × 7 + 1: **32 weeks and 1 day** · 15. Remove the extra 1,200: 3,800 shared equally is 1,900. **1,900 km and 3,100 km.**

**Scoring:** each part out of 5. Mastery: at least 4 in every part.

## Challenge problems {#challenge}

<p class="meta">25 minutes</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

Distance ÷ speed (or ÷ rate) gives time. 384,400 km at 1,000 km/h is 384.4 hours; then convert hours to days (÷24) and leftover hours. For two three-digit numbers using 1–6 once each, the **sum** is largest when the biggest digits are in the hundreds places of both numbers. Light-time: distance = speed × time, with time in seconds.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Hours ÷ 24 gives days and leftover hours. Largest sum: biggest digits in the highest places of *both* addends. distance = 300,000 km/s × seconds.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A craft travels 1,000 km every hour. How many whole days to travel 48,000 km, and how many hours left over?</p><ol class='steps'><li>48,000 ÷ 1,000 = 48 hours.</li><li>48 ÷ 24 = 2 days exactly, no leftover hours.</li></ol>

</div>

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2: Distance</summary>

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

### Practice

1. A spacecraft leaves Earth and travels 1,000 km every hour. How many whole days would it take to reach the Moon (384,400 km)? How many hours are left over in the last day?
2. The digits 1 to 6 are each used once to make two three-digit numbers. What is the largest possible sum? The smallest?
3. A probe sends a photo that takes 1 hour 20 minutes to arrive at the speed of light (300,000 km per second). About how far away is the probe?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 2 days and 2 hours (48 + 2).

1. 384,400 km at 1,000 km/h takes 384.4 hours. 384 ÷ 24 = 16 exactly. So **16 full days**, then **0.4 of an hour** (24 minutes) more, landing on the 17th day.
2. Largest: put the biggest digits in the hundreds places: 6 and 5 in the hundreds, 4 and 3 in the tens, 2 and 1 in the ones: 600 + 500 + 40 + 30 + 2 + 1 = **1,173** (for example 642 + 531). Smallest: 1 and 2 in the hundreds, 3 and 4 in the tens, 5 and 6 in the ones: **381** (for example 135 + 246).
3. 1 h 20 min = 80 minutes = 4,800 seconds. 4,800 × 300,000 = **1,440,000,000 km**: about 1.4 billion km, roughly as far as Saturn is from the Sun.

## Math circle: the broken chessboard {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · an invariant: something that never changes, whatever you do</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A chessboard is coloured in alternating black and white squares. A domino covers two squares that share a side, so it always covers **one black and one white**. If you remove two squares of the same color, the remaining board has unequal colors and cannot be covered.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

A domino covers one black and one white. Opposite corners of a chessboard are the same color.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>A 2 × 2 board, two black and two white. Remove two opposite corners (both the same color, say both black). Two white squares remain, and no blacks. One domino needs one of each, so two leftover whites cannot be covered.</p>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>On a 4 × 4 board, how many black and how many white squares are there to start with? What color are two opposite corners?</p>

<details>
<summary>Hint 1: 4 × 4</summary>

16 squares, 8 black and 8 white.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2: Opposite corners</summary>

The same color. After they are removed: 6 of that color and 8 of the other.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.</p>

1. Why can a single domino never cover two opposite corners?

</div>

### Practice

Draw a 4 × 4 board of squares, coloured like a chessboard. Cross out two opposite corner squares. Can you cover the remaining 14 squares exactly with 7 dominoes, where each domino covers two squares side by side?

Try it. Then count the colors of the remaining squares, and explain what every domino always covers. Does the same argument work for a full 8 × 8 chessboard with opposite corners removed?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Opposite corners do not share a side, and they are the same color; a domino covers two neighbouring squares of opposite colors.

**It is impossible.** Opposite corners are the same color, so removing them leaves 8 squares of one color and only 6 of the other. Every domino, wherever it is placed, covers one dark and one light square. Seven dominoes would cover 7 of each, but the board has 8 and 6. The same argument works on the 8 × 8 board (32 and 30 remain after removing two same-coloured corners, but 31 dominoes would need 31 of each).

## Notebook: design a mission {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes, and as long as you like over the weekend · a short paragraph from an outline · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html) · [Review sheet](../printables/d10-notebook.html)</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

A mission page should show a spacecraft (two views), a destination, and at least one calculated fact you have already learned: a distance, a travel time at a stated speed, or a light-time (distance ÷ 300,000 km per second). Labels first, then a short because/but/so explanation of a design choice or a number on the page.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

### Practice

<p class="note"><strong>How the guide helps today:</strong> the design must be the student's. Help with looking up numbers already in this unit, and with the outline. As on Day 9, notes in the outline are not full sentences. If handwriting is tiring after the unit check, the guide may scribe the paragraph *exactly as spoken*, and the student copies or traces it.</p>

Choose a destination: the Moon, Mars, Jupiter or a place of your choice. On a double page:

**1. Draw** your spacecraft from two views (side and front), with labels, the way Leonardo da Vinci drew machines.

**2. Label** the parts and the destination.

**Word bank:** spacecraft · destination · Moon · Mars · Jupiter · distance · light-time · travel · power · food · message · problem · question

**3. Write.** First an outline:

- Topic sentence (in full): *This mission goes to ________.*
- Notes: distance / light-time · one problem and an idea · a second problem and an idea
- Closing sentence (in full): a question you would need an expert to answer, written as a sentence.

Then write a short paragraph from the outline (four to six sentences is plenty).

**4. Numbers.** Write the distance (from the unit's tables), the light-time for messages, and a travel time if your craft averages a speed you choose (show the division). List three problems the mission must solve and one idea for each, if they did not all fit in the paragraph.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Time = distance ÷ speed, using about **300,000 km per second**.

There is no single answer. A strong page has accurate numbers taken from the lessons, correct calculations shown in full, clear labelled drawings, an outline, and a short paragraph that follows it. Keep it: the notebook will grow into a record of every unit.
