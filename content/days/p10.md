# Level 2 · Day 10 · Level 2 check

**Today's big question:** Can you use places to 10,000, regrouping, and both kinds of bar, and keep NASA and invented heights labeled?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: discs for place value, columns with carries, and the first part-whole and comparison bars. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. The Saturn V height used in this level is 111 meters (363 feet), from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?” Do not replace that figure with an invented classroom height, and do not average it with another official figure. Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes; measure a shadow, not the Sun. If the placement check started the student here, Level 1 greetings and numbers 1–10 may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later bars with larger numbers; today’s bars are a first look.</p>

### Unit check

Work alone. Use discs, columns, or a bar when it helps. For the Saturn V, use 111 m (363 feet) from the NASA student page taught this level. Invented classroom rockets and shadows stay labeled invented. Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: place value to 10,000**

1. Write 4,036 as thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones.
2. What number is 7 thousands, 2 hundreds, 0 tens, and 5 ones?
3. What is 4,036 + 1,000? What is 4,036 − 1,000?
4. How many thousands make 10,000?
5. Why is 4,036 not the same number as 436?

**Part B: addition, and a part-whole bar**

6. Find 285 + 347. Show the carries.
7. Check 285 + 347 with an estimate.
8. Find 186 + 157.
9. Invented (labeled): 63 m and 45 m classroom rockets, stacked. What is the whole? Which bar is this?
10. Find 475 + 268.

**Part C: subtraction, comparison, and this level’s space work**

11. Find 1,000 − 264. Check with an addition.
12. Invented: Rocket A is 63 m, Rocket B is 18 m shorter. How tall is B? How tall are they stacked?
13. Invented shadows: 80 cm in the morning, 20 cm near midday, same stick. How much longer is the morning shadow? Why is the midday shadow shorter?
14. What height does this level use for the Saturn V, and from which page?
15. Why must a 63 m classroom rocket not replace the Saturn V height?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 3. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

**Check.**

6. Ones 5 + 7 = 12, write 2, carry 1; tens 8 + 4 + 1 = 13, write 3, carry 1; hundreds 2 + 3 + 1 = 6. **632**.
7. 300 + 350 = 650 (or a close estimate), so 632 is reasonable.

**Part C.**
