### Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar or a balance drawing when it helps. Keep invented masses, distances, and classroom rules labeled invented. Keep NASA figures labeled as Planetary Fact Sheet figures (page last updated 13 November 2026). Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: unknowns and equations**

1. A bar has a whole of 22, with parts n and 9. Write the equation and find n.
2. n − 7 = 11. Find n, and say what you did to both sides.
3. 5n = 40. Find n, and check.
4. 2n + 6 = 18. Find n, and check.
5. Why must the same letter mean the same number each time it appears in one problem?

**Part B: patterns and rules**

1. The list 5, 9, 13, 17, … follows a rule. What is the rule, and what is the next number?
2. The nth number is 4n + 1. What is the 5th number, and how do you check the 2nd?
3. The rule is n → 2n + 3. What is the output for n = 6?
4. The same rule. The output is 11. What was the input?
5. In the NASA fact-sheet table used on Day 38, Earth is 149.6 million km from the Sun with a period of 365.2 days, and Mars is 228.0 million km with a period of 687.0 days. What pattern do those figures show, and why is “a Mars year is exactly 2 Earth years” a miss?

**Part C: the balance and the lever**

1. A pan balance is level with n + 5 on the left and 12 on the right. Find n.
2. Why do we do the same thing to both sides of an equation?
3. A labeled invented lever has a mass of 3 at 6 units on the left, and a mass of 9 on the right. How far from the fulcrum must the 9 sit to balance, in this course’s first model?
4. In that same model, a labeled invented lever has n at 4 units on the left, and 8 at 3 units on the right, and it balances. Find n.
5. Why will this check not invent a NASA crane figure, and why will it not put the Moon’s 27.3-day fact-sheet period into the “year around the Sun” pattern?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 12. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Three headings, ready for the sitting.
2. A note that extra items are not requested; four out of five is the mastery rule, not a score.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. n + 9 = 22, so n = 13. Check: 13 + 9 = 22.
2. Add 7 to both sides: n = 18. Check: 18 − 7 = 11.
3. Divide both sides by 5: n = 8. Check: 5 × 8 = 40.
4. Subtract 6 from both sides: 2n = 12, so n = 6. Check: 2 × 6 + 6 = 18.
5. The letter is a name for one unknown number in that problem. If it changed mid-problem, the picture and the check would fall apart.

**Part B.**
1. Add 4 each time; the next number is 21.
2. 4 × 5 + 1 = 21; the 2nd is 4 × 2 + 1 = 9.
3. 2 × 6 + 3 = 15.
4. 11 − 3 = 8, and 8 ÷ 2 = 4. Check: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11.
5. Mars is farther from the Sun and has the longer year. 687.0 ÷ 365.2 is about 1.88, a little less than 2, so “exactly 2” would force a neater number than the sheet’s figures give.

**Part C.**
1. n + 5 = 12, so n = 7. Check: 7 + 5 = 12.
2. An equation says the two sides are equal. A move that changes only one side makes that sentence untrue (and would tip a pan-balance picture).
3. 3 × 6 = 18, so 9 × d = 18 and d = 2 units. Invented lever.
4. n × 4 = 8 × 3 = 24, so n = 6. Invented lever. Check: 6 × 4 = 24.
5. This course has no sourced NASA crane figure, so one is not invented. The Moon’s 27.3-day period is marked with an asterisk on the fact sheet: it is about the Moon and Earth, not a year around the Sun.
