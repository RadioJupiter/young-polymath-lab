## Core lesson: dividing into equal groups, and 54 ÷ 6 {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


**Division** shares a whole into equal groups, or asks how many equal groups fit. **54 ÷ 6** can mean: 54 in 6 equal groups, how many in each group? Or: how many 6s fit into 54?

Concrete: 54 counters shared into 6 piles (invented classroom counters, labeled invented). Each pile has **9**. Pictorial: an array of 6 rows of 9 is 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9, and 54 ÷ 9 = 6.

**Abstract:** 6 × 9 = 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9 and 54 ÷ 9 = 6. These four facts are a **fact family**: 6 × 9, 9 × 6, 54 ÷ 6, 54 ÷ 9.

Check a division by multiplying: 6 × 9 = 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9 is right.

Count in 6s to 54: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54 — nine 6s.

<p class="note">The length of the month used in this level is from the NASA Moon fact sheet: the Moon’s synodic period is 29.53 days, taught here as <strong>about 29½ days</strong>. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html This level does not invent a finer official length, and does not begin the sidereal month or Moon phases as fractions (those belong later). Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact used here, not a NASA orbital period.</p>


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

54 ÷ 6 = 9, because 6 × 9 = 54. Division is the other side of multiplication. Check by multiplying. Classroom shares are invented and labeled invented.

</div>

### Practice

1. Write the fact family for 6, 9, and 54.
2. A student writes 54 ÷ 6 = 48 because 54 − 6 = 48. What was mixed?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 9.
2. 4 (because 4 × 9 = 36).

1. 6 × 9 = 54, 9 × 6 = 54, 54 ÷ 6 = 9, 54 ÷ 9 = 6.
2. 48 is taking 6 away once, a subtraction of one 6, not sharing 54 into 6 equal groups. Sharing gives 9 in each group.

## Math circle: why a multiplication checks a division {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

If you share 54 into 6 equal groups of 9, putting the groups back together must make 54 again. That is why 6 × 9 = 54 checks 54 ÷ 6 = 9.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

A division and its multiplication are two views of the same equal groups. The check is the groups put back as a whole.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Why does 6 × 9 = 54 show that 54 ÷ 6 = 9 is right?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>6 groups of 9 make the whole 54.</li>
<li>If the groups make the whole, each group of the share was 9.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>A student checks 54 ÷ 6 by computing 54 − 6 and getting 48, then stopping. Why is that not a complete check?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Subtracting 6 once is not the same as making 6 equal groups.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

A complete check multiplies: 6 × 9 = 54.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These defend the check.</p>

1. What multiplication checks 36 ÷ 4 = 9?
2. What division belongs with 7 × 8 = 56, besides 56 ÷ 7?

</div>

### Practice

1. Write a short argument that 54 ÷ 6 = 9, with a check.
2. Why are the 54 counters labeled invented?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 4 × 9 = 36, or 9 × 4 = 36.
2. 56 ÷ 8 = 7.

1. 6 equal groups of 9 make 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9. Check: 6 × 9 = 54.
2. They are a classroom share, not an official NASA count of rocks or days.

## Notebook: 54 ÷ 6, and a fact family {#notebook}
