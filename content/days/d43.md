# Unit 9 · Day 43 · A line graph of change, and sunspots

**Today's big question:** How does a line graph show change over time, and what do official yearly sunspot means show?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> A line graph shows change over time. The yearly means are from WDC-SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium, file SN_y_tot_V2.0 (2019: 3.6; 2020: 8.8; 2021: 29.6; 2022: 83.2; 2023: 125.5; 2024: 154.7; 2025: 123.2). A mid-year date such as 2024.5 is the mean for that year. A NASA Marshall Solar Physics page (updated 15 March 2017) says sunspots wax and wane with an approximate 11-year cycle; that page is not the source of these yearly numbers. In the same SILSO series, 2008 is 4.2 and 2019 is 3.6, eleven years later: one spacing, not a promise that every cycle lasts exactly eleven years. A rise is not a law that the next official year will rise, and a 2026 official mean is not invented.</p>

A **line graph** is a way to show how a quantity changes over time. Each point is a value at a year, and the line segments join the points in time order so you can see a rise or a fall.

The figures below are **yearly mean total sunspot numbers** from the World Data Center SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium, in the file SN_y_tot_V2.0. A mid-year date such as 2024.5 is the mean for the year 2024. A yearly mean is the average of that year’s daily totals, so one day’s look at the Sun can be different from the yearly mean.

| Year | Yearly mean |
| ---: | ---: |
| 2019 | 3.6 |
| 2020 | 8.8 |
| 2021 | 29.6 |
| 2022 | 83.2 |
| 2023 | 125.5 |
| 2024 | 154.7 |
| 2025 | 123.2 |

A NASA page on the sunspot cycle (Marshall Solar Physics, last updated 15 March 2017) says that the number of sunspots waxes and wanes with an approximate 11-year cycle, and that the International Sunspot Number is compiled in Belgium. That 2017 page is not the source of the yearly numbers in the table; those come from the SILSO file opened for this unit.

In that same SILSO series, the yearly mean was 4.2 in 2008 and 3.6 in 2019, eleven years later. That is one spacing in this official series. It is not a promise that every cycle lasts exactly eleven years, and it is not a number we made up.

On the line graph, 2024 is the highest point in this window (154.7), and 2019 is the lowest (3.6). The 2025 mean is 123.2, which is lower than 2024. A rise is something the line already shows. “It will keep rising next year” is a new claim, and this course has no official 2026 yearly mean to read yet, so that claim is not made here.

## Math circle: a rise is not a law, and a future official mean is not invented {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · a reason about what a line graph can and cannot claim</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>
