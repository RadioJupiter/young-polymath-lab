# Level 1 · Day 2 · Making ten

**Today's big question:** How can a bond of 10 help you add 8 + 7 without guessing?

<p class="note"><strong>For the guide:</strong> Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete step in the core lesson, even if the student already seems quick: discs, a ten-frame, or a bond are the foundation. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. If the placement check started the student here, this is the first mathematics of the course, and Spanish and Latin also begin here. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that recap is for students who skipped these foundation levels.</p>

## Mental math warm-up {#mental}

<p class="meta">10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

If you know a bond of 10, you can use it to add. 8 + 2 = 10. So 8 + 5 is 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = **13**. This is called **making ten**.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

To add a number to 8 or 9, make 10 first, then add what is left.

</div>

</ol>

## Core lesson: making ten, and addition facts {#core}

<p class="meta">45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


The **addition facts** for this level are the sums you should come to know well, especially facts that make 10 and facts through 10 + 10. You do not only memorize them. You **make ten**, and you use a **number bond**.

<div class="nbond" aria-hidden="true"><div class="whole">10</div><div class="arms"></div><div class="parts"><span>8</span><span>2</span></div></div>

8 and 2 make 10. If the second number is larger than the part you need, split it. For **8 + 7**:

- 8 needs 2 more to make 10.
- 7 is 2 + 5.
- 8 + 7 = 10 + 5 = **15**.

You can show this with ten-frames or with counters: fill a ten, then see the extras. That is the concrete step. The bond is the picture. 8 + 7 = 15 is the abstract sentence.

An invented classroom group of 8 shining stars and 7 more stars (labeled invented) is just a story for 8 + 7. It is not an official sky count.


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Making ten: split the second number so that the first number reaches 10, then add what is left. 8 + 7 = 8 + 2 + 5 = 15. A bond of 10 is the picture for the first step. 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 are the same total.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Show 8 + 7 by making ten, and write the addition sentence.</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>8 needs 2 to make 10; 7 = 2 + 5.</li>
<li>10 + 5 = 15. So 8 + 7 = 15.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>What is 7 + 8? Do you need a new calculation, and why or why not?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: Order</summary>

7 + 8 is 8 + 7 with the parts swapped.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The total</summary>

The total is still 15.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These use making ten, or a known bond.</p>

1. Find 9 + 6 by making ten.
2. Find 6 + 9. What stays the same?

</div>

### Practice

1. Find 8 + 7 and 5 + 8, each by making ten.
2. A student writes 8 + 7 = 8 + 7, and stops. What step is missing?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 9 + 1 + 5 = 15.
2. 15. The order of the parts changed; the total did not.

1. 8 + 2 + 5 = 15; 5 + 5 + 3 = 13 (or 8 + 5 = 13, with 5 + 5 = 10 and 3 left, if you start from 5). Either making-ten route is accepted if it is shown.
2. The student has not yet made ten or given the total. Show the split and the 10, then the answer.

## Challenge problems {#challenge}

<p class="meta">25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

If 8 + 7 = 15, then 18 + 7 is one ten more: **25**. The ones are still 8 + 7, which make a ten and 5 ones. This uses making ten, not a new fact family for 18.

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

When you add to a number like 18, you can still make a ten with the ones, then add any new ten you made.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>What is 18 + 7, using 8 + 7 = 15?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>The ones are 8 + 7 = 15, which is 1 ten and 5 ones.</li>
<li>1 ten already in 18, plus 1 new ten, plus 5 ones: **25**.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>What is 19 + 6? Make ten with the ones.</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: Nine</summary>

9 + 1 = 10, and 6 = 1 + 5.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The tens</summary>

19 already has 1 ten; the new ten from the ones makes 2 tens, and 5 ones: 25.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These extend making ten into the teens.</p>

1. Find 28 + 5 by making ten with the ones.
2. Why is 8 + 7 not equal to 87?

</div>

### Practice

1. Find 17 + 8 two ways (start from 17, or start from 8) and show that both give the same total.
2. An invented desk has 9 pencils and 7 more are added. How many then? Label the numbers invented.

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 8 + 2 = 10, and 5 = 2 + 3, so the ones make 13, which is 1 ten and 3 ones. 2 tens + 1 ten + 3 ones = **33**.
2. 8 + 7 is a sum of two parts, 15. 87 is 8 tens and 7 ones, a different place-value number.

1. 17 + 3 + 5 = 25, or 8 + 2 + 15 = 25 (from 17 = 2 + 15). Both **25**.
2. 9 + 7 = 16 (9 + 1 + 6). The 9 and 7 are invented classroom numbers.

## Math circle: all the bonds of 10 {#circle}

<p class="meta">20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>

How many **different** bonds of 10 are there, if we count 1 + 9 and 9 + 1 as the same pair of parts (just swapped), and we include 0 + 10 and 5 + 5?

</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

A complete list needs a plan, like yesterday’s counting plan. List in order: 0 + 10, 1 + 9, 2 + 8, …

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>List the bonds of 10 in order, starting at 0 + 10, and stop when the parts start to repeat in the other order. How many different pairs is that?</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>0 + 10, 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6, 5 + 5. Then 6 + 4 is 4 + 6 swapped, so we stop counting new pairs.</li>
<li>That is **6** different pairs if swaps are the same bond. If the question had asked for ordered additions, there would be more (6 + 4 as well as 4 + 6).</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>If we count 3 + 7 and 7 + 3 as two different addition sentences, how many addition sentences make 10 with whole numbers from 0 to 10?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: A list</summary>

0 + 10, 1 + 9, …, 10 + 0.

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: The count</summary>

There are 11 of those sentences.

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">

<h3>Check</h3>

<p>These are about a complete list, with the rule stated.</p>

1. Why must you say whether 8 + 2 and 2 + 8 count as one bond or two sentences?
2. Is 10 + 0 a bond of 10? Why or why not?

</div>

### Practice

1. List every bond of 5, counting swaps as the same pair.
2. A student lists 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6 and says “that is all.” What was missed, if pairs include 0 and 5?

### Answer key

**Check.**
1. Otherwise two people can both be right and still give different counts. The rule must be stated.
2. Yes: 10 and 0 make 10. Zero is a genuine part in this list, even if one part is 0.

1. 0 + 5, 1 + 4, 2 + 3. (3 + 2 is 2 + 3 swapped.) Three pairs if 2.5 is not allowed; we use whole numbers.
2. 0 + 10 and 5 + 5 (and, as sentences, the swaps of the ones already listed).

## Notebook: a bond of 10, and a short reason {#notebook}

<p class="meta">60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html)</p>

<div class="learn" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Learn</h3>


Draw first: a number bond of 10 (two parts that make 10), and a ten-frame or counters showing the same bond. Label the whole and the parts. Then write from stems.

**Word bank:** bond, ten, part, whole, make, split.

**Stems:**

1. 8 and 2 make 10 because …
2. 8 + 7 is 15, but I did not add the 7 all at once: I …
3. I made ten first, so …

End with the number sentence for 8 + 7 (or another making-ten fact you choose) and one “what next” question.


</div>

<div class="remember" data-listen="en-US">

<p class="kicker">Remember</p>

Draw the bond, label the parts, then write. Because / but / so keep the same opening where the stems ask for it. Say each sentence aloud first.

</div>

<div class="watch" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Watch me</h3>

<p>Finish: “8 and 2 make 10 because …”</p>
<ol class="steps">
<li>A model: “8 and 2 make 10 because they are a bond of 10,” or “because 8 needs 2 more to fill a ten.” Other complete finishes accepted.</li>
</ol>

</div>

<div class="together" data-listen="en-US">

<h3>Together</h3>

<p>Finish the but-stem about 8 + 7. What split should appear?</p>
<details>
<summary>Hint 1: Making ten</summary>

</details>
<details>
<summary>Hint 2: A model</summary>

</details>

</div>

<div class="check">
