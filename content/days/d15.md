### Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar or a number bond when it helps. For Moon and Mars facts, use the figures taught this unit (NASA, with rounding stated). Do not use a calculator.

**Part A: parts of a whole, and phases**

1. Draw a bar in 4 equal parts and shade 3/4. What does the 4 mean, and what does the 3 mean?
2. At first quarter, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? Why is the phase still called “quarter”?
3. The synodic month is about 29½ days. What is 1/4 of 29½ days?
4. At new Moon, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? At full Moon?
5. A classroom model month has 12 equal days (a model, not the real synodic month). What fraction of that model has passed after 3 days? After 9 days?

**Part B: equivalent fractions, and gravity**

6. Fill in: 1/2 = ☐/6 = ☐/12.
7. Write 3/6 in simplest form, and show why it is equivalent to your answer.
8. A 6-part bar stands for the pull of a given mass on Earth, and that pull is called 30 units. About how many units is the pull of the **same** mass on the Moon? Does the mass itself change?
9. Is 19/50 equivalent to 2/5? If someone uses 2/5 for Mars’s gravity, what must they say?
10. Prove that 2/12 = 1/6, with a bar or with the multiply/divide rule.

**Part C: adding and subtracting**

11. 3/8 + 2/8. Give a simpler name if there is one.
12. 7/10 − 3/10. Give a simpler name if there is one.
13. 1/2 + 1/6. Show the renaming.
14. 2/3 − 1/6. Show the renaming.
15. A tank is 1/3 full. Then 1/6 of a full tank is added. Draw a bar. How full is the tank now? What fraction is still empty?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 7. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.


### Answer key

**Check.**
1. 3/6.
2. 3/6 = 1/2.

**Unit check.**

**Part A.**
1. A bar of 4 equal parts with 3 shaded. 4 is how many equal parts the whole was cut into; 3 is how many of those parts are shaded.
2. The disk looks **1/2** lit, not 1/4. “Quarter” means about a quarter of the way through the cycle, not a quarter of the disk.
3. **7⅜ days** (29½ ÷ 4 = 7¼ + ⅛ = 7⅜).
4. New: **0**. Full: **1**.
5. 3/12 = **1/4**; 9/12 = **3/4**. (The 12-day month is a model.)

**Part B.**
6. **3/6** and **6/12**.
7. **1/2**, because 3/6 ÷ 3/3 = 1/2, or because a 6-part bar with 3 shaded is half.
8. 1/6 of 30 = **5 units** of pull. The **mass does not change**.
9. **No**: 2/5 = 20/50, not 19/50. 2/5 is a slightly high **estimate** for Mars (about 0.380) and must be labeled as an estimate.
10. Divide top and bottom by 2, or mark 2 parts of a 12-part bar and 1 part of a 6-part bar of the **same length**.

**Part C.**
11. 5/8 (already simplest).
12. 4/10 = **2/5**.
13. 1/2 = 3/6; 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6 = **2/3**.
14. 2/3 = 4/6; 4/6 − 1/6 = 3/6 = **1/2**.
15. 1/3 = 2/6; 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = **1/2** full, so **1/2** is still empty. The bar should show equal sixths (or a 6-part bar).
