Units 1–7 cover Level 4 (big numbers), Level 5 (multiplication and division), Level 6 (fractions, Moon phases, and gravity on other worlds), Level 7 (metric measurement, perimeter, area, and volume, with a labeled Mars-habitat model), Level 8 (angles, polygons, circumference with a labeled π, symmetry, and a first construction), Level 9 (decimals, negative numbers, and sourced planet temperatures), and Level 10 (ratio, scale, rate, and percent, with sourced Earth–Moon sizes and a labeled ISS speed) of the level map. Each day has mental math, a core lesson, challenge problems, a math circle, a short Spanish lesson, a short Latin lesson, and a notebook project (with writing stems and a printable worksheet set).

Math circle topics so far: systematic counting, logic grids, magic squares, parity, the balance puzzle, counting pairs, the pigeonhole principle, divisibility tests, sand-timer puzzles, colouring invariants. Unit 4 adds a comparing argument (is a rounded 6,800 km still honest?), a same-perimeter / different-area observation, a units argument (why a length is not an area), and a last argument about labeling invented models beside sourced facts. Unit 5 adds a reason that vertically opposite angles are equal, a triangle-count reason for (n − 2) × 180°, a scaling reason that C/d is the same for every circle, a first look at why an orbit can be curved (no invented speed), and a last argument about labeling π. Unit 6 adds a grouping reason that each place is ten times smaller than the place on its left, a caution that 0.4 and 0.40 are the same amount but a written measurement can still claim a finer reading, a reason for lining up the decimal point, a reason that a NASA mean may not describe a given place, and a last argument about labeling precision and means. Unit 7 adds a reason that both sides of a ratio change together, a reason that every length in a model uses the same scale, a reason that a speed needs a time and that two rounded NASA figures are not averaged, a reason that a percent needs a named whole, and a last argument about invented mixes, star charts, and sourced wording.
