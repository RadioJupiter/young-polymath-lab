Math circle: why both sides of a ratio change together

Check. 1. For example 2 : 8, or 3 : 12, or 5 : 20 (multiply both sides by the same number). 2. The order matters. 5 : 2 says the first quantity is larger; 2 : 5 says the second is larger.

  1. Both 6 and 9 divide by 3, and 6 ÷ 3 = 2, 9 ÷ 3 = 3. The equal parts are the same comparison.
  2. The first bar would now be twice as long as it was, but the second bar would be unchanged, so the parts would no longer be 2 to 3.
  3. Simplifying divides both sides by a common factor and keeps the exact comparison. Replacing the figures with 4 : 1 changes the comparison (4 × 3,475 is not 12,756).
  4. The two numbers would lose their job as equal parts, and 2 : 3 would be mixed up with a total of 5, or with 3 : 2.
  5. A note that the circle is a reason, not a reward.
