Challenge problems

Check. 1. 7, because 9 + 7 = 16 (or 16 − 9 = 7). 2. 6, because 8 + 6 = 14 (or 14 − 8 = 6).

  1. 13 − 10 = 3, a subtraction fact from a bond of 13. 10 less than 300 is 290, from the hundreds and tens places (Day 1). Both use “10 less,” but 300 is a place-value job.
  2. 13 − 6 = 7. 31 is 3 tens and 1 one; 31 − 6 needs the ones (or regrouping) and is 25, a different whole. The same 6 is not the same problem.

Math circle: a missing part, and a reason

Check. 1. 7, because 3 + 7 = 10. There is only one whole-number partner of 3 that makes 10. 2. No: 8 + 7 = 15, so the partner is 7 only.

  1. 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 are the same two parts, so they make the same whole, 15. 15 − 8 takes the part 8 away and leaves 7; 15 − 7 takes the part 7 away and leaves 8. Those are the two parts, not two values for the same subtraction.
  2. A model: whole 12, part 5; other part 7, because 12 − 5 = 7 and 5 + 7 = 12. Other honest invented bonds accepted.
