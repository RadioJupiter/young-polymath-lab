Challenge problems: mixed times, and a labeled orbit product

Check. 1. 8 × 0.75 = 6 km. Invented. 2. 6 hours = 360 minutes; 360 ÷ 90 = 4 orbits.

  1. 15 minutes = 0.25 h; 28,000 × 0.25 = 7,000 km, FAQ figure.
  2. The units are not the same: 28,000 km/h against 300,000 km/s. In one second, light goes about 300,000 km; the ISS, at 28,000 km/h, goes 28,000 ÷ 3,600 km in one second, which is a little under 8 km — far slower than light.
  3. 28,000 km/h was a NASA figure for the ISS, not for Earth’s orbit around the Sun. We will not move a labeled figure onto a different body.
  4. 20 minutes is 1/3 of an hour, so 2 ÷ (1/3) = 6 km/h. Or 20 minutes × 3 = 1 hour, and 2 km × 3 = 6 km. Invented run.
  5. A note that the wrap is extra thinking, not a reward.

Math circle: a speed needs a time, and a figure needs a label

Check. 1. Speed = distance ÷ time; distance = speed × time; time = distance ÷ speed. 2. The units: if speed is in km/h, time is in hours (30 minutes written as 0.5 h), or we work in the same time unit throughout.

  1. The units differ. In one second, light goes about 300,000 km; the ISS goes only a few kilometers (28,000 ÷ 3,600, a little under 8 km).
  2. 24 hours is 1,440 minutes, and 1,440 ÷ 90 = 16. The two statements are the same count, worded two ways.
  3. It is a product of two already-rounded FAQ figures, useful as a calculation, not a surveyed circumference given as its own NASA measurement in this lesson.
  4. The first car’s speed is twice the second car’s speed, because the times are equal and speed is distance per time. Both cars are invented.
  5. A note that the circle is a reason, not a score.
