Attempt every exercise in writing before opening this page. Mark your work, then rework every miss from scratch the next day.

Mental math warm-up: halves and quarters of friendly numbers

Check. 1. 60. 2. 10.

  1. 8; 400; 2,500.
  2. 25; 12; 50.
  3. 30 (a quarter of 40 is 10; three quarters are 30).
  4. 45, then 11¼.
  5. The number is 72; a quarter of it is 18.

Core lesson: parts of a whole, and Moon phases

Check. 1. A bar of 4 equal parts with 3 shaded. 4 is how many equal parts the whole was cut into; 3 is how many parts are shaded. 2. Full: 1 (the whole near-side disk). New: 0 (none of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it).

  1. Three clear, equal-part pictures, correctly labeled.
  2. More: from 2/4 to 3/4, so one more quarter of the window is covered.
  3. It tracks about 3/4 of the way through the cycle; the lit fraction of the disk is again 1/2.
  4. 3/4 of 29½ = 3 × 7⅜ = 22⅛ days (3 × 7 = 21 and 3 × 3/8 = 9/8 = 1⅛, and 21 + 1⅛ = 22⅛).
  5. A sentence such as: First quarter is called quarter because about a quarter of the month has passed, but the disk looks half lit.

Challenge problems: fractions of a cycle

Check. 1. 13⅔ days. 2. The synodic month measures phase to phase; the sidereal month measures a trip relative to the stars. Earth has also moved, so the Moon must go a little farther to show the same phase again.

  1. 3/12 = 1/4; 9/12 = 3/4. (The 12-day month is a model.)
  2. 14¾ days.
  3. First quarter looks 1/2 lit, not 1/4; shade half the disk.
  4. 29.53 − 27.32 = 2.21 days, about 2.2 days.

Math circle: sharing equally, and a ‘fair’ cut

Check. 1. 1/3 each; a bar in three equal parts with one part marked. 2. That largest piece is more than 1/4, because the parts were not equal. 1/4 is one of four equal parts.

  1. Two different, actually equal fourths (halves then halves, or midlines both ways, or other equal-area cuts).
  2. 3/12 = 1/4; 4/12 = 1/3.
  3. The pieces might not be equal, so you cannot name them 1/4 until they are.
  4. A real because-sentence about equal parts, and a real ‘if’ about unequal parts.

Spanish · The days of the week, and hoy es…

Check. 1. Hoy es lunes. 2. lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo.

  1. Hoy es jueves. El domingo miro el cielo.
  2. Wednesday; Sunday.
  3. Two grammatical sentences with a day-word and es or el + day.

Latin · Lūna in caelō

Check. 1. Nox est. 2. Lūna est clāra.

  1. Stellae sunt in caelō. Lūna est in caelō.
  2. Stellae sunt in caelō (sunt with a plural).
  3. Two grammatical sentences that really use est and sunt.

Notebook: the phase wheel and a because-sentence

Check. 1. Draw first (wheel and disk). Writing comes after the labels, not as a substitute for the picture. 2. The wheel is not itself a picture of how much of the disk is lit at first quarter.

Practice. A four-part wheel correctly ordered, plus a half-shaded first-quarter disk (not a quarter-shaded disk). Labels from the word bank, said then written. Stems: about 29½ days (or 29.53, with the rounding stated); a quarter of the cycle, but the disk looks half lit; because we see half of the near side lit; a true so-sentence (for example, so what changes is how much of the lit half we can see). Complete sentences, the student's own words.
