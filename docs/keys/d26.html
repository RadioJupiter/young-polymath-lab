Math circle: why the next place is ten times smaller

Check. 1. One whole; one tenth. 2. The point only marks where the ones place ends. The size of each digit comes from its place (tenths, hundredths), not from the point “halving” anything.

  1. 0.3 is 3 tenths; 0.03 is 3 hundredths; ten hundredths make one tenth, so ten times 3 hundredths is 3 tenths.
  2. 0.25 is 25/100, and 25/100 = 1/4. The two digits are 2 tenths and 5 hundredths, not a rule that “two digits means a quarter.”
  3. Place value gives the 1 its job as 1 tenth and the 4 its job as 4 hundredths. Calling 3.14 an approximation of π is a separate label about how we used the number in Level 8.
  4. 0 ones, 1 tenth, 0 hundredths — that is 1 tenth, the same amount as 0.1.
  5. We would no longer know whether a 4 was 4 tenths or 4 hundredths, and we could not compare or add the numbers by place.
