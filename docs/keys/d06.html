Math circle: how many high fives?

15. Way 1: the first astronaut high-fives 5 others; the second has already done the first, so 4 new; then 3, 2, 1: 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15. Way 2: each of the 6 high-fives 5 others, giving 6 × 5 = 30, but that counts every high five twice (once for each person in it), so 30 ÷ 2 = 15. Rule: for n astronauts, n × (n − 1) ÷ 2. Check: 2 → 1, 3 → 3, 4 → 6.

Notebook: spotting the Station

The drawing shows the orbit hugging the Earth, only 4 mm out: the ISS is very close to Earth compared with the Moon (384 cm away on the same scale). It looks slow because it is hundreds of kilometres away; distant fast things cross our view slowly, as an aeroplane high in the sky seems to crawl. Sample but: but it is moving at about 28,000 km per hour. Sample so: so I will need to watch a wide stretch of sky. Other honest endings are fine.
