Challenge problems: a missing length, and two honest scales

Check. 1. 40. 2. 3.5 cm. Invented map.

  1. 4 : 6 multiplies both sides of 2 : 3 by 2. 2 : 6 multiplies only the second side, so it is a different comparison (1 : 3, not 2 : 3).
  2. NASA’s wording is already “roughly 400,” not “exactly 400.” Our already-taught rounded figures give about 390. We keep both, labeled, and we do not blend them into a homemade official 395.
  3. 2.5 × 1,000 = 2,500 km.
  4. The picture would no longer be to scale: the sizes would mean 1 cm to 1,000 km, but the gap would mean something else, and a reader could not trust a measured centimeter.
  5. A note that the wrap is extra thinking, not a reward.

Math circle: every length uses the same scale

Check. 1. Day 5: 1 cm : 70,000 km, to walk a Solar System around a 20 cm Sun. Today: 1 cm : 1,000 km, to see Earth and the Moon as sizes. 2. We keep the sizes to scale and write the gap as 384.4 cm, or about 3.84 m.

  1. Each scale is a labeled ratio for a job. A model of the whole Solar System needs a smaller scale than a model of Earth and the Moon.
  2. No. A 1-by-1 square has area 1; a 2-by-2 square has area 4, which is four times, not two. Length and area are different kinds. Today we only need that caution.
  3. “Roughly 400” already allows a nearby figure. 150,000,000 ÷ 384,400 is about 390. We label both and we do not average them into a new official number.
  4. 1,391,400 ÷ 3,475 is about 400.4, and 150,000,000 ÷ 384,400 is about 390. Forcing both to 400 hides the difference.
  5. A note that the circle is a reason, not a score.
