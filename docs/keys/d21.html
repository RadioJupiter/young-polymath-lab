Core lesson: angles, and a turn

Check. 1. 130°, obtuse; 50° is acute. 2. 360° ÷ 3 = 120° each.

  1. The opposite angle is 125°. The two adjacent angles are 55° each (180° − 125°). Check: 125 + 55 + 125 + 55 = 360.
  2. No. A right angle is 90°, so its partner on a straight line would be 90°, which is right, not acute.
  3. 120°, obtuse (greater than 90° and less than 180°).
  4. A crossing with 40° opposite 40°, and the other pair 140° and 140°.
  5. 360° is the agreed size of a full turn in this degree system. It is not a NASA figure and not a length.

Challenge problems: a missing angle, and a turn that is not a length

Check. 1. The adjacent pair add to 180° and are 1:3, so 45° and 135°. The four angles are 45°, 135°, 45°, 135°. 2. A net turn of 25° from the start. A turn is not a length, so it is not a distance across a crater or across the Moon.

  1. Yes: for example 50° + 60° + 70° = 180°, and each is acute.
  2. 90° is a turn, not a length. Kilometers measure distance. The unit does not match the job.
  3. 2/5 of 180° is 72°. The other angle is 108°.

Math circle: why vertically opposite angles are equal

Check. 1. a + b = 180° on a straight line; the angle opposite a also + b = 180°, so it equals a. 2. Without “adjacent angles on a straight line add to 180°,” we would not have a reason that both angles equal 180° − b.

  1. Any clear sentence that keeps degrees as a turn, not as centimeters or kilometers.

Spanish · A turn: a la derecha, a la izquierda

Check. 1. Gira a la izquierda. 2. Hay un ángulo recto.

  1. un ángulo recto, not *ángulo derecho.
  2. Three heard words, each used.
  3. A la derecha is a direction (to the right). Recto here marks a 90° angle.
