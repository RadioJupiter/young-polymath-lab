Remember

Name the job and the unit (° for a turn, cm or km for a length). Label every approximation of π and every rounding. Invented classroom shapes stay labeled. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A angles and polygons, Part B circles and circumference, Part C symmetry and a construction. Then begin Part A when the guide says.

2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

3. An angle of 47° sits on a straight line with another angle. Find the other angle, and name both (acute or obtuse).

4. Two lines cross. One angle is 110°. Find the other three.

5. A triangle has angles 25° and 90°. Find the third.

6. A quadrilateral has angles 70°, 80°, 95°, and one missing. Find the missing angle.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
