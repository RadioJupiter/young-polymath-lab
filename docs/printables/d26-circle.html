Math circle: why the next place is ten times smaller

Unit 6 · Day 26 · Tenths, hundredths, and place value · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

NameDate

Remember

Each place is ten times the place on its right. The decimal point marks the ones place; it is not a mark that “makes the number small” by itself.

1. Complete: ten tenths make ____, and ten hundredths make ____.

2. Why is the decimal point not a “times a half” mark?

3. Give one reason 0.3 is 10 times 0.03.

4. A friend says 0.25 is “a quarter because there are two digits.” What is the honest reason it is a quarter?

5. Does 3.14 have a tenths digit because it is an approximation of π, or because of place value? (Both facts can be true — say which job each fact does.)

6. Draw a quick place-value chart for 0.10 and say why it is one tenth.
