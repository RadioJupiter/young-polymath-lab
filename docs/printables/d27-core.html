Remember

Compare decimals in the same place, usually hundredths. 0.4 = 0.40 as an amount. 0.08 < 0.1 < 0.8. A trailing zero does not change the amount; on a measurement it can still mark a finer reading. Invented lengths stay labeled.

1. Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58? Write both as hundredths.

2. Write 0.5 as hundredths, and say whether 0.5 and 0.50 are the same amount.

3. Order from least to greatest: 0.4, 0.04, 0.39, 0.40.

4. Mark 0.25 on a tenths number line (it sits halfway from 0.2 to 0.3).

5. Why is 0.09 less than 0.1?

6. A friend says 0.40 is “bigger than 0.4 because of the extra zero.” What do we say about the amount, and what smaller caution can we still keep about a measurement?
