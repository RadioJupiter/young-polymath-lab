Core lesson: rate, and speed as distance per time

Unit 7 · Day 33 · Rate, and speed as distance per time · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

Speed = distance ÷ time, with matching units. Distance = speed × time; time = distance ÷ speed. Invented walks stay labeled. Light is about 300,000 km/s, as already taught. The ISS is about 28,000 km/h from NASA’s Spot the Station FAQ; “five miles per second” is another NASA rounded figure, not averaged in. 28,000 km/h × 1.5 h = 42,000 km is a product of two rounded FAQ figures, not a surveyed orbit length.

1. An invented walk is 20 km in 4 hours, steadily. What is the speed, and how long for 10 km at that speed?

2. How far does light travel in 3 seconds, at the already-taught about 300,000 km/s?

3. At 28,000 km/h, how far in 30 minutes? (First write 30 minutes as a fraction of an hour.)

4. Time = distance ÷ speed. At 28,000 km/h, how many hours for 84,000 km? Label the speed as the FAQ figure.

5. Why do we not average “about 17,500 mph” with “five miles per second” to make a homemade official speed?

6. An invented bus goes 12 km in 15 minutes. What is that in km/h? (15 minutes = 0.25 h, or 12 km in a quarter hour is 48 km in one hour.)
