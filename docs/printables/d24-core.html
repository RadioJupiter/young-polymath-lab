Remember

A line of symmetry folds a figure onto itself. A circle has many (every diameter). The perpendicular bisector of AB is the line of symmetry of AB; it meets AB at its midpoint, at a right angle. Construction: same compass opening from each end; the line through the two crossings is the perpendicular bisector. A real crater is not perfectly symmetric.

1. How many lines of symmetry does the pictured isosceles triangle have, if only two sides are equal (the two long sides in the figure)?

2. Name one line of symmetry of a circle, and say why “every diameter” is a fair answer.

3. A non-square rectangle: how many lines of symmetry? (Not the diagonals.)

4. Why is a diagonal of that rectangle not a line of symmetry?

5. List the four construction steps in your own words, including “same opening.”

6. Tycho is about 85 km across (NASA). Why should we not say “Tycho has infinitely many lines of symmetry” without a caution?
