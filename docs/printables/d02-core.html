Core lesson: comparing and rounding big numbers

Unit 1 · Day 2 · Ordering the planets · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practise only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

NameDate

Remember

Compare from the left. To round to a place, look at the digit immediately to its right: 0–4 stay, 5–9 go up. Round only when a table, a model, or an estimate needs fewer places — not as a drill for its own sake.

1. Which is larger, 778,500 or 77,850? Compare from the left.

2. A table needs Mercury's diameter 4,879 km to the nearest thousand. What do you write?

3. Use place value, comparing from the left. Which is wider, Venus (12,104 km) or Earth (12,756 km)? Mercury (4,879 km) or Mars (6,792 km)? Then list the four from narrowest to widest.

4. A table of planet diameters will only show whole thousands of kilometers. What do you write for Earth's diameter (12,756 km)? Why that thousand, and not 12,000?

5. A scale model only needs the Moon's distance (384,400 km) to the nearest 100,000 km. What number do you use? Why is that not a mistake?

6. Which is closer to 50,000 km: Neptune's diameter (49,528 km) or Uranus's (51,118 km)? Show how far each is from 50,000.

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
