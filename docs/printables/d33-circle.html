Math circle: a speed needs a time, and a figure needs a label

Unit 7 · Day 33 · Rate, and speed as distance per time · one-lesson review

Remember

Speed is distance per time, with matching units. About 28,000 km/h is the FAQ figure we compute with. “Five miles per second” stays a separate rounded NASA figure. We do not invent a planetary orbital speed.

1. Name the three related calculations for speed, distance, and time.

2. What must match before we divide a distance by a time?

3. Give one reason 28,000 km/h is not faster than 300,000 km/s.

4. 16 orbits in 24 hours and 90 minutes per orbit: why do those two NASA statements agree?

5. Why is 42,000 km (28,000 × 1.5) not published here as “NASA’s official orbit length”?

6. An invented car goes twice as far as another in the same time. What can we say about their speeds?
