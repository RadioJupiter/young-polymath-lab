Core lesson: negative numbers, and planet temperatures

Unit 6 · Day 29 · Negative numbers, and planet temperatures · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

Negative numbers sit to the left of 0. Lesser is farther left (colder, in °C). Fact-sheet means: Mercury 167°C, Venus 464°C, Earth 15°C, Moon −20°C, Mars −65°C. A Mercury or Moon mean is not the temperature of a given region. Count up through 0 to find how many degrees warmer. Label means, ranges, and invented examples.

1. Which is colder, the Moon’s mean (−20°C) or Mars’s mean (−65°C)?

2. How many degrees warmer is Venus’s mean (464°C) than Mercury’s mean (167°C)?

3. Mark −65, −20, 0, and 15 on a Celsius number line, and label Mars, the Moon, and Earth as fact-sheet means.

4. Which is greater, −180 or −65? (The first is a NASA Science night-side figure for Mercury; the second is Mars’s fact-sheet mean.) Say which is colder.

5. Why is 167°C not “the temperature of a place on Mercury”?

6. How many degrees is the swing from Mercury’s night side (−180°C) to a sunlit 430°C, as NASA Science gives those two figures?
