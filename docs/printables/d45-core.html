Remember

Tallies group in fives; a table organizes the same counts. A bar graph needs an even scale; a pictograph needs a key. SILSO yearly means are sourced figures: describe the change you can point to, and do not invent a later official mean. Fair chance is “out of” or a fraction; expected is about, not exactly, in a short run. A measurement is written only as finely as the tool allows. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A tallies and tables, Part B graphs and sourced change, Part C chance and measuring with error. Then begin Part A when the guide says.

2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

3. Write a tally for 14, and say the number in groups of five and leftovers.

4. An invented classroom watch has counts 5, 9, 4, and 7. What is the total, and which watch has the most?

5. A labeled invented table of three watches has a total of 20. Watch 1 is 6 and Watch 2 is 8. What is Watch 3?

6. Why do tallies group in fives?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
