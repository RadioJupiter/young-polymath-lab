Core lesson: parts of a whole, and Moon phases

Unit 3 · Day 11 · The Moon's quarters · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

A fraction is equal parts of one whole: denominator = how many equal parts, numerator = how many you mean. First quarter: the disk looks 1/2 lit; “quarter” is about 1/4 of the way through the month (about 29½ days), not 1/4 of the disk.

1. Draw a bar in 4 equal parts and shade 3/4. Write the fraction, and say what the 4 and the 3 mean.

2. At full Moon, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? At new Moon?

3. Shade and label 1/2, 1/4, and 3/4 of three equal circles (or three bars).

4. A window is one whole. A curtain covers 1/2 of it, then you pull it so it covers 3/4. Has more or less of the window been covered, and by how many quarters?

5. Third quarter looks like 1/2 of the disk lit, like first quarter, but on the other side. What is the name ‘quarter’ tracking, if not the lit fraction of the disk?

6. 1/4 of 29½ days was 7⅜. What is 3/4 of 29½ days (about how long from new Moon to third quarter)?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
