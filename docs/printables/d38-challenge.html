Challenge problems: a rule with a letter, and an honest table

Unit 8 · Day 38 · A pattern in a table, and planetary years · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

A letter can stand for the position in a rule. A sourced table is not a license to invent a formula.

1. The nth number is 5n. What is the 6th number, and how do you check the 2nd?

2. Jupiter’s period is 4331 days on the sheet. About how many Earth years is that, using 365.2 days, and why might you leave it as “about 12” rather than a long string of figures?

3. A friend wants to slip the Moon into the table’s “farther from the Sun, longer year” pattern, using 27.3 days and 0.384 million km. Why is that a miss?

4. The rule “the nth number is 2n − 1.” Find the 8th number.

5. Why is noticing the order in the table different from inventing a formula?

Work in writing. Then mark from the answer key and rework every miss the next day.
