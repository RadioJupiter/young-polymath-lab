Math circle: why a mean may not describe a place

Unit 6 · Day 29 · Negative numbers, and planet temperatures · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

NameDate

Remember

A sourced mean and a sourced range can both be honest. The mean is not automatically “the temperature there.” We do not invent a more precise official reading.

1. Name one world whose fact-sheet mean is negative.

2. Why does this lesson refuse a “typical Tuesday on Mars” temperature?

3. Is Earth’s mean of 15°C a promise that every place on Earth is 15°C? Use the fact sheet’s general caution about variation.

4. Venus’s fact-sheet mean is 464°C, and a NASA Science page says the surface is about 467°C. What do we do with the two figures?

5. Finish: 167°C is a sourced mean, but it averages very hot and very cold sides, so I will not say ____.

6. Which comparison is fairer as a mean-to-mean fact: Venus 464 against Mercury 167, or Venus “about 467” against Mercury’s sunlit 430? Say why.
