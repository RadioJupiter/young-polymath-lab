Core lesson: volume of a box, and the liter

Unit 4 · Day 19 · A box, a liter, and a labeled habitat · one-lesson review

Remember

V = L × W × H, in cm³ or m³. 1 L = 1,000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1,000 L (BIPM). The 6 m by 3 m by 2.5 m module (45 m³) and the 240 L tank are invented classroom numbers. Mars’s mean surface pressure is 6.36 mb (NASA) against the standard atmosphere 1,013.25 mb, less than 1/100. (NASA’s Earth sheet lists 1,014 mb; the fraction is the same.) No invented life-support rates.

1. A 10 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm box: volume in cm³ and in liters.

2. An invented tank holds 240 L. Write that volume in m³. (1 m³ = 1,000 L.)

3. A 2 m by 2 m by 2 m cube: volume in m³ and in liters.

4. Why does this lesson refuse a “liters of water per sol” fact?

5. Mars’s mean surface pressure is 6.36 mb (NASA). The standard atmosphere at sea level is 1,013.25 mb. About what fraction is that (less than 1/100 is enough), and what is that fact not?

6. A box is 1 m by 50 cm by 20 cm. Use one unit, then find the volume in liters. (50 cm = 0.5 m, 20 cm = 0.2 m, or 100 cm by 50 cm by 20 cm.)
