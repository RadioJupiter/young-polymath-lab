Core lesson: a pattern in a table, and planetary years

Unit 8 · Day 38 · A pattern in a table, and planetary years · one-lesson review

Review sheet — use this after the lesson. These questions practice only what this lesson taught. Answers are on the day's answer-key page, not here.

Remember

A pattern is a rule you can say and use. In the NASA fact-sheet table, a larger distance from the Sun goes with a longer year. We will not invent a formula or an orbital speed. The Moon’s 27.3-day figure is about Earth, not a year around the Sun.

1. In the table, how do Earth’s 149.6 million km and 365.2 days sit beside Mercury’s figures?

2. Why will we not put the Moon’s 27.3 days in the same “year around the Sun” pattern?

3. The rule “start at 10, add 4 each time.” What is the 5th number?

4. Which planet in the table has both the smallest distance and the shortest period?

5. A friend writes “period = distance × 3” from two rounded numbers, and calls it a NASA law. What would you say?

6. Why will this lesson not invent an orbital speed for Mars?
