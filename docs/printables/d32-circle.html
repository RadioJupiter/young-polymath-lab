Unit 7 · Day 32 · Equivalent ratios, and a scale · one-lesson review

Remember

One scale for every length. A star chart is usually angles, not a distance scale. NASA’s “about / roughly 400” is kept as NASA’s wording beside our about-400 and about-390 figures.

1. Name the two honest scales used in this unit so far, and one job for each.

2. What do we write instead of squeezing a 3.84 m gap onto a small page?

3. Give one reason 1 cm : 1,000 km and 1 cm : 70,000 km can both be right.

4. If we doubled every length in a model, would a square’s area also merely double? (A first look: draw a 1-by-1 square and a 2-by-2 square.)

5. How can NASA’s “roughly 400 times closer” sit beside our about-390 calculation without a fight?

6. Why is “exactly 400 : 1” for both diameter and distance a neater story than the figures support?
