Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 11

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

This warm-up mixes the mental work of the level: a missing number, an undo, a short pattern, and an input-output rule.

Remember

A letter stands for an unknown. Undo an operation, or a rule, to find it. A pattern keeps the same rule.

Watch me

n + 8 = 20, and the list 2, 6, 10, 14, … : what is n, and what is the next number in the list?

  1. n = 12, because 20 − 8 = 12.
  2. The list adds 4 each time, so the next number is 18.

Together

The rule is “multiply by 3.” The output is 21. What was the input?

Hint 1: Undo

Divide by 3.

Hint 2: The input

21 ÷ 3 = 7. Check: 3 × 7 = 21.

Check

These mix the level’s mental facts.

  1. 5n = 30. What is n?
  2. The rule is “add 9.” Input 6: what is the output?

Practice

  1. The list 40, 32, 24, … : what is the next number?
  2. n − 11 = 9. What is n?

Level 11 check

35–40 minutes · end-of-level mastery check · four out of five or better on each part; not a score or a reward

Learn

This sitting gathers the level: a letter for an unknown; equations, with a pan-balance picture and the habit of doing the same to both sides; patterns and input-output rules; the NASA fact-sheet pattern that a larger distance from the Sun goes with a longer year, without inventing a formula; and this course’s first lever model, mass × distance on each side, with invented numbers labeled.

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

Remember

A letter stands for one unknown in a problem. Do the same to both sides, then check. A pattern is a rule you can say and use. Larger distance from the Sun, longer year, from the fact sheet — no invented formula or orbital speed. For the lever, mass × distance equals mass × distance, in this model, when it balances. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Watch me

A short layout example, not one of the unit-check items: n + 5 = 12, and the rule n → 2n. Find n, and find the output for 7.

  1. n = 7, because 12 − 5 = 7. Check: 7 + 5 = 12.
  2. The output is 14.
  3. After a Together item for layout, do the Unit check alone.

Together

The Together item is practice for layout. After it, do the Unit check alone. What will you write beside a fact-sheet figure, besides the number?

Hint 1: The source

That it is a NASA Planetary Fact Sheet figure.

Hint 2: Invented numbers

If a mass, a distance, or a classroom rule is invented, it stays labeled invented.

Check

The Together item is only layout practice. The Unit check is the mastery sitting.

  1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A unknowns and equations, Part B patterns and rules, Part C the balance and the lever. Then begin Part A when the guide says.
  2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar or a balance drawing when it helps. Keep invented masses, distances, and classroom rules labeled invented. Keep NASA figures labeled as Planetary Fact Sheet figures (page last updated 13 November 2026). Do not use a calculator.

Part A: unknowns and equations

  1. A bar has a whole of 22, with parts n and 9. Write the equation and find n.
  2. n − 7 = 11. Find n, and say what you did to both sides.
  3. 5n = 40. Find n, and check.
  4. 2n + 6 = 18. Find n, and check.
  5. Why must the same letter mean the same number each time it appears in one problem?

Part B: patterns and rules

  1. The list 5, 9, 13, 17, … follows a rule. What is the rule, and what is the next number?
  2. The nth number is 4n + 1. What is the 5th number, and how do you check the 2nd?
  3. The rule is n → 2n + 3. What is the output for n = 6?
  4. The same rule. The output is 11. What was the input?
  5. In the NASA fact-sheet table used on Day 38, Earth is 149.6 million km from the Sun with a period of 365.2 days, and Mars is 228.0 million km with a period of 687.0 days. What pattern do those figures show, and why is “a Mars year is exactly 2 Earth years” a miss?

Part C: the balance and the lever

  1. A pan balance is level with n + 5 on the left and 12 on the right. Find n.
  2. Why do we do the same thing to both sides of an equation?
  3. A labeled invented lever has a mass of 3 at 6 units on the left, and a mass of 9 on the right. How far from the fulcrum must the 9 sit to balance, in this course’s first model?
  4. In that same model, a labeled invented lever has n at 4 units on the left, and 8 at 3 units on the right, and it balances. Find n.
  5. Why will this check not invent a NASA crane figure, and why will it not put the Moon’s 27.3-day fact-sheet period into the “year around the Sun” pattern?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 12. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

25 minutes · mixed Level 11 thinking, after the check · not a second mastery sitting

Learn

These mix the level’s ideas. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Remember

Same skills as the check. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Watch me

3n + 4 = 19, and the rule n → 2n − 1 with output 9. Find both unknowns.

  1. 3n = 15, so n = 5 in the equation. Check: 3 × 5 + 4 = 19.
  2. For the rule, 9 + 1 = 10, and 10 ÷ 2 = 5. Check: 2 × 5 − 1 = 9.
  3. The two n’s belong to two different problems, so it is all right that both happened to be 5.

Together

A labeled invented lever has 4 at 9 units on the left, and 6 on the right. Find the right distance that balances, in this model. Then say why “exactly 2 Earth years” is a miss for Mars.

Hint 1: The lever

4 × 9 = 36, so 6 × d = 36 and d = 6 units. Invented lever.

Hint 2: Mars

The fact-sheet figures are 687.0 days and 365.2 days; 687.0 ÷ 365.2 is about 1.88, a little less than 2.

Check

These mix the level and stay labeled.

  1. The nth number is 5n − 2. Find the 4th number.
  2. n + 9 = 2n + 3. Find n.

Practice

  1. A pan balance is level with 4n on the left and 28 on the right. Find n.
  2. Why is inventing a formula from two fact-sheet rows not the same as stating the distance-and-year pattern?
  3. This wrap is not a second score or a badge.

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · a last argument about letters, patterns, and labels

Learn

This sitting gathers three habits from the level: a letter keeps one meaning in one problem; a pattern we can point to is not automatically a formula; invented and sourced figures stay labeled.

Remember

A letter names one unknown in a problem. A fact-sheet pattern can be described honestly, without inventing a law. Invented lever and balance numbers stay labeled invented.

Watch me

Why is it honest to say “farther from the Sun, longer year,” but not honest to invent a NASA formula from two rows of the table?

  1. The order is something the sourced figures already show.
  2. A formula would be a new claim, and this course has no NASA formula for that job.
  3. The habit is to describe what the table shows, and to leave unsourced rules unclaimed.

Together

How does “the same letter, the same number” sit beside “a new problem may reuse n”?

Hint 1: Inside a problem

Inside one problem, n cannot change mid-way, or the check fails.

Hint 2: A new problem

A new sitting can reuse the name for a new unknown.

Check

These check the last argument.

  1. Give one reason a one-sided move on an equation is like taking a mass from one pan only.
  2. Why do invented lever numbers need the word invented beside them?

Practice

  1. Why does the Moon’s 27.3-day fact-sheet figure stay out of the Sun-orbit pattern?
  2. This argument is not a score or a badge.

la balanza lah bah-LAHN-sah the balance
la palanca lah pah-LAHNG-kah the lever
el planeta el plah-NEH-tah the planet
el año el AH-nyoh the year
sube SOO-beh goes up
baja BAH-hah goes down
pesado peh-SAH-doh heavy
ligero lee-HEH-roh light

The story, to point to and retell: Hay una balanza. Falta un peso. El platillo sube. El platillo baja. La balanza está igual. Hay una palanca. Hay un punto. El peso es pesado. El peso es ligero. Hay un planeta. El año es largo.

Present tense only. Equations, fact-sheet figures, and lever products stay in the English mathematics.

For the guide: the student retells while pointing. No letters, no 687.0, and no mass × distance in the Spanish. This is not a second mastery score.

Remember

la balanza; falta; el platillo; sube; baja; está igual; la palanca; el punto; pesado; ligero; el planeta; el año. Present tense only. Hear for Spanish words.

Watch me

  1. Hay una balanza. Falta un peso.
  2. The student may say them slowly, pointing as they go.

Check

This is a retell, not a new word list.

Notebook: a Level 11 page — an equation, a pattern, and a lever

about 1 hour · one page that gathers the level, with because / but / so

Learn

Make one notebook page that shows an equation with a bar or a balance, a short sourced pattern note, and a labeled invented lever. Then write one because / but / so sentence that keeps those jobs honest.

Remember

The page is a record of the level, not a second mastery check. Labels stay honest.

Watch me

Choose one equation from the week (for example 2n + 6 = 18), show the working, and start a stem about labels: I can use a letter and a lever because ________.

  1. The because can name the bar, the balance, or the products.
  2. A sound because: …they help me write an unknown, or show why two sides match…

Together

Finish: I can use a letter and a lever because ________, but I will not invent a NASA formula or a crane figure, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…those would be unsourced claims…

Hint 2: The so

…I will keep invented numbers labeled and fact-sheet figures sourced.

Check

The page should gather the level and complete the stems.

  1. Show one solved equation, one fact-sheet pattern note (Earth and Mars are enough), and one labeled invented lever with both products.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about letters, patterns, and labels.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: unknown, equation, pattern, lever, invented, fact sheet.
  2. The page is a record, not a score.
  3. File the page. It is not a reward.
  4. This page is not a second mastery sitting.
