Mental math warm-up: halves and quarters of friendly numbers

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Half of a number is that number shared into two equal parts. A quarter is one of four equal parts, or half of a half. For even numbers, half is a whole number: half of 80 is 40. A quarter of 80 is 20, because half of 80 is 40 and half of 40 is 20.

Remember

Half means ÷2 (two equal parts). A quarter means ÷4, or half of a half. Use number bonds: 80 = 40 + 40; 80 = 20 + 20 + 20 + 20.

Watch me

What is a quarter of 60?

  1. Half of 60 is 30 (60 = 30 + 30).
  2. Half of 30 is 15.
  3. So a quarter of 60 is 15.

Together

What is a half of 90, and a quarter of 90?

Hint 1: Half

90 = 45 + 45, so half is 45.

Hint 2: Quarter

Half of 45: 45 = 40 + 5, half is 20 + 2½, so 22½. Or 90 ÷ 4 = 22½.

Check

Use only halves and quarters of these numbers.

  1. What is half of 120?
  2. What is a quarter of 40?

Practice

  1. Half of 16, half of 800, half of 5,000.
  2. A quarter of 100, a quarter of 48, a quarter of 200.
  3. Three quarters of 40 (three of the four equal parts).
  4. Half of 90, then a quarter of that answer.
  5. A number's half is 36. What is the number? What is a quarter of it?

Core lesson: parts of a whole, and Moon phases

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (a paper circle), pictorial (a bar or a shaded disk), then the fraction written

Learn

A fraction names equal parts of one whole. In 1/2, the bottom number (the denominator) tells how many equal parts the whole is cut into; the top number (the numerator) tells how many of those parts you mean.

The Moon, honestly. The Moon does not change shape. Sunlight lights half of the Moon almost all the time. What changes is how much of the lit half we can see from Earth, as the Moon goes around us.

  • At new Moon, the lit half faces away from us: the near side looks dark (about 0 of the disk is lit, as we see it).
  • At first quarter, we see half of the near side lit (a straight edge down the middle). It is called “quarter” because it is about one quarter of the way through the cycle, not because the disk looks one-quarter lit. The lit part we see is 1/2, not 1/4.
  • At full Moon, the near side is lit: about 1 whole disk.
  • At third quarter (last quarter), we again see 1/2 of the disk lit, on the other side.

The cycle from new Moon to new Moon is the synodic month: about 29.53 days, which we will usually say as about 29½ days (NASA Moon fact sheet). A quarter of that cycle is a quarter of 29½ days, not a quarter of the disk.

Concrete step. Cut or draw a paper circle. Fold it into two equal parts, then into four. Shade 1/2, 1/4, 3/4. Hold the “1/2 shaded” circle up and say: this is what the disk looks like at first quarter — half lit — even though the name is quarter.

Remember

A fraction is equal parts of one whole: denominator = how many equal parts, numerator = how many you mean. First quarter: the disk looks 1/2 lit; “quarter” is about 1/4 of the way through the month (about 29½ days), not 1/4 of the disk.

Watch me

A student says, ‘First quarter means a quarter of the Moon is lit, so I will shade 1/4 of my circle.’ Correct the error, then shade the right amount.

  1. The name ‘first quarter’ is about the cycle, not about 1/4 of the disk.
  2. At first quarter, half the near side is lit, so shade 1/2 of the circle.
  3. 1/4 of the disk would be the wrong picture.

Together

About how many days after new Moon is first quarter, if the month is 29½ days? Find 1/4 of 29½.

Hint 1: A quarter

1/4 of 29½ is 29½ ÷ 4.

Hint 2: Split

29 ÷ 4 = 7 remainder 1, so 7¼, and ½ ÷ 4 = ⅛, so 7¼ + ⅛ = 7⅜ days (about 7.4 days).

Check

These questions use only equal parts of a whole, and the Moon facts just taught.

  1. Draw a bar in 4 equal parts and shade 3/4. Write the fraction, and say what the 4 and the 3 mean.
  2. At full Moon, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? At new Moon?

Practice

  1. Shade and label 1/2, 1/4, and 3/4 of three equal circles (or three bars).
  2. A window is one whole. A curtain covers 1/2 of it, then you pull it so it covers 3/4. Has more or less of the window been covered, and by how many quarters?
  3. Third quarter looks like 1/2 of the disk lit, like first quarter, but on the other side. What is the name ‘quarter’ tracking, if not the lit fraction of the disk?
  4. 1/4 of 29½ days was 7⅜. What is 3/4 of 29½ days (about how long from new Moon to third quarter)?
  5. Finish: First quarter is called quarter because ________, but the disk looks ________.

Challenge problems: fractions of a cycle

20 minutes · a bit harder, same skills

Learn

A fraction of a length of time uses the same equal-parts idea. The synodic month is about 29½ days. Half of that cycle is about from new Moon to full Moon. You can also meet a second figure, for interest: the sidereal month (the Moon’s trip around Earth relative to the stars) is about 27.32 days, usually about 27⅓ days (NASA). The two are not the same, because Earth has also moved around the Sun, so the Moon must go a little farther to line up with the Sun again and show the same phase.

Remember

A fraction of 29½ is an equal-parts problem. The sidereal month is shorter (about 27⅓ days) than the synodic month (about 29½ days); the difference is a fact to notice, not a formula to invent today.

Watch me

What is half of 29½ days?

  1. Half of 29 is 14½.
  2. Half of ½ is ¼.
  3. 14½ + ¼ = 14¾ days (about new Moon to full Moon).

Together

About how much longer is the synodic month than the sidereal month, if we take 29½ and 27⅓?

Hint 1: Common parts

½ = 3/6 and ⅓ = 2/6, but first subtract the whole days: 29 − 27 = 2.

Hint 2: The fractions

½ − ⅓ = 3/6 − 2/6 = 1/6. So 2 + 1/6 = 2⅙ days. (This uses a unlike-denominator step you will be taught fully on Day 14; if it is new, leave it as ‘a little more than 2 days’ and return later.)

Check

Use the cycle lengths just given, and equal parts.

  1. What is half of 27⅓ days? (Half of 27 is 13½; half of ⅓ is ⅙. Combine them.)
  2. Why are the two ‘months’ not the same length, in one or two sentences of your own?

Practice

  1. A classroom model uses a 12-day ‘month’ (this is a model, not the real 29½-day month). What fraction of that model has passed after 3 days? After 9 days?
  2. On the real 29½-day month, about how many days is 1/2 of the way (new to full)?
  3. If a drawing of the disk at first quarter is shaded 1/4, what is wrong, and what should be shaded?
  4. (Stretch.) The sidereal month is about 27.32 days and the synodic about 29.53 days (NASA, unrounded here). Subtract and say about how many days they differ, rounding to the nearest tenth.

Math circle: sharing equally, and a ‘fair’ cut

20 minutes · Method: a diagram, then a short argument

Learn

A share is fair when the parts are equal. If two people share one flatbread, each should get 1/2, not a large piece and a small piece both called ‘half’. If four people share, each should get 1/4. A picture can prove a share is fair: the parts must match when you cut them out, or the bars must be the same length.

Remember

Equal parts make a fraction honest. A ‘half’ that is not equal to the other half is not 1/2.

Watch me

Two people share a bar. One piece is clearly longer. A student writes 1/2 + 1/2 = 1. What has gone wrong?

  1. The two pieces are not equal, so neither is 1/2 of that bar.
  2. You cannot write 1/2 + 1/2 until the parts are equal, or until you have found the true fraction of each piece.
  3. The picture must be redrawn, or the pieces measured.

Together

Can you cut a square into two equal parts with a cut that is not a straight midline? Is each part still 1/2?

Hint 1: Equal area

If the two parts have the same area, each is 1/2 of the square.

Hint 2: A zigzag

A zigzag cut that gives two matching pieces (one can be placed on the other) still makes halves.

Check

The skill is equal parts, not a new kind of fraction.

  1. Three people share a ribbon equally. What fraction does each get? Shade a bar to show it.
  2. A pizza is cut into 4 parts that are not equal, and someone takes the largest part and calls it 1/4. What would you say?

Practice

  1. Draw two different ways to cut a rectangle into fourths (four equal parts).
  2. A bar is 12 units long. A piece 3 units long is what fraction of the bar? A piece 4 units long?
  3. Why does ‘I broke it into four pieces’ not automatically mean each piece is 1/4?
  4. Write a two-sentence argument: This is 1/2 because ________. It would not be 1/2 if ________.

Spanish · The days of the week, and hoy es…

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words

Learn

The days of the week are not capitalised in Spanish: lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo. Hoy es viernes — today is Friday. El lunes can mean ‘on Monday’. Es is from ser, which you have used for time and identity.

Word How to say it Meaning
lunes LOO-ness Monday
martes MAR-tess Tuesday
miércoles mee-EHR-koh-less Wednesday
jueves HWEH-bess Thursday
viernes bee-EHR-ness Friday
sábado SAH-bah-doh Saturday
domingo doh-MEEN-goh Sunday
hoy es oy ess today is

There is no ‘on’ word needed in el lunes for ‘on Monday’.

Remember

lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo. Hoy es viernes. El lunes = on Monday.

Watch me

Say in Spanish: ‘Today is Saturday.’

  1. Hoy es sábado.
  2. The day-word is not capitalised.
  3. es, not está, for the day.

Together

Say: ‘On Friday I look at the Moon.’ Use miro la luna and el viernes.

Hint 1: On Friday

El viernes.

Hint 2: The rest

El viernes miro la luna. (A simple present is enough.)

Check

Use only today’s day-words and hoy es / el….

  1. Write: Today is Monday.
  2. Write the seven days in order, in Spanish.

Exercises

  1. Translate: Today is Thursday. On Sunday I look at the sky (miro el cielo).
  2. Which day is miércoles? Which is domingo?
  3. Write two original sentences: Hoy es… and El… miro la luna.

Latin · Lūna in caelō

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences (the books are not copied). Instruction in English.

Learn

You already know lūna and sōl (Day 8). Today: caelum (sky) and in caelō (in the sky), with the ablative you can treat as a set phrase for now. Lūna est in caelō. Stellae sunt in caelō. Nox is night: Nox est. Lūna est clāra — the moon is bright.

Word How to say it Meaning
caelum KAI-loom sky
in caelō in KAI-loh in the sky
stella STEL-lah star
stellae STEL-lai stars
nox noks night
clāra KLAH-rah bright (feminine)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Est with one thing, sunt with more than one, as on Day 1.

Remember

Lūna est in caelō. Stellae sunt in caelō. Nox est. Lūna est clāra. est / sunt still agree in number.

Watch me

Say in Latin: ‘The moon is in the sky.’

  1. Lūna est in caelō.
  2. Lūna is one thing, so est, not sunt.
  3. in caelō, the set phrase from the table.

Together

Say: ‘The stars are in the sky.’

Hint 1: Plural

Stellae, and sunt.

Hint 2: Where

in caelō.

Check

Use only today’s words and est/sunt.

  1. Write: It is night.
  2. Write: The moon is bright.

Exercises

  1. Translate into Latin: The stars are in the sky. The moon is in the sky.
  2. Correct: Stellae est in caelō.
  3. Write two original Latin sentences, one with est and one with sunt.

Notebook: the phase wheel and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

A phase wheel is a circle divided into four equal parts, labeled new, first quarter, full, and third quarter, going around in order. It is a model of the cycle, not a picture of how much of the disk is lit at first quarter (that disk picture is a separate drawing, shaded 1/2).

Remember

The wheel tracks the cycle in fourths. A separate disk drawing shows the lit fraction. First quarter sits at 1/4 of the wheel and looks 1/2 lit on the disk. Use because / but / so to explain that, not a one-word label alone.

Watch me

A student labels 1/4 of the wheel ‘looks 1/4 lit’. Rewrite their sentence correctly with but.

  1. The wheel’s 1/4 mark is first quarter in the cycle.
  2. First quarter looks half lit on the disk.
  3. A sentence: First quarter is a quarter of the way around the wheel, but the disk looks half lit.

Together

Finish aloud, then write: The disk at first quarter is shaded 1/2 because ________.

Hint 1: What we see

We see half of the near side lit.

Hint 2: Not the name

Do not say ‘because it is called quarter’ as the reason for shading 1/2.

Check

The notebook work is the wheel, the disk, and the stems.

  1. What do you draw first, and what do you not write until after the labels?
  2. Name one thing the wheel is not (a picture of the lit fraction at first quarter).

Practice

How the guide helps today: stay for the writing steps. The student draws first, with no writing. Then labels from the word bank. Then says each stem sentence aloud; you confirm it is a complete sentence before it is written. If handwriting is a struggle, write the student's own spoken sentence in light pencil for tracing or copying. Do not invent sentences for the student.

1. Draw. A four-part phase wheel, and beside it a disk shaded for first quarter (1/2, not 1/4). You may add a tiny disk for new (unshaded) and full (fully shaded).

2. Label, from the word bank. Point and say each word, then write it on the sketch.

Word bank: new Moon · first quarter · full Moon · third quarter · disk · lit · half · cycle · 29½ days

3. Write. Say each stem aloud, then write it.

  • The synodic month is about ________.
  • First quarter is ________ of the way through the cycle, but the disk looks ________.
  • The disk at first quarter is shaded 1/2 because ________.
  • I know the Moon itself does not change shape, so ________.
