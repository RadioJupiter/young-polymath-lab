Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 6

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

This warm-up mixes the mental work of the level: halves and quarters, equivalent names, like parts, and a common denominator for 1/2 and 1/6. Use number bonds and a bar in your head, or sketch one.

Remember

Half is ÷2. A quarter is half of a half. Equivalent fractions keep the amount. Like parts: add or subtract numerators. Unlike parts: rename first.

Watch me

What is 1/2 + 1/6? Say the renaming.

  1. 1/2 = 3/6.
  2. 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6.
  3. 4/6 = 2/3.

Together

What is a quarter of 80, and what is 2/8 of 80? Are they the same?

Hint 1: A quarter

80 ÷ 4 = 20.

Hint 2: 2/8

2/8 = 1/4, so 2/8 of 80 is also 20. They are the same amount.

Check

Mixed Level 6 skills only.

  1. 3/6 of 12, and 1/2 of 12.
  2. 5/8 − 3/8, then a simpler name if there is one.

Practice

  1. Half of 90; a quarter of 40; 3/4 of 40.
  2. 1/2 = ☐/6; 2/3 = ☐/6.
  3. 1/6 + 2/6; 1/2 − 1/6.
  4. 1/3 + 1/6.
  5. A 6-part bar: 1 part is what fraction? Two parts?

Level 6 check

45 minutes · alone, no calculator · a check of this level, not a new lesson

Learn

This is a check of skills already taught in Unit 3 (Level 6), not a new lesson. Use a paper circle or a bar when a picture would help. The facts you may need: the synodic month is about 29½ days (NASA); first quarter looks 1/2 lit; Moon gravity is about 1/6 of Earth’s; Mars is about 19/50, and 2/5 is only a slightly high estimate.

Remember

A fraction is equal parts of one whole. Equivalent fractions name the same amount. Like denominators: add or subtract numerators. Unlike denominators: rename first. Mass does not change on the Moon; the pull is about 1/6.

Watch me

A model of how to show work, so the check can be written in the same shape. Find 1/2 + 1/6.

  1. Draw a 6-part bar (or write 1/2 = 3/6).
  2. 3/6 + 1/6 = 4/6.
  3. 4/6 = 2/3. Write the renaming, not only the last answer.

Together

Find 1 − 1/6, and say in one sentence what the 5/6 means if the 1 is Earth’s pull and the 1/6 is the Moon’s.

Hint 1: The subtraction

1 = 6/6, so 6/6 − 1/6 = 5/6.

Hint 2: The meaning

5/6 is the difference of the pulls, not a change of mass, and not a third world’s gravity.

Check

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

  1. Write 1/2 as sixths.
  2. What is 2/6 + 1/6, in sixths and in simplest form?

Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar or a number bond when it helps. For Moon and Mars facts, use the figures taught this unit (NASA, with rounding stated). Do not use a calculator.

Part A: parts of a whole, and phases

  1. Draw a bar in 4 equal parts and shade 3/4. What does the 4 mean, and what does the 3 mean?
  2. At first quarter, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? Why is the phase still called “quarter”?
  3. The synodic month is about 29½ days. What is 1/4 of 29½ days?
  4. At new Moon, what fraction of the near-side disk is lit, as we see it? At full Moon?
  5. A classroom model month has 12 equal days (a model, not the real synodic month). What fraction of that model has passed after 3 days? After 9 days?

Part B: equivalent fractions, and gravity

  1. Fill in: 1/2 = ☐/6 = ☐/12.
  2. Write 3/6 in simplest form, and show why it is equivalent to your answer.
  3. A 6-part bar stands for the pull of a given mass on Earth, and that pull is called 30 units. About how many units is the pull of the same mass on the Moon? Does the mass itself change?
  4. Is 19/50 equivalent to 2/5? If someone uses 2/5 for Mars’s gravity, what must they say?
  5. Prove that 2/12 = 1/6, with a bar or with the multiply/divide rule.

Part C: adding and subtracting

  1. 3/8 + 2/8. Give a simpler name if there is one.
  2. 7/10 − 3/10. Give a simpler name if there is one.
  3. 1/2 + 1/6. Show the renaming.
  4. 2/3 − 1/6. Show the renaming.
  5. A tank is 1/3 full. Then 1/6 of a full tank is added. Draw a bar. How full is the tank now? What fraction is still empty?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 7. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

These problems stay inside Level 6: fractions of 29½, equivalent names, and unlike denominators. The sidereal month (about 27⅓ days, NASA) may appear as a given, not as a new formula.

Remember

Use only this level’s methods. Label models and estimates. Keep mass and pull distinct.

Watch me

What is 3/4 of 29½ days? (You found 1/4 on Day 11 as 7⅜.)

  1. 3 × 7⅜ = 21 + 9/8.
  2. 9/8 = 1⅛.
  3. 21 + 1⅛ = 22⅛ days.

Together

Earth’s pull is 12 units for a given mass. About how many units on the Moon (1/6) and on Mars (19/50 of 12)? Leave Mars as a fraction if it does not come out whole, then round only if you label the rounding.

Hint 1: Moon

1/6 of 12 is 2 units.

Hint 2: Mars

19/50 × 12 = 228/50 = 4 28/50 = 4 14/25 units (or 4.56, if you write a decimal and call it exact enough; 4.6 if rounded to one tenth and labeled).

Check

Level 6 skills only.

  1. 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6.
  2. Is 14¾ days half of 29½? Show it.

Practice

  1. Half of 27⅓ days (the sidereal month, about; NASA).
  2. A tank is 1/2 full; 1/6 is poured out. How much is left?
  3. Write one careful sentence about first quarter that uses because and but.
  4. (Stretch.) 29.53 − 27.32 (NASA, unrounded here). About how many days longer is the synodic month, to the nearest tenth?

Math circle: a last short argument

15–20 minutes · a diagram and a written reason

Learn

Choose one claim and prove it with a bar and with a sentence: either (A) 1/2 + 1/6 = 2/3, or (B) first quarter is not a quarter-lit disk, or (C) 2/5 is not equivalent to 19/50. A proof here is a reason you can show, not a guess.

Remember

A Level 6 argument uses a bar or a renaming step, and says what the claim is not.

Watch me

Here is a model argument for (A), so you can write (B) or (C) in the same shape — or write (A) in your own words.

  1. A 6-part bar shows 1/2 as 3 parts and 1/6 as 1 part.
  2. Together that is 4 parts: 4/6, which is 2/3 of the same bar.
  3. It is not 2/8, because we do not add the denominators as they stand.

Together

Pick (B) or (C) and outline your argument in two hints’ worth of notes, then write it.

Hint 1: (B)

Name what ‘quarter’ tracks, and what fraction of the disk is lit.

Hint 2: (C)

Rename 2/5 as fiftieths and compare with 19/50; say ‘estimate’ if you mention Mars.

Check

The skill is a short, shown reason, not a new rule.

  1. Which claim did you pick, and what picture or renaming did you use?
  2. What false step did you rule out?

Practice

  1. Write the argument in four to six sentences, with a bar or a renaming you actually drew.
  2. Exchange with the guide: can they follow the reason without guessing what you meant?
  3. If one part of today’s unit check was missed, write one new item like it and solve it here. (This is review, not a second score.)

Spanish · A short Moon story

15 minutes · Instruction in English; a review of Days 11–14 words

Learn

This is a review story. The words are from Days 11 to 14: days of the week, weather, phases, quiero ver and puedo ver.

Word How to say it Meaning
hoy es viernes oy ess bee-EHR-ness today is Friday
está nublado es-TAH noo-BLAH-doh it is cloudy
la luna llena lah LOO-nah YEH-nah the full Moon
el cuarto creciente el KWAR-toh kreh-see-EN-teh first quarter
Quiero ver la luna kee-EH-roh behr lah LOO-nah I want to see the Moon

For the guide: tell the story twice, pointing. The third time, pause for the student to fill a word. End with a silent pointing walk-through, then, if ready, a spoken retell. This is a check of the unit’s Spanish, not a test to pass or fail.

The story. Hoy es viernes. Está nublado. Quiero ver la luna llena, pero no puedo ver la luna. El lunes no está nublado. Puedo ver el cuarto creciente.

In English, for the guide: Today is Friday. It is cloudy. I want to see the full Moon, but I cannot see the Moon. On Monday it is not cloudy. I can see the first quarter.

Remember

hoy es…; está nublado / hace sol; quiero ver / puedo ver / no puedo ver; la luna llena, el cuarto creciente, la luna nueva. Instruction stays in English.

Watch me

From the story, say in Spanish: ‘Today is Friday. It is cloudy.’

  1. Hoy es viernes. Está nublado.
  2. es with the day; está with nublado.
  3. Not hace nublado.

Together

Say: ‘I want to see the full Moon, but I cannot see the Moon.’

Hint 1: I want

Quiero ver la luna llena,

Hint 2: But I cannot

pero no puedo ver la luna.

Check

Use only words from this unit’s Spanish.

  1. Translate: On Monday I can see the first quarter.
  2. Write one new sentence with no puedo ver.

Exercises

  1. Retell the story in Spanish, looking away from the page as soon as you can.
  2. Correct: Hoy está viernes. Hace nublado. Quiero veo la luna.
  3. Write three sentences: a day, a weather phrase, and quiero ver or puedo ver with a phase word.

Latin · A short night-sky story

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original review story. Instruction in English.

Learn

This is a review story. The words are from Days 11 to 14: lūna, stellae, caelum, nox, clāra, ubi, spectat, and the accusative lūnam / stēllās.

Word How to say it Meaning
Nox est clāra noks est KLAH-rah The night is bright
Ubi est lūna? OO-bee est LOO-nah Where is the moon?
in caelō in KAI-loh in the sky
Puella lūnam spectat poo-EL-lah LOO-nam SPEK-tat The girl looks at the moon
stēllās clārās STEL-lahs KLAH-rahs the bright stars (object)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible; you supply a word only after a real try. Acting spectat is a valid way to show meaning. This is a check of the unit’s Latin, not a test to pass or fail.

The story. Nox est clāra. Ubi est lūna? Lūna est in caelō. Puella lūnam clāram spectat. Puer stēllās clārās spectat. Stellae sunt in caelō.

Remember

Nox est clāra. Ubi est lūna? Lūna est in caelō. Puella lūnam spectat. Puer stēllās spectat. Adjectives match: lūnam clāram, stēllās clārās, stellae clārae.

Watch me

Read and say: ‘The night is bright. Where is the moon? The moon is in the sky.’

  1. Nox est clāra. Ubi est lūna? Lūna est in caelō.
  2. est with one moon; clāra with nox.
  3. in caelō, the set place phrase.

Together

Say: ‘The girl looks at the bright moon.’

Hint 1: The girl

Puella (nominative).

Hint 2: The object

lūnam clāram spectat.

Check

Use only this unit’s Latin.

  1. Write: The boy looks at the stars.
  2. Correct: Ubi sunt lūna? Puella lūna spectat.

Exercises

  1. Retell the story in Latin, with the book closed as soon as you can.
  2. Translate into Latin: The bright stars are in the sky. The boy looks at the moon.
  3. Write two original sentences: one ubi question and answer, and one with spectat and an accusative.

Notebook: a Level 6 page — phases, a bar, and a short explanation

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

This page gathers the level: a phase wheel and a first-quarter disk (1/2 shaded, not 1/4), a 6-part gravity bar (1 part for the Moon), and because / but / so sentences that keep cycle and disk, and mass and pull, distinct. It is a record of what was mastered, not a decoration and not a score.

Remember

The wheel tracks the cycle; the disk shows the lit fraction. The 6-part bar shows about 1/6 of the pull; mass does not change. Write complete stems. No points, badges, or extra credit — the page is the work.

Watch me

A model sentence: First quarter sits at 1/4 of the wheel, but the disk is shaded 1/2 because we see half of the near side lit.

  1. The wheel and the disk are different pictures.
  2. ‘Quarter’ is about the cycle; 1/2 is about the disk.
  3. The because names what we see, not the name of the phase alone.

Together

Finish aloud, then write: The pull on the Moon is about 1/6 of the pull on Earth, so ________, but ________.

Hint 1: So

so a 6-part Earth bar needs 1 part for the Moon

Hint 2: But

but the mass is still the same

Check

The notebook work is the two pictures, the bar, and the stems.

  1. What two Moon pictures do you need, and how is the first-quarter disk shaded?
  2. What must the gravity sentences not claim about kilograms of mass?

Practice

How the guide helps today: stay for the writing steps. Draw first, then label, then say each stem aloud before it is written. If a part of the unit check was missed, the same page can hold one correction item, without a score. Do not invent sentences for the student.

1. Draw. A four-part phase wheel, a first-quarter disk shaded 1/2 (not 1/4), and a 6-part gravity bar with 1 part marked for the Moon. Optional: a small 6-part bar showing 1/2 + 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3.

2. Label, from the word bank.

Word bank: new · first quarter · full · third quarter · disk · cycle · 29½ days · mass · pull · 1/6 · 1/2 · NASA

3. Write.

  • The synodic month is about ________.
  • First quarter is ________ of the cycle, but the disk looks ________ because ________.
  • The Moon’s pull is about ________ of Earth’s, so ________, but mass ________.
  • 1/2 + 1/6 = ________. I renamed 1/2 because ________.
