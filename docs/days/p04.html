Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

To add or subtract a whole number of tens, change the tens place. 46 + 30 is 4 tens + 3 tens, with the 6 ones unchanged: 76. 46 + 3 would be a ones job, not a tens job.

Remember

Adding tens changes the tens digit (and maybe the hundreds, if you make 10 tens). The ones digit stays, unless you are not adding a whole number of tens.

Watch me

What is 46 + 30?

  1. 46 has 4 tens and 6 ones. 30 is 3 tens.
  2. 4 tens + 3 tens = 7 tens, and 6 ones: 76.

Together

What is 46 + 3? Why is it not 76?

Hint 1: Ones

3 is 3 ones, not 3 tens.

Hint 2: The sum

46 + 3 = 49.

Check

Add or subtract tens, or a ten from a hundred.

  1. 46 + 30
  2. 52 + 40
  3. 80 − 20

Practice

  1. 25 + 50
  2. 300 − 10
  3. 46 + 3

Core lesson: adding and subtracting tens and hundreds; day and night

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract; then a sourced fact about day and night

Learn

Yesterday’s facts still hold. Today uses place value to add and subtract tens and hundreds, including numbers up to 1,000.

100100100100101010

430 is 4 hundreds and 3 tens. 430 + 200 is 2 more hundreds: 630. 430 − 30 is 3 tens taken away: 400. 10 less than 300 is 2 hundreds and 9 tens: 290.

46 + 30 is the same idea with tens: 76. If the ones make a new ten, use making ten, as in 28 + 5 = 33 (Day 2’s challenge, used again).

Day and night, from NASA’s “What Is Earth? (Grades 5–8)”: at all times, half of Earth is lighted by the Sun and half is in darkness. Places facing toward the Sun have daytime; places facing away have nighttime. As Earth spins, most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours.

The picture is a model, not a photograph. This lesson does not invent a different official length of a day. A solar day is taught as about 24 hours. Night does not come because the Sun “switches off.” It comes because we turn away from the Sun.

Day and night follow NASA’s explanation in “What Is Earth? (Grades 5–8)”: at all times, half of Earth is lighted by the Sun and half is in darkness; areas facing toward the Sun have daytime, and areas facing away have nighttime; as the planet spins, most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours. https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-earth-grades-5-8/ A solar day is taught as about 24 hours. A sidereal day is not begun in this level. Star and crater counts in classroom pictures are invented and labeled invented.

Remember

Add or subtract tens and hundreds by changing those places. 46 + 30 = 76. 10 less than 300 is 290. NASA: half of Earth is lighted at all times; we have day when we face the Sun and night when we face away; most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours.

Watch me

What is 46 + 30, and what is 10 less than 300?

  1. 46 + 30 = 76 (4 tens + 3 tens, 6 ones unchanged).
  2. 10 less than 300 is 290 (2 hundreds and 9 tens).

Together

Why is it night for a place on Earth, according to the NASA explanation used today, and what official hour-count does this lesson use for one cycle?

Hint 1: Facing

Night is when that place is facing away from the Sun.

Hint 2: The spin

As Earth spins, most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours. The lesson uses about 24 hours, not an invented official figure.

Check

These add or subtract tens and hundreds, or use the NASA day-and-night fact.

  1. Find 215 + 100 and 215 + 10.
  2. Find 80 − 30 and 800 − 300.

Practice

  1. Find 46 + 30 and 28 + 5.
  2. In the NASA wording used today, is half of Earth in darkness at all times? Why is that not the same as “it is night everywhere”?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

10 less than 300 is 290, not 200. A jump of 10 is one ten, not one hundred. 100 less than 300 is 200. The place you change has to match the number you subtract.

Remember

Change the place that matches what you are adding or subtracting. A 10 and a 100 are different places.

Watch me

What is 10 less than 300, and what is 100 less than 300?

  1. 290.
  2. 200.

Together

What is 46 + 70? You will make a new hundred.

Hint 1: Tens

4 tens + 7 tens = 11 tens.

Hint 2: Regroup

11 tens are 1 hundred and 1 ten, and 6 ones remain: 116.

Check

These go a step further with tens and hundreds.

  1. Find 990 + 10. What number have you reached?
  2. Find 503 − 10.

Practice

  1. A student says night lasts 24 hours because “a day is 24 hours.” What was mixed, using today’s NASA fact?
  2. Find 28 + 5 + 30.

Math circle: why 8 + 7 is 7 + 8, and why places matter

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

8 + 7 and 7 + 8 are the same total because they are the same two parts. 46 + 30 and 30 + 46 are the same total too. But 46 + 30 is not 46 + 3, because 30 and 3 are not the same part: one is 3 tens and one is 3 ones.

Remember

You may swap the parts of an addition. You may not swap a ten for a one and keep the same total.

Watch me

Why is 46 + 30 equal to 30 + 46, but not equal to 46 + 3?

  1. 46 + 30 and 30 + 46 use the same two parts, so they make the same whole, 76.
  2. 46 + 3 uses 3 ones, not 3 tens, so the sum is 49, not 76.

Together

A student writes 8 + 7 = 87 because “you just put the digits together.” What is wrong?

Hint 1: Places

87 is 8 tens and 7 ones, not the sum of 8 and 7.

Hint 2: The fact

8 + 7 makes 15, by making ten.

Check

These defend swapping parts, and refuse mixing places.

  1. Is 13 − 6 equal to 6 − 13, in this level’s whole numbers starting from a whole of 13? What stays true instead?
  2. Why does a 0 in 305 matter, in one short argument?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 10 less than 300 is 290, not 200.
  2. Using NASA’s wording, why does a globe and a lamp (an invented classroom model, labeled) show day and night without inventing a new official hour-count?

Spanish · Es de día, es de noche

15 minutes · Method: hear and sort pictures. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today’s phrases are es de día (it is day) and es de noche (it is night). El sol and la luna from yesterday help you sort a drawing: a bright Sun picture with es de día; a night picture with es de noche.

Word How to say it Meaning
es de día es deh DEE-ah it is day / daytime
es de noche es deh NOH-cheh it is night / nighttime
el sol el sohl the Sun
la luna lah LOO-nah the Moon

The guide shows a daytime drawing and says es de día, then a nighttime drawing and es de noche. The student points, then may say the phrase. Do not retell the NASA explanation in Spanish; that explanation stays in English in the mathematics and notebook blocks.

Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes. Classroom drawings are the pictures for el sol.

Instructions to the guide are in English. Only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish. Hear clips use es-MX. If this is the student’s first Spanish, begin here. Students who later start Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that is by design for those who skipped these foundation levels.

Remember

Es de día: it is day. Es de noche: it is night. Use them with a drawing, not as a new science speech in Spanish.

Watch me

The guide shows a night drawing and says es de noche. What does the phrase mean, and what should the student do?

  1. It is night (nighttime).
  2. Point at the night drawing, and say the phrase when ready.

Together

What is the English of es de día, and which picture would you choose for it?

Hint 1

It is day / daytime.

Hint 2

A daytime drawing, often with the Sun, not a night drawing.

Check

These check the two phrases.

  1. Give the English of es de día and es de noche.
  2. Hear la luna and es de noche. When might both be used with the same drawing?

Exercises

  1. Say the Spanish for “it is day” and for “the Sun.”
  2. Why does this block not retell NASA’s day-and-night explanation in Spanish?

Latin · Diēs and nox

15 minutes · Method: two new nouns in short original captions.

Learn

Today names diēs (day) and nox (night), still in short original captions, not a science speech.

Word How to say it Meaning
diēs DEE-ace day
nox noks night
sōl sohl the Sun
lūna LOO-nah the Moon

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original captions:

  • Diēs est. — It is day.
  • Nox est. — It is night.
  • Nox nōn est. — It is not night.

Diēs is a fifth-declension noun; this lesson only needs the form diēs as you see it here, not a full declension table. Nox, noctis is third declension; today you only need nox in these captions.

The NASA explanation of why we have day and night stays in English, in the mathematics block. These captions name day and night; they do not replace that explanation.

All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; c is always like k; v is like English w; vowels are pure (a as in father, e as in they, i as in machine, o as in note, u as in rude).

Remember

Diēs: day. Nox: night. Diēs est and Nox est are original captions. Nōn est still means “is not.” The reason for day and night stays in English.

Watch me

A daytime drawing is shown. Which caption matches, Diēs est or Nox est?

  1. Diēs est.

Together

What does nox mean, and what is the English of Nox nōn est?

Hint 1

Nox means night.

Hint 2

Nox nōn est: it is not night.

Check

These check diēs, nox, and nōn.

  1. Give the English of Diēs est and of Nox est.
  2. Write a true “is not” caption for a daytime drawing, using nox.

Exercises

  1. Hear diēs and nox. Give the English of each.
  2. Why is the NASA spin-and-facing explanation not written as a Latin paragraph today?

Notebook: day and night, in a drawing and in sentences

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: a model of Earth and the Sun (a circle and a lamp or a marked “Sun”), with one side of Earth labeled day and the other night. Label Sun, Earth, day, night, spin. Then write from stems. Use the NASA wording you were taught, not a homemade official hour-count.

Word bank: Sun, Earth, day, night, spin, face, twenty-four, half.

Because / but / so — keep the same opening:

  1. We have night because …
  2. We have night, but …
  3. Earth spins, so …

Safety: never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes. The drawing is the model.

End with one fact sentence you can source (“Most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours,” from NASA) and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw the model first. Because: we face away from the Sun. But: it is not night everywhere at once; half is in darkness, half is in light. So: most places cycle through day and night about every 24 hours. Label the model; do not invent a new official day length.

Watch me

Finish: “We have night because …”

  1. A model: “We have night because our place on Earth is facing away from the Sun.” Other finishes that use facing away / the spin, as taught, are accepted.

Together

Finish: “We have night, but …” What contrast should appear?

Hint 1: Half

Half of Earth is in darkness at all times; the other half has day.

Hint 2: A model

“… but it is not night everywhere at once.”

Check

These check the stems against the NASA wording.

  1. What official cycle length does this level use, and where does it come from?
  2. What must you never do with the real Sun?

Practice

  1. Finish all three because / but / so stems in writing, after the drawing is labeled.
  2. Write one “what next” question about the model.
