Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Subtraction can use the same bond. If 6 + 4 = 10, then 10 − 4 = 6 and 10 − 6 = 4. The bond holds all three facts.

Remember

An addition bond gives two subtraction facts. 13 − 6 means “6 and what make 13?”

Watch me

If 6 + 7 = 13, what is 13 − 7, and what is 13 − 6?

  1. 13 − 7 = 6.
  2. 13 − 6 = 7.

Together

What is 13 − 6? Use a bond, not a guess.

Hint 1: The question

6 + ☐ = 13.

Hint 2: Making ten

6 + 4 = 10, and 3 more is 13, so 6 + 7 = 13. Then 13 − 6 = 7.

Check

Use a bond to subtract.

  1. 10 − 3
  2. 10 − 8
  3. 13 − 5

Practice

  1. 15 − 7
  2. 12 − 8
  3. 14 − 6

Core lesson: subtraction facts, from the same bonds

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract

Learn

Subtraction answers “how many are left?” and also “how many more to reach …?” Both can use a number bond. If two parts make a whole, taking one part away leaves the other part.

13
67

Because 6 + 7 = 13:

  • 13 − 7 = 6
  • 13 − 6 = 7

For 13 − 6, you may also make ten in reverse: 13 − 3 = 10, then 10 − 3 = 7, because 6 = 3 + 3. Either method is taught; the bond and making ten stay the tools.

A concrete step: 13 counters, take 6 away, 7 remain. The bond is the picture. 13 − 6 = 7 is the sentence.

Invented story, labeled invented: 13 classroom “stars” on a card, 6 covered. 7 remain visible. This is not an official sky count.

Remember

Subtraction uses the same bond as addition. 13 − 6 = 7 because 6 + 7 = 13. You can also subtract to 10, then subtract the rest. “How many left?” and “how many more?” can use the same fact.

Watch me

Show 13 − 6 with a bond, and give the related addition.

  1. The whole is 13; one part is 6; the other part is 7.
  2. 6 + 7 = 13, so 13 − 6 = 7.

Together

A student computes 13 − 6 by counting back 6 steps from 13 and lands on 8, having counted 13 as the first step. What went wrong, and what is the fact?

Hint 1: The start

If you count back, you start at the number before 13, or you use objects; counting 13 itself as a jump is a miss.

Hint 2: The bond

6 + 7 = 13, so 13 − 6 = 7, not 8.

Check

These use the bond or making ten, not a lucky count-back.

  1. Find 15 − 8, and write the related addition.
  2. Find 14 − 9 by going back to 10 first.

Practice

  1. Find 13 − 6 and 16 − 9.
  2. Invented (labeled): 12 cards, 5 taken away. How many remain? Write the subtraction and the addition bond.

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

“How many more is 15 than 8?” is the same job as 15 − 8, or as 8 + ☐ = 15. The missing part is 7. Comparison is not a new operation in this level; it uses the same bond.

Remember

How many more, how many left, and a missing part can all use one bond.

Watch me

How many more is 15 than 8? Write a subtraction and an addition.

  1. 15 − 8 = 7.
  2. 8 + 7 = 15.

Together

A number is 6 less than 13. What is the number? Show a bond.

Hint 1: Less than

The number is 13 − 6.

Hint 2: The fact

13 − 6 = 7, because 6 + 7 = 13.

Check

These use subtraction as comparison or as a missing part.

  1. How many more is 16 than 9?
  2. A number plus 8 makes 14. What is the number?

Practice

  1. What is 10 less than 13, and what is 10 less than 300? Which job uses today’s facts, and which uses place value from Day 1?
  2. Why is 13 − 6 not the same as 31 − 6, even though both have a 6?

Math circle: a missing part, and a reason

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

If you are told the whole and one part, the other part is fixed. There is not a second correct part for the same whole and the same given part (when we use whole numbers and one bond).

Remember

One whole and one part name the other part. That is why a subtraction fact has one answer.

Watch me

The whole is 13 and one part is 6. Why can the other part not be 8?

  1. 6 + 8 = 14, not 13.
  2. The part that makes 13 with 6 is 7, so 13 − 6 = 7, not 8.

Together

A student says 13 − 6 could be 7 or 8 “depending on how you count.” What would you say?

Hint 1: The bond

The parts of 13 that use a 6 are 6 and 7 only.

Hint 2: Counting

A count-back error can land on 8, but that is a miss, not a second answer.

Check

These defend a single subtraction answer with a bond.

  1. If the whole is 10 and one part is 3, what is the other part, and how do you know you have them all?
  2. Can 15 have two different partners for the part 8, if we stay with whole numbers?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument: why 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 give the same whole, but 15 − 8 and 15 − 7 do not give the same part.
  2. Invent a whole and a part (labeled invented) and name the other part two ways: as a subtraction and as a missing-addend sentence.

Spanish · El sol, la luna, and una estrella

15 minutes · Method: hear, point, and act. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today names el sol, la luna, and una estrella, and reuses two actions: mira (look) and señala (point). Numbers from yesterday may be reused to count invented paper stars, still labeled invented.

Word How to say it Meaning
el sol el sohl the Sun
la luna lah LOO-nah the Moon
una estrella OO-nah es-TREH-yah a star
mira MEE-rah look
señala seh-NYAH-lah point

The guide says mira el sol (look at the Sun) while pointing at a drawing, then señala la luna. The student looks and points. Instruction around the phrases stays in English. Do not retell today’s science in a Spanish paragraph; the words are enough.

Instructions to the guide are in English. Only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish. Hear clips use es-MX. If this is the student’s first Spanish, begin here. Students who later start Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that is by design for those who skipped these foundation levels.

Remember

El sol: the Sun. La luna: the Moon. Una estrella: a star. Mira: look. Señala: point. Act first; speak when ready.

Watch me

The guide says señala la luna and points at a Moon drawing. What should the student do?

  1. Point at the Moon drawing.

Together

What does una estrella mean, and what does el sol mean?

Hint 1

Una estrella: a star.

Hint 2

El sol: the Sun.

Check

These check the new words and the two actions.

  1. Give the English of mira, señala, and la luna.
  2. Hear el sol and una estrella. Give the English of each.

Exercises

  1. What would you do if you heard mira el sol?
  2. Say the Spanish for “the Moon” and for “a star.”

Latin · Nōn est

15 minutes · Method: a picture that matches, and a picture that does not. Original sentences only.

Learn

Today adds nōn (not) to est, so a caption can tell a mismatch.

Word How to say it Meaning
nōn nohn not
est est is
sōl sohl the Sun
lūna LOO-nah the Moon
stella STEL-lah a star

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original captions:

  • Sōl est. — It is the Sun. (pointing at a Sun drawing)
  • Sōl nōn est. — It is not the Sun. (pointing at a Moon drawing, or a star)

The same pattern works with lūna and stella. Do not invent long stories. The job is to say when the picture matches the word, and when it does not.

All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; c is always like k; v is like English w; vowels are pure (a as in father, e as in they, i as in machine, o as in note, u as in rude).

Remember

Nōn est means “is not.” Sōl nōn est is said when the picture is not the Sun. Est still means “is” when the picture matches.

Watch me

You point at a Moon drawing. Which caption is true, Lūna est or Sōl est? Why?

  1. Lūna est is true: it is the Moon.
  2. Sōl est would be false; you could say Sōl nōn est.

Together

What does nōn mean, and what is the English of Stella nōn est?

Hint 1

Nōn means not.

Hint 2

Stella nōn est: it is not a star.

Check

These check est and nōn est.

  1. Write or say a true caption for a drawing of the Sun, and a true “is not” caption for the same drawing if someone said it was the Moon.
  2. What does salvē mean, still?

Exercises

  1. Hear nōn and lūna. Give the English of Lūna nōn est.
  2. Point at a star drawing and say two Latin captions: one that matches, and one that uses nōn about the Sun.

Notebook: a subtraction bond, and “how many left”

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: 13 counters or marks, then 6 taken away or covered, and a number bond of 13 with parts 6 and 7. Label whole, part, left. Then write from stems.

Word bank: whole, part, left, bond, subtract, thirteen, seven.

Stems:

  1. 13 − 6 is 7 because …
  2. I could count back from 13, but …
  3. 6 and 7 make 13, so …

End with the two sentences 13 − 6 = 7 and 6 + 7 = 13, and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw the objects and the bond first. The because-clause should name the bond, not only the answer. A count-back can help, but the bond is the reason this level wants in writing.

Watch me

Finish: “13 − 6 is 7 because …”

  1. A model: “13 − 6 is 7 because 6 and 7 make 13.” Other complete finishes that name the bond are accepted.

Together

Finish the but-stem about counting back. What miss can that stem name?

Hint 1

A count-back can land on the wrong number if you count 13 as a step.

Hint 2: A model

“… but if I counted 13 as the first step I might land on 8, so I used the bond.”

Check

These check the bond in the notebook.

  1. What two number sentences must appear at the end?
  2. What do you draw before you write?

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question about a missing part.
