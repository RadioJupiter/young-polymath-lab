Unit 3 · Day 12 · One sixth as strong

Today's big question: The Moon's gravity is about 1/6 of Earth's. What does that 1/6 actually mean — and what does it not change?

For the guide: keep mass and pull distinct; do not let “weigh 1/6 as much” turn into “become 1/6 as many kilograms.” The 6-part bar is the concrete step. NASA’s figures (about 1.62 against 9.80 m/s², about 1/6) are on the sources page. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done.

Mental math warm-up: renaming a number as equal parts

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A number can be written as equal parts without changing its value. 1/2 of 12 is 6, and 2/4 of 12 is also 6, because 2/4 is the same amount as 1/2. Half of 8 is 4; two quarters of 8 are also 4. You are not making a new number; you are naming the same amount in a different way.

Remember

Equal amounts can have different names: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6. Finding half, then checking that two quarters give the same answer, is a first test of that idea.

Watch me

Is 2/4 of 20 the same as 1/2 of 20? Show both.

  1. 1/2 of 20 is 10.
  2. 2/4 means two of four equal parts of 20. Four equal parts of 20 are 5 each, and two of them are 10.
  3. The two answers match, so 2/4 of 20 = 1/2 of 20.

Together

What is 3/6 of 12? Compare it with 1/2 of 12.

Hint 1: Six equal parts

12 ÷ 6 = 2, so each sixth is 2, and three sixths are 6.

Hint 2: Half

1/2 of 12 is also 6, so 3/6 = 1/2.

Check

Use only equal-part renaming and halves.

  1. What is 2/4 of 16? What is 1/2 of 16?
  2. What is 4/8 of 24? What is 1/2 of 24?

Practice

  1. 1/2 of 40 and 3/6 of 40.
  2. 2/4 of 100 and 1/2 of 100.
  3. A quarter of 32, and two eighths of 32. Are they equal?
  4. Name two fractions that both mean half of a 12-unit bar.
  5. 3/3 of 18 (three of three equal parts). What is that, in one word?

Core lesson: equivalent fractions, and gravity as 1/6

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (fold or cut equal parts), pictorial (bars), then the written rule

Learn

Two fractions are equivalent when they name the same amount of the same whole. You can see this on a bar: a bar cut into 2 equal parts with 1 shaded is the same length shaded as a bar of the same size cut into 6 equal parts with 3 shaded. So 1/2 = 3/6.

The rule, after the picture. Multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator by the same number, not zero, and the amount stays the same: 1/2 = 2/4 = 3/6. You are making finer equal parts, or grouping them, not changing the length.

Gravity on the Moon (NASA). The pull of gravity at the Moon’s surface is about 1.62 m/s², against about 9.80 m/s² on Earth, a ratio of about 0.165, which is about 1/6 (NASA Moon fact sheet). We will use about 1/6.

Mass and pull, carefully. A person’s mass does not change on the Moon. The pull (weight) is about 1/6 as strong. If a bar of 6 equal units stands for the pull of a given mass on Earth, 1 unit is the pull of that same mass on the Moon. Do not write “you become 1/6 as heavy in kilograms” as if the mass changed. Kilograms measure mass; the pull is what becomes about 1/6.

Concrete step. Take a 6-part bar (or six cubes). One part is 1/6. Two parts are 2/6, which is the same as 1/3 of the same bar.

Remember

Equivalent fractions name the same amount: multiply or divide top and bottom by the same number. Moon gravity is about 1/6 of Earth’s (NASA; 1.62 against 9.80 m/s²). Mass stays the same; the pull is about 1/6 as strong. A 6-part bar: 1 part is the Moon’s pull compared with Earth’s.

Watch me

A 6-part bar stands for the pull of a given mass on Earth. How many parts stand for the pull of the same mass on the Moon? Write that as a fraction, and name an equivalent fraction with denominator 12.

  1. Earth’s pull is the whole bar, 6/6.
  2. The Moon’s pull is about 1/6, so 1 part.
  3. 1/6 = 2/12 (multiply top and bottom by 2).

Together

A student says, ‘On the Moon I would be 10 kg if I am 60 kg on Earth, because 1/6 of 60 is 10.’ What is right, and what is wrong?

Hint 1: The calculation

1/6 of 60 is 10, and that is the right size of the pull compared with a 60-unit Earth pull.

Hint 2: The unit

Mass is still 60 kg. The pull is about as strong as the pull on 10 kg would be on Earth. Do not say the mass became 10 kg.

Check

These questions use equivalent fractions and the 1/6 gravity fact as taught.

  1. Write three fractions equivalent to 1/6.
  2. On a 6-part gravity bar, what fraction is 2 parts? Give a simpler name if there is one.

Practice

  1. Shade 1/2, 2/4, and 3/6 of three equal bars and say why they are equivalent.
  2. Fill in: 1/3 = ☐/6 = ☐/12. 2/3 = ☐/6 = ☐/12.
  3. A spring scale on Earth shows a pull we call 24 units for a given mass. About how many units would the same mass show on the Moon? Write the fraction first.
  4. Which is equivalent to 3/6: 1/2, 1/3, or 2/3? Show a bar or the multiply/divide step.
  5. Write one sentence: My mass on the Moon is ________, but the pull is ________ because ________. (Use a made-up mass, not a real person’s.)

Challenge problems: gravity on Mars, and a careful estimate

20 minutes · same skills, a new number from NASA

Learn

Mars’s mean surface gravity is about 3.73 m/s², against about 9.82 m/s² on Earth, a ratio of about 0.380 (NASA Mars fact sheet). 0.380 is 38/100, which simplifies to 19/50. 2/5 = 0.4, which is a little more than 0.380, so “about 2/5 of Earth’s gravity” is a convenient estimate, slightly high. When you use 2/5, say that it is an estimate.

Remember

Mars gravity ≈ 0.380 ≈ 19/50 of Earth’s. 2/5 = 0.4 is a slightly high estimate. Equivalent fractions must be exact; estimates must be labeled.

Watch me

Is 19/50 equivalent to 2/5? Show why, or why not.

  1. 2/5 = 20/50 (multiply top and bottom by 10).
  2. 19/50 is not 20/50, so they are not equivalent.
  3. 2/5 is a little larger than 19/50. That is why 2/5 is only an estimate for Mars.

Together

A 50-part bar stands for Earth’s pull on a given mass. About how many parts stand for the same mass on Mars, using 19/50? Using the 2/5 estimate?

Hint 1: Exact-ish from NASA

19/50 of the bar is 19 parts.

Hint 2: Estimate

2/5 = 20/50, so 20 parts. Say it is an estimate.

Check

Use the Mars numbers as taught, and equivalent fractions.

  1. Write 0.380 as a fraction of 1000 (380/1000) and simplify as far as you can.
  2. If Earth’s pull is 50 units, about how many units on Mars (use 19/50)?

Practice

  1. Which is larger, 19/50 or 2/5? How much larger, as a fraction of 50?
  2. A student writes 1/6 = 1/5 because ‘both are small.’ What has gone wrong? Show 1/6 and 1/5 on bars of equal length, or with a common denominator.
  3. Earth pull 30 units. About how many units on the Moon (1/6) and on Mars (19/50 of 30)? For Mars you may leave an unsimplified form and then a mixed or whole-number rounding, labeled as rounded.
  4. (Stretch.) 19/50 and 38/100: are they equivalent? Why does that matter for 0.38 and 0.380?

Math circle: proving two fractions are the same amount

20 minutes · a diagram, then a short argument

Learn

A proof here is a reason you can show, not a guess. To prove 2/4 = 1/2, you can: (1) draw two bars of equal length and show the shaded parts match; or (2) start with 1/2 and multiply top and bottom by 2; or (3) cut a halved bar each half into two, making fourths, and see that two fourths cover one half.

Remember

Equivalent fractions can be proved with a matching bar, or by multiplying or dividing top and bottom by the same number. A matching length is the test, not a similar-looking scribble.

Watch me

A student shades 2/6 of a short bar and 1/3 of a longer bar and says they are equivalent because both look ‘about a third’. What is missing?

  1. The wholes are not the same size, so the shaded amounts are not being compared as parts of one whole.
  2. Equivalent fractions compare parts of equal wholes.
  3. Redraw both fractions on bars of the same length.

Together

Prove, in two ways, that 4/8 = 1/2.

Hint 1: A picture

Two equal bars: one split into 2 with 1 shaded; one split into 8 with 4 shaded. The shaded lengths match.

Hint 2: The rule

Divide top and bottom of 4/8 by 4, or multiply 1/2 by 4/4.

Check

The skill is a reason, not a new fraction fact.

  1. Can 3/6 = 2/4? Prove it or show they both equal 1/2.
  2. Why is multiplying top and bottom by 0 not allowed?

Practice

  1. Prove 2/6 = 1/3 with a 6-part bar and with the rule.
  2. A friend says 2/5 = 3/6 because ‘you add 1 on the top and 1 on the bottom.’ Show that this is not a legal step.
  3. Write a two-sentence argument: These are equivalent because ________. They would not be equivalent if ________.
  4. On one 12-part bar, mark 1/2, 2/4, 3/6, and 6/12. What do you notice?

Spanish · Weather and the sky

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words

Learn

Useful sky and weather phrases, with hace and está. Hace frío, hace calor, hace sol, hace viento. Está nublado. Está lloviendo. Hace is used with many weather words; está is used with a condition like nublado or with lloviendo. El cielo está nublado. You already know el cielo and la luna (Days 4 and 5).

Word How to say it Meaning
hace frío AH-seh FREE-oh it is cold
hace calor AH-seh kah-LOR it is hot
hace sol AH-seh sohl it is sunny
hace viento AH-seh bee-EN-toh it is windy
está nublado es-TAH noo-BLAH-doh it is cloudy
está lloviendo es-TAH yoh-bee-EN-doh it is raining
el cielo el SYEH-loh the sky

Remember

Hace frío / calor / sol / viento. Está nublado. Está lloviendo. El cielo está nublado. Instruction stays in English; Hear is for the Spanish words.

Watch me

Say in Spanish: ‘It is cloudy.’

  1. Está nublado.
  2. está, not hace, with nublado.
  3. Or: El cielo está nublado.

Together

Say: ‘It is cold, and it is not sunny.’

Hint 1: Cold

Hace frío.

Hint 2: Not sunny

y no hace sol.

Check

Use only today’s weather phrases.

  1. Write: It is raining.
  2. Write: It is hot.

Exercises

  1. Translate: It is windy. The sky is cloudy.
  2. Correct: Hace nublado. and Está frío.
  3. Write two original sentences, one with hace and one with está, about the sky or the weather.

Latin · Nox clāra and stellae

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Yesterday: Lūna est in caelō. Stellae sunt in caelō. Nox est. Lūna est clāra. Today add magna (large) and a short pattern: Nox est clāra. Stellae clārae sunt. The adjective must match the noun: lūna clāra, stellae clārae, caelum is neuter — today, keep caelum in the set phrase in caelō and do not force a new adjective onto it yet.

Word How to say it Meaning
nox clāra noks KLAH-rah a bright night
stellae clārae STEL-lai KLAH-rai bright stars
magna MAG-nah large (feminine)
lūna magna LOO-nah MAG-nah a large moon

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Remember

Adjectives match the noun: lūna clāra, stellae clārae, lūna magna. Nox est clāra. Stellae clārae sunt in caelō.

Watch me

Say in Latin: ‘The night is bright.’

  1. Nox est clāra.
  2. Nox is one thing, so est.
  3. clāra matches nox.

Together

Say: ‘The bright stars are in the sky.’

Hint 1: The stars

Stellae clārae (plural, so clārae).

Hint 2: Are in the sky

sunt in caelō.

Check

Use only these words and agreement.

  1. Write: The moon is large.
  2. Write: The stars are bright.

Exercises

  1. Translate: The night is bright. The large moon is in the sky.
  2. Correct: Stellae clāra est in caelō.
  3. Write two original sentences, one singular and one plural.

Notebook: a gravity bar, and because / but / so

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

A gravity bar is a 6-part bar for Earth’s pull on a chosen mass, with 1 part marked for the Moon. Beside it, write the equivalent names 1/6 = 2/12. Then write because / but / so sentences that keep mass and pull distinct.

Remember

The bar is 6 equal parts for Earth’s pull; 1 part is the Moon’s pull on the same mass. Mass does not change. Use because / but / so, not a one-word label alone.

Watch me

Finish: The pull on the Moon is about 1/6 of the pull on Earth because ________.

  1. Because the Moon’s surface gravity is about 1/6 of Earth’s (NASA: about 1.62 against 9.80 m/s²).
  2. Do not say ‘because I would weigh 1/6 as many kilograms’ as if mass changed.
  3. The because should name the pull, not a change of mass.

Together

Finish aloud, then write: My mass is the same, but ________, so ________.

Hint 1: But

but the pull is only about 1/6 as strong

Hint 2: So

so a 6-part Earth bar needs only 1 part for the Moon.

Check

The notebook work is the bar, the equivalent names, and the stems.

  1. What do you draw first?
  2. What must the sentences not claim about kilograms of mass?

Practice

How the guide helps today: stay for the writing steps. The student draws first, with no writing. Then labels from the word bank. Then says each stem sentence aloud; you confirm it is a complete sentence before it is written. If handwriting is a struggle, write the student's own spoken sentence in light pencil for tracing or copying. Do not invent sentences for the student.

1. Draw. A 6-part bar for Earth’s pull, with 1 part marked for the Moon. Under it, a 12-part bar of the same length with 2 parts marked, to show 1/6 = 2/12. Optional: a smaller sketch of a person (no name) standing on a 6-part bar and on a 1-part bar.

2. Label, from the word bank. Point and say each word, then write it.

Word bank: Earth · Moon · mass · pull · gravity · 1/6 · 2/12 · equivalent · bar · NASA

3. Write. Say each stem aloud, then write it.

  • The Moon’s pull is about ________ of Earth’s pull.
  • 1/6 is equivalent to ________ because ________.
  • Mass stays the same, but ________.
  • The pull is weaker, so ________.
