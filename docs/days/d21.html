Mental math warm-up: halves of a turn, and a right angle

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A full turn is 360°. A straight line is half of that, 180°. A right angle is a quarter of a full turn, 90°. These are facts you will use all week, not a new kind of arithmetic.

Remember

Full turn 360°. Straight angle 180°. Right angle 90°. Half of 180° is 90°. Half of 90° is 45°.

Watch me

What is a right angle, as a fraction of a full turn, and in degrees?

  1. A full turn is 360°.
  2. A quarter of 360 is 90.
  3. A right angle is one quarter of a full turn, 90°.

Together

How many degrees are two right angles? Why is that the same as a straight line?

Hint 1: Two quarters

Two quarters of a full turn are a half turn.

Hint 2: Degrees

2 × 90° = 180°, and 180° is a straight angle.

Check

These use only the turn facts just named.

  1. Half of a full turn, in degrees.
  2. Three right angles, in degrees.
  3. How many right angles make a full turn?

Practice

  1. A 45° angle is what fraction of a right angle?
  2. What is 360° − 90°?
  3. Is 80° a right angle, an acute angle, or an obtuse angle? (Acute means less than 90° but more than 0°; obtuse means more than 90° and less than 180°. Those names are used from the core lesson onward; if this warm-up comes first, say 80° is less than a right angle.)

Core lesson: angles, and a turn

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (two strips or a door), pictorial (a drawing with a marked turn), then the degree facts · no protractor required for today’s numbers

Learn

An angle is a turn between two rays (or two line segments) that share a point, the vertex. We measure the turn in degrees. A full turn is 360°. A straight angle is 180°. A right angle is 90°.

Names by size. An acute angle is greater than 0° and less than 90°. An obtuse angle is greater than 90° and less than 180°. The picture shows a 35° angle (acute) next to a 145° angle (obtuse). They sit on the straight line ABC, and 35° + 145° = 180°.

Three facts you will use. 1. Angles on a straight line add to 180°. 2. Angles around a point add to 360°. 3. When two lines cross, the vertically opposite angles are equal (the ones that face each other, not the ones that sit side by side).

A telescope, or a door, is pointed in a direction. When you turn it, the change is an angle. Today we do not invent a real sky coordinate or a mission pointing; we learn the turn itself.

Remember

Angle = a turn, in degrees. Full turn 360°, straight 180°, right 90°. Acute: between 0° and 90°. Obtuse: between 90° and 180°. Straight line: the two angles add to 180°. Around a point: they add to 360°. Vertically opposite angles are equal.

Watch me

In the picture, one angle on the straight line is 35°. What is the other, and why?

  1. The two angles on the straight line add to 180°.
  2. 180° − 35° = 145°.
  3. 35° is acute; 145° is obtuse. They belong together on one straight line.

Together

Two lines cross. One of the angles is 70°. What is the angle opposite it, and what is an angle next to it?

Hint 1: Opposite

Vertically opposite angles are equal, so the opposite angle is 70°.

Hint 2: Adjacent

A 70° angle and its neighbor sit on a straight line, so the neighbor is 180° − 70° = 110°.

Check

These use only the turn facts taught today.

  1. An angle of 50° sits on a straight line with another angle. Find the other angle, and name both (acute or obtuse).
  2. Three equal angles meet around a point, with no leftover space. How large is each?

Practice

  1. Two lines cross. One angle is 125°. Find the other three angles.
  2. Can a right angle sit on a straight line with an acute angle? Say why, with degrees.
  3. A door opens 90°, then 30° more in the same direction. What is the total turn, and is the total acute, right, or obtuse?
  4. Draw two lines that cross, mark one angle 40°, and fill in the other three.
  5. Why is “a full turn is 360°” a definition we are using, not a measurement of Mars or of a crater?

Challenge problems: a missing angle, and a turn that is not a length

20–25 minutes · the same turn facts, with two steps

Learn

These problems use only today’s facts, sometimes in two steps. An angle is not a length; 90° is not “longer than” 40° in the way 90 cm is longer than 40 cm.

Remember

Straight line 180°, around a point 360°, vertically opposite equal. Degrees are a turn, not a length.

Watch me

Four angles meet around a point. Three of them are 80°, 100°, and 90°. Find the fourth.

  1. The four add to 360°.
  2. 80 + 100 + 90 = 270.
  3. 360 − 270 = 90°. The fourth is a right angle.

Together

On a straight line, one angle is twice the other. Find both angles.

Hint 1: Parts

Call the smaller angle x. Then the other is 2x, and x + 2x = 180°.

Hint 2: The numbers

3x = 180°, so x = 60°, and 2x = 120°.

Check

These stay with today’s turn facts.

  1. Two lines cross. The adjacent (next-door) angles are in the ratio 1 to 3. Find all four angles.
  2. A telescope is turned 40°, then turned back 15° the other way. What is the net turn from the start, and why is that not a distance on the Moon?

Practice

  1. Can three acute angles add to a straight angle? Give an example, or say why not.
  2. If someone writes “the angle is 90 km,” what has gone wrong?
  3. Draw a straight line and a ray that cuts it, with one angle 2/5 of 180°. Find both angles.

Math circle: why vertically opposite angles are equal

20 minutes · a short argument, not a new unit

Learn

We used “vertically opposite angles are equal” as a fact. Here is a short reason, from the straight-line fact you already have. It is an argument, not a new measuring skill.

Remember

Each adjacent pair on a straight line adds to 180°. Two angles that are each 180° minus the same neighbor must be equal.

Watch me

Two lines cross at O. Call one angle a, and the angle next to it (on the straight line) b. What is the angle opposite a, and why?

  1. a + b = 180°, because they sit on a straight line.
  2. The angle opposite a also sits on a straight line with that same b, so it plus b is 180°.
  3. Two angles that both make 180° with the same b must be equal. The opposite angle is also a.

Together

If a is 65°, use the argument (not “just because they are opposite”) to find the opposite angle.

Hint 1: The neighbor

The neighbor b is 180° − 65° = 115°.

Hint 2: The opposite

The opposite also makes 180° with 115°, so it is 65°.

Check

The point is the reason, not a new formula.

  1. Write the argument in two sentences, using a and b, without a diagram of a particular size.
  2. Why does this argument need the straight-line fact first?

Practice

  1. Give one sentence you want to keep: an angle is a turn, not a length.

Spanish · A turn: a la derecha, a la izquierda

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
gira HEE-rah he/she/it turns, or a command-like present: turns
a la derecha ah lah deh-REH-chah to the right
a la izquierda ah lah ees-kee-EHR-dah to the left
un ángulo oon AHNG-goo-loh an angle
recto REK-toh right (as in a right angle: un ángulo recto)

Today’s words name a turn. Instruction stays in English. Un ángulo recto is a right angle; recto here is not “a straight line” in English, even though a straight angle is 180°.

Original sentences: Gira a la derecha. Gira a la izquierda. Hay un ángulo recto.

For the guide: point right and left as you say the phrases. This is present-tense description, not a telescope command from a mission.

Remember

gira; a la derecha; a la izquierda; un ángulo; un ángulo recto. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words.

Watch me

Say: “Turn to the right.”

  1. Gira a la derecha.
  2. Gira is the turn. A la derecha is the direction.
  3. This is a present-tense pattern, not a past narrative.

Together

How do you say “a right angle”?

Hint 1: The noun

un ángulo

Hint 2: The phrase

un ángulo recto

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “Turn to the left.”
  2. Write: “There is a right angle.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *un ángulo derecho for a right angle, if someone used the “right/left” word by mistake.
  2. Hear gira, derecha, and ángulo, then use each.
  3. Why is recto in un ángulo recto not the same job as a la derecha?
  4. A pointing retell: right, left, a right angle.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · angulus and a right angle

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
angulus ANG-goo-lus an angle (nominative)
Angulus rēctus est ANG-goo-lus RAYK-tus est The angle is right (a right angle)
linea LIN-eh-ah a line
Linea longa est LIN-eh-ah LONG-ah est The line is long

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Angulus rēctus est. Linea longa est. Angulus nōn rēctus est.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. No degree numbers and no SI units in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

angulus; Angulus rēctus est; linea; Linea longa est. Adjectives match. No degrees in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read: Angulus rēctus est. Why rēctus, not *rēcta?

  1. angulus is masculine.
  2. The adjective matches: rēctus.
  3. The sentence says the angle is a right angle, not that a line is long.

Together

Say: “The line is long.”

Hint 1: The noun

linea, feminine.

Hint 2: The sentence

Linea longa est.

Check

These use only today’s patterns.

  1. Write: “The angle is not right.”
  2. Write: “The line is not long.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *angulus longa est and *linea rēctus est.
  2. Read the four model sentences aloud.
  3. Draw a right angle and a long line, labeled in Latin.
  4. Why are the 35° and 145° left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy one model sentence.

Notebook: two strips, a turn, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw two strips (or two rays) that make a 35° angle on a straight line, and label the 145° partner. Mark a right angle in a different drawing. Then complete because / but / so stems: an angle is a turn in degrees, but it is not a length, so you write °, not cm or km, for a turn.

Word bank: angle, degree, right, acute, obtuse, straight, vertex, turn, unit.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. Degrees are a turn, not a length. No points or badges — the page is the work.

Watch me

A model sentence: The other angle is 145° because it sits on a straight line with 35°, but 145° is not “longer than” 35°, so I will not write those numbers in centimeters.

  1. 180° − 35° = 145°.
  2. The but names the job: a turn, not a length.
  3. The so chooses the unit °.

Together

Finish: A right angle is 90°, because ________, but a 90 km length is a different job, so ________.

Hint 1: The turn

…it is a quarter of a full turn, or half of a straight angle.

Hint 2: The unit

…I will not write 90° as 90 km.

Check

The page should show the drawings, the labels, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the straight-line pair (35° and 145°) and a separate right angle, with units marked as degrees.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) that keeps a turn apart from a length.

Practice

  1. Add a crossing with one angle 70° and the other three filled in.
  2. Tick the word-bank words you used.
  3. The page is a record of today’s skill, not a badge.
  4. A note that 360° is the agreed full turn, not a NASA figure.
  5. File the page. It is not a reward.
