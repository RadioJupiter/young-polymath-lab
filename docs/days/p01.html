Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A number bond of 10 is two parts that make 10. 6 + 4 = 10, and 4 + 6 = 10. 7 + 3 = 10. If you know one bond, you know the other way around too.

Remember

Two parts that make 10 are a bond of 10. The order of the parts does not change the total.

Watch me

What is 6 + 4, and what is 4 + 6?

  1. 6 + 4 = 10, a bond of 10.
  2. 4 + 6 is the same bond the other way around, still 10.

Together

What is 7 + 3? Say the bond.

Hint 1: Ten

7 and 3 make 10.

Hint 2: The other way

3 + 7 is the same total.

Check

These are bonds of 10, said aloud.

  1. 5 + 5
  2. 8 + 2
  3. 9 + 1

Practice

  1. 2 + 8
  2. 1 + 9
  3. 0 + 10

Core lesson: numbers to 1,000, and a classroom sky

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract

Learn

A number tells how many. We write numbers with places: ones, tens, and hundreds. Ten ones make 1 ten. Ten tens make 1 hundred. Ten hundreds make 1,000.

100100100100100100100100100100

Ten hundred-discs make one thousand. 1,000 is the largest whole number this level names as a first look. Later levels will go further.

The number 247 is 2 hundreds, 4 tens, and 7 ones.

10010010101010111

We read it “two hundred forty-seven,” but the important job is the places, not writing the number as a long word.

Today’s sky picture is an invented classroom picture, labeled invented. It has 12 stars, in 3 rows of 4. It is not an official count of stars in the real sky. No one in this lesson is asked to count all the stars that exist.

To count without missing any, count in a fixed order: left to right in the first row, then the next row, and so on. 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.

Remember

Ones, tens, hundreds: ten of one place make one of the next place. 247 is 2 hundreds, 4 tens, and 7 ones. 1,000 is ten hundreds. Classroom star counts are invented and labeled invented. Count in a fixed order so you do not miss any.

Watch me

Show 247 with hundreds, tens, and ones, and say what each digit means.

  1. The 2 is 2 hundreds (200). The 4 is 4 tens (40). The 7 is 7 ones (7).
  2. 200 + 40 + 7 = 247.

Together

The invented sky has 3 rows of 4 stars. How many stars are there, and why is this not an official star count?

Hint 1: Equal groups

4 + 4 + 4, or 3 groups of 4.

Hint 2: The label

The picture is an invented classroom picture, not a count of the real sky.

Check

These read a number in places, or count a labeled invented picture in order.

  1. What is 305 in hundreds, tens, and ones?
  2. What number is 6 hundreds, 0 tens, and 4 ones?

Practice

  1. Write 1,000 as hundreds.
  2. An invented picture has 2 rows of 5 stars. How many stars? Why must you still count in order?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

A 0 in a place is not “nothing to think about.” In 305, the 0 says there are no tens, and it holds the tens place so that the 3 can stay as hundreds. 35 is a different number: 3 tens and 5 ones.

Remember

A zero can hold a place. 305 and 35 are not the same number.

Watch me

Why is 305 not the same as 35?

  1. 305 has 3 hundreds, 0 tens, and 5 ones.
  2. 35 has no hundreds; it is 3 tens and 5 ones. The 0 in 305 is doing a job.

Together

What number is 4 hundreds and 12 ones, after you regroup the ones?

Hint 1: Tens

12 ones make 1 ten and 2 ones.

Hint 2: The number

4 hundreds, 1 ten, and 2 ones is 412.

Check

These stretch place value a little, still within 1,000.

  1. Which is greater, 702 or 720? How do you know from the places?
  2. An invented sky has 5 rows of 4 stars. How many stars?

Practice

  1. Write 1 hundred, 10 tens, and 0 ones as a three-digit number, after you regroup.
  2. What is 10 less than 200? Use places, not a guess.

Math circle: counting without missing any

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

To be sure you have counted every object, and have not counted one twice, you need a plan. A good plan is an order that does not jump around: rows, or columns, or a path that visits each object once.

Remember

A count you can trust has a plan. “I just looked and saw 12” is not a plan.

Watch me

How would you count the 12 invented stars in today’s picture so that you can prove the total?

  1. Count left to right along the first row (4), then the second row (4 more, running total 8), then the third (4 more, 12).
  2. Or count in columns: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12. Either plan works if it visits each star once.

Together

An invented picture has 4 rows of 4 stars. A student counts 15, then 16, then 17, by pointing at random. What is wrong, and what is the true total if there are really 4 rows of 4?

Hint 1: The plan

Pointing at random can miss a star or count one twice, so the three totals disagree.

Hint 2: Equal groups

4 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 16, or 4 groups of 4.

Check

These are about a counting plan, not about official stars.

  1. Why can two careful people get the same total for the same invented picture?
  2. If a row is hidden, can you still be sure of the total? What must you know?

Practice

  1. Invent (and label invented) a 2-by-6 star picture. Count it two ways and show that both ways agree.
  2. Why is “count the real sky tonight and get a number for all the stars” not this lesson’s job?

Spanish · Hola, adiós, and two actions

15 minutes · Method: comprehensible input and Total Physical Response. Listen and act first; speak when ready. Instructions are in English.

Learn

We learn Spanish by hearing short, clear phrases and acting them out. Study the words first, then watch the actions. Explanations are in English; only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish.

Word How to say it Meaning
hola OH-lah hello
adiós ah-DYOHS goodbye
levántate leh-VAHN-tah-teh stand up
siéntate SYEN-tah-teh sit down

Hola is a greeting. Adiós is a goodbye. Levántate means stand up. Siéntate means sit down. The guide says the word and does the action; the student acts first, and may speak when ready.

Instructions to the guide are in English. Only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish. Hear clips use es-MX. If this is the student’s first Spanish, begin here. Students who later start Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that is by design for those who skipped these foundation levels.

Remember

Hola: hello. Adiós: goodbye. Levántate: stand up. Siéntate: sit down. Act first; speak when ready. Instruction stays in English.

Watch me

The guide says hola and waves, then says levántate and stands. What should the student do first?

  1. Wave or show that hola was heard, as a greeting.
  2. Stand up when they hear levántate, without needing to say it yet.

Together

The guide says siéntate. What does the student do, and what must the guide not do first?

Hint 1: The action

The student sits down.

Hint 2: Translation

The guide should not translate every word into English before the action. English is for the instructions around the words.

Check

These check the four phrases by meaning and by action.

  1. What does adiós mean, and when would you use it?
  2. Hear hola and levántate. What does each one ask you to do or say?

Exercises

  1. Match: siéntate — sit down; levántate — stand up.
  2. Why is it fine if the student stays quiet today, if the actions are right?

Latin · Salvē, and stella

15 minutes · Method: a picture, then a short original caption. All text here is original.

Learn

We read Latin from a picture: first look, then hear the caption, then say it. Study the words first. Explanations are in English.

Word How to say it Meaning
salvē SAL-way hello (to one person)
stella STEL-lah a star
est est is

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

A simple original caption, pointing at one star in a drawing:

Stella est. — It is a star. / There is a star.

Salvē is a greeting to one person. (A greeting to more than one person is reserved.)

All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; c is always like k; v is like English w; vowels are pure (a as in father, e as in they, i as in machine, o as in note, u as in rude).

Remember

Salvē: hello, to one person. Stella: a star. Est: is. Stella est is an original caption for a picture of a star.

Watch me

Point at a drawn star and say the Latin caption, then give the English meaning.

  1. Stella est.
  2. It is a star, or there is a star.

Together

The guide says salvē. What is the English meaning, and is this a greeting to one person or to many?

Hint 1: Meaning

Hello.

Hint 2: How many

Salvē is the form for one person, as taught today.

Check

These use today’s three words only.

  1. What does stella mean?
  2. Write or say Stella est, and give the English.

Exercises

  1. Hear salvē and stella. Give the English of each.
  2. Why does this lesson use an Italian voice for Hear, and what is that voice not?

Notebook: a classroom sky, counted in order

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: copy today’s invented 12-star picture (3 rows of 4), or make your own invented sky of equal rows, and label it invented. Then label: star, row, count. Then write from stems. Do not start with a blank paragraph.

Word bank: star, row, count, invented, twelve (or your total), order.

Stems (say each finished sentence aloud, then write it):

  1. I counted the stars in order, because …
  2. The picture is invented, but …
  3. I counted ___ stars, so …

End with a number sentence for your count (for example 4 + 4 + 4 = 12) and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw, label, then write. Count in a planned order. Label invented pictures invented. One complete sentence is better than a long unfinished one.

Watch me

Finish this stem aloud: “I counted the stars in order, because …”

  1. A model: “I counted the stars in order, because I did not want to miss one or count one twice.” Other complete, honest finishes are accepted.

Together

Finish: “The picture is invented, but …” What must the but-clause not do?

Hint 1: A contrast

A model: “The picture is invented, but I can still count it with a plan.”

Hint 2: Official counts

Do not treat the total as an official count of the real sky.

Check

These check the notebook order and the stems.

  1. In what order do you work: write first, or draw first?
  2. Write one number sentence for 3 rows of 4.

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing, after you have drawn and labeled.
  2. Write one “what next” question about counting a sky picture.
