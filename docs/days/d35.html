Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 10

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

This warm-up mixes the mental work of the level: a simplified ratio, a missing part, a short speed, and a percent of a quantity. Use the facts as taught.

Remember

Scale both sides of a ratio together. Speed = distance ÷ time. 10% is a tenth; 25% is a quarter.

Watch me

Simplify 6 : 9, and find 25% of 40.

  1. 6 : 9 divides by 3, so 2 : 3.
  2. 25% is a quarter, so 25% of 40 is 10.
  3. The 40 can be invented, labeled invented.

Together

2 : 5 = 6 : □, and an invented walk of 12 km in 3 hours: what is the speed?

Hint 1: The ratio

2 × 3 = 6, so □ = 15.

Hint 2: The walk

12 ÷ 3 = 4 km/h, invented.

Check

These mix the level’s mental facts.

  1. What is 10% of 80?
  2. Write 1 : 4 with both sides times 3.

Practice

  1. At 5 km/h, how far in 2 hours? (Invented.)
  2. Simplify 8 : 12.

Level 10 check

35–40 minutes · end-of-level mastery check · four out of five or better on each part; not a score or a reward

Learn

This sitting gathers the level: ratio as a comparison by equal parts, including simplifying and equivalent ratios; a scale as a ratio, with the 1 cm : 1,000 km Earth–Moon model and an honesty note about star charts; speed as distance per time, with invented walks and the ISS’s about-28,000 km/h FAQ figure; and percent as per hundred, with a named whole.

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

Remember

A ratio compares equal parts, in order. A scale is a ratio for every length. Speed = distance ÷ time, with matching units. Percent means per hundred of a named whole. Invented mixes and walks stay labeled. NASA figures keep their labels. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Watch me

A short layout example, not one of the unit-check items: simplify 10 : 15, then find 10% of 40.

  1. 10 : 15 divides by 5, so 2 : 3.
  2. 10% of 40 is 4.
  3. After a Together item for layout, do the Unit check alone.

Together

The Together item is practice for layout. After it, do the Unit check alone. What will you write beside 28,000 km/h, besides the number?

Hint 1: The source

That it is NASA’s Spot the Station FAQ figure, about 28,000 km/h.

Hint 2: Invented numbers

If a mix, a map, or a walk is invented, it stays labeled invented.

Check

The Together item is only layout practice. The Unit check is the mastery sitting.

  1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A ratio and equivalent ratios, Part B scale and rate, Part C percent. Then begin Part A when the guide says.
  2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

Unit check

Work alone. Use a bar model or a rate strip when it helps. Keep invented mixes, maps, and walks labeled. Keep NASA figures labeled as fact-sheet diameters, already-taught distances, the FAQ’s about-28,000 km/h, or NASA’s “about / roughly 400” wording. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: ratio and equivalent ratios

  1. Write the ratio of 6 to 8 in simplest form, and say how many parts there are in all.
  2. 2 : 5 = 8 : □. Find the missing number.
  3. A 3 : 4 mix has 21 in the first amount. What is the second amount? (Invented classroom mix, labeled invented.)
  4. Earth’s fact-sheet diameter is 12,756 km and the Moon’s is 3,475 km. Why is “exactly 4 : 1” a little high?
  5. Why is 2 : 3 not the same comparison as 3 : 2?

Part B: scale and rate

  1. At the model scale 1 cm : 1,000 km, how many centimeters across is the Moon (3,475 km)? You may leave the exact value or round to the nearest tenth of a centimeter.
  2. At the same scale, the already-taught mean Earth–Moon distance is 384,400 km. How many centimeters, and about how many meters, is that gap?
  3. An invented walk covers 24 km in 4 hours, steadily. What is the speed, and how long for 18 km at that speed?
  4. The ISS travels at about 28,000 km/h (NASA FAQ). How far, at that labeled figure, in 3 hours?
  5. Why will this course not invent a “1 cm to 4 light-years” scale for a printed star chart?

Part C: percent

  1. Write 25% as a fraction in lowest terms and as a decimal.
  2. Find 10% of 60, and then 30% of 60.
  3. Find 25% of 80.
  4. An invented box holds 50 tiles, and 10 are blue. What percent are blue?
  5. Why is “25% of 40 is 25” not sound?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 11. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

These items mix the level, still with labeled invented mixes and walks, and labeled NASA figures. They are extra thinking, not extra credit and not a second score.

Remember

Same skills as the check. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Watch me

An invented 2 : 3 mix has 10 cups in all. How many of each kind, and what is 20% of those 10 cups?

  1. 2 + 3 = 5 parts; each part is 2 cups; the kinds are 4 cups and 6 cups. Invented mix.
  2. 20% of 10 is 2 cups.
  3. The mix and the 20% are both of invented wholes, labeled invented.

Together

At 1 cm : 1,000 km, about how wide is Earth in the model, and how far does the ISS go in 0.5 h at about 28,000 km/h?

Hint 1: Earth

12,756 ÷ 1,000 = 12.756 cm, about 12.8 cm.

Hint 2: The ISS

28,000 × 0.5 = 14,000 km, FAQ figure.

Check

These mix the level and stay labeled.

  1. Simplify 9 : 15, then find 50% of 18.
  2. An invented map is 1 cm : 4 m. A 3 cm line on the map is how many real meters? Label the map invented.

Practice

  1. 3 : 4 = 12 : □.
  2. At 28,000 km/h, how many hours for 70,000 km?
  3. Write 0.3 as a percent, and find 10% of 30.
  4. Why is forcing 150,000,000 ÷ 384,400 to equal 400 not the same as quoting NASA’s “roughly 400”?
  5. This wrap is not a second score or a badge.

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · a short reason, then a check

Learn

The level has one honesty habit in three clothes: a ratio, a scale, and a percent all need a named job. An invented 2 : 3 mix is not a NASA fuel. A star chart is not automatically a distance scale. 25% is not the bare number 25. Sourced figures keep the wording they came with (about 28,000 km/h; about / roughly 400), and we do not blend them into a homemade official number.

Remember

Name the whole, name the scale, name the source. Invented mixes and walks stay invented. This circle is not a second score.

Watch me

Give one invented figure from this unit and one sourced figure, and say the job of each.

  1. Invented: for example a 2 : 3 powder, a 1 cm : 2 m classroom map, or a 4 km/h walk.
  2. Sourced: for example Earth 12,756 km, the Moon 3,475 km, 384,400 km, light about 300,000 km/s, or the ISS about 28,000 km/h (NASA FAQ).
  3. The first kind is a classroom tool, labeled invented. The second kind keeps its source and its wording.

Together

Finish: I can use a neat 2 : 3 in a classroom mix because ________, but I will not write Earth and the Moon as exactly 4 : 1, so ________.

Hint 1: The because

…the mix was invented, and 2 : 3 is exact for that mix…

Hint 2: The so

…I will keep 12,756 : 3,475 and say “a little less than 4 to 1,” rather than forcing a neater official ratio.

Check

These close the argument.

  1. Name one scale from Day 5 and one scale from this unit, and one job for each.
  2. Why is a percent without a named whole not yet a quantity?

Practice

  1. Are “about 28,000 km/h” and “five miles per second” two votes to average?
  2. What is 42,000 km (28,000 × 1.5) allowed to be, and what is it not?
  3. Why is a printed star chart usually the wrong place to write a cm-to-kilometer scale?
  4. How does 25% sit beside 0.25 and 1/4, and why is that not the same as writing “25”?
  5. This argument is not a badge or a reward.

Spanish · A short part-and-map story

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only · Review of Days 31–34

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la parte lah PAR-teh the part
el grupo el GROO-poh the group
el mapa el MAH-pah the map
cerca SEHR-kah near
camina kah-MEE-nah he or she walks
despacio des-PAH-syoh slowly
cien syehn a hundred
la mitad lah mee-TAHD the half

A short review story, original sentences: Hay una parte. Hay un grupo. La parte es igual. Hay un mapa. El mapa está cerca. Camina despacio. Hay cien. Es la mitad.

For the guide: the student retells while pointing. No ratios, km/h, or percents in the Spanish. This is not a second mastery score.

Remember

la parte; el grupo; el mapa; cerca; camina; despacio; cien; la mitad. Present tense only. Hear for Spanish words.

Watch me

Retell the first three sentences, pointing.

  1. Hay una parte. There is a part.
  2. Hay un grupo. There is a group.
  3. La parte es igual. The part is equal.

Together

How do you say “There are a hundred,” and “It is the half”?

Hint 1: A hundred

Hay cien.

Hint 2: The half

Es la mitad.

Check

These use only this unit’s phrases.

  1. Write: “The map is near.”
  2. Write: “He or she walks slowly.”

Exercises

  1. Retell the whole eight-sentence story, pointing.
  2. Hear parte, mapa, and mitad, then use each.
  3. Why does the story never say 2 : 3, 28,000, or 25% in Spanish?
  4. Correct: *La mapa está cerca or *El mitad, if they appear.
  5. Copy two model sentences into the notebook.

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

A short review story, original sentences: Pars parva est. Tōtum magnum est. Imāgō parva est. Canis celer est. Canis tardus nōn est. Centum adsunt. Dīmidium est.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. No ratios, no km/h, and no percent signs in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. This is not a second mastery score.

Watch me

Canis celer est.

  2. Write: “A hundred are here.”

Exercises

Notebook: a Level 10 page — a ratio, a scale or rate, and a percent

60 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw one 2 : 3 bar, one Earth–Moon size pair (or a rate strip plus a labeled ISS product), and one 25% bar. Then complete because / but / so stems that gather the level: a neat classroom ratio is exact because it was invented, but a sourced size or speed keeps its wording, so I will label invented mixes and I will not force 4 : 1, average two NASA speeds, or invent a NASA percent.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. This page is a record, not a second score or a badge.

Watch me

A model sentence: 2 : 3 is exact for an invented mix because the mix was made that way, but 12,756 : 3,475 is a little less than 4 : 1, so I will keep the fact-sheet diameters and I will not force a neater official ratio.

  1. The because restates a classroom exactness.
  2. The but keeps a sourced comparison honest.
  3. The so states the habit for the whole level.

Together

Finish: A percent needs a named whole because ________, but a star chart is usually not a distance scale, so ________.

Hint 1: The because

…25% of 80 is 20 and 25% of 40 is 10…

Hint 2: The so

…I will not invent a cm-to-light-year scale I did not measure, and I will name the whole when I write a percent.

Check

The page should show the drawings and complete stems.

  1. Draw the ratio bar, the scale or rate picture, and the percent bar, with invented and sourced figures labeled.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) that gathers honest labels for ratios, scales or rates, and percents.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: ratio, scale, rate, percent, invented, FAQ, whole, about.
  2. The page is a record of the level, not a badge.
  3. A note that the unit check is the mastery decision, and this page is not a second score.
  4. File the page. It is not a reward.
  5. A note that four out of five on each part, not this page, is the mastery rule.
