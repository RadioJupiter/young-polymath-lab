Unit 6 · Day 27 · Comparing decimals, and 0.4 = 0.40

Today's big question: How do we compare decimals, and what is the difference between the same amount and a finer written reading?

For the guide: Compare in the same place, usually hundredths. 0.4 and 0.40 are the same amount. A written 0.40 can still mark a measurement to the nearest hundredth; that is a caution, not a claim that 0.40 is a larger amount, and not a course in significant figures. Do not invent zeros on a NASA diameter.

Mental math warm-up: same amounts, and which is greater

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

To compare decimals, put them in the same place — usually hundredths — and then compare, just as we compared fractions with the same-size parts. 0.4 is 40 hundredths; 0.37 is 37 hundredths.

Remember

0.4 = 0.40 as an amount. Compare tenths by writing them as hundredths when you need to. 0.09 is less than 0.10, which is 0.1.

Watch me

Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58?

  1. 0.6 is 6 tenths, which is 60 hundredths.
  2. 0.58 is 58 hundredths.
  3. 60 hundredths is greater, so 0.6 is greater than 0.58.

Together

Is 0.30 the same amount as 0.3?

Hint 1: Places

0.3 is 3 tenths. 0.30 is 3 tenths and 0 hundredths.

Hint 2: Amount

Yes, they are the same amount: 30 hundredths = 3 tenths.

Check

These use only comparing and equivalent amounts.

  1. Which is greater, 0.2 or 0.19?
  2. Write 0.8 as hundredths.

Practice

  1. Order from least to greatest: 0.5, 0.05, 0.45.
  2. Is 0.10 the same amount as 0.1, or is it ten times as much?

Core lesson: comparing decimals, and 0.4 = 0.40

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (tenths strips and a hundred-square), pictorial (the number line in tenths), then comparing in the same place

Learn

Comparing. To tell which of two decimals is greater, write them with the same places and compare those amounts. 0.8 is 80 hundredths; 0.75 is 75 hundredths; so 0.8 > 0.75. On the number line, 0.75 sits between 0.7 and 0.8, a little closer to 0.8 if you mark the halfway hundredth (0.75 is halfway from 0.7 to 0.8).

A common mix-up. 0.08 is 8 hundredths, which is less than 0.1 (10 hundredths) and much less than 0.8 (80 hundredths). The “8” looking the same is not enough; the place matters.

Equivalent decimals. 0.4 and 0.40 are the same amount: 4 tenths = 40 hundredths. Writing a zero in the hundredths place does not change the value. This is the same idea as 4/10 = 40/100.

A caution about measurements. When two written numbers are amounts, 0.4 and 0.40 are equal. When they are readings from a scale, a person who writes 0.40 may be saying the length was given to the nearest hundredth, while 0.4 may mean it was given only to the nearest tenth. The amounts are equal; the writing can still carry a different claim about how finely something was measured. We will not treat that as a full topic in significant figures — only as a reason not to say “the extra zero is never doing any job.”

Classroom lengths stay labeled invented. A NASA figure from an earlier day (for example, about 6,792 km) stays as we used it; we will not invent a “0.40 km more precise” Mars diameter.

Remember

Compare decimals in the same place, usually hundredths. 0.4 = 0.40 as an amount. 0.08 < 0.1 < 0.8. A trailing zero does not change the amount; on a measurement it can still mark a finer reading. Invented lengths stay labeled.

Watch me

Order 0.8, 0.75, and 0.08 from least to greatest.

  1. Write them as hundredths: 0.80, 0.75, and 0.08.
  2. 8 hundredths, then 75 hundredths, then 80 hundredths.
  3. So 0.08, 0.75, 0.8.

Together

Place 0.3 and 0.30 on a tenths number line. Are they the same point?

Hint 1: Amounts

0.3 is 3 tenths; 0.30 is 3 tenths and 0 hundredths.

Hint 2: The point

They mark the same point on the line. The amounts are equal.

Check

These use only comparing and equivalent amounts.

  1. Which is greater, 0.6 or 0.58? Write both as hundredths.
  2. Write 0.5 as hundredths, and say whether 0.5 and 0.50 are the same amount.

Practice

  1. Order from least to greatest: 0.4, 0.04, 0.39, 0.40.
  2. Mark 0.25 on a tenths number line (it sits halfway from 0.2 to 0.3).
  3. Why is 0.09 less than 0.1?
  4. A friend says 0.40 is “bigger than 0.4 because of the extra zero.” What do we say about the amount, and what smaller caution can we still keep about a measurement?
  5. An invented classroom ribbon is 0.7 m; another invented ribbon is 0.65 m. Which is longer? Label both invented.

Challenge problems: close decimals, and a reading that is not a NASA size

20 minutes · after the core, or later if the core took the full sitting

Learn

These items compare close decimals and keep the amount-versus-reading caution honest. We still do not invent a more precise NASA diameter.

Remember

Compare in the same place. 0.4 = 0.40 as an amount. Invented readings stay labeled.

Watch me

Which is closer to 0.5: 0.48 or 0.53?

  1. 0.5 is 50 hundredths.
  2. 0.48 is 2 hundredths below; 0.53 is 3 hundredths above.
  3. 0.48 is closer to 0.5.

Together

A classroom ruler reading is written 0.40 m (invented). Another person writes the same stick as 0.4 m. Same amount? Same claimed precision?

Hint 1: Amount

Yes, 0.40 m and 0.4 m are the same amount.

Hint 2: The writing

The written 0.40 can claim a reading to the nearest hundredth; 0.4 may claim only the nearest tenth. Label the stick invented.

Check

These stay with comparing and the caution.

  1. Order 1.05, 0.95, and 1.5 from least to greatest.
  2. Which is greater, 0.99 or 1? By how many hundredths?

Practice

  1. Find a decimal in hundredths that sits between 0.4 and 0.5.
  2. Is 0.200 the same amount as 0.2? (Thousandths were named yesterday; 0.200 is 2 tenths and 0 hundredths and 0 thousandths.)
  3. Why is “whichever has more digits is greater” false for 0.8 and 0.75?
  4. An invented tank mark is 0.08 L from empty; another invented mark is 0.8 L from empty. Which is closer to 1 L? Label both invented.
  5. This course used Mars as about 6,792 km across (NASA). Why is “6,792.00 km” not a more careful NASA measurement from us?

Math circle: same amount, different claimed precision

20 minutes · a short reason, then a check

Learn

0.4 and 0.40 are equal as numbers. The extra zero is not “free size.” On a scale, though, writing 0.40 can mean the reading was taken (or written) to the nearest hundredth, while 0.4 may mean only to the nearest tenth. That is a caution about records, not a new comparison rule, and not a course in significant figures.

Remember

Equal amounts stay equal. A written trailing zero can still mark a finer reading when the number is a measurement. We do not invent precision that was not measured.

Watch me

A stick is between 0.35 m and 0.44 m. If we write the length to the nearest tenth of a meter, what do we write? If we could write it to the nearest hundredth, would 0.40 and 0.4 still be the same amount?

  1. To the nearest tenth, anything from 0.35 m up to (but not all the way to) 0.45 m writes as 0.4 m. (If the length were 0.45 m, it would typically round to 0.5 m; we do not need a hard edge case today.)
  2. So a written 0.4 m, as a nearest-tenth reading, covers a range of actual lengths.
  3. If we instead knew the length to the nearest hundredth and it was 0.40 m, we would write 0.40 m. The amount 0.40 still equals 0.4; the writing now claims a finer reading. The stick, if invented, stays labeled invented.

Together

Why is “0.40 is more than 0.4” the wrong sentence, and what is a careful sentence instead?

Hint 1: Amount

They are the same amount, so “more than” is false.

Hint 2: A careful sentence

“0.40 and 0.4 are the same amount; a written 0.40 may still claim a reading to the nearest hundredth.”

Check

These check the amount-versus-reading distinction.

  1. Are 0.70 and 0.7 the same amount?
  2. Give one reason a written measurement 0.70 m might still be worth writing with the zero.

Practice

  1. If a scale only has tenths, can it honestly write 0.35? Say why.
  2. Does writing 3.14 for π claim that π is exactly 3.14, or is 3.14 still a labeled approximation?
  3. A friend wants to write Mars as 6,792.00 km “to be more precise.” What do we say?
  4. On the tenths number line, do 0.4 and 0.40 mark two different points?
  5. Finish: The amounts are equal, but the writing can still ____, so I will not say the extra zero makes the number larger.

Spanish · primero, segundo, and el último

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
primero pree-MEH-roh first
segundo seh-GOON-doh second
tercero tehr-SEH-roh third
el último el OOL-tee-moh the last
el orden el OR-den the order

Today’s words name a place in an order: first, second, third, and last. Instruction stays in English. Comparing decimals stays in the English mathematics; the Spanish is the everyday words for an order of things you can point to (three books, three pencils).

Original sentences: Es el primero. Es el segundo. Es el tercero. Es el último.

For the guide: line up three objects and point as you say first, second, last. Present tense only. No decimal comparison in the Spanish.

Remember

primero; segundo; tercero; el último; el orden. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No decimal comparison in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “It is the first.”

  1. Es el primero.
  2. Es is “it is.” El primero is the first.
  3. This is a present-tense pattern.

Together

How do you say “the last”?

Hint 1: The words

el último

Hint 2: A sentence

Es el último.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “It is the second.”
  2. Write: “It is the third.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *Es el primera, if someone used a feminine form about a masculine noun without a reason.
  2. Hear primero, segundo, and último, then use each.
  3. Why is primero not the same job as más largo (Day 17)?
  4. A pointing retell: first, second, third, last, with three objects.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · prīmus, secundus, tertius

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
prīmus PREE-mus first (masculine)
secundus seh-KUN-dus second (masculine)
tertius TEHR-tee-us third (masculine)
Prīmus est PREE-mus est He/it is first

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Prīmus est. Secundus est. Tertius est. Nōn prīmus est.

These are masculine forms, used of a masculine noun or as a short “he/it is first.” Decimal comparisons stay in the English mathematics. No degree numbers and no SI units in the Latin.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to a first, second, and third object. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

prīmus; secundus; tertius; Prīmus est. No decimals in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read: Secundus est. What does it say?

  1. secundus is “second.”
  2. est is “is.”
  3. The sentence says he or it is second.

Together

Say: “He/it is first.”

Hint 1: The adjective

prīmus

Hint 2: The sentence

Prīmus est.

Check

These use only today’s patterns.

  1. Write: “He/it is third.”
  2. Write: “He/it is not first.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *prīmus nōn est written with the nōn in an odd place if needed — the model is Nōn prīmus est, or Prīmus nōn est; either is accepted if nōn is clear.
  2. Read the four model sentences aloud.
  3. Draw three marks, labeled prīmus, secundus, tertius.
  4. Why are 0.8, 0.75, and 0.08 left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy one model sentence.

Notebook: a tenths line, an order, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw a number line from 0 to 1 in tenths, and mark 0.4, 0.75, and 0.08. Then complete because / but / so stems: 0.4 and 0.40 are the same amount because 4 tenths is 40 hundredths, but a written 0.40 can still mark a finer reading if it is a measurement, so I will not say the extra zero makes the number larger, and I will not invent zeros on a NASA size.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. Compare in the same place. No points or badges — the page is the work.

Watch me

A model sentence: 0.8 is greater than 0.75 because 80 hundredths is more than 75 hundredths, but 0.08 is much smaller, so I will not be fooled by the look of the 8.

  1. 80 hundredths against 75 hundredths against 8 hundredths.
  2. The but names the mix-up.
  3. The so chooses place value over “seeing an 8.”

Together

Finish: 0.4 and 0.40 mark the same point, because ________, but a written 0.40 may still ________, so ________.

Hint 1: Amount

…they are the same amount (4 tenths = 40 hundredths)…

Hint 2: The caution

…claim a reading to the nearest hundredth, so I will not say 0.40 is a larger amount, and I will not write 6,792.00 km as a new NASA measurement.

Check

The page should show the line, the marks, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the tenths line with 0.08, 0.4, and 0.75 marked in the right order.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) that keeps equal amounts equal and still allows a measurement caution.

Practice

  1. Add 0.40 as the same point as 0.4, labeled “same amount.”
  2. Tick the word-bank words you used: compare, hundredth, equivalent, amount, measurement, invented, order.
  3. The page is a record of today’s skill, not a badge.
  4. A note that a 0.40 m classroom reading, if you include one, is invented.
  5. File the page. It is not a reward.
