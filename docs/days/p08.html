Level 2 · Day 8 · Taking from 1,000, and a comparison bar

Today's big question: How do we find 1,000 − 264, and how much shorter is an invented rocket that is 18 m shorter than 63 m?

For the guide: Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: discs for place value, columns with carries, and the first part-whole and comparison bars. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. The Saturn V height used in this level is 111 meters (363 feet), from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?” Do not replace that figure with an invented classroom height, and do not average it with another official figure. Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes; measure a shadow, not the Sun. If the placement check started the student here, Level 1 greetings and numbers 1–10 may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later bars with larger numbers; today’s bars are a first look.

Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

To subtract 1,000 − 200, take 2 hundreds from 10 hundreds: 800. To find 1,000 − 264, you may count up from 264 to 1,000: 264 + 36 = 300, 300 + 700 = 1,000, so 36 + 700 = 736.

Remember

Subtracting hundreds changes the hundreds. Counting up from the smaller number to the whole is often clear when the whole is 1,000.

Watch me

What is 1,000 − 264, by counting up?

  1. 264 + 36 = 300, then 300 + 700 = 1,000.
  2. 36 + 700 = 736.

Together

What is 1,000 − 200?

Hint 1

1,000 is 10 hundreds.

Hint 2

10 hundreds − 2 hundreds = 8 hundreds: 800.

Check

Subtract hundreds, or count up to 1,000.

  1. 1,000 − 264
  2. 1,000 − 200
  3. 800 − 300

Practice

  1. 1,000 − 500
  2. 1,000 − 70
  3. 63 − 10

Core lesson: subtraction, counting up, and a comparison bar

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract

Learn

A comparison bar is a new picture in this level. Draw two bars lined up at the left. The extra piece on the longer bar is the difference.

Invented classroom rockets, labeled invented: Rocket A is 63 m tall. Rocket B is 18 m shorter. How tall is Rocket B? How tall are they together if stacked?

Rocket Asame as B? 18 m
Rocket A’s whole bar = 63 m (invented)
Rocket B45 m

Rocket B is 63 − 18 = 45 m. Together, 63 + 45 = 108 m. Both heights, the 18 m, and the 108 m, are invented classroom numbers, not NASA figures.

1,000 − 264. Count up: 264 + 36 = 300, 300 + 700 = 1,000, so 1,000 − 264 = 736. You may also trade in columns: you cannot take 4 ones from 0, so trade a thousand into hundreds, tens, and ones, then subtract, and check by adding 264 + 736 = 1,000.

The Saturn V’s 111 m stays the NASA student-page figure. Do not replace it with 63 m.

The Saturn V height used in this level is from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?”: the Saturn V rocket was 111 meters (363 feet) tall. https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-saturn-v/ Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA measurements. This level does not average 111 m with any other figure.

Remember

A comparison bar: two bars lined up at the left; the extra piece is the difference. Invented rockets: A 63 m, B 18 m shorter, so B is 45 m; together 108 m. 1,000 − 264 = 736, by counting up or by trading, checked by addition.

Watch me

Rocket A is 63 m, Rocket B is 18 m shorter (invented). How tall is B, and how tall are they stacked?

  1. B is 63 − 18 = 45 m.
  2. Together: 63 + 45 = 108 m. All of these are invented classroom numbers.

Together

Find 1,000 − 264 by counting up, and check with an addition.

Hint 1

264 + 36 = 300, 300 + 700 = 1,000.

Hint 2

736 + 264 = 1,000.

Check

These use a comparison bar or 1,000 − a number.

  1. Find 1,000 − 264.
  2. A 90 m invented rocket is 25 m taller than a second invented rocket. How tall is the second?

Practice

  1. Find 80 − 27 by counting up.
  2. Why is 108 m not a Saturn V height in this lesson?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

If B is 18 m shorter than A, then A is 18 m taller than B. The difference is the same piece of the comparison bar. Stacked height is a part-whole job, not a comparison job.

Remember

Comparison asks for the difference. Part-whole asks for a total. Use the bar that matches the question.

Watch me

Rocket A is 63 m, B is 45 m (invented). What is the difference, and what is the stacked whole?

  1. Difference: 18 m (comparison).
  2. Whole: 108 m (part-whole).

Together

Find 2,000 − 264. You may count up to 2,000, or use 1,000 − 264 first.

Hint 1

1,000 − 264 = 736, so 2,000 − 264 is 1,000 more: 1,736.

Hint 2

Or count up: 264 + 736 = 1,000, then + 1,000 = 2,000.

Check

These choose the bar, or extend 1,000 − 264.

  1. Find 1,000 − 408 by counting up.
  2. An invented shadow is 80 cm, another is 20 cm. How much longer is the first? Which bar is this?

Practice

  1. A student stacks 63 m and 18 m and says that is Rocket B’s height. What was mixed?
  2. Find 1,000 − 264 and check.

Math circle: which bar, and why a check addition?

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

A comparison bar and a part-whole bar answer different questions. “How much shorter?” is a difference. “How tall together?” is a whole. Every subtraction can be checked by an addition: the part you took plus the part that remains makes the whole.

Remember

Choose the bar that matches the question. Check a subtraction by adding.

Watch me

Why is “how much shorter is Rocket B?” not answered by 63 + 45?

  1. 63 + 45 is the stacked whole, a part-whole sum.
  2. The shorter question is the difference, 63 − 45 = 18, on a comparison bar.

Together

Why does 264 + 736 = 1,000 show that 1,000 − 264 = 736 is right?

Hint 1

264 and 736 are the two parts of 1,000.

Hint 2

If the parts make the whole, the subtraction of one part leaves the other.

Check

These choose a bar or defend a check.

  1. When would you draw a part-whole bar for the invented rockets?
  2. When would you draw a comparison bar?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 1,000 − 264 = 736, with a check.
  2. Why are 63 m and 18 m labeled invented next to the Saturn V’s 111 m?

Spanish · Más alto and más bajo, with two pictures

15 minutes · Method: compare two drawings. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today uses más alto, más bajo, más, and menos with two invented rocket drawings (labeled invented). No new number words. Do not give official heights in Spanish.

Instructions are in English. Hear clips use es-MX. Greetings and numbers 1–10 from Level 1 may be reused, not retaught as a first look. If a student began at Level 2, the guide may first use Level 1 Days 1–2 Spanish for hola and uno–diez, then continue here.

Remember

Más alto: taller. Más bajo: shorter / lower. Point first, then speak when ready. Heights in meters stay in the mathematics block, in English.

Watch me

The guide shows a taller invented rocket and a shorter one, and says ésta es más alta (or es más alto, as taught). What should the student do?

  1. Point at the taller drawing. (If the guide uses a slightly different ending, the job is still to choose the taller picture.)

Together

What is the English of más bajo?

Hint 1

Shorter, or lower.

Check

These reuse comparison words with two pictures.

  1. Give the English of más alto and menos.
  2. Hear bajo and más. Give the English of each.

Exercises

  1. Say the Spanish for “more” and “less.”
  2. Why are the rocket drawings labeled invented in this sitting too?

Latin · A short pair of captions for two rockets

15 minutes · Method: two original captions, not a height in meters.

Learn

Today uses alta and nōn alta (or brevis) with two rocket drawings, still original and short. Do not write 111 or 63 in Latin as if they were Roman official figures.

Original captions:

  • Alta est. — It is tall.
  • Alta nōn est. — It is not tall.

(Or: Umbra longa est / Umbra brevis est if the pictures are shadows.)

All Latin here is original. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Vowels are pure; c like k; v like English w.

Remember

Alta est and Alta nōn est are original captions for a taller and a not-tall picture. Numbers of meters stay in English.

Watch me

A shorter invented rocket is shown. Which caption matches, Alta est or Alta nōn est?

  1. Alta nōn est. (Or a taught alternative such as brevis if the guide has used it for this picture.)

Together

What is the English of Alta est?

Hint 1

It is tall.

Check

These check the two captions.

  1. Give the English of Alta nōn est.
  2. What does umbra mean, from earlier this level?

Exercises

  1. Write one original caption for a tall drawing.
  2. Why does this lesson not put 111 m into the Latin caption?

Notebook: a comparison bar for two invented rockets

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: a comparison bar for invented Rocket A 63 m and Rocket B 18 m shorter (B = 45 m), and a part-whole bar for the stacked 108 m. Label invented, difference, whole. Then write from stems.

Word bank: comparison, difference, part, whole, stacked, invented, shorter.

Stems: 1. Rocket B is 45 m because … 2. I could have added 63 and 18 to find B, but … 3. They are 108 m stacked, so …

End with 63 − 18 = 45 and 63 + 45 = 108, both labeled invented, and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw both bars first. The but-stem should refuse using the difference as if it were B’s height, or adding 63 and 18 to get B.

Watch me

Finish: “Rocket B is 45 m because …”

  1. A model: “… because it is 18 m shorter than 63 m, and 63 − 18 = 45.” Other complete finishes accepted.

Together

Finish the but-stem. What miss should it name?

Hint 1

18 m is the difference, not B’s height.

Hint 2

Adding 63 and 18 would make 81, which is not B.

Check

These check both bars in writing.

  1. Which bar shows the 18 m, and which shows the 108 m?
  2. What label must stay on 63, 45, and 108?

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question about a sourced height beside these invented ones.
