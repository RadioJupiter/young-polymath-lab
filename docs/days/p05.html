Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 1

10 minutes · a short mix of this level, then the check

Learn

This warm-up mixes bonds of 10, making ten, a subtraction fact, and adding tens. It is not a new lesson.

Remember

Bonds of 10; make ten; subtraction from the same bond; add or subtract tens and hundreds.

What is 8 + 7, and what is 46 + 30?

Level 1 check

45 minutes · alone, no calculator · a check of this level, not a new lesson

Learn

This is a check of skills already taught in Level 1, not a new lesson. Use discs, a number bond, or a ten-frame when a picture would help. The space fact you may need: NASA’s explanation that half of Earth is lighted and half is in darkness at all times, and that most places cycle through day and night once every 24 hours.

Remember

Places: ones, tens, hundreds. Make ten to add. Subtract with the same bond. Add or subtract tens and hundreds by changing those places. Classroom star counts are invented. Day and night: we face toward or away from the Sun as Earth spins.

Watch me

A model of how to show work, so the check can be written in the same shape. Find 8 + 7.

  1. 8 needs 2 to make 10; 7 = 2 + 5.
  2. 10 + 5 = 15. Write the split, not only the last answer.

Together

Find 13 − 6, and say the related addition.

Hint 1: The bond

6 + ☐ = 13.

Hint 2: The fact

13 − 6 = 7, because 6 + 7 = 13.

Check

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

  1. What is 46 + 30?
  2. What is 10 less than 300?

Unit check

Work alone. Use discs, a bond, or a ten-frame when it helps. For day and night, use the NASA facts taught on Day 4. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: numbers to 1,000, and counting

  1. Write 247 in hundreds, tens, and ones.
  2. What number is 6 hundreds, 0 tens, and 4 ones?
  3. What is 10 less than 300?
  4. An invented classroom sky picture (labeled invented) has 3 rows of 4 stars. How many stars? Why is this not an official count of the real sky?
  5. Why is 305 not the same number as 35?

Part B: addition, and making ten

  1. What is 6 + 4? What is 4 + 6? What do you call this?
  2. Find 8 + 7 by making ten. Show the split.
  3. Find 9 + 6 by making ten.
  4. Find 46 + 30.
  5. Find 28 + 5 by making ten with the ones.

Part C: subtraction, and day and night

  1. Find 13 − 6, and write the related addition.
  2. Find 15 − 8, and write the related addition.
  3. How many more is 16 than 9?
  4. In the NASA wording taught this level, why is there day and night? Use facing toward or away from the Sun, and Earth’s spin.
  5. How long is the cycle of day and night for most places, in the figure this level uses? Is half of Earth in darkness at all times?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 2. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

This wrap uses only Level 1 methods: places to 1,000, making ten, bonds, and tens or hundreds. No new operation is begun.

Remember

Same methods as the week. New puzzles, not new tools.

Watch me

A number has 4 hundreds, some tens, and 6 ones. Ten more than the number is 506. What was the number?

  1. Ten more than the number is 506, so the number is 10 less than 506, which is 496.
  2. Check: 4 hundreds, 9 tens, 6 ones. Ten more is 5 hundreds and 6 ones: 506.

Together

Why can 8 + 7 = 15 help you find 18 + 7, and what is 18 + 7?

Hint 1: The ones

The ones are still 8 + 7 = 15, a ten and 5 ones.

Hint 2: The tens

18 already has 1 ten; one more ten makes 2 tens and 5 ones: 25.

Check

A short wrap of Level 1, after the check.

  1. Find 990 + 10 and 1,000 − 10.
  2. An invented crater picture (labeled invented) shows 8 craters, then 7 more are drawn. How many then? Show making ten.

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 46 + 30 is not 46 + 3.
  2. Using NASA’s wording, why is “the Sun goes around Earth once a day” not the explanation this level uses?

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

A good Level 1 argument names the places or the bond, and it does not treat an invented classroom count as an official sky figure.

Remember

Places, bonds, making ten, and honest labels. That is this level’s idea of a reason.

Watch me

A student says, “I counted 12 stars in the real sky, so there are 12 stars.” What two jobs have been mixed?

  1. A classroom invented picture can be counted completely, with a plan, and may have 12 stars.
  2. The real sky is not that picture, and this level does not invent an official star count. A complete count needs a set you can plan for.

Together

Write a short argument that 13 − 6 = 7, using a bond, and say why a count-back that lands on 8 is not a second answer.

Hint 1

6 + 7 = 13, so the other part is 7.

Hint 2

Landing on 8 is a counting miss, not another bond of 13 with a part 6.

Check

A last short argument in this level’s tools.

  1. Why does 8 + 7 equal 7 + 8, in one or two sentences?
  2. Why must a 0 be written in 305?

Practice

  1. What is still true about day and night if a classroom globe-and-lamp model (invented, labeled) is a bit lopsided?
  2. Name one invented figure and one NASA fact from this level, and say how you tell them apart.

Spanish · Friday mix: greetings, numbers, and day or night

15 minutes · Mix of this level’s Spanish, not a new tense

Learn

This Friday mixes hola, adiós, levántate, siéntate, uno to diez, el sol, la luna, una estrella, mira, señala, es de día, and es de noche. No new phrase is begun. Instruction stays in English.

Instructions to the guide are in English. Only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish. Hear clips use es-MX. If this is the student’s first Spanish, begin here. Students who later start Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that is by design for those who skipped these foundation levels.

Remember

Greetings and two actions; uno to diez; el sol, la luna, una estrella; mira and señala; es de día and es de noche. Act first; speak when ready.

Watch me

The guide says levántate, then señala el sol. What two actions should the student do?

  1. Stand up, then point at the Sun drawing (not the real Sun).

Together

What is the English of es de noche, and of ocho?

Hint 1

Es de noche: it is night.

Hint 2

Ocho: eight.

Check

A short mix of this level’s Spanish.

  1. Give the English of hola, adiós, and diez.
  2. Say the Spanish for 5, for “the Moon,” and for “it is day.”

Exercises

  1. Hear siéntate, una estrella, and es de día. Give the English of each, or the action for siéntate.
  2. Why does this mix not retell the NASA day-and-night explanation in Spanish?

Latin · Friday mix: captions for a star, the Sun, and day or night

15 minutes · Mix of this level’s Latin, original captions only

Learn

This Friday mixes salvē, stella, sōl, lūna, est, nōn est, diēs, and nox. No new noun is begun. Captions stay short and original.

All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; c is always like k; v is like English w; vowels are pure (a as in father, e as in they, i as in machine, o as in note, u as in rude).

Remember

Salvē: hello, to one person. Stella, sōl, lūna; est and nōn est; diēs and nox. Short original captions only.

Watch me

A Moon drawing is shown. Give one true est caption and one true nōn est caption.

  1. Lūna est. Sōl nōn est. (Stella nōn est is also true if the picture is clearly the Moon, not a star.)

Together

What is the English of Diēs est and of Nox est?

Hint 1

Diēs est: it is day.

Hint 2

Nox est: it is night.

Check

A short mix of this level’s Latin.

  1. What does salvē mean, and to how many people, as taught?
  2. Write Stella est and Sōl nōn est in English.

Exercises

  1. Hear lūna and nox. Give the English of each, and one caption that uses nox.
  2. Why are the captions today still not a copied textbook story?

Notebook: a Level 1 page — a bond, a count, and day and night

60 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting · draw first, then label, then write

Learn

This is an explain-the-week page. Draw three small pictures: (1) a bond of 10 or the bond for 13 − 6, (2) an invented star row, labeled invented, with a planned count, (3) the Earth–Sun day-and-night model. Label each. Then write from stems.

Word bank: bond, ten, count, invented, Sun, Earth, day, night, spin.

Stems:

  1. 8 + 7 is 15 because …
  2. The star picture is invented, but …
  3. Earth spins, so …

End with one NASA fact in your own words (the 24-hour cycle, or half in light and half in darkness) and one “what next” question for Level 2.

Remember

Three drawings, then three stems, then one sourced fact and one question. Do not treat an invented count as official. Do not invent a new official day length.

Watch me

Finish: “8 + 7 is 15 because …”

  1. A model: “8 + 7 is 15 because I made ten (8 + 2 + 5).” Other complete finishes that show making ten or a bond are accepted.

Together

Finish: “The star picture is invented, but …”

Hint 1

You can still count it with a plan.

Hint 2

Do not call the total an official sky count.

Check

These check the week’s notebook ideas together.

  1. What NASA cycle length should appear, in your own words, at the end?
  2. Name one invented figure that must stay labeled invented on this page.

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing, after the three drawings are labeled.
  2. Write one “what next” question for Level 2.
