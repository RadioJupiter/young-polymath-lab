Mental math warm-up: tenths and hundredths as groups

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Today we start decimals by grouping, the same way ones and tens group. Ten tenths make one whole. Ten hundredths make one tenth. You already used a decimal as a labeled stand-in for π (3.14). Today we say what each digit stands for.

Remember

10 tenths = 1. 10 hundredths = 1 tenth. 100 hundredths = 1 whole. 0.1 is one tenth; 0.01 is one hundredth.

Watch me

How many tenths make 2 wholes?

  1. One whole is 10 tenths.
  2. Two wholes are 2 × 10 = 20 tenths.
  3. We can write that as 2, or as 2.0, which is 2 ones and 0 tenths.

Together

How many hundredths make 3 tenths?

Hint 1: One tenth

One tenth is 10 hundredths.

Hint 2: Three tenths

3 × 10 = 30 hundredths, which we can write as 0.30 or as 0.3.

Check

These use only today’s groupings.

  1. How many tenths is 0.7?
  2. Write 4 hundredths as a decimal.

Practice

  1. How many hundredths make one whole?
  2. Write 2 tenths and 5 hundredths as a decimal.

Core lesson: tenths, hundredths, and decimal place value

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (cut a strip into ten equal parts; a hundred-square), pictorial (the bar, the grid, and the place-value chart), then the digits in their places

Learn

A tenth is one of ten equal parts of a whole. We write one tenth as 0.1, or as the fraction 1/10. The pictured bar is one whole, split into ten equal parts; three parts are shaded, so the shaded length is 0.3, or 3/10.

A hundredth is one of a hundred equal parts of a whole. We write one hundredth as 0.01, or as 1/100. The pictured grid is one whole, split into a ten-by-ten square. Twenty-five small squares are shaded, so the shaded part is 0.25, which is 25/100, and that is the same amount as 1/4.

Place value. Each place to the right of the ones is ten times smaller than the place before it. After the ones comes a decimal point, then the tenths, then the hundredths. In 2.45, the 2 is 2 ones, the 4 is 4 tenths, and the 5 is 5 hundredths: 2 + 4/10 + 5/100. We can also say 2.45 is 245 hundredths, or 2 ones and 45 hundredths.

Reading the digits by their places. 0.4 is 4 tenths (not 4 hundredths). 0.04 is 4 hundredths. 0.40 is 4 tenths and 0 hundredths, which is the same amount as 0.4 — Day 27 will say more about that. For now, put each digit in its column; do not count “how many numbers after the point” as a substitute for place value.

A number you already use. The approximation π ≈ 3.14 is 3 ones, 1 tenth, and 4 hundredths. That is why 3.14 × 10 was 31.4 in Level 8: each digit moves one place to the left. 3.14 is still a labeled approximation of π, not π itself.

The next column. The place after hundredths is thousandths (0.001, or 1/1,000). We name it so the pattern is clear. Today’s checks stay with tenths and hundredths.

Classroom lengths stay labeled. A rod “1.25 m long” in a practice item is an invented classroom length, not a NASA figure.

Remember

0.1 = 1/10. 0.01 = 1/100. After the ones: decimal point, tenths, hundredths. Each place is ten times smaller than the place on its left. 0.4 is 4 tenths; 0.04 is 4 hundredths. Invented classroom lengths stay labeled.

Watch me

Write 0.07 and 0.70 in a place-value chart, and say what each one is.

  1. 0.07 has 0 ones, 0 tenths, and 7 hundredths — seven hundredths.
  2. 0.70 has 0 ones, 7 tenths, and 0 hundredths — seven tenths (the same amount as 0.7).
  3. 7 tenths is ten times 7 hundredths, so 0.70 is greater than 0.07.

Together

Write 1.03 in a place-value chart, and say it in ones, tenths, and hundredths.

Hint 1: The ones

There is 1 one.

Hint 2: The places after the point

0 tenths and 3 hundredths. So 1.03 is 1 one and 3 hundredths.

Check

These use only today’s place values.

  1. Which is greater, 0.4 or 0.04? Say what each digit stands for.
  2. Write 5 tenths and 2 hundredths as a decimal.

Practice

  1. Write 0.6 as a fraction of a whole, in tenths.
  2. In 3.14 (the labeled approximation of π), what does the 1 stand for, and what does the 4 stand for?
  3. Shade 0.08 on a hundred-square, or say how many small squares that is.
  4. An invented classroom rod is 2.05 m long. How many ones, tenths, and hundredths is that? Label the length invented.
  5. Why is “0.25 has two digits after the point, so it is bigger than 0.4” not a sound reason?

Challenge problems: mixed places, and a length that is not a planet

20 minutes · after the core, or later if the core took the full sitting

Learn

These items mix tenths and hundredths, and they keep invented classroom lengths labeled. A NASA diameter from an earlier level is a whole number of kilometers; we will not invent a decimal “more precise” NASA size.

Remember

Same places as the core. Invented lengths stay labeled. Do not invent a decimal NASA measurement.

Watch me

0.09 and 0.9: write each in a place-value chart, and say which is greater.

  1. 0.09 is 9 hundredths.
  2. 0.9 is 9 tenths, which is 90 hundredths.
  3. 0.9 is greater.

Together

An invented stick is 0.4 m long, and a second invented stick is 0.04 m long. Which is longer, and by how many hundredths of a meter?

Hint 1: Same units

0.4 m is 40 hundredths of a meter; 0.04 m is 4 hundredths of a meter.

Hint 2: The difference

40 − 4 = 36 hundredths of a meter, which is 0.36 m. Both lengths are invented.

Check

These stay with place value and labeled invented lengths.

  1. Write 13 tenths as a decimal greater than 1, and as ones and tenths.
  2. Is 0.25 the same amount as 1/4? Say why, in hundredths.

Practice

  1. Write 7 hundredths and 7 tenths, and compare them.
  2. A hundred-square has 40 small squares shaded. Write that as a decimal and as tenths.
  3. Why will this course not write “Mars is 6,792.35 km across” as a NASA fact from this lesson?
  4. 3.14 × 10 = 31.4. In 31.4, what does the 4 stand for now?
  5. An invented ribbon is 1.1 m. Is that 11 tenths of a meter, or 11 hundredths? Label it invented.

Math circle: why the next place is ten times smaller

20 minutes · a short reason, then a check

Learn

We write decimals the way we write whole numbers: each step to the right is a group ten times smaller. That is why ten tenths make a one, and ten hundredths make a tenth, just as ten ones make a ten. The argument is about grouping, not about a new kind of number.

Remember

Each place is ten times the place on its right. The decimal point marks the ones place; it is not a mark that “makes the number small” by itself.

Watch me

Why is 0.1 ten times 0.01?

  1. 0.01 is one hundredth.
  2. Ten hundredths make one tenth.
  3. So 10 × 0.01 = 0.1, the same grouping that makes 10 ones into 1 ten.

Together

If we named the next place after hundredths, why would ten of those make one hundredth?

Hint 1: The pattern

Each place is ten times smaller than the place on its left.

Hint 2: The name

Those are thousandths: 10 × 0.001 = 0.01. Today we only need the reason, not a long practice set in thousandths.

Check

These check the grouping reason.

  1. Complete: ten tenths make ____, and ten hundredths make ____.
  2. Why is the decimal point not a “times a half” mark?

Practice

  1. Give one reason 0.3 is 10 times 0.03.
  2. A friend says 0.25 is “a quarter because there are two digits.” What is the honest reason it is a quarter?
  3. Does 3.14 have a tenths digit because it is an approximation of π, or because of place value? (Both facts can be true — say which job each fact does.)
  4. Draw a quick place-value chart for 0.10 and say why it is one tenth.
  5. What would go wrong if we treated every new place as “just another digit after the point,” with no size?

Spanish · la regla, exacto, and casi

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la regla lah REH-glah the ruler
el cuaderno el kwah-DEHR-noh the notebook
exacto ek-SAHK-toh exact
casi KAH-see almost
mide MEE-deh it measures / he or she measures (you know this from Day 16)

Today’s words name a ruler, a notebook, and the difference between exacto (exact) and casi (almost). Instruction stays in English. ¿Cuánto mide? was taught on Day 16; we can reuse mide here. Decimal place value and the NASA figures stay in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: La regla mide. El cuaderno es exacto. Casi es exacto. Hay una regla.

For the guide: hold a ruler and a notebook as you say the words. This is present-tense description, not a measurement lesson in Spanish.

Remember

la regla; el cuaderno; exacto; casi; mide. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No decimal arithmetic in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “There is a ruler.”

  1. Hay una regla.
  2. Hay introduces it. Una regla is the ruler.
  3. This is a present-tense pattern, not a past narrative.

Together

How do you say “almost”?

Hint 1: The word

casi

Hint 2: A sentence

For example: Casi es exacto.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “The notebook is exact.”
  2. Write: “The ruler measures.” (using mide)

Exercises

  1. Correct: *El regla mide, if someone used the wrong article.
  2. Hear regla, cuaderno, and casi, then use each.
  3. Why is exacto not the same job as casi?
  4. A pointing retell: the ruler, the notebook, almost, exact.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · numerus and punctum

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
numerus NOO-meh-rus a number (nominative, masculine)
Numerus parvus est NOO-meh-rus PAR-wus est The number is small
punctum PUNK-tum a point, a dot (nominative, neuter)
Punctum parvum est PUNK-tum PAR-wum est The point is small

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Numerus parvus est. Punctum parvum est. Numerus nōn parvus est. Punctum nōn parvum est.

numerus is masculine, so parvus matches. punctum is neuter, so parvum matches. The decimal point and the NASA figures stay in the English mathematics; no degree numbers and no SI units in the Latin.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to a written number and to a dot. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

numerus; Numerus parvus est; punctum; Punctum parvum est. Adjectives match (parvus / parvum). No decimals in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read: Punctum parvum est. Why parvum, not *parvus?

  1. punctum is neuter.
  2. The adjective matches: parvum.
  3. The sentence says the point is small, not that a number is small.

Together

Say: “The number is small.”

Hint 1: The noun

numerus, masculine.

Hint 2: The sentence

Numerus parvus est.

Check

These use only today’s patterns.

  1. Write: “The number is not small.”
  2. Write: “The point is not small.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *numerus parvum est and *punctum parvus est.
  2. Read the four model sentences aloud.
  3. Draw a small number and a small dot, labeled in Latin.
  4. Why are 0.1 and 0.01 left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy one model sentence.

Notebook: a tenths strip, a hundred-square, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw a strip of one whole split into ten equal tenths, and shade 0.3. Draw a ten-by-ten hundred-square and shade 0.25. Add a place-value chart for 2.45. Then complete because / but / so stems: 0.4 is 4 tenths because the 4 sits in the tenths place, but 0.04 is 4 hundredths, so I will not treat “a 4 after the point” as if every 4 were the same size.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. 0.1 is a tenth; 0.01 is a hundredth. No points or badges — the page is the work.

Watch me

A model sentence: 0.25 is a quarter because it is 25 hundredths, but “two digits after the point” is not the reason, so I will name the places.

  1. 0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4.
  2. The but refuses a digit-count trick.
  3. The so names tenths and hundredths.

Together

Finish: 0.70 is 7 tenths, because ________, but 0.07 is 7 hundredths, so ________.

Hint 1: The places

…the 7 sits in the tenths place, with 0 hundredths…

Hint 2: The comparison

…0.70 is greater than 0.07, and I will not say they are the same because both have a 7.

Check

The page should show the drawings, the labels, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the tenths strip (0.3 shaded), the hundred-square (0.25 shaded), and a place-value chart for 2.45.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) that keeps 0.4 apart from 0.04.

Practice

  1. Add 0.07 and 0.70 to a place-value chart.
  2. Tick the word-bank words you used: tenth, hundredth, decimal point, place, whole, invented, amount.
  3. The page is a record of today’s skill, not a badge.
  4. A note that a 1.25 m classroom rod, if you draw one, is invented.
  5. File the page. It is not a reward.
