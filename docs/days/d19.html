Mental math warm-up: boxes of cubes

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Volume is how much space a solid takes, in cubic units. A box (a rectangular prism, or cuboid) that is 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm holds 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 unit cubes, so 24 cm³. Picture 2 layers of a 4-by-3 array.

Remember

Volume of a cuboid: L × W × H, in cm³ or m³. This is not area and not perimeter.

Watch me

A 5 cm by 2 cm by 2 cm box: what is its volume?

  1. A layer is 5 × 2 = 10 unit squares, as an area, but we are stacking cubes.
  2. 2 layers: 10 × 2 = 20 unit cubes.
  3. Volume = 20 cm³. Or 5 × 2 × 2 = 20 cm³.

Together

A 3 m by 3 m by 3 m cube: volume?

Hint 1: Three factors

3 × 3 × 3.

Hint 2: Units

27 m³.

Check

Give the volume with a cubic unit.

  1. A 6 cm by 1 cm by 1 cm box: volume.
  2. A 2 m by 2 m by 5 m box: volume.

Practice

  1. 4 × 3 × 1, in cm³ if the sides are in cm.
  2. Why is 24 cm² the wrong unit for a 4 by 3 by 2 box of volume 24?
  3. Two layers of 12 cubes: volume.
  4. An invented 6 m by 3 m by 1 m loft: volume, labeled invented.
  5. Which job is 18 m², on a 6 m by 3 m floor, and which job will 2.5 m of height add tomorrow (or today, if you already know the 45 m³ module)?

Core lesson: volume of a box, and the liter

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (cubes or a picture of layers), then L × W × H · BIPM: the liter is a special name for the cubic decimeter

Learn

Volume of a cuboid. V = L × W × H, in cubic units (cm³, m³). A 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm box is 24 cm³.

The liter. The liter (US spelling; the BIPM also uses litre) is a unit of volume accepted for use with the SI. 1 liter (L) = 1 cubic decimeter = 1,000 cm³. Also, 1 m³ = 1,000 L. Those are official relationships, not classroom inventions. (BIPM SI Brochure, 9th ed.; the liter is not itself an SI base unit, but it is accepted for use with the SI.)

An invented module (labeled invented, not a NASA specification). A habitat box 6 m long, 3 m wide, and 2.5 m high has volume 6 × 3 × 2.5 = 45 m³. If that box were completely filled with water, that would be 45,000 L, because 1 m³ = 1,000 L. That is a volume conversion, not a claim about how much water a crew needs, and not a life-support budget. We do not invent a liters-per-sol figure.

A smaller invented tank: 240 L of water in a tank (classroom number, labeled invented) is 240,000 cm³, or 0.24 m³.

A sourced atmosphere fact, not a volume of “air needed.” The NASA Mars fact sheet gives a mean surface pressure of 6.36 mb (at mean radius; it varies by season, about 4.0 to 8.7 mb). Compared with the standard atmosphere at sea level, 1,013.25 mb (101,325 Pa, the conventional standard value), that is less than 1/100 of Earth’s. (The NASA Earth fact sheet lists a surface pressure of 1,014 mb, a nearby rounded mean; the fraction is still less than 1/100.) That is why a habitat is a closed box in our design problem — but we still will not invent how many liters of air a person uses.

Remember

V = L × W × H, in cm³ or m³. 1 L = 1,000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1,000 L (BIPM). The 6 m by 3 m by 2.5 m module (45 m³) and the 240 L tank are invented classroom numbers. Mars’s mean surface pressure is 6.36 mb (NASA) against the standard atmosphere 1,013.25 mb, less than 1/100. (NASA’s Earth sheet lists 1,014 mb; the fraction is the same.) No invented life-support rates.

Watch me

Find the volume of the invented 6 m by 3 m by 2.5 m module, and write it also in liters as a filled-box conversion only.

  1. 6 × 3 = 18, times 2.5 = 45, so 45 m³.
  2. 1 m³ = 1,000 L, so 45 m³ = 45,000 L if the box were filled.
  3. That 45,000 L is a conversion, not a water ration.

Together

How many cubic centimeters is 3 L?

Hint 1: The official relation

1 L = 1,000 cm³.

Hint 2: Three liters

3,000 cm³.

Check

These use V = L × W × H or a liter conversion, and they keep invented numbers labeled.

  1. A 10 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm box: volume in cm³ and in liters.
  2. An invented tank holds 240 L. Write that volume in m³. (1 m³ = 1,000 L.)

Practice

  1. A 2 m by 2 m by 2 m cube: volume in m³ and in liters.
  2. Why does this lesson refuse a “liters of water per sol” fact?
  3. Mars’s mean surface pressure is 6.36 mb (NASA). The standard atmosphere at sea level is 1,013.25 mb. About what fraction is that (less than 1/100 is enough), and what is that fact not?
  4. A box is 1 m by 50 cm by 20 cm. Use one unit, then find the volume in liters. (50 cm = 0.5 m, 20 cm = 0.2 m, or 100 cm by 50 cm by 20 cm.)
  5. Write a two-sentence caption for the 45 m³ module, with the word invented.

Challenge problems: a tank in a module, and mixed units

20–25 minutes · invented tanks and modules, clearly labeled; official liter relations

Learn

In the invented module of 45 m³ (45,000 L if filled), a 240 L tank is 0.24 m³. A bookkeeping subtraction, not a mission plan: 45,000 L − 240 L = 44,760 L, or 45 − 0.24 = 44.76 m³ left in that comparison. Keep both numbers labeled invented.

Remember

Use one unit at a time. Invented tanks stay labeled. 1 L = 1,000 cm³; 1 m³ = 1,000 L.

Watch me

The invented module is 45,000 L if filled. An invented tank is 240 L. How much of that filled-box figure is left after you subtract the tank (as a bookkeeping comparison, not as a claim that the module is a water bottle)?

  1. 45,000 − 240 = 44,760 L.
  2. Or 45 − 0.24 = 44.76 m³.
  3. Both are classroom bookkeeping, not a mission water plan.

Together

A box is 80 cm by 25 cm by 10 cm. Find the volume in cm³ and in liters.

Hint 1: Product

80 × 25 = 2,000, times 10 = 20,000 cm³.

Hint 2: Liters

20,000 ÷ 1,000 = 20 L.

Check

These convert, or subtract, with official liter relations.

  1. A 5 m by 2 m by 0.4 m invented cistern: volume in m³ and in liters.
  2. A cuboid has volume 60 cm³, length 5 cm, and width 4 cm. Find the height.

Practice

  1. Why is “the crew needs 45,000 L of water” a misuse of today’s 45 m³?
  2. 1,500 cm³ in liters, and 2.5 L in cm³.
  3. A 1 m by 1 m by 1 m cube next to a 100 cm by 100 cm by 100 cm cube: same volume or not?
  4. Write a two-sentence warning to put at the top of a design page: what is sourced, and what is invented.
  5. A layer of 24 cubes, 3 layers: volume, in cm³ if each is a centimeter cube.

Math circle: a liter is a cube, not a guess

20 minutes · a short argument from the official relation

Learn

1 L = 1,000 cm³ means a liter is the volume of a 10 cm by 10 cm by 10 cm cube (because 10×10×10=1,000). That is why a “liter carton” and a “thousand centimeter cubes” can name the same volume. The relation is official (BIPM), not a guess from a kitchen bottle’s shape.

Remember

1 L = 1,000 cm³ = a 10 cm cube. 1 m³ = 1,000 L. The shape of a bottle can change; the official relation does not.

Watch me

Show that a 10 cm cube has volume 1 L.

  1. 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000 cm³.
  2. 1 L = 1,000 cm³, official.
  3. So the 10 cm cube has volume 1 L, whatever we later pour it into.

Together

Why is “a liter is whatever a bottle holds” not a good definition in this lesson?

Hint 1: Bottles vary

Bottles come in many sizes.

Hint 2: Official

The lesson uses 1 L = 1,000 cm³, not a particular kitchen bottle.

Check

These tie the liter to a cube.

  1. What cube, in centimeters, has volume 8 L? (Think 8,000 cm³.)
  2. A 5 cm cube: volume in cm³ and as a fraction of a liter.

Practice

  1. How many 10 cm cubes fill a 1 m cube? (1 m = 10 of those 10 cm edges.)
  2. Write one sentence a guide could post: liter, official, cube.
  3. Why did we not use a Mars “air liter” number today?

Spanish · hay agua, and we need water (present)

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only; invented amounts in English only if used

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
el agua el AH-gwah water (use el agua; las aguas is not needed today)
el aire el EY-reh the air
hay agua eye AH-gwah there is water
no hay agua noh eye AH-gwah there is no water
necesitamos neh-seh-see-TAH-mohs we need
Necesitamos agua neh-seh-see-TAH-mohs AH-gwah We need water

The pattern. Hay agua. No hay aire. Necesitamos agua. If you mention 240 liters, say the number in English as an invented classroom amount, or skip the number; do not invent a Spanish life-support claim.

¿Hay agua? Sí, hay agua. Necesitamos aire también.

Remember

hay agua / no hay agua; necesitamos agua / aire. Instruction in English. No invented Spanish science figures.

Watch me

Say: “There is water.” Then: “We need air.”

  1. Hay agua.
  2. Necesitamos aire.
  3. Both are present-tense patterns; hay does not change for amount in this use.

Together

Hint 1: no hay

No hay agua.

Hint 2: Full sentence

No hay agua.

Check

  1. Write a question with ¿Hay…? and a short answer.
  2. Write one sentence with necesitamos.

Exercises

Latin · aqua in cistā, and est / nōn est

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
aqua AH-kwah water
in cistā in KIS-tah in the box (ablative: place where)
Aqua in cistā est AH-kwah in KIS-tah est Water is in the box
Aqua nōn est in cistā AH-kwah nohn est in KIS-tah Water is not in the box

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

The pattern. Aqua in cistā est. Aqua nōn est in cistā. The box, as a place where, takes in + ablative: in cistā (you have already met in hortō in spirit if you have seen in caelō: place where).

Original sentences: Aqua in cistā est. Cista est alta. Aqua nōn est in murō. We still do not write liters in the Latin.

Remember

Aqua in cistā est. in + ablative for place where (in cistā). nōn est for ‘is not.’ No liters in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Say: “Water is in the box.” Then: “Water is not in the box.”

  1. Aqua in cistā est.
  2. Aqua nōn est in cistā.
  3. The same place, two opposite claims.

Together

Why is it in cistā, not *in cista, in the pattern taught today?

Hint 1: Place where

in + ablative.

Hint 2: The form

cistā is the ablative of cista, in the form this lesson uses.

Check

Use in cistā and nōn est carefully.

  1. Write: “The box is tall, and water is in the box.”
  2. Correct: *Aqua in cista est if the lesson wants the ablative *cistā.

Exercises

  1. Write a nōn-sentence about water and a wall or a box.
  2. Read the sentences aloud twice.
  3. Draw a box with water, labeled Aqua in cistā est.
  4. Write one new sentence with alta or parva and cista.
  5. Why are liters left in the English mathematics?

Notebook: a module box, a tank, and a careful because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw the invented 6 m by 3 m by 2.5 m module (45 m³, 45,000 L if filled — a conversion, not a ration) and a 240 L tank (0.24 m³), both labeled invented. Write the official relations 1 L = 1,000 cm³ and 1 m³ = 1,000 L. Note, in a short line, Mars’s mean surface pressure, 6.36 mb (NASA), less than 1/100 of the standard atmosphere 1,013.25 mb. Then because / but / so stems that keep conversions apart from life-support claims.

Word bank: volume, cuboid, liter, cubic meter, invented, BIPM, filled-box, pressure, not a ration.

Remember

Draw and label first. Official relations are sourced; the box and tank are invented. Complete stems. The page is the work, not a score.

Watch me

A model sentence: 45,000 L is the volume of the module if it were filled, but that is not a water ration, because we invented the box and we did not source a crew use.

  1. 45 m³ = 45,000 L, official conversion.
  2. The 6×3×2.5 m box is invented.
  3. The but blocks a misuse of the number.

Together

Finish: 1 L = 1,000 cm³, so a 10 cm cube ________, but a kitchen bottle’s shape ________.

Hint 1: The cube

…has volume 1 L.

Hint 2: The bottle

…is not the definition; the official relation is.

Check

The page should show the box, the tank, official relations, and a pressure note, plus complete stems.

  1. Draw and label the module and the tank, with m³ and L, and write the two official liter relations.
  2. Complete a so-sentence about Mars’s surface pressure that does not invent an air-use number.

Practice

  1. Tick the word bank.
  2. Add a because-sentence about why the tank subtraction (44,760 L) is only bookkeeping.
  3. The page is the work; no points or badges.
