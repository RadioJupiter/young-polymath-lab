Mental math warm-up: distance around a rectangle

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

The perimeter of a rectangle is the distance around it: two lengths and two widths, or 2 × (length + width). A 5 cm by 3 cm rectangle has perimeter 2 × (5 + 3) = 16 cm. You can also add 5 + 3 + 5 + 3.

Remember

Perimeter of a rectangle: 2 × (L + W). Add the sides in order, and do not count a side twice by mistake, or skip a side.

Watch me

A rectangle is 6 cm long and 2 cm wide. What is its perimeter?

  1. L + W = 6 + 2 = 8 cm.
  2. Two of those: 2 × 8 = 16 cm.
  3. Or 6 + 2 + 6 + 2 = 16 cm.

Together

A square has side 4 cm. What is its perimeter?

Hint 1: Four equal sides

A square is a rectangle with all sides equal.

Hint 2: Arithmetic

4 × 4 = 16 cm, or 2 × (4 + 4) = 16 cm.

Check

Say the perimeter, then the adding.

  1. A 10 cm by 1 cm rectangle: perimeter.
  2. A 7 cm by 7 cm square: perimeter.

Practice

  1. 5 cm by 5 cm square.
  2. 3 m by 2 m rectangle (keep the unit).
  3. Why is 5 × 3 the area of a 5 cm by 3 cm rectangle, not the perimeter?
  4. A rectangle’s sides are 8 cm and 3 cm. Walk around and add.
  5. Double 9 cm (that is L + W for some rectangle). What is the perimeter?

Core lesson: perimeter of rectangles and of an L-shaped figure

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (a walk around a cut-out), pictorial (a labeled outline), then 2 × (L + W) or a side-by-side sum

Learn

Perimeter is the distance around. For a rectangle, P = 2 × (L + W). For a rectilinear L-shape, walk every outer edge; do not add the inner edges that were cut away, and do not skip a step.

The L-shape in the picture. The base is 8 cm, the left height is 5 cm, the right height is 3 cm, and the step is 4 cm across. Walking around: 8 + 3 + 4 + 2 + 4 + 5 = 26 cm. (The 2 cm is the little vertical step from height 3 cm up to height 5 cm.) A useful check: the L-shape can be thought of as a large 8 cm by 5 cm rectangle with a 4 cm by 2 cm rectangle cut from a corner — but for perimeter, cutting a corner out adds the two new cut edges and removes the two old outer edges in a way you must redraw; it is safer today to walk the outline than to invent a shortcut.

A course design problem (invented, labeled). A habitat module’s floor is a 6 m by 3 m rectangle. The distance around that floor (a tape along the wall line) is 2 × (6 + 3) = 18 m. Those 6 m and 3 m are classroom numbers, not a NASA specification.

Remember

Perimeter = distance around. Rectangle: 2 × (L + W). L-shape: walk every outer edge (the pictured one is 26 cm). Invented habitat measures must be labeled invented. Do not count inner edges as if they were outer.

Watch me

Find the perimeter of a 6 m by 3 m rectangle. Then say, in a word, why 6 × 3 would be the wrong tool for this question.

  1. L + W = 9 m.
  2. P = 2 × 9 = 18 m.
  3. 6 × 3 would be the area (tomorrow), the covering, not the walk around.

Together

Using the L-shape in the Learn picture, point to the 2 cm edge and say why it is part of the perimeter.

Hint 1: The step

The left part is 5 cm tall and the right part is 3 cm tall.

Hint 2: The difference

5 − 3 = 2, and that 2 cm step is an outer edge you walk.

Check

These are walks around, not coverings.

  1. A 9 cm by 4 cm rectangle: find the perimeter.
  2. A 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle has a 3 cm by 3 cm square cut from the top-right corner. Sketch, walk the outline, and find the perimeter.

Practice

  1. A square of side 12 m: perimeter. (Invented habitat courtyard, labeled invented.)
  2. Why is “add all the numbers you see on the diagram” sometimes too much, for an L-shape?
  3. A 7 cm by 2 cm rectangle and a 7 cm by 2 cm rectangle joined along a 2 cm side to make one long rectangle: what is the new perimeter, and what happened to the joined sides?
  4. Walk around a 1 m by 80 cm rectangle, in centimeters.
  5. Draw any L-shape, label every outer edge, and add.

Challenge problems: a fence around a module, and missing sides

20–25 minutes · invented habitat numbers, clearly labeled

Learn

In this course’s design problem (invented, not a NASA specification), a rectangular module is 8 m by 4 m. A tape around the outer wall line is the perimeter, 24 m. If a 2 m doorway sits on one long side, the wall length you actually build is 24 − 2 = 22 m, but the perimeter of the floor is still 24 m. Those are different questions; name which one you are answering.

Remember

Name the path you are measuring: the full outline, or the outline minus a door. Invented module sizes must stay labeled invented.

Watch me

An invented 8 m by 4 m module has a 2 m door in one long side. What is the floor perimeter, and what is the length of wall (no door) to build?

  1. Floor perimeter: 2 × (8 + 4) = 24 m.
  2. Wall to build, if the door is a 2 m gap: 24 − 2 = 22 m.
  3. The 24 m and the 22 m answer different questions.

Together

An L-shaped invented pad has outer edges 6 m, 5 m, 3 m, 2 m, 3 m, and 3 m, walked in order around. Find the perimeter.

Hint 1: Add the walk

6+5+3+2+3+3.

Hint 2: Sum

22 m.

Check

These name the path, and they label invented numbers.

  1. A rectangle has perimeter 20 m and length 6 m. Find the width.
  2. An invented 5 m by 5 m square pad with a 1 m by 1 m square bite from one corner: is the perimeter 20 m, or more, or less? Explain with a sketch.

Practice

  1. Why might “how much fencing?” need an extra fact besides the perimeter of the floor?
  2. A 12 cm by 5 cm rectangle: perimeter in centimeters and in meters.
  3. If every outer edge of an L is labeled except one, and the walk must close, how do you find the missing edge? (Use a horizontal or vertical sum.)
  4. Write a two-sentence caption for the 8 m by 4 m module, including the word invented.
  5. A square and a 9 cm by 3 cm rectangle have the same perimeter. Find the square’s side.

Math circle: does a bite change the perimeter?

20 minutes · a short argument from a sketch

Learn

When you cut a rectangle from the corner of a larger rectangle, the two outer edges you remove have the same lengths as the two new edges you create, so the perimeter stays the same. When you cut a rectangle from the middle of a side (a bay, not a corner), you typically add more edge than you remove, and the perimeter grows. The argument is the sketch, not a memorized slogan for every cut.

Remember

A rectangular bite at a corner: perimeter unchanged. A bay cut in the middle of a side: usually a longer walk. Sketch first, then add.

Watch me

Show, with a 10 by 6 rectangle and a 3 by 3 corner bite, why the perimeter stays 32 cm.

  1. The original rectangle has P = 2 × (10+6) = 32 cm.
  2. You remove 3 cm of the top and 3 cm of the right side, and you add two new 3 cm edges along the cut.
  3. 3 + 3 removed, 3 + 3 added, so P is still 32 cm.

Together

If instead you cut a 3 cm by 3 cm square into the middle of the 10 cm top side (a bay), does the perimeter stay 32 cm? Sketch and explain.

Hint 1: What you remove

You remove 3 cm of the top side.

Hint 2: What you add

You add two 3 cm sides and a 3 cm back of the bay: 9 cm added, 3 cm removed, so P grows by 6 cm to 38 cm.

Check

These need a sketch and a remove-and-add argument.

  1. State the corner-bite rule in one sentence, and one case it does not cover.
  2. A 8 by 5 rectangle, 2 by 2 corner bite: perimeter, with a reason.

Practice

  1. Why is “cutting always makes the perimeter smaller” false here?
  2. Invent a bay cut (labeled invented) on a 6 m by 3 m module and find the new walk.
  3. Write a two-sentence argument a classmate could check without you there.

Spanish · más largo and más corto

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
más largo mahs LAR-goh longer
más corta mahs KOR-tah shorter (feminine)
más corto mahs KOR-toh shorter (masculine)
que keh than (in this comparison)
La mesa es más larga que el lápiz lah MEH-sah es mahs LAR-gah keh el LAH-pees The table is longer than the pencil

The pattern. más + adjective + que. La mesa es más larga que el lápiz. El lápiz es más corto que la mesa. The adjective agrees: largo / larga, corto / corta.

You already compared in other ways in earlier units if at all; today the job is length. Instruction stays in English.

Remember

más largo / más corta que… The adjective agrees. Instruction in English.

Watch me

Say: “The table is longer than the pencil.”

  1. La mesa es más larga que el lápiz.
  2. mesa is feminine, so larga.
  3. que means ‘than’ in this pattern.

Together

Say: “The pencil is shorter than the table.”

Hint 1: corto

lápiz is masculine: más corto.

Hint 2: Sentence

El lápiz es más corto que la mesa.

Check

Agree the adjective, and use que.

  1. Write one sentence with más larga que.
  2. Write one sentence with más corto que.

Exercises

  1. Why is *el lápiz es más corta que la mesa wrong?
  2. Hear más largo and más corta, then use each.
  3. Ask and answer: ¿Qué es más largo, la mesa o el lápiz?
  4. A three-line comparison of two classroom objects, in the present.
  5. Copy the pattern más + adjective + que in your notebook.

Latin · brevis, and a first comparison with nōn

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
brevis BREH-wis short (masculine or feminine nominative singular, in the form taught today)
nōn longus nohn LONG-us not long
sed sed but
et et and

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Today’s contrast. Murus longus est. Linea brevis est. Via est longa, sed nōn lāta.

Original sentences only. We still will not force modern meters into the Latin. Adjectives match: murus longus, via longa, linea brevis, via lāta.

Remember

brevis: short. sed: but. The adjective still matches. No SI units in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read and make sense of: Via est longa, sed nōn lāta.

  1. via is feminine, so longa and lāta.
  2. sed joins a contrast: long, but not wide.
  3. The sentence means ‘The road is long, but not wide.’

Together

Say: “The wall is long, but the line is short.”

Hint 1: Two clauses

Murus longus est, sed linea brevis est.

Hint 2: Match

murus longus; linea brevis.

Check

Match, and keep a contrast with sed or nōn.

  1. Write: “The line is not long.”
  2. Write a sed-sentence about a wall and a road.

Exercises

  1. Correct: *linea longus est.
  2. Read today’s sentences aloud twice.
  3. Draw a short road and a long wall, labeled in Latin.
  4. Write one new sentence with et.
  5. Why is a modern ‘decem metra’ not required in this lesson?

Notebook: a walk around a pad, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw a 6 m by 3 m rectangle (invented habitat floor, labeled invented) and an L-shape like today’s picture, with every outer edge labeled. Walk each outline in writing. Then a because / but / so set: perimeter is the walk around, but a doorway might change a fencing question, so you name the path you mean.

Word bank: perimeter, rectangle, L-shape, outer edge, corner bite, invented, doorway, 2 × (L + W).

Remember

Draw and label first. Invented sizes stay labeled. Complete stems. The page is the work, not a score.

Watch me

A model sentence: The 6 m by 3 m floor has perimeter 18 m because I walk two sixes and two threes, but 6 × 3 would be the covering, not the walk.

  1. 2 × (6+3) = 18 m.
  2. 6 × 3 is a different job (area).
  3. The but keeps the two jobs apart.

Together

Finish: A rectangular bite at a corner does not change the perimeter, because ________, so a 10 by 6 rectangle with a 3 by 3 corner bite still has perimeter ________.

Hint 1: Remove and add

The two new edges match the two removed edges.

Hint 2: The number

32 cm (or 32, with the unit you used).

Check

The page should show two labeled walks and complete stems.

  1. Draw and label the rectangle and the L-shape, with each perimeter written.
  2. Complete a but-sentence about a door in a wall versus the floor perimeter.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used.
  2. Add one so-sentence about naming the path.
  3. The page is the work; no points or badges.
