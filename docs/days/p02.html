Level 1 · Day 2 · Making ten

Today's big question: How can a bond of 10 help you add 8 + 7 without guessing?

For the guide: Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete step in the core lesson, even if the student already seems quick: discs, a ten-frame, or a bond are the foundation. Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

If you know a bond of 10, you can use it to add. 8 + 2 = 10. So 8 + 5 is 8 + 2 + 3 = 10 + 3 = 13. This is called making ten.

Remember

To add a number to 8 or 9, make 10 first, then add what is left.

Watch me

What is 8 + 5, by making ten?

  1. 8 + 2 = 10, and 5 is 2 + 3.
  2. 10 + 3 = 13.

Together

What is 9 + 4, by making ten?

Hint 1: Nine

9 + 1 = 10.

Hint 2: The rest

4 is 1 + 3, so 10 + 3 = 13.

Check

Make ten, then add the rest.

  1. 8 + 6
  2. 9 + 5
  3. 7 + 4

Practice

  1. 8 + 3
  2. 9 + 6
  3. 7 + 5

Core lesson: making ten, and addition facts

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract

Learn

The addition facts for this level are the sums you should come to know well, especially facts that make 10 and facts through 10 + 10. You do not only memorize them. You make ten, and you use a number bond.

10
82

8 and 2 make 10. If the second number is larger than the part you need, split it. For 8 + 7:

  • 8 needs 2 more to make 10.
  • 7 is 2 + 5.
  • 8 + 7 = 10 + 5 = 15.

You can show this with ten-frames or with counters: fill a ten, then see the extras. That is the concrete step. The bond is the picture. 8 + 7 = 15 is the abstract sentence.

An invented classroom group of 8 shining stars and 7 more stars (labeled invented) is just a story for 8 + 7. It is not an official sky count.

Remember

Making ten: split the second number so that the first number reaches 10, then add what is left. 8 + 7 = 8 + 2 + 5 = 15. A bond of 10 is the picture for the first step. 8 + 7 and 7 + 8 are the same total.

Watch me

Show 8 + 7 by making ten, and write the addition sentence.

  1. 8 needs 2 to make 10; 7 = 2 + 5.
  2. 10 + 5 = 15. So 8 + 7 = 15.

Together

What is 7 + 8? Do you need a new calculation, and why or why not?

Hint 1: Order

7 + 8 is 8 + 7 with the parts swapped.

Hint 2: The total

The total is still 15.

Check

These use making ten, or a known bond.

  1. Find 9 + 6 by making ten.
  2. Find 6 + 9. What stays the same?

Practice

  1. Find 8 + 7 and 5 + 8, each by making ten.
  2. A student writes 8 + 7 = 8 + 7, and stops. What step is missing?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

If 8 + 7 = 15, then 18 + 7 is one ten more: 25. The ones are still 8 + 7, which make a ten and 5 ones. This uses making ten, not a new fact family for 18.

Remember

When you add to a number like 18, you can still make a ten with the ones, then add any new ten you made.

Watch me

What is 18 + 7, using 8 + 7 = 15?

  1. The ones are 8 + 7 = 15, which is 1 ten and 5 ones.
  2. 1 ten already in 18, plus 1 new ten, plus 5 ones: 25.

Together

What is 19 + 6? Make ten with the ones.

Hint 1: Nine

9 + 1 = 10, and 6 = 1 + 5.

Hint 2: The tens

19 already has 1 ten; the new ten from the ones makes 2 tens, and 5 ones: 25.

Check

These extend making ten into the teens.

  1. Find 28 + 5 by making ten with the ones.
  2. Why is 8 + 7 not equal to 87?

Practice

  1. Find 17 + 8 two ways (start from 17, or start from 8) and show that both give the same total.
  2. An invented desk has 9 pencils and 7 more are added. How many then? Label the numbers invented.

Math circle: all the bonds of 10

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

How many different bonds of 10 are there, if we count 1 + 9 and 9 + 1 as the same pair of parts (just swapped), and we include 0 + 10 and 5 + 5?

Remember

A complete list needs a plan, like yesterday’s counting plan. List in order: 0 + 10, 1 + 9, 2 + 8, …

Watch me

List the bonds of 10 in order, starting at 0 + 10, and stop when the parts start to repeat in the other order. How many different pairs is that?

  1. 0 + 10, 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6, 5 + 5. Then 6 + 4 is 4 + 6 swapped, so we stop counting new pairs.
  2. That is 6 different pairs if swaps are the same bond. If the question had asked for ordered additions, there would be more (6 + 4 as well as 4 + 6).

Together

If we count 3 + 7 and 7 + 3 as two different addition sentences, how many addition sentences make 10 with whole numbers from 0 to 10?

Hint 1: A list

0 + 10, 1 + 9, …, 10 + 0.

Hint 2: The count

There are 11 of those sentences.

Check

These are about a complete list, with the rule stated.

  1. Why must you say whether 8 + 2 and 2 + 8 count as one bond or two sentences?
  2. Is 10 + 0 a bond of 10? Why or why not?

Practice

  1. List every bond of 5, counting swaps as the same pair.
  2. A student lists 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6 and says “that is all.” What was missed, if pairs include 0 and 5?

Spanish · Numbers uno to diez

15 minutes · Method: hear and point, then say when ready. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today’s new job is the numbers uno to diez. Yesterday’s greetings and actions may be reused at the start, not retaught as a first look.

Word How to say it Meaning
uno OO-noh one
dos dohs two
tres trehs three
cuatro KWAH-troh four
cinco SEEN-koh five
seis sayss six
siete SYEH-teh seven
ocho OH-choh eight
nueve NWEH-beh nine
diez dyess ten

The guide counts objects (fingers, or invented paper stars, labeled invented) and says the Spanish number. The student points, then may say the number. Do not treat these as official sky counts.

A bond of 10 can be said with the Spanish numbers once they are familiar: ocho y dos son diez may be heard, with the English “eight and two make ten” given after, not as a new Spanish grammar lesson. The job today is the ten number words.

Instructions to the guide are in English. Only the Spanish words themselves are spoken in Spanish. Hear clips use es-MX. If this is the student’s first Spanish, begin here. Students who later start Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet a short recap of greetings there; that is by design for those who skipped these foundation levels.

Remember

Uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez are 1 to 10. Hear and point first. Instruction stays in English.

Watch me

The guide holds up 5 fingers and says cinco. What should the student do?

  1. Point to the five, or show five, to show the word was understood.
  2. They may say cinco when ready.

Together

What number is ocho, and what number with it makes diez, as a bond of 10?

Hint 1: Eight

Ocho is 8.

Hint 2: The bond

8 + 2 = 10; dos is 2.

Check

These check the number words 1 to 10.

  1. Say, in order, uno to cinco, and give the English.
  2. What is diez in English, and what is 10 in Spanish?

Exercises

  1. Hear seis, siete, and nueve. Give the English of each.
  2. Point and say the Spanish for 3 and for 8.

Latin · Sōl and lūna

15 minutes · Method: a picture and a short original caption. Original sentences only.

Learn

Yesterday: salvē, stella, est. Today names sōl (the Sun) and lūna (the Moon), still with original captions.

Word How to say it Meaning
sōl sohl the Sun
lūna LOO-nah the Moon
stella STEL-lah a star

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original captions, each with a drawing:

  • Sōl est. — It is the Sun.
  • Lūna est. — It is the Moon.
  • Stella est. — It is a star. (from yesterday)

Do not copy a textbook story. A picture of the Sun or the Moon is enough. These captions do not invent official sizes.

All Latin here is original; no textbook story is copied. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Pronounce every letter; c is always like k; v is like English w; vowels are pure (a as in father, e as in they, i as in machine, o as in note, u as in rude).

Remember

Sōl: the Sun. Lūna: the Moon. Stella: a star. Sōl est and Lūna est are original captions. Salvē remains a greeting to one person.

Watch me

Point at a drawing of the Moon and say the Latin caption, then the English.

  1. Lūna est.
  2. It is the Moon.

Together

What does sōl mean, and how is Stella est different from Sōl est?

Hint 1: Sōl

The Sun.

Hint 2: The caption

Stella est is about a star; Sōl est is about the Sun. Both use est.

Check

These check sōl, lūna, and stella.

  1. Give the English of Lūna est and of Sōl est.
  2. What does salvē mean, from yesterday?

Exercises

  1. Hear sōl, lūna, and stella. Give the English of each.
  2. Write one original Latin caption for a drawing of the Sun.

Notebook: a bond of 10, and a short reason

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: a number bond of 10 (two parts that make 10), and a ten-frame or counters showing the same bond. Label the whole and the parts. Then write from stems.

Word bank: bond, ten, part, whole, make, split.

Stems:

  1. 8 and 2 make 10 because …
  2. 8 + 7 is 15, but I did not add the 7 all at once: I …
  3. I made ten first, so …

End with the number sentence for 8 + 7 (or another making-ten fact you choose) and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw the bond, label the parts, then write. Because / but / so keep the same opening where the stems ask for it. Say each sentence aloud first.

Watch me

Finish: “8 and 2 make 10 because …”

  1. A model: “8 and 2 make 10 because they are a bond of 10,” or “because 8 needs 2 more to fill a ten.” Other complete finishes accepted.

Together

Finish the but-stem about 8 + 7. What split should appear?

Hint 1: Making ten

7 is 2 + 5, and the 2 finishes the 10.

Hint 2: A model

“… but I did not add the 7 all at once: I split 7 into 2 and 5.”

Check

These check the stems and the bond drawing.

  1. What must you draw before you write?
  2. Write the sentence 8 + 7 = 15 with the making-ten split shown beside it.

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question about making ten.
