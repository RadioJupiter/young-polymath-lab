Unit check

45 minutes · alone, no calculator

Part A: number 1. Write 2,867,000,000 in words. 2. What is the value of the 6 in 1,632,450? 3. Round 49,528 to the nearest thousand and to the nearest ten thousand. 4. Put in order, smallest first: 120,536; 51,118; 142,984; 49,528. 5. What number is 100,000 less than 1,042,000?

Part B: calculation 6. 68,457 + 24,986 7. 90,000 − 37,465 8. 248 × 6 9. 35 × 24 (show your area model) 10. 952 ÷ 7

Part C: problems (draw a bar model for each) 11. The Moon is about 384,400 km away. A spacecraft has 129,850 km still to go. How far has it travelled? 12. A rover drives 125 m each day. How far does it drive in 4 weeks? 13. Radio messages take 500 seconds to come from a probe. How many minutes and seconds is that? 14. A planet's year lasts 225 Earth days (close to Venus's 224.7). How many full weeks is that, and how many days left over? 15. Two moons have diameters that add up to 5,000 km. One is 1,200 km wider than the other. How wide is each?

Challenge problems

25 minutes

  1. A spacecraft leaves Earth and travels 1,000 km every hour. How many whole days would it take to reach the Moon (384,400 km)? How many hours are left over in the last day?
  2. The digits 1 to 6 are each used once to make two three-digit numbers. What is the largest possible sum? The smallest?
  3. A probe sends a photo that takes 1 hour 20 minutes to arrive at the speed of light (300,000 km per second). About how far away is the probe?

Math circle: the broken chessboard

20 minutes · an invariant: something that never changes, whatever you do

Draw a 4 × 4 board of squares, coloured like a chessboard. Cross out two opposite corner squares. Can you cover the remaining 14 squares exactly with 7 dominoes, where each domino covers two squares side by side?

Try it. Then count the colours of the remaining squares, and explain what every domino always covers. Does the same argument work for a full 8 × 8 chessboard with opposite corners removed?

Spanish · Review story

15 minutes · Method: comprehensible input (Krashen) and Total Physical Response (Asher). Listen and act first; speak when ready. Instructions to the guide are in English.

For the guide: tell the story twice, pointing. The third time, pause for the student to fill a word. End with a silent pointing walk-through, then, if ready, a spoken retell. This is a check of the two weeks, not a test to pass or fail.

The story.

  1. Es de noche.
  2. Ana está en el jardín.
  3. Ana mira el cielo.
  4. Ana ve la luna. La luna es blanca y grande.
  5. Un perro corre en el jardín.
  6. Ana dice: —¡Hola, luna!

Exercises

  1. Point through the story as the guide reads it out of order; put it back in order.
  2. Retell, in Spanish or with pointing and single words.
  3. Write (or copy) two sentences you can now read alone.

Latin · Review story

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin reading (in the spirit of Ørberg and of *Minimus*). All text here is original; those books are not copied.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible; you supply a word only after a real try. Acting *currit* and *spectā* is a valid way to show meaning.

The story.

  1. Aulus est puer. Gaia est puella.
  2. Celer est canis magnus.
  3. Aulus et Gaia sunt in hortō.
  4. Celer currit. Aulus canem videt.
  5. Gaia lūnam spectat.
  6. Gaia dicit: —Salvē, lūna! Valē, Aule!

Exercises

  1. Who is in the garden? What does Aulus see? What does Gaia look at?
  2. Read the story aloud.
  3. Copy two lines you can now read alone.

Notebook: design a mission

60 minutes, and as long as you like over the weekend · a short paragraph from an outline · [How the notebook works](../notebook.html) · [Printable sheets](../printables/d10.html)

How the guide helps today: the design must be the student's. Help with looking up numbers already in this unit, and with the outline. As on Day 9, notes in the outline are not full sentences. If handwriting is tiring after the unit check, the guide may scribe the paragraph *exactly as spoken*, and the student copies or traces it.

Choose a destination: the Moon, Mars, Jupiter or a place of your choice. On a double page:

1. Draw your spacecraft from two views (side and front), with labels, the way Leonardo da Vinci drew machines.

2. Label the parts and the destination.

Word bank: spacecraft · destination · Moon · Mars · Jupiter · distance · light-time · travel · power · food · message · problem · question

3. Write. First an outline:

  • Topic sentence (in full): This mission goes to ________.
  • Notes: distance / light-time · one problem and an idea · a second problem and an idea
  • Closing sentence (in full): a question you would need an expert to answer, written as a sentence.

Then write a short paragraph from the outline (four to six sentences is plenty).

4. Numbers. Write the distance (from the unit's tables), the light-time for messages, and a travel time if your craft averages a speed you choose (show the division). List three problems the mission must solve and one idea for each, if they did not all fit in the paragraph.
