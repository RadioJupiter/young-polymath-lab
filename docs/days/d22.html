Mental math warm-up: angles in a triangle, in easy cases

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Today’s core lesson will show, with a picture, that the three angles of a triangle add to 180°. This warm-up uses that fact in easy cases after it has been named. If the warm-up is done first, the guide should say: the three angles of a triangle add to 180°, and the reason comes in the core lesson.

Remember

The three angles of a triangle add to 180°. A right angle is 90°. A straight angle is 180°.

Watch me

A triangle has a right angle and a 40° angle. What is the third angle?

  1. The three angles add to 180°.
  2. 90° + 40° = 130°.
  3. 180° − 130° = 50°.

Together

An equilateral triangle has three equal angles. How large is each?

Hint 1: Equal parts

180° in three equal parts.

Hint 2: The number

180° ÷ 3 = 60°.

Check

These use only 180° in a triangle, once that fact has been named.

  1. A triangle has angles 70° and 50°. Find the third.
  2. A triangle has two right angles. Is that possible? Why?

Practice

  1. What is 180° − 90° − 30°?
  2. Three angles 60°, 60°, and 60° make what kind of triangle, if the sides also match?

Core lesson: polygons, and the angles of a triangle

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (tear the corners of a paper triangle, or walk the outline), pictorial (the triangle and the hexagon in the figures), then the angle-sum rule

Learn

A polygon is a closed shape made of straight sides. A triangle has 3 sides, a quadrilateral 4, a pentagon 5, a hexagon 6. A polygon is regular when all sides are equal and all interior angles are equal. An invented hexagonal landing pad in this lesson is a classroom shape, and must be labeled invented.

The angles of a triangle add to 180°. One picture: tear the three corners of a paper triangle and place them on a straight line; they fill the straight angle. Another: the triangle in the figure is right-angled at one vertex, with 40° in a second corner, so the third is 50°, because 90 + 40 + 50 = 180.

A quadrilateral can be cut into two triangles, so its interior angles add to 2 × 180° = 360°.

A regular hexagon can be split, from the center, into six equilateral triangles. Each interior angle of the hexagon is 120° (two 60° angles from two of those triangles). The same 120° comes from the general rule: the interior angle sum of an n-gon is (n − 2) × 180°, so a hexagon has (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°, and 720° ÷ 6 = 120° if it is regular. (The math circle will say why the (n − 2) count is the number of triangles.)

A crater rim is not a polygon: it is not made of straight sides. We will not pretend a Moon crater is a hexagon.

Remember

Polygon: closed, straight sides. Triangle 180°. Quadrilateral 360°. n-gon sum: (n − 2) × 180°. Regular: all sides equal and all interior angles equal. Invented pads stay labeled. A crater rim is not a polygon.

Watch me

In the pictured triangle, the angles are 90°, 40°, and one missing. Find it, and say why the three add to 180°.

  1. 90° + 40° = 130°; 180° − 130° = 50°.
  2. The three corners of a triangle make a straight angle (180°).
  3. The figure should be labeled 90°, 40°, and 50°.

Together

A regular pentagon: first find the interior angle sum, then each interior angle.

Hint 1: The sum

(5 − 2) × 180° = 540°.

Hint 2: Regular

540° ÷ 5 = 108° each.

Check

These use only the polygon facts taught today.

  1. A triangle has angles 62° and 38°. Find the third.
  2. An invented regular hexagonal pad: what is each interior angle? Label the pad invented.

Practice

  1. A quadrilateral has angles 80°, 95°, 100°, and one missing. Find the missing angle.
  2. Why can a crater rim not be called a polygon in this lesson?
  3. Is every rectangle regular? Say why.
  4. A regular octagon: find the interior angle sum, then each interior angle.
  5. Draw any triangle, label two angles, find the third, and show that the three add to 180°.

Challenge problems: mixed polygons, and a pad that is not a crater

20–25 minutes · the same angle sums, with a reason

Learn

These problems use the triangle sum, the n-gon formula, and the word regular. An invented pad stays labeled. A crater stays a crater.

Remember

Triangle 180°. n-gon sum (n − 2) × 180°. Regular means equal sides and equal interior angles. Invented pads labeled. Crater rims are not polygons.

Watch me

An invented landing pad is a regular hexagon. Next to it, a triangular access yard has angles 90° and 35°. Find the hexagon’s interior angle and the yard’s third angle, and label both invented.

  1. Regular hexagon: each interior angle is 120° (from 720° ÷ 6, or from two 60° angles).
  2. Triangle: 90 + 35 = 125, so the third is 55°.
  3. Both shapes are invented classroom plans, not a NASA pad and not a crater.

Together

Can a regular polygon have an interior angle of 90°? Which one, and why?

Hint 1: The sum

If each angle is 90° and there are n angles, the sum is 90n, and that must equal (n − 2) × 180.

Hint 2: Solve

90n = 180n − 360, so 360 = 90n, n = 4. A square.

Check

These stay with today’s polygon facts.

  1. A five-sided invented courtyard is not regular. Its interior angles are 100°, 110°, 120°, 90°, and one missing. Find the missing angle.
  2. Why is “the crater is a regular hexagon 120° at each corner” not an honest sentence in this course?

Practice

  1. An isosceles triangle has two equal angles of 70°. Find the third. Is the triangle also equilateral?
  2. A regular decagon (10 sides): each interior angle.
  3. Write one sentence that could head a design page: invented polygon listed, crater not claimed as a polygon.

Math circle: why the angle sum is (n − 2) × 180°

20 minutes · a short argument, not a new unit

Learn

The formula (n − 2) × 180° is not a memory trick. From one vertex of a simple convex n-gon you can draw diagonals to all the other vertices except itself and its two neighbors. Those diagonals cut the polygon into (n − 2) triangles. Each triangle’s angles add to 180°, and together they make up the interior angles of the polygon.

Remember

A convex n-gon splits into (n − 2) triangles from one vertex. That is why the interior sum is (n − 2) × 180°.

Watch me

For a hexagon, how many triangles do you get from one vertex, and what is the angle sum?

  1. From one vertex you skip itself and two neighbors, so you draw diagonals to the three vertices left? A hexagon has 6 vertices; from one vertex there are 6 − 3 = 3 vertices you may join, but the number of triangles is 6 − 2 = 4.
  2. Four triangles: 4 × 180° = 720°.
  3. If the hexagon is regular, each interior angle is 720° ÷ 6 = 120°, which matches today’s core lesson.

Together

How many triangles from one vertex of a pentagon, and what is the sum?

Hint 1: The count

n − 2 = 5 − 2 = 3 triangles.

Hint 2: The sum

3 × 180° = 540°.

Check

The point is the count of triangles, not a new formula.

  1. Why is a triangle the (n − 2) = 1 case, and why is that a check, not a surprise?
  2. What would go wrong if you tried this cut on a crater outline?

Practice

  1. Write one sentence you want to keep: the formula counts triangles.

Spanish · A triangle and a side

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
un triángulo oon tree-AHNG-goo-loh a triangle
un cuadrado oon kwah-DRAH-doh a square
un lado oon LAH-doh a side
tiene tres lados tee-EH-neh tres LAH-dohs it has three sides
un hexágono oon ek-SAH-goh-noh a hexagon

Original sentences: El triángulo tiene tres lados. El cuadrado tiene cuatro lados. El hexágono tiene seis lados. If the hexagon is the invented pad, say in English that the pad is invented.

For the guide: point to a drawing. Present tense only. Instruction in English.

Remember

un triángulo; un cuadrado; un lado; tiene tres / cuatro / seis lados; un hexágono. Invented pads, if used, are labeled in English.

Watch me

Say: “The triangle has three sides.”

  1. El triángulo tiene tres lados.
  2. Tiene is present. Lados are the sides.
  3. This is a description of a drawing, not a NASA claim.

Together

How do you say “The square has four sides”?

Hint 1: The noun

el cuadrado

Hint 2: The sentence

El cuadrado tiene cuatro lados.

  2. Write one tiene-sentence about a triangle or a square.

Latin · triāngulum, and latus the side

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
triāngulum tree-AHNG-goo-lum a triangle (neuter)
Triāngulum tria latera habet tree-AHNG-goo-lum TREE-ah LAH-teh-rah HAH-bet The triangle has three sides
latus, latera LAH-tus, LAH-teh-rah side, sides (not the same word as lātus, “wide”)
Quadrātum quattuor latera habet kwah-DRAH-tum KWAT-too-or LAH-teh-rah HAH-bet The square has four sides

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

A word to keep apart. You already know lātus, lāta, lātum (“wide”). Today’s latus, lateris (plural latera) means “a side.” They are different words. The macron on lātus (“wide”) is part of how this course writes the difference.

Original sentences: Triāngulum tria latera habet. Quadrātum quattuor latera habet. Triāngulum nōn quattuor latera habet.

For the guide: no degree numbers in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

triāngulum tria latera habet; quadrātum quattuor latera habet. latus (side) is not lātus (wide). No degrees in the Latin.

Watch me

Read: Triāngulum tria latera habet. Why latera, not *lāta?

  1. The word wanted is “sides,” latus, latera.
  2. lāta would be the feminine of “wide.”
  3. tria latera: three sides.

Together

Say: “The square has four sides.”

Hint 1: The noun

quadrātum, neuter.

Hint 2: The sentence

Quadrātum quattuor latera habet.

Check

These use only today’s patterns.

  1. Write: “The triangle does not have four sides.”
  2. Write one new habet-sentence about a triangle or a square.

Exercises

  1. Correct: *triāngulum tria lāta habet, if someone used “wide.”
  2. Read the model sentences aloud.
  3. Draw a triangle and a square, labeled in Latin with latera.
  4. Why are the 50° and 120° left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy one model sentence.

Notebook: a triangle, a hexagon, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw the 90°-40°-50° triangle and a regular hexagon split into six equilateral triangles (or the invented pad, labeled invented). Then because / but / so: a triangle’s angles add to 180°, but a crater rim is not a polygon, so I will not give a crater a 120° “corner.”

Word bank: polygon, triangle, quadrilateral, hexagon, regular, invented, interior, crater.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. Invented pads tagged. Craters not called polygons. The page is the work, not a badge.

Watch me

A model sentence: Each interior angle of a regular hexagon is 120° because the angle sum is 720°, but that is a straight-sided shape, so I will not use 120° as a corner of a crater.

  1. (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°, and 720° ÷ 6 = 120°.
  2. The but names the kind of shape.
  3. The so blocks a misuse.

Together

Finish: The pictured triangle has a 50° angle, because ________, but the three angles are turns, so ________.

Hint 1: The sum

…90° + 40° + 50° = 180°, or 180° − 130° = 50°.

Hint 2: The unit

…I write 50°, not 50 cm.

Check

The page should show the drawings, the labels, and complete stems.

  1. Draw and label the triangle and the hexagon (invented, if it is the pad), with the angle sums visible.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation that keeps a polygon apart from a crater.

Practice

  1. Add the n-gon formula in your own words, as a triangle count.
  2. Tick the word-bank words you used.
  3. The page is not a badge or a score.
  4. A note that the pad, if drawn, is invented.
  5. File the page. It is not a reward.
