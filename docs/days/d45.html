Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 12

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

This warm-up mixes the mental work of the level: a total, a skip-count, a change, and a short “out of.”

Remember

A total is a sum. A change is up or down by a difference. 1 out of 4 is 1/4.

Watch me

A labeled invented list is 4, 8, and 6, and a count goes from 10 to 7. What is the total of the list, and how did the count change?

  1. The total is 18.
  2. The count went down by 3.

Together

What is 1 out of 6 as a fraction, and what is 3 out of 6 as a simpler fraction?

Hint 1: One face

1 out of 6 is 1/6.

Hint 2: Three faces

3 out of 6 is 3/6, the same amount as 1/2.

Check

These mix the level’s mental facts.

  1. Two groups of five, and three leftovers: what is the total?
  2. From 3.6 to 8.8: did the number rise or fall?

Practice

  1. Skip-count by twos from 0 to 10.
  2. 1 out of 2 as a fraction.

Level 12 check

35–40 minutes · end-of-level mastery check · four out of five or better on each part; not a score or a reward

Learn

This sitting gathers the level: tallies grouped in fives and tables with headings; bar graphs with an even scale and pictographs with a key; a line graph of SILSO yearly mean sunspot numbers, read as sourced change and not as a law about the next official year; and a first look at fair chance (“1 out of n,” and a fraction) together with honest measurement (“about,” or a range, not a fake extra digit).

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

Remember

Tallies group in fives; a table organizes the same counts. A bar graph needs an even scale; a pictograph needs a key. SILSO yearly means are sourced figures: describe the change you can point to, and do not invent a later official mean. Fair chance is “out of” or a fraction; expected is about, not exactly, in a short run. A measurement is written only as finely as the tool allows. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Watch me

A short layout example, not one of the unit-check items: a labeled invented tally of 12, and a fair coin’s chance of heads.

  1. 12 is two groups of five and two leftovers.
  2. The chance of heads is 1 out of 2, or 1/2.
  3. After a Together item for layout, do the Unit check alone.

Together

The Together item is practice for layout. After it, do the Unit check alone. What will you write beside a SILSO yearly mean, besides the number?

Hint 1: The source

That it is a SILSO yearly mean from the SN_y_tot_V2.0 file.

Hint 2: Invented numbers

If a meteor count or another classroom list is invented, it stays labeled invented.

Check

The Together item is only layout practice. The Unit check is the mastery sitting.

  1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A tallies and tables, Part B graphs and sourced change, Part C chance and measuring with error. Then begin Part A when the guide says.
  2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

Unit check

Work alone. Use the graphs and tables already taught when a question names them, or make a quick drawing when it helps. Keep invented classroom counts labeled invented. Keep SILSO yearly means labeled as SILSO figures from the SN_y_tot_V2.0 file. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: tallies and tables

  1. Write a tally for 14, and say the number in groups of five and leftovers.
  2. An invented classroom watch has counts 5, 9, 4, and 7. What is the total, and which watch has the most?
  3. A labeled invented table of three watches has a total of 20. Watch 1 is 6 and Watch 2 is 8. What is Watch 3?
  4. Why do tallies group in fives?
  5. Why must an invented classroom meteor total stay labeled invented, and not be called an official rate for a named shower?

Part B: graphs and sourced change

  1. A bar graph of a labeled invented watch has an even scale from 0 to 12 in steps of 2. The bar for Watch 2 reaches 8. What count does that bar show?
  2. A pictograph has the key “one star = 2 meteors.” A row shows 5 stars. What count does that row show?
  3. In the SILSO yearly means used on Day 43, which year in 2019–2025 has the highest mean, and what is that mean?
  4. The 2025 SILSO yearly mean is 123.2, and the 2024 mean is 154.7. How did the mean change, and why is “it will keep rising in 2026” a miss?
  5. Why would a bar-graph scale marked 0, 2, 4, 10, 12 be a problem, and why is a pictograph without a key not ready to be read?

Part C: chance and measuring with error

  1. A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the chance of a 4, as “out of” and as a fraction?
  2. A fair coin is tossed 10 times. About how many heads would you expect, and must you get exactly that many?
  3. A spinner is divided into four parts that are not equal, and the shaded part is the small one. Why is “1 out of 4” the wrong chance for that shaded part?
  4. A rod ends a little past 8 cm and does not reach 9 cm on a centimeter ruler. Write an honest length, and say why 8.27 cm is a miss.
  5. A fair coin comes up heads five times in a row. Why does that, by itself, not prove the coin is unfair?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before moving on. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

25 minutes · mixed Level 12 thinking, after the check · not a second mastery sitting

Learn

These mix the level’s ideas. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Remember

Same skills as the check. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Watch me

A labeled invented pictograph has the key “one square = 4,” and a row shows 2½ squares. What count is that? Then, from the SILSO table, how did the yearly mean change from 2024 to 2025?

  1. Two squares stand for 8, and a half-square stands for 2, so the row shows 10. Invented pictograph.
  2. The SILSO yearly mean went from 154.7 in 2024 to 123.2 in 2025, so it went down.

Together

A fair die is rolled. What is the chance of an even number? Then say why writing 12.37 cm from a centimeter ruler is a miss.

Hint 1: Even faces

2, 4, and 6: 3 out of 6, which is 1/2.

Hint 2: The ruler

A centimeter ruler does not support hundredths. “About 12 cm,” or “between 12 and 13 cm,” is the honest wording when the rod is past 12 and not yet to 13.

Check

These mix the level and stay labeled.

  1. A labeled invented table has 3, 11, and n, and the total is 20. Find n.
  2. Why is a rise from 2019 to 2024 in the SILSO window not a law that 2026 must be higher?

Practice

  1. Why does a short run of heads not, by itself, prove a coin is unfair?
  2. Why do invented meteor counts stay out of the sunspot line graph?

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · a last argument about labels, scales, and “about”

Learn

This sitting gathers three habits from the level: invented classroom counts stay labeled invented; sourced SILSO means stay labeled as SILSO; a graph needs an even scale or a key; and “about,” or a range, is the honest wording when a tool or a short run does not support a finer claim.

Remember

Invented and sourced figures stay labeled. A graph is only as honest as its scale or its key. “About” is not a dodge; it is a fair reading.

Watch me

Why is it honest to say “the 2024 SILSO yearly mean is the highest in this 2019–2025 window,” but not honest to invent a 2026 official yearly mean from that rise?

  1. The 2024 high is something the sourced table already shows.
  2. A 2026 official mean would be a new figure, and this course has not opened one.
  3. The habit is to describe what the table shows, and to leave unsourced future figures unclaimed.

Together

How does “a pictograph needs a key” sit beside “a measurement needs the marks the ruler actually has”?

Hint 1: The key

Without a key, a reader invents what one picture stands for.

Hint 2: The marks

Without finer marks, a reader who writes 8.27 cm invents a precision the ruler does not have.

Check

These check the last argument.

  1. Give one reason an invented meteor total and a SILSO yearly mean must not be dropped into the same unlabeled graph.
  2. Why is “about 5 heads in 10 fair tosses” a stronger sentence than “the coin is unfair, because I just got 7”?

Practice

  1. This argument is not a score or a badge.

Notebook: a level wrap, and a because-sentence

about 1 hour · one page that gathers the level, then because / but / so · not a second mastery sitting

Learn

On one page, show a labeled invented tally or graph, one SILSO yearly-mean point with its source, and a fair-chance or ruler note. Then write a because / but / so sentence about keeping those labels honest.

Remember

Invented counts, sourced yearly means, and honest “about” readings stay distinct. This page is a record, not a second mastery sitting and not a score.

Watch me

Start: I keep an invented meteor count and a SILSO yearly mean labeled differently because ________.

  1. The because restates that one is a classroom invention and the other is an official series.
  2. A sound because: …one comes from a labeled classroom watch, and the other comes from the SILSO file…

Together

Finish: I keep an invented meteor count and a SILSO yearly mean labeled differently because ________, but they must not share an unlabeled graph, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…mixing them without labels would make a classroom watch look like a sunspot record…

Hint 2: The so

…I will label each, keep scales and keys honest, and write measurements as about, or as a range, when the tool is not finer.

Check

The page should show the three small records and complete stems.

  1. Draw or copy one invented graph or tally, one SILSO figure with its source, and one fair-chance or ruler note.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about honest labels and honest precision.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: invented, SILSO, key, about, fair.
  2. The page is a record, not a score, and not a second mastery sitting.
  3. File the page. It is not a reward.
