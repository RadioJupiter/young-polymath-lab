Unit 1 · Day 4 · How much farther?

Today's big question: Mars is farther from the Sun than Earth, and Jupiter is bigger than Saturn. By how much? Subtraction answers "how much more" questions.

For the guide: the comparison bar model is the key picture today: two bars, one longer, and the question is the difference. Also teach "counting up" (adding on from the smaller number), which is often faster than written subtraction across zeros.

Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · try counting up from the smaller number

Learn

Subtraction can be done by counting on to the next ten or thousand, or by compensation (subtract a nice number, then put back the extra). 1,003 − 997: both are close, so count on from 997 to 1,003, which is 6. 6,000 − 1,999: take 2,000 then put 1 back, because you took 1 too many.

Remember

Count on from the smaller number, or subtract a nice number and compensate.

Watch me

6,000 − 1,999.

  1. 1,999 is 1 less than 2,000. Subtract 2,000: 6,000 − 2,000 = 4,000.
  2. You took away 1 too many, so add 1 back: 4,001.

Together

1,003 − 997. Count on from 997.

Hint 1: To 1,000

997 + 3 = 1,000.

Hint 2: Then

1,000 + 3 = 1,003, so you counted on 6. The difference is 6.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. 10,000 − 9,950.
  2. 384,400 − 4,400.

Practice

  1. 1,000 − 1
  2. 1,000 − 375
  3. 5,000 − 2,500
  4. 10,000 − 9,950
  5. 384,400 − 4,400
  6. 700 − 698
  7. 1,003 − 997
  8. 6,000 − 1,999

Core lesson: subtraction and the comparison model

45 minutes

Learn

1. Concrete. Build 1,000 with base-ten pieces (one thousand-stack). Take away 375. You must first trade the thousand for ten hundreds, then one hundred for ten tens, then one ten for ten ones. Trading down is the same idea as carrying, run backwards.

2. Pictorial: the comparison model. To compare two quantities, draw two bars lined up at the left. The extra piece on the longer bar is the difference.

Earthsame as Mars? (the difference)
Earth's whole bar = 12,756 km
Mars6,792 km

3. Abstract. 12,756 − 6,792. Ones: 6 − 2 = 4. Tens: 5 − 9 does not work, so trade one hundred for ten tens: 15 − 9 = 6. Hundreds: now 6 − 7 does not work, so trade one thousand: 16 − 7 = 9. Thousands: now 1 − 6 does not work, so trade the ten-thousand: 11 − 6 = 5. Answer: 5,964 km.

Check by adding back: 6,792 + 5,964 = 12,756. ✓ Every subtraction can be checked with an addition.

Remember

Trading down is carrying run backwards: one disc is ten of the next smaller disc. Comparison bars: when two amounts are compared, the difference is the extra part of the longer bar. Take-away bars: the whole minus a part leaves the other part.

Watch me

Find 1,000 − 375 by trading, as in the concrete step above.

  1. You cannot take 5 ones from 0 ones, so trade a ten (after first trading the thousand, then a hundred, then a ten).
  2. The result is 625.

Together

Earth is 12,756 km across; Mars is 6,792 km across. Draw a comparison bar and find how much wider Earth is.

Hint 1: Bars

The longer bar is 12,756; the shorter is 6,792; the extra piece is the difference.

Hint 2: Subtract

12,756 − 6,792 = 5,964 km.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. 700 − 698, by counting on.
  2. A tank holds 9,000 liters and 3,875 have been used. How much is left? Which bar is this — part-whole or comparison?

Practice

  1. How much wider is Jupiter (142,984 km) than Saturn (120,536 km)?
  2. Mars is about 228 million km from the Sun and Earth about 150 million km (rounded). How much farther from the Sun is Mars? Why is your answer only approximate?
  3. What must be added to 384,400 to make 1,000,000?
  4. Saturn takes about 10,747 days to go once around the Sun; Jupiter takes 4,331. How many more days does Saturn take? Draw the comparison model.
  5. 3,000 − 1,457
  6. The Moon's distance from Earth changes during each orbit, from about 363,300 km at its closest to about 405,500 km at its farthest. What is the difference?

Challenge problems

25 minutes

Learn

Work backwards when you know the result of a change: if you subtracted 2,375 and got 6,880, the original is the part-whole 6,880 + 2,375. When two numbers add to a whole and differ by a given amount, draw two bars, cut the extra off the longer one, and share what remains equally.

Remember

For a sum S and a difference D: the smaller is (S − D) ÷ 2; the larger is the smaller plus D.

Watch me

Two numbers add to 10,000 and one is 1,250 more than the other.

10,000
smallersmaller + 1,250
  1. Take 1,250 off the whole: 10,000 − 1,250 = 8,750. That 8,750 is two equal smaller parts.
  2. Each smaller part is 4,375. The larger is 4,375 + 1,250 = 5,625.
  3. Check: 4,375 + 5,625 = 10,000, and 5,625 − 4,375 = 1,250.

Together

I subtract 2,375 and get 6,880. What was my number?

Hint 1: Backwards

Adding back undoes the subtraction.

Hint 2: Answer

6,880 + 2,375 = 9,255.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Two lengths add to 5,000 km and one is 1,200 km longer. How long is the shorter?

Practice

  1. I think of a number. I subtract 2,375 and get 6,880. What was my number?
  2. Two numbers add up to 10,000, and one is 1,250 more than the other. Find them. (Draw two bars.)
  3. A rocket's fuel tank holds 9,000 liters. After the first burn, 3,875 liters are left; after the second burn, 1,290 liters are left. How much fuel did the second burn use? How much did both burns use together?

Math circle: odd and even

20 minutes · parity, one of the most powerful ideas in the math circle tradition

Learn

Odd + odd = even, odd + even = odd, even + even = even. A sum of an even count of odd numbers is even; a sum of an odd count of odd numbers is odd. For handshakes: each handshake uses two hands, so the total number of 'hands used' must be even.

Remember

An odd number of odd addends cannot make an even total. The number of people with an odd number of handshakes must itself be even.

Watch me

Can three odd numbers add to 10?

  1. Odd + odd = even; even + odd = odd.
  2. Three odds make an odd total. 10 is even. So no.

Together

Can you find four odd numbers that add to 25? Try, then use the rule.

Hint 1: How many odds?

Four is even, so four odds add to an even number.

Hint 2: 25 is odd

An even total cannot equal 25. It is impossible.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What is odd + odd + odd: odd or even?
  2. Why must a count of 'hands used' in a room of handshakes be even?

Practice

  1. Can you find four odd numbers that add up to 25? Try, and then explain why you can or cannot.
  2. Five astronauts meet. Can each one shake hands with exactly three of the others? (Hint: count how many "hands used" there would be in total, and remember that every handshake uses exactly two hands.)

Spanish · El cielo

15 minutes · Method: comprehensible input (Krashen) and Total Physical Response (Asher). Listen and act first; speak when ready. Instructions to the guide are in English.

Learn

These words name what you see in the sky, and whether it is day or night.

Word How to say it Meaning
el cielo el SYEH-loh the sky
el sol el sohl the sun
la luna lah LOO-nah the moon
una estrella OO-nah es-TREH-yah a star
de día deh DEE-ah in the daytime
de noche deh NOH-cheh at night
ve beh sees / (he or she) sees
mira MEE-rah looks at

For the guide: point to the picture, then, if it is dark enough or the Moon is up, look out of a window after the lesson. Review colors: El sol es amarillo. La luna es blanca.

The sun, the moon, and a star above a person looking up.

Look and listen.

  • El sol. La luna. Una estrella. El cielo.
  • Veo la luna. Veo el sol. Veo una estrella.
  • El sol es grande y amarillo. La luna es blanca.

Act. Mira el cielo. (look up) Señala el sol. Señala la luna. Señala una estrella.

Remember

Ana ve la luna — Ana sees the moon. Es de noche — it is night. El sol and la luna take el and la as shown.

Watch me

Es de noche. Ana ve la luna. First sentence: it is night. Second: Ana sees the moon. The two sentences make a tiny story.

Together

Say in Spanish: 'Ana looks at the sky.'

Hint 1: Words

Ana, mira, el cielo.

Hint 2: Sentence

Ana mira el cielo.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does la luna mean?
  2. What does de día mean?

Exercises

  1. Point: el sol, la luna, una estrella, el cielo.
  2. Say Veo la luna while pointing at the Moon in the picture.
  3. Copy: Veo el sol. El sol es grande.

Latin · Canis Celer

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin reading (in the spirit of Ørberg and of Minimus). All text here is original; those books are not copied.

Learn

Canis is a dog. The dog in our pictures is called Celer (the name means 'swift'). Currit means he or she or it runs.

Word How to say it Meaning
canis KAH-niss dog
Celer KEH-ler the dog's name (Swift)
currit KUR-rit runs
magnus MAG-nuss large
parvus PAR-wuss small

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

For the guide: act currit (run in place) and dormit (head on hands). The student may act instead of speaking.

A dog running; a small moon in the sky.

Read, looking at the picture.

  • Celer est canis.
  • Celer est magnus.
  • Celer currit.
  • Celer dormit.
  • Celer est in hortō.

Remember

Celer est canis. Celer currit. Magnus and parvus tell size, as grande and pequeño do in Spanish — but here we are speaking Latin.

Watch me

Celer est canis magnus. Celer is a large dog. Celer currit in hortō. Celer runs in the garden.

Together

Say in Latin: 'Celer is a dog.'

Hint 1: Words

Celer, est, canis.

Hint 2: Sentence

Celer est canis.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does currit mean?
  2. What does canis mean?

Exercises

  1. Act currit and dormit when you hear them.
  2. Read the five lines.
  3. Complete: Celer est _____. Celer est in _____.

Notebook: the Moon journal begins

60 minutes now, plus two minutes each evening for a month · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

The Moon's shape, as we see it, changes through the month because sunlight lights different parts of the Moon as the Moon goes around Earth. A Moon journal is a careful record: date, time, shape, and where it sat in the sky. If cloud hides it, write 'cloudy' — that is still a record. The aim is a set of drawings you can later use to predict the next full Moon, not a pretty guess.

Remember

Each entry needs a date, a time, a shape, and a place in the sky (or 'cloudy'). Draw only what you see.

Watch me

A sample entry: 17 November, 6:40 p.m., a thin crescent above the western trees, horns pointing left. The drawing is a small circle with one edge lit. The words name the place so you can find it again.

Together

If it is cloudy tonight, what do you write, and do you still date the page?

Hint 1: Write

Write 'cloudy' (and the date and time you looked).

Hint 2: Why

A gap you have recorded is more useful than a blank page.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Name the four things a good Moon-journal entry includes when the Moon is visible.

Practice

How the guide helps today: help set up a week's boxes. For the prediction sentence, use because: the student says why the Moon might look bigger, smaller or the same in a week. If tonight is cloudy, draw the last Moon that was actually seen and write "cloudy tonight".

Start a Moon journal: for the next 30 days, whenever the Moon is visible, draw its shape, note the date and time, and sketch where it is in the sky (above which tree, roof or landmark). If clouds hide it, write "cloudy". The Moon is often visible in the daytime too; look for it.

Today, prepare the journal: a page for each week, with a box for each day. Then draw a careful picture of the Moon as it looks tonight (or the last time you saw it).

Never look at the Sun, even when looking for the daytime Moon.

1. Draw today's (or the last seen) Moon, large, with any markings you can really see. Add the week of empty boxes for the journal.

2. Label: date, time, and the shape. Use the word bank.

Word bank: Moon · date · time · sky · cloudy · shape · crescent · half · full · prediction · week

3. Write.

  • Tonight the Moon looks ________.
  • I saw it ________ (where, or "I did not see it").

Then: In one week the Moon will look ________ because ________.

4. You will check the prediction in a week (Day 8 is on that path if you work each school day, but the journal runs every evening, not only on school days).
