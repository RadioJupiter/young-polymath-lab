Mental math warm-up: covering a rectangle

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

Area is how much it takes to cover a shape, in square units. A 5 cm by 3 cm rectangle is covered by 5 × 3 = 15 square centimeters (cm²). You can picture 3 rows of 5 unit squares.

Remember

Area of a rectangle: L × W, in square units (cm², m²). This is not the perimeter.

Watch me

A 6 cm by 2 cm rectangle: what is its area, and what is its perimeter?

  1. Area: 6 × 2 = 12 cm².
  2. Perimeter: 2 × (6+2) = 16 cm.
  3. The two answers have different units and different jobs.

Together

A square has side 5 cm. What is its area?

Hint 1: A square is a rectangle

5 × 5.

Hint 2: Units

25 cm².

Check

Give area and, when asked, perimeter, with units.

  1. A 10 cm by 3 cm rectangle: area.
  2. A 4 m by 4 m square: area.

Practice

  1. 7 cm by 1 cm: area and perimeter.
  2. Why is 14 the wrong number for the area of a 7 by 1 rectangle, if someone added?
  3. 3 rows of 4 squares: area.
  4. A 2 m by 8 m invented pad: area, labeled invented.
  5. Which is larger: 20 cm of perimeter, or 20 cm² of area? (A trick: they are different kinds of quantity.)

Core lesson: area of rectangles, L-shapes, and a right triangle

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (unit squares), pictorial (an array or a bar), then L × W; a right triangle as half of a rectangle

Learn

Area is covering, in square units. A 5 by 3 rectangle holds 15 unit squares: 15 cm² if the side is in centimeters. We write cm² (square centimeters) and m² (square meters).

An L-shape. Split it into rectangles, or complete a rectangle and subtract the missing part. For the picture: an 8 cm by 3 cm rectangle (24 cm²) plus a 4 cm by 2 cm rectangle on the left (8 cm²) totals 32 cm². Or: an 8 cm by 5 cm rectangle (40 cm²) minus a 4 cm by 2 cm bite (8 cm²) is also 32 cm².

A right triangle as half a rectangle. A right triangle with legs 6 cm and 4 cm is half of a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle, so its area is ½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm². (That is also ½ × base × height, with those legs as base and height.) We are not doing non-right triangles today, except to note that the same ½ × base × height formula will wait for a careful height in a later lesson.

Invented habitat floor (labeled invented). A 6 m by 3 m module has floor area 18 m². That is a classroom number, not a NASA floor plan.

Remember

Area of a rectangle: L × W, in cm² or m². L-shapes: add or subtract rectangles. A right triangle is half the rectangle on its legs: ½ × b × h. Perimeter and area are different jobs. Invented floors stay labeled.

Watch me

Find the area of the L-shape in the picture in two ways (add two rectangles, or subtract a bite).

  1. Add: 8 × 3 = 24, and 4 × 2 = 8, total 32 cm².
  2. Subtract: 8 × 5 = 40, minus a 4 × 2 bite of 8, total 32 cm².
  3. Both ways agree, which is a useful check.

Together

A right triangle has legs 6 cm and 4 cm. What is its area, and why?

Hint 1: Half a rectangle

It is half of a 6 cm by 4 cm rectangle.

Hint 2: Arithmetic

½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm².

Check

These are covering questions, with units.

  1. A 9 m by 4 m invented pad: area, labeled invented.
  2. The pictured L-shape has perimeter 26 cm (yesterday) and area 32 cm². Why can you not say which number is “bigger” without naming the job?

Practice

  1. A 7 cm by 5 cm rectangle: area and perimeter.
  2. A right triangle with legs 5 cm and 8 cm: area.
  3. An L made of a 10 by 6 rectangle minus a 3 by 3 corner bite: area.
  4. A 2 m by 3 m rectangle beside a 2 m by 3 m rectangle, joined along a 3 m side: what is the combined area? (Does joining change area the way it can change perimeter?)
  5. Draw a right triangle, mark the two legs, and write ½ × b × h beside it.

Challenge problems: a floor plan, and a missing length

20–25 minutes · invented habitat numbers, clearly labeled

Learn

In this course’s design problem (invented), a module floor is an L: a 6 m by 5 m rectangle with a 2 m by 2 m bite from one corner. Floor area: 6×5 − 2×2 = 30 − 4 = 26 m². If a second room is a 3 m by 3 m square, the combined floor area is 26 + 9 = 35 m². Those are classroom figures, not a mission specification.

Remember

Split or subtract rectangles. Label invented plans as invented. Keep area in square units.

Watch me

Find the area of the invented L-floor: 6 m by 5 m with a 2 m by 2 m corner bite.

  1. The large rectangle is 30 m².
  2. The bite is 4 m².
  3. The floor is 26 m², invented.

Together

A rectangle has area 24 m² and length 8 m. Find the width.

Hint 1: L × W

8 × W = 24.

Hint 2: Width

W = 3 m.

Check

These invert a product, or add invented rooms.

  1. The invented 6 m by 3 m module has area 18 m². A right-triangle annex with legs 6 m and 3 m is attached. What is the annex area, and the combined area?
  2. A rectangle’s area is 35 cm² and one side is 7 cm. Find the other side, and then the perimeter.

Practice

  1. Why does a 2 m doorway usually not change the floor area the way it can change a wall-length question?
  2. Two invented rooms, 4 m by 3 m and 2 m by 5 m: total area, and a sketch.
  3. A right triangle is half of a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle. Find its area.
  4. If 1 m² is 10,000 cm² (because 1 m = 100 cm, and 100 × 100 = 10,000), write 2 m² in square centimeters. (A first look at area-unit shifts.)
  5. Write a two-sentence caption for your plan, with the word invented.

Math circle: why the units are square

20 minutes · a short argument, not a new formula

Learn

A centimeter is a length. A square centimeter is a little square, 1 cm on a side — a covering. That is why the unit is written cm², not cm, and why 15 cm and 15 cm² are different kinds of quantity. If you only multiply 5 × 3 and forget the unit, you have not yet named an area.

Remember

cm is a length unit; cm² is an area unit (a 1-by-1 square). Name the unit, or the number is unfinished.

Watch me

A student writes “the area is 15 cm.” What is missing, and how would you repair it?

  1. 15 cm is a length, not a covering.
  2. The rectangle 5 cm by 3 cm has area 15 cm².
  3. The repair is the square in the unit, and the word area.

Together

Why is 1 m² not the same as 100 cm², even though 1 m = 100 cm?

Hint 1: A square

A 1 m by 1 m square is 100 cm by 100 cm.

Hint 2: The product

100 × 100 = 10,000, so 1 m² = 10,000 cm², not 100 cm².

Check

These are about what the unit means.

  1. Draw 1 cm² and 1 cm, and write a caption for each.
  2. A 1 m by 1 m invented tile: area in m² and in cm².

Practice

  1. Why can two shapes have the same area and different perimeters? Give one pair you can actually draw (for example, a 6 by 2 rectangle and a 4 by 3 rectangle).
  2. Write one sentence a guide could post: length unit, area unit.
  3. What would be wrong with calling Mars’s diameter 6,792 km²?

Spanish · largo, ancho, and a simple plan

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
el largo el LAR-goh the length
el ancho el AHN-choh the width
tiene TYE-neh it has
El cuarto tiene el KWAR-toh TYE-neh the room has
seis metros de largo seys MEH-trohs deh LAR-goh six meters of length / six meters long
tres metros de ancho trehs MEH-trohs deh AHN-choh three meters of width / three meters wide

The pattern. El cuarto tiene seis metros de largo y tres metros de ancho. (Invented classroom measures, if you use the habitat numbers — say they are invented, in English.)

¿Cuánto mide de largo? Mide seis metros.

Remember

tiene … de largo / de ancho. Instruction in English. Classroom numbers may be invented; say so in English.

Watch me

Say: “The room is six meters long and three meters wide,” with tiene.

  1. El cuarto tiene seis metros de largo y tres metros de ancho.
  2. largo is length; ancho is width.
  3. The numbers may be today’s invented 6 m by 3 m; the Spanish is still the present pattern.

Together

Ask: “How long is it?” with de largo.

Hint 1: ¿Cuánto mide…

¿Cuánto mide de largo?

Hint 2: Full

¿Cuánto mide el cuarto de largo?

Check

Use largo and ancho in complete sentences.

  1. Write a tiene-sentence for a 4 m by 2 m invented room, labeled invented in English.
  2. Write ¿Cuánto mide de ancho? and an answer.

Exercises

  1. Hear largo and ancho twice, then use each.
  2. A two-line plan caption in Spanish, plus one English line that says the measures are invented.
  3. Why is *el cuarto mide largo wrong, and what does the pattern need?
  4. Add más largo que to compare two invented rooms.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · altus, and three dimensions named (not SI)

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
altus, alta, altum AL-tus, AL-tah, AL-tum high, tall, or deep (in the sense taught today: height)
cista KIS-tah a box, a chest
cista alta est KIS-tah AL-tah est the box is tall
parvus, parva, parvum PAR-wus, PAR-wah, PAR-wum small

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Three adjectives, still without modern meters. Cista longa, lāta, alta est. Cista parva est, sed nōn alta.

Original sentences: Cista est longa et lāta. Cista nōn est alta. Murus altus est. We describe a box or a wall; we do not write “six meters” in Latin in this lesson.

Remember

altus / alta: high or tall. cista: a box. Adjectives match. No SI units in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Say: “The box is tall.” Then: “The box is not tall.”

  1. Cista alta est. (cista is feminine, so alta.)
  2. Cista nōn est alta.
  3. The same noun, two opposite claims, with matching adjectives.

Together

Say: “The wall is high, but the box is small.”

Hint 1: Match

murus altus; cista parva.

Hint 2: Sentence

Murus altus est, sed cista parva est.

Check

Match altus / alta, and keep sentences original.

  1. Write: “The box is long and wide.”
  2. Correct: *cista altus est.

Exercises

  1. Write a three-adjective description of a box (long, wide, not tall — or another honest combination).
  2. Read the sentences aloud twice.
  3. Draw a tall wall and a small box, labeled in Latin.
  4. Write one new sentence with parva or parvus.
  5. Why are meters left in the English mathematics today?

Notebook: a floor plan, unit squares, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw the invented 6 m by 3 m floor (18 m²) and today’s L-shape (32 cm² if you use the lesson picture), with a split or a bite shown. Add one right triangle and the half-rectangle picture. Then because / but / so: area is covering in square units, but it is not perimeter, so you write cm² or m², not cm or m, for a covering.

Word bank: area, square centimeter, square meter, array, L-shape, right triangle, half, invented, unit.

Remember

Draw and label first. Write cm² or m² for coverings. Complete stems. The page is the work, not a score.

Watch me

A model sentence: I write 18 m², not 18 m, because area is a covering, but 18 m would be a length.

  1. 18 m² is 6 × 3, invented floor.
  2. m is a length unit.
  3. The but keeps the unit honest.

Together

Finish: A right triangle with legs 6 cm and 4 cm has area 12 cm² because ________, so I can also write ________.

Hint 1: Half

It is half of a 6 by 4 rectangle.

Hint 2: The formula

½ × 6 × 4 = 12 cm².

Check

The page should show plans with areas, a triangle, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the rectangle and the L, with areas, and one right triangle with its half-rectangle.
  2. Complete a so-sentence about why 26 cm and 32 cm² are not compared as “bigger” without a job.

Practice

  1. Tick the word bank.
  2. Add a but-sentence about invented habitat numbers.
  3. The page is the work; no points or badges.
