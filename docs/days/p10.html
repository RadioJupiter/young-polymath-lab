Level 2 · Day 10 · Level 2 check

Today's big question: Can you use places to 10,000, regrouping, and both kinds of bar, and keep NASA and invented heights labeled?

For the guide: Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: discs for place value, columns with carries, and the first part-whole and comparison bars. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. The Saturn V height used in this level is 111 meters (363 feet), from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?” Do not replace that figure with an invented classroom height, and do not average it with another official figure. Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes; measure a shadow, not the Sun. If the placement check started the student here, Level 1 greetings and numbers 1–10 may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later bars with larger numbers; today’s bars are a first look.

Level 2 check

45 minutes · alone, no calculator · a check of this level, not a new lesson

Learn

This is a check of skills already taught in Level 2, not a new lesson. Use discs, columns, or a bar when a picture would help. Saturn V: 111 m (363 feet) from the NASA student page. Classroom rockets and shadows stay labeled invented.

Remember

Places to 10,000. 285 + 347 = 632, with carries. Part-whole for a total; comparison for a difference. 1,000 − 264 = 736. Invented rockets: 63 m, 18 m shorter, B = 45 m, stacked 108 m.

Watch me

A model of how to show work. Find 285 + 347.

  1. Ones: 5 + 7 = 12, write 2, carry 1 ten.
  2. Tens: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13, write 3, carry 1 hundred. Hundreds: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6. The sum is 632.

Together

Rocket A is 63 m, B is 18 m shorter (invented). How tall is B?

Hint 1

63 − 18.

Hint 2

45 m, invented.

Check

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

  1. What is 4,036 + 1,000?
  2. Write 4,036 as thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones.

Unit check

Work alone. Use discs, columns, or a bar when it helps. For the Saturn V, use 111 m (363 feet) from the NASA student page taught this level. Invented classroom rockets and shadows stay labeled invented. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: place value to 10,000

  1. Write 4,036 as thousands, hundreds, tens, and ones.
  2. What number is 7 thousands, 2 hundreds, 0 tens, and 5 ones?
  3. What is 4,036 + 1,000? What is 4,036 − 1,000?
  4. How many thousands make 10,000?
  5. Why is 4,036 not the same number as 436?

Part B: addition, and a part-whole bar

  1. Find 285 + 347. Show the carries.
  2. Check 285 + 347 with an estimate.
  3. Find 186 + 157.
  4. Invented (labeled): 63 m and 45 m classroom rockets, stacked. What is the whole? Which bar is this?
  5. Find 475 + 268.

Part C: subtraction, comparison, and this level’s space work

  1. Find 1,000 − 264. Check with an addition.
  2. Invented: Rocket A is 63 m, Rocket B is 18 m shorter. How tall is B? How tall are they stacked?
  3. Invented shadows: 80 cm in the morning, 20 cm near midday, same stick. How much longer is the morning shadow? Why is the midday shadow shorter?
  4. What height does this level use for the Saturn V, and from which page?
  5. Why must a 63 m classroom rocket not replace the Saturn V height?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 3. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

This wrap uses only Level 2 methods. No new operation is begun.

Remember

Places, regrouping, bars, labeled heights and shadows.

Watch me

An invented rocket is 20 m shorter than the Saturn V’s 111 m NASA figure. How tall is the invented rocket, and what must the 20 m and the answer be labeled?

  1. 111 − 20 = 91 m.
  2. 111 m is the NASA student-page figure; 20 m and 91 m are invented classroom numbers, not a new official NASA height.

Together

Find 3,000 − 264, using 1,000 − 264 if it helps.

Hint 1

1,000 − 264 = 736.

Hint 2

3,000 − 264 = 2,736.

Check

A short wrap after the check.

  1. Find 4,036 + 2,000.
  2. Invented shadows 80 cm and 20 cm: how many times as long is the morning shadow, and why is that not a NASA ratio?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 285 + 347 is 632, not 522.
  2. Why is stacking 63 m and 45 m a part-whole job?

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

A good Level 2 argument names the places or the bar, and it keeps NASA figures and invented classroom figures labeled.

Remember

Places, carries, comparison or part-whole, and honest labels.

Watch me

A student says the Saturn V is 63 m “because that was in the rocket problem.” What two jobs were mixed?

  1. 63 m is the invented classroom Rocket A.
  2. The Saturn V is 111 m on the NASA student page. Invented and sourced heights are not swapped.

Together

Write a short argument that 1,000 − 264 = 736, with a check addition.

Hint 1

Count up 36 to 300 and 700 to 1,000.

Hint 2

264 + 736 = 1,000.

Check

A last short argument in this level’s tools.

  1. Why does a 0 belong in 4,036?
  2. Which bar answers “how much shorter?”

Practice

  1. Name one NASA figure and two invented figures from this level.
  2. What may you claim about shadows and the Sun, without inventing an official length?

Spanish · Friday mix: comparison words and 11 to 15

15 minutes · Mix of this level’s Spanish, not a new tense

Learn

This Friday mixes alto, bajo, más, menos, ¿cuánto?, once to quince, por la mañana, and al mediodía, with Level 1 words only as a recap. No new phrase is begun.

Instructions are in English. Hear clips use es-MX. Greetings and numbers 1–10 from Level 1 may be reused, not retaught as a first look. If a student began at Level 2, the guide may first use Level 1 Days 1–2 Spanish for hola and uno–diez, then continue here.

Remember

Comparison words; 11 to 15; morning and midday. Act and point first.

Watch me

Give the English of más alto, quince, and por la mañana.

  1. Taller; fifteen; in the morning.

Together

What is doce in English, and más bajo?

Hint 1

Doce: twelve.

Hint 2

Más bajo: shorter / lower.

Check

A short mix of this level’s Spanish.

  1. Say once to quince, or write them with the English.
  2. Give the English of menos and al mediodía.

Exercises

  1. Hear alto, trece, and bajo. Give the English of each.
  2. Why does this mix not retell the Saturn V height in Spanish?

Latin · Friday mix: umbra, alta, longa, brevis

15 minutes · Mix of this level’s Latin, original captions only

Learn

This Friday mixes umbra, alta, longa, brevis, est, and nōn est. No new noun is begun.

All Latin here is original. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Vowels are pure; c like k; v like English w.

Remember

Umbra; alta; longa; brevis; est and nōn est. Short original captions only.

Watch me

Give a matching caption for a long shadow and for a short shadow.

  1. Umbra longa est. Umbra brevis est.

Together

What is the English of Umbra alta est, as taught?

Hint 1

The shadow is tall, or it is a tall shadow.

Check

A short mix of this level’s Latin.

  1. Give the English of Umbra est and of Alta nōn est.
  2. What does brevis mean?

Exercises

  1. Write two original captions, one with longa and one with brevis.
  2. Why are 80 cm and 111 m not written as Latin official figures?

Notebook: a Level 2 page — places, a bar, and two kinds of height

60 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting · draw first, then label, then write

Learn

Draw three small pictures: (1) 4,036 in a place-value chart, (2) a comparison or part-whole bar for the invented rockets, (3) the Saturn V’s 111 m labeled NASA next to the 63 m invented rocket. Then write from stems.

Word bank: thousands, carry, comparison, whole, invented, NASA, Saturn V, meters.

Stems: 1. 285 + 347 is 632 because … 2. The 63 m rocket is invented, but … 3. The Saturn V is 111 m, so …

End with one NASA fact (111 m / 363 feet) and one invented fact, clearly labeled, and one “what next” question for Level 3.

Remember

Three drawings, then three stems, then one sourced fact and one invented fact, labeled. Do not swap 63 m for 111 m.

Watch me

Finish: “285 + 347 is 632 because …”

  1. A model: “… because I added the ones, carried a ten, and so on, and the columns gave 632.” Other complete finishes that name a carry or the places are accepted.

Together

Finish: “The 63 m rocket is invented, but …”

Hint 1

The Saturn V has a different, sourced height.

Hint 2

A model: “… but the Saturn V is 111 m on the NASA page.”

Check

These check the week’s notebook ideas together.

  1. What NASA height should appear at the end?
  2. Name one invented height or shadow that must stay labeled invented.

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question for Level 3.
