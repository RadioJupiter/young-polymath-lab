Mental math warm-up: undo the operation

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

To find a missing number, undo the operation that was done to it.

Remember

Addition undoes subtraction. Subtraction undoes addition. Division undoes multiplication.

Watch me

n + 9 = 17. What is n?

  1. 9 was added. Undo it by subtracting 9.
  2. n = 17 − 9 = 8. Check: 8 + 9 = 17.

Together

4n = 24. What is n?

Hint 1: The operation

n was multiplied by 4.

Hint 2: The undo

Divide both sides by 4: n = 6. Check: 4 × 6 = 24.

Check

These are undo facts.

  1. n − 6 = 11.
  2. n ÷ 5 = 3.

Practice

  1. n + 15 = 40.
  2. 7n = 35.

Core lesson: a balance, and keeping both sides equal

40 minutes · Method: a pan balance, then doing the same to both sides

Learn

An equation says that two sides are equal. A pan balance is a useful picture of that. When the balance is level, the two pans have the same weight, in this classroom model.

The drawing shows 8 on the left and 5 + n on the right, and the balance is level. So:

n + 5 = 8

All masses in this lesson’s exercises are labeled invented classroom numbers, not a measured lab weight and not a NASA figure.

Keeping both sides equal. If you take 5 from the right pan, you must take 5 from the left pan too, or the balance will tip. In symbols: subtract 5 from both sides.

n + 5 − 5 = 8 − 5, so n = 3. Check: 3 + 5 = 8.

The same habit for multiplication. If 3n = 12, there are three equal n’s on one side, making 12. Divide both sides by 3, and n = 4. Check: 3 × 4 = 12.

Whatever you do to one side, do to the other, so the two sides stay equal.

Remember

An equation means both sides are equal. A level pan balance is a picture of that. Do the same to both sides, then check. Invented masses stay labeled.

Watch me

The balance is level with n + 4 on the left and 11 on the right. Find n.

  1. The equation is n + 4 = 11.
  2. Subtract 4 from both sides: n = 7.
  3. Check: 7 + 4 = 11.

Together

The balance is level with 2n on the left and 10 on the right. Find n.

Hint 1: The equation

2n = 10.

Hint 2: Both sides

Divide both sides by 2: n = 5. Check: 2 × 5 = 10.

Check

Do the same to both sides, then check.

  1. n − 3 = 9. Find n, and say what you did to both sides.
  2. Why does taking 3 from only one pan tip the balance?

Practice

  1. 5n = 30. Find n, and check.
  2. n + 8 = 8 + 15. Find n.
  3. A labeled invented mass of 6 sits on the left. The right holds 2 and n, and the balance is level. Find n.
  4. Why will this lesson not invent a NASA “official balance number”?

Challenge problems: two moves, still a balance

25 minutes · two-step equations, still with a check

Learn

Some equations need two moves, always the same to both sides. Invented masses stay labeled.

Remember

Do the same to both sides, one move at a time, then check.

Watch me

2n + 5 = 17. Find n.

  1. Subtract 5 from both sides: 2n = 12.
  2. Divide both sides by 2: n = 6.
  3. Check: 2 × 6 + 5 = 17.

Together

3n − 4 = 11. Find n.

Hint 1: First move

Add 4 to both sides: 3n = 15.

Hint 2: Second move

Divide both sides by 3: n = 5. Check: 3 × 5 − 4 = 11.

Check

Two moves, then a check.

  1. 4n + 6 = 26. Find n.
  2. n is on both sides: n + 7 = 2n + 1. Find n.

Practice

  1. A labeled invented left pan holds n + 9. The right pan holds 20, and the balance is level. Find n.
  2. Why is “I moved 5 from one side only, and then divided” a miss?
  3. 2(n + 3) = 14. Find n. (2(n + 3) means two lots of n + 3.)

Math circle: why both sides change together

20 minutes · a short argument, then a check of the reason

Learn

Why must we do the same thing to both sides of an equation?

Remember

An equation says the two sides are equal. A move that changes only one side makes that sentence untrue.

Watch me

A friend has n + 6 = 10, takes 6 from the left only, and writes n = 10. What would you say?

  1. The left became n, but the right was left as 10, so the two sides are no longer the same.
  2. The true move is to subtract 6 from both sides: n = 4.
  3. Check: 4 + 6 = 10, not 10 + nothing.

Together

How does the pan-balance picture help you see the same reason?

Hint 1: A level balance

A level balance is a picture of two sides that match.

Hint 2: One pan

Taking a mass from one pan only tips the balance, so the picture of “equal” is gone.

Check

These check the reason.

  1. In 5n = 20, why do we divide both sides, not only the side that has n?
  2. Give one reason a check (putting n back) catches a one-sided move.

Practice

  1. n − 4 = 9. What happens if you add 4 to the left only?
  2. This argument is not a score or a badge.

Spanish · sube, baja, está igual, and el platillo

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
sube SOO-beh goes up
baja BAH-hah goes down
está igual es-TAH ee-GWAHL is level / is the same
el platillo el plah-TEE-yoh the pan (of a balance)

Today’s words name going up, going down, being level, and a pan of a balance. Instruction stays in English. igual is already known; today it is used in está igual. Balance arithmetic stays in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: El platillo sube. El platillo baja. La balanza está igual. No sube.

For the guide: tip a drawn balance, then bring it level, as you say the words. Present tense only. la balanza is yesterday’s word, reused, not a new first look.

Remember

sube; baja; está igual; el platillo. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No equations in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “The pan goes up.”

  1. El platillo sube.
  2. El platillo is the pan. sube is goes up.

Together

How do you say “The balance is level”?

Hint 1: Level

está igual

Hint 2: The sentence

La balanza está igual.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “The pan goes down.”
  2. Write: “It does not go up.”

Exercises

  1. Hear sube, baja, and platillo, then use each.
  2. Why does the Spanish not subtract from both sides?
  3. A pointing retell: up, down, level, a pan.
  4. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.
  5. Correct: *La platillo, if someone used the wrong article.

Latin · lībra and aequa

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
lībra LEE-brah a pair of scales / a balance (nominative, feminine)
Lībra aequa est LEE-brah EYE-kwah est The balance is level
Lībra nōn aequa est LEE-brah nohn EYE-kwah est The balance is not level
aequa EYE-kwah level / even (feminine, to match lībra)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Lībra aequa est. Lībra nōn aequa est. Lībra magna est. Pondus in lībrā est.

lībra is feminine, so aequa and magna match. pondus, from yesterday, can sit in a short caption with in lībrā. Balance arithmetic stays in the English mathematics.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to a level balance, then to a tipped one. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

lībra; Lībra aequa est; Lībra nōn aequa est; aequa. Instruction in English. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. No equations in the Latin.

Watch me

Read: “The balance is level.”

  1. Lībra aequa est.
  2. lībra is the balance. aequa matches, because lībra is feminine.

Together

How do you say “The balance is not level”?

Hint 1: Not

Hint 2: The sentence

Lībra nōn aequa est.

Check

These use only today’s captions.

  1. Write: “The balance is large.”
  2. Write a caption that uses pondus with in lībrā.

Exercises

Notebook: a balance drawing, and a because-sentence

about 1 hour · a level pan balance, then because / but / so

Learn

Draw a level pan balance for an equation, then write a because / but / so sentence about doing the same to both sides.

Remember

The drawing shows two sides that match. The stems explain the habit: a one-sided move would tip the picture.

Watch me

For n + 4 = 11, draw the balance, then start: I change both sides because ________.

  1. The because restates that the two sides have to stay equal.
  2. A sound because: …the equation says the two sides match…

Together

Finish: I change both sides because ________, but I must not change one side only, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…taking a mass from one pan only would tip the balance…

Hint 2: The so

…I will do the same move to both sides, then check.

Check

The page should show the drawing and complete stems.

  1. Draw a level balance for n + 4 = 11, and write n = 7 with a check.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about both sides.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: balance, equal, both sides, check, invented.
  2. The page is a record, not a score.
  3. File the page. It is not a reward.
  4. A note that invented masses stay labeled invented.
