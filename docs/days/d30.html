Mental math warm-up: mixed, Level 9

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

This warm-up mixes the mental work of the level: tenths and hundredths, a comparison, a short sum, and a count through zero. Use the facts as taught.

Remember

10 tenths = 1. Compare in the same place. Line up the point when you add. Count through 0 to find a rise on a number line.

Watch me

Which is greater, 0.4 or 0.37?

  1. 0.4 is 40 hundredths.
  2. 0.37 is 37 hundredths.
  3. 0.4 is greater.

Together

0.8 + 0.5, and then count up 5 from −3.

Hint 1: The sum

8 tenths + 5 tenths = 13 tenths = 1.3.

Hint 2: The count

From −3: −2, −1, 0, 1, 2. The landing point is 2.

Check

These mix the level’s mental facts.

  1. Write 9 hundredths as a decimal.
  2. Which is colder, −20 or −65?

Practice

  1. 0.6 + 0.6.
  2. 0.04 × 10.

Level 9 check

35–40 minutes · end-of-level mastery check · four out of five or better on each part; not a score or a reward

Learn

This sitting gathers the level: tenths and hundredths and decimal place value; comparing decimals and equivalent amounts (0.4 = 0.40), with a short caution about a measurement’s written precision; adding and subtracting by lining up the point; negative numbers on a number line; and sourced NASA temperatures, with means and ranges labeled, and with the fact-sheet warning that a Mercury or Moon mean is not the temperature of a given region.

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

Remember

Name the place of each digit. Line up the point. Count through 0 for a temperature difference. Label means, ranges, and invented classroom numbers. Four out of five or better on each part is mastery; this is not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Watch me

A short layout example, not one of the unit-check items: 0.4 + 0.25.

  1. Write 0.4 as 0.40 if it helps, and line up the points.
  2. 40 hundredths + 25 hundredths = 65 hundredths.
  3. The sum is 0.65. After a Together item for layout, do the Unit check alone.

Together

The Together item is practice for layout. After it, do the Unit check alone. What will you write beside a planet temperature, besides the number?

Hint 1: The unit

°C, and whether the figure is a fact-sheet mean or a NASA Science range.

Hint 2: Invented numbers

If a freezer or a classroom rod is invented, it stays labeled invented.

Check

The Together item is only layout practice. The Unit check is the mastery sitting.

  1. Write, as a heading in your book: Part A place value and comparing, Part B adding and subtracting decimals, Part C negative numbers and temperatures. Then begin Part A when the guide says.
  2. When you finish a part, do not add extra credit items; the five questions are the part.

Unit check

Work alone. Use a place-value chart or a number line when it helps. For planet temperatures, use the figures taught this unit, and keep means, ranges, and invented classroom numbers labeled. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: place value and comparing

  1. Write 0.07 and 0.70 in ones, tenths, and hundredths, and say which is greater.
  2. Write 3 ones, 2 tenths, and 5 hundredths as a decimal.
  3. Write 0.4 as hundredths, and as a fraction of a whole.
  4. Order from least to greatest: 0.8, 0.08, 0.75.
  5. Why is 0.40 the same amount as 0.4, and what can a written 0.40 still mark, if the number is a measurement?

Part B: adding and subtracting decimals

  1. 0.6 + 0.25.
  2. 1.36 + 0.4.
  3. 2.05 − 0.7.
  4. 3.14 − 0.04. (Use 3.14 as a decimal to compute with; it stays a labeled approximation of π, not a changed value of π.)
  5. An invented rod is 1.25 m, and a second invented rod is 0.8 m. What is the total length? Label the total invented.

Part C: negative numbers and temperatures

  1. Which is colder, the Moon’s fact-sheet mean (−20°C) or Mars’s fact-sheet mean (−65°C)?
  2. Sketch a Celsius number line and mark −65, 0, and 15. Label −65 and 15 as fact-sheet means for Mars and Earth.
  3. How many degrees warmer is Earth’s mean (15°C) than Mars’s mean (−65°C)?
  4. NASA’s fact-sheet mean for Mercury is 167°C. NASA Science gives a sunlit high of 430°C and a night-side low of −180°C. Why is 167°C not “the temperature of a place on Mercury”?
  5. How many degrees hotter is Venus’s fact-sheet mean (464°C) than Mercury’s fact-sheet mean (167°C)?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Level 10. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a reward.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

These items mix the level, still with labeled means and ranges and labeled invented lengths. They are extra thinking, not extra credit and not a second score.

Remember

Same skills as the check. Labels stay honest. This is not a second mastery sitting.

Watch me

An invented ribbon is 0.4 m, and a second invented ribbon is 0.35 m. What is the total, and which fact-sheet mean sits 80 degrees below Earth’s 15°C?

  1. 0.40 + 0.35 = 0.75 m, invented.
  2. 80 degrees below 15°C is −65°C, which is Mars’s fact-sheet mean.
  3. The ribbon sum is invented; the −65°C is a sourced mean.

Together

Order 0.99, 1.05, and 0.9, then say how many degrees warmer 15°C is than −20°C.

Hint 1: Order

0.9, 0.99, 1.05.

Hint 2: The rise

15 − (−20) = 35 degrees, Earth mean against Moon mean.

Check

These mix the level and stay labeled.

  1. 1.02 − 0.75.
  2. Which is greater, −180 or −20, and which is colder?

Practice

  1. Write 13 tenths as a decimal.
  2. 0.08 + 0.93.
  3. How many degrees is the swing from −180°C to 430°C (Mercury, NASA Science range figures)?
  4. Why is “Mars is 6,792.00 km across” not a more careful NASA size from this level?
  5. This wrap is not a second score or a badge.

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · a short reason, then a check

Learn

The level has two honesty habits that look alike: we do not invent decimal precision we did not measure, and we do not treat a mean as the temperature of a given place when the source says it is not. Both habits are about labeling what a number is.

Remember

Equal amounts stay equal. A trailing zero can still mark a finer reading, but it cannot invent a measurement. A sourced mean and a sourced range stay labeled. This circle is not a second score.

Watch me

Give one pair of figures that are the same amount, and one pair that must not be mixed as if they were the same kind of temperature.

  1. Same amount: 0.4 and 0.40.
  2. Different kinds: Mercury’s fact-sheet mean 167°C and a NASA Science sunlit high of 430°C (or Venus’s mean 464°C and a Science surface figure of about 467°C).
  3. The first pair may be written differently and still be equal. The second pair can both be honest and still not be interchangeable.

Together

Finish: I will not write extra zeros on a NASA diameter, because ____, but I will still write 0.40 when ____, so the zero is a record, not a decoration and not a new measurement.

Hint 1: The diameter

…we did not measure a hundredth of a kilometer…

Hint 2: The 0.40

…the amount is 4 tenths, or when a reading was taken to the nearest hundredth…

Check

These close the argument.

  1. Name one sourced mean from this unit and one invented classroom number.
  2. Why is a mean not automatically “the temperature there”?

Practice

  1. Are 0.4 and 0.40 the same point on a number line?
  2. What must stay labeled when we compute 3.14 − 0.04?
  3. Why did we line up the decimal point, not the last digits?
  4. Venus’s mean is hotter than Mercury’s mean, from the same fact sheet. What comparison did we not invent a cause for, beyond that fact?
  5. This argument is not a badge or a reward.

Spanish · A short measure-and-weather story

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only · review of Days 26–29

Learn

This is a short review story using this unit’s phrases, still in the present tense. Instruction stays in English. Decimal arithmetic and NASA figures stay in the English mathematics.

The story, to point to and retell: Hay una regla. La regla mide. Es el primero. Pone la caja. Quita la caja. Hace mucho frío. Hay hielo. Está bajo cero.

Words you may Hear again: la regla, primero, pone, quita, hace mucho frío, el hielo, bajo cero.

For the guide: the student retells while pointing. No temperature numbers in the Spanish. This is not a second mastery score.

Remember

la regla; mide; primero; pone; quita; hace mucho frío; el hielo; bajo cero. Present tense only. Hear for Spanish words.

Watch me

Retell the first three sentences, pointing.

  1. Hay una regla. There is a ruler.
  2. La regla mide. The ruler measures.
  3. Es el primero. It is the first.

Together

How do you say “There is ice,” and “It is below zero”?

Hint 1: Ice

Hay hielo.

Hint 2: Below zero

Está bajo cero.

Check

These use only this unit’s phrases.

  1. Write: “He/she puts the box.”
  2. Write: “It is very cold.”

Exercises

  1. Retell the whole eight-sentence story, pointing.
  2. Hear regla, quita, and hielo, then use each.
  3. Why does the story never say −65 or 0.25 in Spanish?
  4. Correct: *Hace helado or *Pone el caja, if they appear.
  5. Copy two model sentences into the notebook.

Latin · A short number-and-water story

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English. Review of Days 26–29.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
Numerus parvus est NOO-meh-rus PAR-wus est The number is small
Punctum parvum est PUNK-tum PAR-wum est The point is small
Prīmus est PREE-mus est He/it is first
Tabula et rēgula adsunt TAH-boo-lah et RAY-goo-lah AHD-sunt The tablet and the ruler are here
Aqua frīgida est AH-kwah FREE-gih-dah est The water is cold

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

A short review story, original sentences: Numerus parvus est. Punctum parvum est. Prīmus est. Tabula et rēgula adsunt. Aqua frīgida est. Aqua nōn calida est.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. No decimals, no degrees, and no SI units in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. This is not a second mastery score.

Remember

numerus parvus; punctum parvum; prīmus; tabula et rēgula adsunt; aqua frīgida. Adjectives match. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read: Tabula et rēgula adsunt. Why adsunt?

  1. There are two things.
  2. adsunt is the form for more than one.
  3. adest would be used for one thing only.

Together

Say: “The water is cold.”

Hint 1: The noun

aqua, feminine.

Hint 2: The sentence

Aqua frīgida est.

Check

These use only this unit’s patterns.

  1. Write: “The point is small.”
  2. Write: “The water is not warm.”

Exercises

  1. Read the six-sentence story aloud.
  2. Correct: *punctum parvus est and *aqua frīgidus est.
  3. Draw a small point, a ruler, and cold water, labeled in Latin.
  4. Why are 0.4, 1.76, and −65°C left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy two model sentences.

Notebook: a Level 9 page — a decimal, a temperature line, and a short explanation

60 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw one place-value chart (for example 1.36 + 0.40 = 1.76) and one Celsius number line with Mars −65°C, 0, and Earth 15°C marked as fact-sheet means. Then complete because / but / so stems that gather the level: amounts and places, but a trailing zero or a mean can still be a kind of record, so I will label what each number is, and I will not invent precision or a daily Mars weather report.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. This page is a record, not a second score or a badge.

Watch me

A model sentence: 0.4 and 0.40 are the same amount because 4 tenths is 40 hundredths, but I will not write 6,792.00 km as a new NASA size, so a zero is a record of place or of a reading, not a new measurement.

  1. The because restates equivalence.
  2. The but refuses invented precision on a sourced size.
  3. The so states the habit for the whole level.

Together

Finish: Earth’s mean is 80 degrees warmer than Mars’s mean, because ________, but a mean is not automatically “the temperature there,” so ________.

Hint 1: The count

…it is 65 degrees up to 0 and 15 degrees more…

Hint 2: The so

…I will keep means and ranges labeled, and I will not invent a Tuesday temperature for Mars.

Check

The page should show the chart, the line, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the lined-up sum (or a clear place-value chart) and the temperature line with the two means labeled.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) that gathers place value, honest labels, or means versus ranges.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: tenth, hundredth, equivalent, mean, range, invented, point, negative.
  2. Add one invented classroom length, labeled invented, if it helps the page.
  3. The page is a record of the level, not a badge.
  4. A note that the unit check is the mastery decision, and this page is not a second score.
  5. File the page. It is not a reward.
