Mental math warm-up: equal groups, and a comparison

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by equal parts. Today’s warm-up practices equal groups you already know, so the bar model for a ratio is ready.

Remember

Equal groups can be counted. 2 + 3 = 5 parts in a 2 : 3 comparison. Dividing both numbers by the same number keeps the comparison the same, which we will teach in the core.

Watch me

A box holds 2 red cubes and 3 blue cubes, all the same size. How many cubes is that, and how many of the five equal parts are red?

  1. 2 + 3 = 5 cubes in all.
  2. The five cubes are five equal parts.
  3. 2 of the 5 parts are red.

Together

Share 12 counters into 2 equal groups. How many in each group, and how many in 3 of those groups?

Hint 1: Two groups

12 ÷ 2 = 6 in each group.

Hint 2: Three groups

3 × 6 = 18.

Check

These use only equal groups.

  1. How many equal parts are there in a comparison of 4 and 5?
  2. Share 20 into 4 equal groups. How many in one group?

Practice

  1. A 2-to-3 comparison has how many parts in all?
  2. Share 18 into 3 equal groups. How many in two of those groups?

Core lesson: ratio as a comparison

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (two kinds of cubes in equal groups), pictorial (bars with equal parts), then the written ratio a:b

Learn

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind by equal parts. We write the ratio of 2 to 3 as 2 : 3, and we read it “2 to 3.” The pictured bars use the same part size: the first bar is 2 parts long, the second is 3 parts long. The order matters. 2 : 3 is not the same comparison as 3 : 2.

A bar model. Draw equal parts, as you already do for fractions and for part-whole problems. For 2 : 3, draw a bar of 2 parts and a bar of 3 parts, all parts the same size. Together there are 5 parts. If each part stands for 4 cubes, the first quantity is 8 cubes and the second is 12 cubes, and the ratio is still 2 : 3.

Simplifying a ratio. If both numbers in a ratio have a common factor, we can divide both by that factor. 6 : 9 divides by 3, and becomes 2 : 3. The comparison is the same, just written with smaller equal parts. We only divide (or multiply) both sides by the same number. We do not add the same number to both sides: 6 : 9 is not 7 : 10.

An invented classroom mix. “2 cups of blue powder to 3 cups of yellow powder” is an invented classroom mix, and it stays labeled invented. It is not a NASA fuel recipe. We will not invent a real rocket’s mixture and call it a sourced fact.

A sourced size comparison. The fact-sheet diameters this course already uses are Earth 12,756 km and the Moon 3,475 km. Their ratio is 12,756 : 3,475. That does not simplify to a small whole-number pair in a useful way, and it is not exactly 4 : 1: 4 × 3,475 = 13,900, which is 1,144 km more than 12,756. So Earth is a little less than 4 times as wide as the Moon. We keep the sourced figures and the words “a little less than,” rather than forcing a neat 4 : 1.

You already met a similar honesty with Mars: 6,792 : 12,756 is a little more than 1 : 2, because 2 × 6,792 = 13,584, which is more than 12,756.

Remember

A ratio a : b compares two quantities of the same kind, in that order, by equal parts. 2 : 3 is not 3 : 2. Together, 2 : 3 has 5 parts. Divide or multiply both sides by the same number to write an equivalent ratio. Invented mixes stay labeled. Earth 12,756 km to the Moon 3,475 km is a little less than 4 : 1, not exactly 4 : 1.

Watch me

Write the ratio of 8 green tiles to 12 yellow tiles in simplest form, and say how many parts there are in all.

  1. The ratio is 8 : 12, in that order.
  2. Both 8 and 12 divide by 4, so 8 : 12 = 2 : 3.
  3. 2 + 3 = 5 parts in all. The tiles are an invented classroom set, labeled invented.

Together

A 2 : 3 mix uses 5 equal parts. If there are 20 cups in all, how many cups of each kind are there?

Hint 1: One part

20 ÷ 5 = 4 cups in each part.

Hint 2: The two kinds

2 parts are 8 cups; 3 parts are 12 cups. The mix is invented, so the answer stays labeled invented.

Check

These use only today’s ratios.

  1. Write the ratio of 3 to 5, and the ratio of 5 to 3. Are they the same comparison?
  2. Simplify 10 : 15.

Practice

  1. Simplify 4 : 10.
  2. A 1 : 4 bar has 5 parts. If each part is 3 cm, how long is each bar? (Invented classroom lengths, labeled invented.)
  3. Earth’s fact-sheet diameter is 12,756 km and the Moon’s is 3,475 km. Why is “exactly 4 : 1” a little high?
  4. A friend writes 6 : 9 = 7 : 10 because “I added 1 to both.” What is the sound way to simplify 6 : 9?
  5. An invented classroom fuel is mixed 2 : 3. If the first amount is 6 liters, what is the second amount? Label the mix invented.

Challenge problems: a missing part, and a size that stays sourced

20 minutes · after the core, or later if the core took the full sitting

Learn

These items use today’s ratios. Invented mixes stay labeled. A NASA diameter stays the figure this course already uses; we will not invent a neater ratio and call it official.

Remember

Same ratio method as the core. Labels stay honest. Do not force 4 : 1 on Earth and the Moon.

Watch me

A 3 : 5 comparison uses 8 equal parts. If the first quantity is 12, what is the second, and what is the total?

  1. 3 parts are 12, so one part is 4.
  2. 5 parts are 20; the total is 8 × 4 = 32.
  3. If the quantities are an invented classroom set, the 12, 20, and 32 stay labeled invented.

Together

Mars’s fact-sheet diameter is about 6,792 km, and Earth’s is about 12,756 km. Why is “exactly 1 : 2” a little low?

Hint 1: Double Mars

2 × 6,792 = 13,584.

Hint 2: Compare

13,584 is more than 12,756, so Mars is a little more than half as wide as Earth. You already met this in Level 7.

Check

These stay with ratios and honest labels.

  1. Simplify 9 : 12.
  2. A 2 : 7 mix has 18 in the first amount. What is the second amount? (Invented, labeled invented.)

Practice

  1. Write 14 : 21 in simplest form, and say how many parts in all.
  2. Why will this course not call an invented 2 : 3 powder a “NASA rocket fuel”?
  3. 3 : 3 simplifies to what, and what does that comparison mean?
  4. The Moon is 3,475 km across. Four times that is 13,900 km. How much larger is that than Earth’s 12,756 km?
  5. A comparison of 2 meters to 5 meters is 2 : 5. Why is a comparison of 2 meters to 5 minutes not a ratio of the same kind?

Math circle: why both sides of a ratio change together

20 minutes · a short reason, then a check

Learn

A ratio is a comparison by equal parts. If we change only one side, the parts are no longer equal in the same way, and the comparison changes. That is why we multiply or divide both sides by the same number, and why adding 1 to both sides is not a simplifying step.

Remember

Equivalent ratios come from scaling both parts together. Adding the same number to both sides does not keep the ratio. 2 : 3 is not 3 : 2.

Watch me

Why is 2 : 3 the same comparison as 4 : 6, but not the same as 3 : 4?

  1. 4 : 6 is 2 : 3 with each part doubled, so the equal parts are the same comparison.
  2. 3 : 4 is a different pair of parts: the first bar would be 3 parts and the second 4 parts.
  3. Doubling both sides keeps the comparison. Changing the parts to 3 and 4 does not.

Together

A friend says 8 : 12 = 9 : 13 because “I added 1 to both.” What would you say?

Hint 1: The parts

8 : 12 is 2 : 3 after dividing by 4. The parts stayed equal.

Hint 2: The add

Adding 1 to both sides makes 9 : 13, which is not 2 : 3. The extra 1 is not an equal part of the original comparison.

Check

These check the reason.

  1. Name one way to write a ratio equivalent to 1 : 4.
  2. Why is 5 : 2 not the same comparison as 2 : 5?

Practice

  1. Give one reason 6 : 9 = 2 : 3.
  2. If we only doubled the first number in 2 : 3, we would write 4 : 3. Why is that a different comparison?
  3. Earth : Moon as 12,756 : 3,475 is a little less than 4 : 1. Why is forcing 4 : 1 not the same as simplifying?
  4. What would go wrong if we treated a ratio as “just two numbers to add”?
  5. This argument is not a score or a badge.

Spanish · la parte, el grupo, igual, and cada

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la parte lah PAR-teh the part
el grupo el GROO-poh the group
igual ee-GWAHL equal / the same
cada KAH-dah each

Today’s words name a part, a group, equal, and each. Instruction stays in English. Ratio arithmetic and the NASA diameters stay in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: Hay una parte. Hay un grupo. La parte es igual. Cada parte es igual.

For the guide: hold two equal blocks, then a small group of cubes, as you say the words. This is present-tense description, not a ratio lesson in Spanish.

Remember

la parte; el grupo; igual; cada. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No ratio arithmetic in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “There is a part.”

  1. Hay una parte.
  2. Hay introduces it. Una parte is the part.
  3. This is a present-tense pattern, not a past narrative.

Together

How do you say “each,” and “The part is equal”?

Hint 1: Each

cada

Hint 2: The sentence

La parte es igual.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “There is a group.”
  2. Write: “Each part is equal.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *El parte es igual, if someone used the wrong article.
  2. Hear parte, grupo, and igual, then use each.
  3. Why is igual not a licence to do 2 : 3 in Spanish?
  4. A pointing retell: a part, a group, equal, each.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · pars and tōtum

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
pars pars a part (nominative, feminine)
Pars parva est pars PAR-wah est The part is small
tōtum TOH-tum the whole (nominative, neuter)
Tōtum magnum est TOH-tum MAG-num est The whole is large

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Pars parva est. Tōtum magnum est. Pars nōn parva est. Tōtum nōn magnum est.

pars is feminine, so parva matches. tōtum is neuter, so magnum matches. Ratio arithmetic and the NASA figures stay in the English mathematics; no scale numbers and no percent signs in the Latin.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to one part of a drawing, then to the whole drawing. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

pars; Pars parva est; tōtum; Tōtum magnum est. Adjectives match (parva / magnum). No ratios in the Latin. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Watch me

Read: Tōtum magnum est. Why magnum, not *magnus?

  1. tōtum is neuter.
  2. The adjective matches: magnum.
  3. The sentence says the whole is large, not that a part is large.

Together

Say: “The part is small.”

Hint 1: The noun

pars, feminine.

Hint 2: The sentence

Pars parva est.

Check

These use only today’s patterns.

  1. Write: “The part is not small.”
  2. Write: “The whole is not large.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *pars parvus est and *tōtum magnus est.
  2. Hear pars and tōtum, then use each in a sentence.
  3. Draw a small part and a large whole, labeled in Latin.
  4. Why are 2 : 3 and 12,756 km left in the English mathematics?
  5. Copy one model sentence.

Notebook: a ratio bar, and a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw a 2 : 3 bar (five equal parts, two for the first quantity and three for the second). Label one invented classroom mix as invented. Then complete a because / but / so stem that keeps Earth and the Moon from being forced into exactly 4 : 1.

Remember

Draw and label first. Complete stems. Invented mixes stay labeled. This page is a record, not a score or a badge.

Watch me

A model sentence: 2 : 3 has five equal parts because 2 + 3 = 5, but 12,756 : 3,475 is not exactly 4 : 1, so I will say “a little less than 4 to 1” and keep the fact-sheet diameters.

  1. The because restates the equal parts.
  2. The but refuses a forced official ratio.
  3. The so states the habit for sourced sizes.

Together

Finish: I can mix an invented 2 : 3 powder because ________, but I will not call it NASA fuel, so ________.

Hint 1: The because

…the mix was invented for this lesson…

Hint 2: The so

…I will keep invented mixes labeled invented.

Check

The page should show the bar and complete stems.

  1. Draw the 2 : 3 bar with equal parts and a clear 2 : 3 label.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about invented mixes or about Earth and the Moon.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: ratio, part, equal, invented, diameter, simplify.
  2. Add the simplified form of one classroom ratio, if it helps the page.
  3. The page is a record, not a score.
  4. File the page. It is not a reward.
  5. A note that the NASA diameters stay sourced.
