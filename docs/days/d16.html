Mental math warm-up: tens and hundreds of centimeters

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

These are place-value shifts, not a new kind of arithmetic. 100 cm = 1 m, so 300 cm is 3 m, and 3 m 40 cm is 340 cm. Say “3 meters, because 300 centimeters is 3 groups of 100.”

Remember

100 cm = 1 m. 10 mm = 1 cm. Shifting by 100 or by 10 is a place-value change, not a new operation.

Watch me

How many centimeters is 2 m 50 cm? How many meters is 400 cm?

  1. 2 m is 200 cm, plus 50 cm is 250 cm.
  2. 400 cm is 4 groups of 100 cm, so 4 m.
  3. Both answers are place-value shifts of the same length.

Together

How many centimeters is 5 m?

Hint 1: One meter

1 m = 100 cm.

Hint 2: Five

5 × 100 = 500 cm.

Check

Say the answer, then the reason.

  1. 3 m 20 cm in centimeters.
  2. 700 cm in meters.

Practice

  1. 6 m in centimeters.
  2. 1 m 5 cm in centimeters.
  3. 90 cm: is that more or less than 1 m, and by how much?
  4. 2,000 mm in meters (you may wait until the core lesson if millimeters are still new — or use 1,000 mm = 1 m if you already know it).
  5. 250 cm + 50 cm, in meters.

Core lesson: meters, centimeters, and the size of Mars

35–40 minutes · Method: concrete (a meter stick or a 10 cm strip), pictorial (a place-value shift), then the written conversion · NASA figures, honestly rounded

Learn

The meter is the SI unit of length. This course uses the US spelling meter. (The BIPM’s international spelling is metre.) 1 meter (m) = 100 centimeters (cm) = 1,000 millimeters (mm). 1 kilometer (km) = 1,000 m.

A conversion is a place-value shift. To change meters to centimeters, the digits move two places toward the smaller unit (×100). To change kilometers to meters, they move three places (×1,000). You are not inventing a new kind of multiplying; you are renaming the same length.

A sourced size. The NASA Mars fact sheet gives Mars’s equatorial radius as 3,396.2 km, so the equatorial diameter is 2 × 3,396.2 = 6,792.4 km, which we will say as about 6,792 km. Earth’s equatorial diameter, from the same sheet (radius 6,378.1 km), is about 12,756 km. The ratio 6,792.4 ÷ 12,756.2 is about 0.532, a little more than half. “Mars is about half as wide as Earth” is a fair first sentence if you also say “a little more than half.”

A friendlier rounding, about 6,800 km, is to the nearest hundred kilometers and must be labeled as rounded. We will compute with 6,792 km when we want the nearer figure, and we will not invent a circumference (that waits for a later level, when the circle is taught).

A sol, already in the sources, is the Martian solar day: about 24 hours 39 minutes 35 seconds. That is a time, not a length; it belongs on a mission clock, not on today’s meter stick.

Remember

1 km = 1,000 m; 1 m = 100 cm = 1,000 mm. Conversions are place-value shifts. Mars’s equatorial diameter is about 6,792 km (NASA fact sheet: 2 × 3,396.2). About 6,800 km is a rounding to the nearest hundred and must be labeled. Mars is a little more than half as wide as Earth (ratio about 0.532). No circumference today.

Watch me

Change 3 km to meters, and 3 m to centimeters. Then say which fact, from the NASA sheet, is Mars’s equatorial diameter in kilometers as we are using it.

  1. 3 km = 3 × 1,000 m = 3,000 m (three places).
  2. 3 m = 3 × 100 cm = 300 cm (two places).
  3. About 6,792 km (from 2 × 3,396.2 km). “About 6,800 km” would be a labeled rounding, not the nearer figure.

Together

How many meters is 6,792 km? (You may write the number with zeros rather than computing a new kind of product.)

Hint 1: The shift

1 km = 1,000 m, so the digits move three places.

Hint 2: The number

6,792 km = 6,792,000 m.

Check

These use only the metric shifts and the NASA diameter, honestly rounded.

  1. Write 4.5 m in centimeters, and 250 cm in meters.
  2. Earth’s equatorial diameter is about 12,756 km. Is “Mars is exactly half as wide as Earth” true, a little high, or a little low, using 6,792 km for Mars? Show a short comparison.

Practice

  1. 12 mm in centimeters, and 3 cm in millimeters.
  2. 2 km 300 m in meters.
  3. A classroom tape is 2 m 5 cm. Write that length in centimeters.
  4. Why does this lesson refuse to find the distance around Mars?
  5. Write one careful sentence about Mars’s width, using either 6,792 km or a rounding that you label.

Challenge problems: mixed units, and a comparison with Earth

20–25 minutes · the same shifts, with two units in one length

Learn

A length can be written in mixed units (3 m 40 cm) or in one unit (340 cm). They name the same length. To add 3 m 40 cm and 2 m 75 cm, you may rename everything as centimeters (340 + 275 = 615 cm = 6 m 15 cm), or add meters and centimeters and then regroup 115 cm as 1 m 15 cm.

Remember

Mixed units name one length. Regroup 100 cm as 1 m. Keep NASA comparisons labeled, and do not invent a new Mars figure.

Watch me

Add 3 m 40 cm and 2 m 75 cm, and write the sum in meters and centimeters.

  1. 40 cm + 75 cm = 115 cm, which is 1 m 15 cm.
  2. 3 m + 2 m + 1 m = 6 m, plus 15 cm.
  3. The sum is 6 m 15 cm (or 615 cm).

Together

Which is longer: 1.2 m or 105 cm? By how much, in centimeters?

Hint 1: One unit

1.2 m = 120 cm.

Hint 2: Compare

120 cm is 15 cm longer than 105 cm.

Check

These stay with renaming and comparison.

  1. Subtract 1 m 80 cm from 3 m 10 cm.
  2. A model rover path in a course problem (invented, labeled) is 2 km 500 m. Write it in meters, then say how that invented path compares with Mars’s real diameter (it should be tiny).

Practice

  1. 4 m 90 cm + 30 cm, in meters and centimeters.
  2. Order from shortest to longest: 2,000 mm, 1.5 m, 180 cm. (Use one unit.)
  3. Half of 6,792 km is how many kilometers? (That is a number fact, not a claim that a “half-Mars” exists.)
  4. Why must “about 6,800 km” be labeled as rounded if you use it in a sentence with 12,756 km?
  5. Write 7,000 mm in meters.

Math circle: same length, different names

20 minutes · a short argument, not a new unit

Learn

Two names can be the same length: 2 m and 200 cm, or 1 km and 1,000 m. A useful circle question: How do you know they are the same, without a new kind of tool? The reason is the definition (the place-value shift), not a measurement of Mars. If someone says 2 m is longer than 200 cm “because meters are bigger,” that confuses the size of the unit with the size of the length. A big unit can name a short length (0.5 m) or a long one (5,000 m).

Remember

The size of the unit is not the same as the size of the length. 2 m = 200 cm, even though a meter is a larger unit than a centimeter.

Watch me

A student says, ‘200 cm must be shorter than 2 m because centimeters are small.’ What is the mix-up, and what would you say?

  1. The student has mixed up the size of the unit with the size of the length.
  2. 200 cm is exactly 2 m, by the definition 1 m = 100 cm.
  3. A small unit can still add up to a large length.

Together

Which is a length, and which is a unit: 6,792 km, and “the kilometer”?

Hint 1: A length

6,792 km is a length (Mars’s equatorial diameter, as we are using it).

Hint 2: A unit

The kilometer is a unit: a way of naming lengths.

Check

These are about units versus lengths, not new conversions.

  1. Give a pair of names for the same length, one in meters and one in centimeters.
  2. Why is “kilometers are always longer than meters” a confused sentence, and how would you repair it?

Practice

  1. Can a length of 80 cm be longer than a length of 0.5 m? Show the names in one unit.
  2. If a drawing of Mars is 6.8 cm across, and the card says “1 cm stands for 1,000 km,” what diameter is being modeled, and is that a rounding?
  3. Write one sentence that a guide could post: unit, not length / length, not unit.

Spanish · ¿Cuánto mide? Meters and centimeters

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
el metro el MEH-troh the meter
el centímetro el sen-TEE-meh-troh the centimeter
¿Cuánto mide? KWAN-toh MEE-deh How long / how tall is it? (how much does it measure?)
mide MEE-deh it measures / it is … long
dos metros dohs MEH-trohs two meters
cincuenta centímetros sin-KWEN-tah sen-TEE-meh-trohs fifty centimeters

The pattern. ¿Cuánto mide? Mide dos metros. Mide cincuenta centímetros.

You already have numbers; today you use them with a length. Instruction stays in English. Original sentences, not a copied textbook dialogue.

La mesa mide dos metros. El lápiz mide quince centímetros. ¿Cuánto mide el papel?

Remember

¿Cuánto mide? Mide … metros / centímetros. Instruction stays in English. Hear the Spanish words.

Watch me

Say in Spanish: “It measures two meters.” Then ask “How long is it?”

  1. Mide dos metros.
  2. ¿Cuánto mide?
  3. The question can come first or second; the measure uses mide, not a new past tense.

Together

Say: “The pencil measures fifteen centimeters.”

Hint 1: mide

Start with El lápiz mide…

Hint 2: Sentence

El lápiz mide quince centímetros.

Check

Use today’s words in complete sentences.

  1. Write: “How long is the table?” and a two-meter answer.
  2. Write one new sentence with centímetros.

Exercises

  1. Give the Spanish for meter and centimeter (with the article).
  2. Write two sentences: a question with ¿Cuánto mide? and an answer with metros.
  3. Hear mide and centímetro twice, then use each in a sentence.
  4. Why is this still the present, not a new tense?
  5. A three-line mini-dialogue (question, answer, one more measure).

Latin · longus, lātus, and a first length sentence

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
longus, longa, longum LONG-us, LONG-ah, LONG-um long
lātus, lāta, lātum LAH-tus, LAH-tah, LAH-tum wide, broad
est est is
murus MOO-rus a wall
linea LIN-eh-ah a line
via WEE-ah a road

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

The pattern. An adjective matches the noun: murus longus est. Via longa est. Linea longa est.

Original sentences: Murus longus est. Via nōn est lāta. Linea est longa. We are not putting modern meters into the Latin sentence; the length words are the new piece, and a number can wait until you have ūnus, duo, trēs in a later line if they are already known, or stay with longus / brevis for now.

brevis, breve (short) is a useful opposite: Linea brevis est.

Remember

longus / longa: long. lātus / lāta: wide. The adjective matches the noun. Instruction in English. Hear uses an Italian voice, labeled as an approximation. No modern SI units in the Latin sentence.

Watch me

Say in Latin: “The wall is long.” Then: “The road is not wide.”

  1. Murus longus est. (murus is masculine, so longus.)
  2. Via nōn est lāta. (via is feminine, so lāta.)
  3. The verb est stays; the adjective changes to match.

Together

Say: “The line is short.” (Use brevis if you met it, or nōn longa.)

Hint 1: Feminine

linea is feminine: longa or brevis.

Hint 2: Sentence

Linea brevis est or Linea nōn longa est.

Check

Match the adjective, and keep the sentences original.

  1. Write: “The road is long.”
  2. Correct: *Murus longa est.

Exercises

  1. Write a pair: a long wall and a wide road, if it is wide, or nōn lāta if it is not.
  2. Why does *via longus est fail?
  3. Read today’s three original sentences aloud twice.
  4. Draw a long line and a short line and label them in Latin.
  5. Write one new sentence with nōn.

Notebook: a meter strip, and Mars’s width in a because-sentence

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

Draw a 10 cm strip and label it as 10 cm, and as 100 mm, and (if the scale helps) as 0.1 m. Beside it, write Mars’s equatorial diameter as about 6,792 km, and, if you use it, about 6,800 km, rounded to the nearest hundred, clearly labeled. Then write because / but / so sentences that keep a unit and a length apart, and that keep a rounded figure labeled.

Word bank: meter, centimeter, millimeter, kilometer, place-value, equatorial diameter, rounded, NASA fact sheet, a little more than half.

This is a record of the measurement system and of one sourced fact, not a decoration and not a score.

Remember

Draw and label first. Use the NASA-based 6,792 km, or label a rounding. Complete because / but / so stems. No points or badges — the page is the work.

Watch me

A model sentence: 6,800 km can be used for Mars’s width, but only if I label it as rounded to the nearest hundred, because the fact-sheet diameter we computed is about 6,792 km.

  1. 6,792 km is the nearer figure from 2 × 3,396.2.
  2. 6,800 is a rounding.
  3. The but keeps the label attached, so the number is not pretended to be exact.

Together

Finish aloud, then write: A centimeter is a smaller unit than a meter, so 200 cm ________, but a student who thinks “centimeters are always shorter” ________.

Hint 1: Same length

200 cm is the same as 2 m.

Hint 2: The mix-up

…has mixed up the size of the unit with the size of the length.

Check

The page should show a labeled strip, the Mars figure, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the 10 cm strip with two names for the same length, and write the Mars diameter with its source or a labeled rounding.
  2. Complete: Mars is a little more than half as wide as Earth, because ________, so I will not say “exactly half” without ________.

Practice

  1. Write a so-sentence about converting 3 km to meters.
  2. Add a but-sentence about circumference (why we did not compute one).
  3. Label the strip in millimeters as well, if there is room.
  4. Copy the word bank words you actually used, and tick them.
  5. The page is the work; no points, badges, or extra credit.
