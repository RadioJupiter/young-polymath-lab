Mental math warm-up: reading a change

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A change is how much a number goes up or down. Subtract the earlier number from the later one, then say up or down.

Remember

Up means the later number is greater. Down means the later number is smaller. The size of the change is the difference.

Watch me

A count goes from 8 to 13. How did it change?

  1. 13 − 8 = 5.
  2. It went up by 5.

Together

A count goes from 20 to 14. How did it change?

Hint 1: Compare

14 is smaller than 20.

Hint 2: The difference

20 − 14 = 6, so it went down by 6.

Check

These are short changes.

  1. From 3 to 11.
  2. From 18 to 9.

Practice

  1. From 40 to 40.
  2. From 7.0 to 3.6 is a drop; about how much, to the nearest whole number?

Core lesson: a line graph of change, and sunspots

40 minutes · Method: a table of sourced yearly means, then the same figures on a line graph

Learn

A line graph is a way to show how a quantity changes over time. Each point is a value at a year, and the line segments join the points in time order so you can see a rise or a fall.

The figures below are yearly mean total sunspot numbers from the World Data Center SILSO at the Royal Observatory of Belgium, in the file SN_y_tot_V2.0. A mid-year date such as 2024.5 is the mean for the year 2024. A yearly mean is the average of that year’s daily totals, so one day’s look at the Sun can be different from the yearly mean.

Year Yearly mean
2019 3.6
2020 8.8
2021 29.6
2022 83.2
2023 125.5
2024 154.7
2025 123.2

A NASA page on the sunspot cycle (Marshall Solar Physics, last updated 15 March 2017) says that the number of sunspots waxes and wanes with an approximate 11-year cycle, and that the International Sunspot Number is compiled in Belgium. That 2017 page is not the source of the yearly numbers in the table; those come from the SILSO file opened for this unit.

In that same SILSO series, the yearly mean was 4.2 in 2008 and 3.6 in 2019, eleven years later. That is one spacing in this official series. It is not a promise that every cycle lasts exactly eleven years, and it is not a number we made up.

On the line graph, 2024 is the highest point in this window (154.7), and 2019 is the lowest (3.6). The 2025 mean is 123.2, which is lower than 2024. A rise is something the line already shows. “It will keep rising next year” is a new claim, and this course has no official 2026 yearly mean to read yet, so that claim is not made here.

Remember

A line graph shows change over time. The yearly means in this lesson come from SILSO’s SN_y_tot_V2.0 file. In this window, 2019 is lowest and 2024 is highest; 2025 is lower than 2024. A rise already on the graph is not a promise that the next year will rise too, and a future official mean is not invented.

Watch me

In the SILSO table, which year in 2019–2025 has the highest yearly mean, and what is that mean?

  1. 2024 is the highest year in this window.
  2. The yearly mean is 154.7.

Together

How did the yearly mean change from 2024 to 2025, and why is “it will keep rising” a miss for 2026?

Hint 1: The two figures

2024 is 154.7 and 2025 is 123.2, so the mean went down.

Hint 2: The next year

A drop is already on the graph, and this course has no official 2026 yearly mean to invent from.

Check

These use the SILSO table and line graph above.

  1. Between 2019 and 2021, did the yearly mean rise or fall, and what are the two means?
  2. About how much greater is the 2023 mean than the 2022 mean? (125.5 − 83.2.)

Practice

  1. In the same SILSO series, 2008 is 4.2 and 2019 is 3.6. What spacing do those two low years show, and what do they not prove by themselves?
  2. Why are the invented meteor counts from Days 41–42 kept out of this sunspot graph?

Challenge problems: reading a sourced line carefully

25 minutes · harder work with the same SILSO figures

Learn

Stay with the SILSO yearly means from the lesson. Do not invent a later official year, and do not blend in a number from a different table.

Remember

Read the change from the figures you have. A future official mean is not invented. A rise is not a law.

Watch me

From 2020 to 2024, the SILSO yearly mean goes from 8.8 to 154.7. Is that a rise, and does that fact alone tell you the 2025 mean?

  1. It is a rise.
  2. It does not tell you the 2025 mean. That figure is read from the table (123.2), not guessed from the rise.

Together

Which year in this window is closer to the 2025 mean of 123.2: 2023 (125.5) or 2022 (83.2), and by about how much in each case?

Hint 1: The differences

125.5 − 123.2 = 2.3, and 123.2 − 83.2 = 40.

Hint 2: Closer

2023 is much closer; the difference is only 2.3.

Check

These stay with the sourced figures.

  1. Name the lowest year and the highest year in the 2019–2025 SILSO window, with their means.
  2. Why would it be a miss to average the NASA 2017 page’s wording of an “approximate 11-year cycle” with one homemade “exactly 11” rule, and then call that a SILSO measurement?

Practice

  1. The 2021 mean is 29.6 and the 2022 mean is 83.2. About how many times as large is 2022, to the nearest whole number?

Math circle: a rise is not a law, and a future official mean is not invented

20 minutes · a reason about what a line graph can and cannot claim

Learn

A line graph can show a change that already happened. It cannot, by itself, write next year’s official number, and a stretch of rising points is not a law that the next point must rise too.

Remember

Describe the change you can point to. Leave unsourced future figures unclaimed. A pattern you can see is not automatically a rule that must continue.

Watch me

From 2019 to 2024 the SILSO yearly means in this window rise. Why is that not enough to publish a 2026 official yearly mean in this lesson?

  1. The rise is something the sourced points already show.
  2. A 2026 official yearly mean would be a new figure, and this course has not opened one.
  3. Inventing that figure would mix a description of the past with a number no official table here has given.

Together

The 2025 mean is lower than the 2024 mean. How does that fact help a reader who was ready to say “it always goes up”?

Hint 1: The later point

The next sourced year in this window already went down.

Hint 2: The claim

“Always goes up” is stronger than the line, and 2025 already disagrees with it.

Check

These check the reason.

  1. Give one honest sentence the line graph does support, and one sentence it does not.
  2. Why do we keep NASA’s “approximate 11-year cycle” wording labeled as NASA’s, rather than rewriting it as an exact law from two low years?

Practice

  1. If a later SILSO file revises a yearly mean, what should a graph that used the older figure do?

Spanish · la mancha

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la mancha lah MAHN-chah the spot, the mark
el año el AH-nyoh the year (already known; used again)
sube SOO-beh goes up (already known; used again)
baja BAH-hah goes down (already known; used again)

Today’s new word names a spot. el año is already known from Day 38, and sube and baja are already known from Day 37; they are used again, not taught as new. Instruction stays in English. SILSO yearly means and the line graph stay in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: Hay una mancha. Hay un año. El año sube. El año baja.

For the guide: point to a drawn sun with a dark spot, then to a year label on the line graph, as you say the words. el sol is already known and may be used in pointing, but it is not a new word today. Do not read official yearly means in Spanish.

Remember

la mancha is new. el año, sube, and baja are used again, not new. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No SILSO figures in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “There is a spot.”

  1. Hay una mancha.
  2. Hay introduces it. Una mancha is the spot.

Together

How do you say “The year goes down”?

Hint 1: Goes down

baja, already known

Hint 2: The sentence

El año baja.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “There is a year.”
  2. Write: “The year goes up.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *El mancha, if someone used the wrong article.
  2. Hear mancha and año, then use each. Hear sube and baja again.
  3. Why does the Spanish not name the 2024 SILSO mean?
  4. A pointing retell: a spot, a year, goes up, goes down.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · macula

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
macula MAH-ku-lah a spot (nominative, feminine)
Macula adest MAH-ku-lah AH-dest A spot is here
Macula in sōle est MAH-ku-lah in SOH-leh est A spot is on the sun
annus AHN-nus a year (nominative, masculine; already known)

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Macula adest. Macula nōn adest. Macula in sōle est. Annus longus est.

macula is feminine. annus is already known from Day 38 (masculine, so longus matches) and is used again, not taught as a first look. sōle is the ablative of sōl, already known from Day 8, in the set phrase in sōle. SILSO figures stay in the English mathematics.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to a drawn spot on the sun, then to a year label. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. Do not read official yearly means in Latin.

Remember

macula; Macula adest; Macula in sōle est. annus is used again, not new. Instruction in English. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. No SILSO figures in the Latin.

Notebook: a line graph of SILSO means, and a because-sentence

about 1 hour · the sourced line graph, then because / but / so

Learn

Copy the 2019–2025 SILSO yearly means as a table and as a line graph, then write a because / but / so sentence about what a rise can and cannot claim.

Remember

The page shows the sourced figures and a line through them. The stems explain that a rise already on the graph is not a promise about a later official year.

Watch me

Start: I can say the 2024 mean is the highest in this window because ________.

  1. The because points to the SILSO table, not to a guess.
  2. A sound because: …the SN_y_tot_V2.0 figures show 154.7 in 2024, larger than the other years in this window…

Together

Finish: I can say the 2024 mean is the highest in this window because ________, but a rise is not a law about the next official year, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…2025 is already lower, at 123.2, and a 2026 official mean is not in the file we opened…

Hint 2: The so

…I will describe the change I can point to, and I will not invent a 2026 official yearly mean.

Check

The page should show the table, the line graph, and complete stems.

  1. Copy the seven SILSO yearly means (2019–2025) and draw the line graph with an even scale, labeled as SILSO.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about the 2024 high and why a future official mean is not invented.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: line graph, yearly mean, SILSO, rise, invented.
  2. Write a short source line: WDC-SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium, SN_y_tot_V2.0.
  3. The page is a record, not a score.
  4. File the page. It is not a reward.
