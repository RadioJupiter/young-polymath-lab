Level 3 · Day 15 · Level 3 check

Today's big question: Can you use equal groups, arrays, and division, and keep four weeks distinct from the NASA synodic month?

For the guide: Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: counters for equal groups, arrays, and sharing before the written fact. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. The Moon’s synodic period used in this level is 29.53 days from the NASA Moon fact sheet, taught as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html Do not replace that figure with 28 days (four weeks), and do not begin Moon phases as fractions or the sidereal month. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact, not a NASA orbital period. If the placement check started the student here, earlier foundation greetings and numbers may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later multiplication with larger numbers; today’s facts and arrays are a first look.

Level 3 check

45 minutes · alone, no calculator · a check of this level, not a new lesson

Learn

This is a check of skills already taught in Level 3, not a new lesson. Use counters or an array when a picture would help. Moon: about 29½ days from 29.53 on the NASA Moon fact sheet. Bags and arrays stay labeled invented.

Remember

7 × 8 = 56. 4 rows of 9 = 36. 54 ÷ 6 = 9, checked by 6 × 9. Three bags of 24: 8 each; five bags: 40, invented. Four weeks: 28 days. Synodic month: about 29½ days.

Watch me

A model of how to show work. Find 7 × 8.

  1. Seven 8s, or 7 rows of 8.
  2. The product is 56.

Together

Three bags hold 24 rocks equally (invented). How many in 5 such bags?

Hint 1

24 ÷ 3 = 8 in each bag.

Hint 2

5 × 8 = 40, invented.

Check

The Together item is practice for layout. The Unit check below is done alone.

  1. What is 54 ÷ 6?
  2. How many are in 4 rows of 9?

Unit check

Work alone. Use counters, an array, or a fact family when it helps. For the Moon’s month, use about 29½ days from 29.53 days on the NASA Moon fact sheet taught this level. Classroom bags, rocks, and arrays stay labeled invented. Do not use a calculator.

Part A: equal groups and multiplication

  1. Find 7 × 8. You may show equal groups or an array.
  2. Why is 7 × 8 the same as 8 × 7?
  3. How many are in 4 rows of 9?
  4. Find 4 × 7. What can that count, if each 7 is a week?
  5. A student writes 7 × 8 = 15. What was mixed?

Part B: division and fact families

  1. Find 54 ÷ 6. Show a multiplication that checks it.
  2. Find 54 ÷ 9.
  3. Write the fact family for 6, 9, and 54.
  4. Find 36 ÷ 4.
  5. A student writes 54 ÷ 6 = 48 because 54 − 6 = 48. What was mixed?

Part C: scaling equal groups, the week, and the Moon

  1. Three bags hold 24 rocks shared equally (invented). How many rocks are in each bag? How many rocks are in 5 such bags?
  2. How many days are in four weeks?
  3. What length does this level use for the Moon’s synodic month, and from which source?
  4. Why is about 29½ days not replaced by 28 days?
  5. Two invented boxes hold 18 cards equally. How many cards are in 5 such boxes?

Four out of five or better on each part means this level is mastered. Any part below that is reviewed before Unit 1 (Level 4), if that is the next step, or before a review sitting. This is a mastery check, not a score, a badge, or a race.

Challenge problems: a level wrap

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

This wrap uses only Level 3 methods. No new operation is begun. Remainders as a written form, and Moon phases as fractions, are not begun here.

Remember

Equal groups, arrays, fact families, labeled bags, 28 days, and about 29½ days.

Watch me

An invented pack has 4 rows of 8 stamps. How many stamps, and how many in 6 such packs if each pack is alike?

  1. 4 × 8 = 32 stamps, invented.
  2. 6 × 32 = 192, or first 32 ÷ 4 is not needed if the pack is the group: 6 packs of 32 is 192, invented.

Together

About how many days are in two synodic months, using about 29½ + 29½, and what must you label?

Hint 1

29½ + 29½ = 59.

Hint 2

About 59 days, from twice the rounded NASA figure, not a new official NASA measurement.

Check

A short wrap after the check.

  1. Find 8 × 9, from 4 × 9 if it helps.
  2. Find 63 ÷ 7, using 7 × 9 if it helps.

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 54 ÷ 6 is 9, not 48.
  2. Why is 4 × 7 = 28 not the NASA synodic month?

Math circle: a last short argument

20 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting

Learn

A good Level 3 argument names equal groups or an array, and it keeps a calendar week and the NASA synodic month labeled as different lengths.

Remember

Equal groups, a check multiplication, and honest labels.

Watch me

A student says the Moon’s month is 28 days “because 4 × 7 is easier.” What two lengths were mixed?

  1. 28 days is four calendar weeks.
  2. The NASA synodic period is 29.53 days, about 29½ days, a little more than 28.

Together

Write a short argument that five bags like the 24-rock bags hold 40 rocks, with the counts labeled invented.

Hint 1

24 ÷ 3 = 8.

Hint 2

5 × 8 = 40.

Check

A last short argument in this level’s tools.

  1. What check multiplication goes with 54 ÷ 6 = 9?
  2. How many are in 4 rows of 9?

Practice

  1. Name one NASA figure and one invented figure from this level.
  2. What may you claim about four weeks and the synodic month, without inventing a finer official length?

Notebook: a Level 3 page — an array, equal bags, and two lengths

60 minutes · after the check, or later if the check took the full sitting · draw first, then label, then write

Learn

Draw three small pictures: (1) 7 × 8 or 4 rows of 9 as an array, (2) three bags of 8 and five bags of 8, invented, (3) 28 days beside about 29½ days, labeled calendar and NASA. Then write from stems.

Word bank: array, divide, bags, weeks, NASA, invented, twenty-eight, twenty-nine and a half.

Stems: 1. 54 ÷ 6 is 9 because … 2. Five such bags hold 40 rocks, but … 3. Four weeks are 28 days, so …

End with one NASA fact (about 29½ days from 29.53) and one invented fact, clearly labeled, and one “what next” question for Unit 1 (Level 4).

Remember

Three drawings, then three stems, then one sourced fact and one invented fact, labeled. Do not replace 29½ with 28.

Watch me

Finish: “54 ÷ 6 is 9 because …”

  1. A model: “… because 6 groups of 9 make 54, and 6 × 9 = 54 checks it.” Other complete finishes accepted.

Together

Finish: “Five such bags hold 40 rocks, but …”

Hint 1

The counts are invented, not official NASA rocks.

Hint 2

A model: “… but those rock counts are invented classroom numbers.”

Check

These check the week’s notebook ideas together.

  1. What NASA length should appear at the end?
  2. Name one invented count that must stay labeled invented.

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question for Unit 1 (Level 4).
