Level 3 · Day 14 · Five bags, four weeks, and about 29½ days

Today's big question: If three bags hold 24 rocks equally, how many are in 5 such bags, and how does about 29½ days compare with four weeks?

For the guide: Work through the blocks in order. Do not skip the concrete or pictorial step in the core lesson: counters for equal groups, arrays, and sharing before the written fact. Answers go in the math notebook; check them with the key only after the work is done. Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. The Moon’s synodic period used in this level is 29.53 days from the NASA Moon fact sheet, taught as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html Do not replace that figure with 28 days (four weeks), and do not begin Moon phases as fractions or the sidereal month. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact, not a NASA orbital period. If the placement check started the student here, earlier foundation greetings and numbers may be used first as a recap, then continue. Students who later move to Unit 1 (Level 4) will meet later multiplication with larger numbers; today’s facts and arrays are a first look.

Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

If 3 groups make 24, each group is 8, because 3 × 8 = 24. Then 5 such groups are 5 × 8 = 40. This is the bag problem in short form.

Remember

Find one equal group first, then scale to a new number of groups.

Watch me

Three bags hold 24 rocks equally. How many in one bag?

  1. 24 ÷ 3 = 8, or 3 × 8 = 24.

Together

Then how many rocks in 5 such bags?

Each bag holds 8.

Hint 2

5 × 8 = 40.

Check

One group, then more groups.

  1. 24 ÷ 3
  2. 5 × 8
  3. 7 × 8

Practice

  3. 3 × 8

Core lesson: equal bags, and about 29½ days

45 minutes · the bag method already prepared; then a sourced month beside four weeks

Learn

Invented classroom bags, labeled invented: three bags hold 24 rocks shared equally. How many rocks are in each bag? How many rocks are in 5 such bags?

24 ÷ 3 = 8, so each bag holds 8. Then 5 bags hold 5 × 8 = 40.

Do not jump from 24 to 40 by a guess. Find the equal group first (8), then the new number of groups (5). The 24, 8, and 40 are invented classroom counts, not official NASA rock counts.

The week and the Moon. A week has 7 days (calendar). Four weeks have 4 × 7 = 28 days. The NASA Moon fact sheet gives the Moon’s synodic period as 29.53 days, taught here as about 29½ days. That is a little more than four weeks (28 days). 29½ is not 28, and it is not 4 × 7; it is a sourced length, rounded to a half-day for this level, not a finer invented official figure. This level does not teach Moon phases as fractions (for example “first quarter”). A sidereal month is not begun.

The length of the month used in this level is from the NASA Moon fact sheet: the Moon’s synodic period is 29.53 days, taught here as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html This level does not invent a finer official length, and does not begin the sidereal month or Moon phases as fractions (those belong later). Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact used here, not a NASA orbital period.

Remember

Three equal bags of 24 rocks: 8 in each; five such bags: 40. All invented and labeled invented. Four weeks are 28 days. The NASA synodic period is 29.53 days, about 29½ days, a little more than four weeks. No phases as fractions.

Watch me

Three bags hold 24 rocks equally (invented). How many in each bag, and how many in 5 such bags?

  1. 8 in each bag (24 ÷ 3 = 8).
  2. 5 × 8 = 40 rocks, invented.

Together

Hint 1

4 × 7 = 28 days.

Hint 2

About 29½ days, from 29.53 days on the NASA Moon fact sheet.

Check

These use the bag method or the month beside four weeks.

  1. Three bags hold 24 equally. How many in 5 such bags?
  2. How many days in four weeks?

Practice

  1. Two invented boxes hold 18 cards equally. How many cards in 5 such boxes?
  2. Why is about 29½ days not replaced by 28 days?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

If each bag holds 8, then 6 such bags hold 48, and 10 such bags hold 80. The equal group stays 8; only the number of bags changes. 29½ is about 1½ days more than 28, because 28 + 1½ = 29½. That 1½ is a reading of the two labeled lengths, not a new official NASA “extra” with more digits.

Remember

Scale the equal group. Compare 28 days and about 29½ days without inventing a finer official difference.

Watch me

How many invented rocks in 6 bags of 8, and in 10 bags of 8?

  1. 6 × 8 = 48.
  2. 10 × 8 = 80.

Together

About how much longer is 29½ days than 28 days, and what must you not claim?

Hint 1

29½ − 28 = 1½ days.

Hint 2

1½ days is the difference of the calendar four weeks and the rounded NASA figure, not a new official NASA measurement with extra digits.

Check

These scale the bags, or compare 28 and about 29½.

  1. Four invented bags hold 32 equally. How many in 7 such bags?
  2. What is 4 × 7, and what is about 29½ − 28?

Practice

  1. Why must you not say the Moon’s month is 28 days “because that is four weeks”?
  2. Find 24 ÷ 3 and 5 × 8 again, as a pair.

Math circle: find the equal group first, and keep two lengths distinct

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

The bag problem is two steps: first the equal group (24 ÷ 3 = 8), then the new whole (5 × 8 = 40). The month problem is a comparison of two labeled lengths, not a reason to change NASA’s figure into 28.

Remember

Find the unit group before you scale. Do not replace a sourced length with a nearby calendar product.

Watch me

A student jumps from “three bags hold 24” to “five bags hold 26” by adding 2, because 5 is 2 more than 3. What was missed?

  1. Adding 2 bags is not adding 2 rocks. Each bag holds 8.
  2. The new whole is 5 × 8 = 40, not 24 + 2.

Together

Why may you say the synodic month is a little more than four weeks, but not that it is four weeks?

Hint 1

Four weeks are 28 days; the NASA figure is 29.53 days, about 29½.

Hint 2

A little more than 28 is honest; calling it 28 is replacing the sourced length.

Check

These defend the two-step method and the labels.

  1. What two calculations solve the 24-rock bag problem for 5 bags?
  2. What two lengths are compared for the month and the weeks?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that five such bags hold 40 rocks, invented.
  2. Write a short argument that about 29½ days is not 28 days.

Notebook: five bags of 8, and about 29½ days

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: three invented bags of 8 (24 in all), then five bags of 8 (40), and a number line or bar comparing 28 days (four weeks) with about 29½ days (NASA, from 29.53). Label invented counts and the NASA month. Then write from stems.

Word bank: bags, equal, forty, weeks, twenty-eight, twenty-nine and a half, NASA, invented.

Because / but / so: 1. Five such bags hold 40 rocks because … 2. Four weeks are 28 days, but … 3. The NASA figure is 29.53 days, so …

End with 24 ÷ 3 = 8 and 5 × 8 = 40 (invented), and about 29½ days from the fact sheet, and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw the bags and the two lengths first. Because: the equal group is 8. But: the official month is not 28 days. So: about 29½ days, from 29.53.

Watch me

Finish: “Five such bags hold 40 rocks because …”

  1. A model: “… because each bag holds 8, and 5 × 8 = 40.” Other complete finishes accepted. Counts stay invented.

Together

Finish: “Four weeks are 28 days, but …”

Hint 1

The NASA synodic month is a little longer.

Hint 2

About 29½ days, from 29.53.

Check

These check the two-step bag method and the two lengths.

  1. What two calculations must appear for the bags?
  2. What source names 29.53 days?

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question that does not begin Moon phases as fractions.
