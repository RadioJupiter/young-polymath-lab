Core lesson: bars in two jobs, and shadows through the day

45 minutes · bars already taught; then a classroom shadow observation, labeled invented where a length is measured

Learn

Yesterday’s comparison bar and the part-whole bar are both used today. Choose the bar that matches the question.

Invented classroom rockets, still labeled invented: 63 m and 45 m. Difference: 18 m (comparison). Stacked whole: 108 m (part-whole).

Shadows through the day. A stick of fixed height casts a longer shadow when the Sun is lower in the sky (morning or evening) and a shorter shadow when the Sun is higher (near midday). That is an observation you can make with a stick and a shadow, not a NASA official length.

Invented classroom measurements, labeled invented: in the morning the shadow of a stick is 80 cm; near midday the shadow of the same stick is 20 cm. The shadow is 60 cm longer in the morning (comparison). The stick’s height did not change; the Sun’s height in the sky did. These 80 cm and 20 cm figures are not official NASA measurements, and they are not the Saturn V.

Never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes. Measure a shadow, not the Sun.

The Saturn V height used in this level is from NASA, “What Was the Saturn V?”: the Saturn V rocket was 111 meters (363 feet) tall. https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-was-the-saturn-v/ Classroom rocket heights (for example 63 m, and 18 m shorter) and classroom shadow lengths are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA measurements. This level does not average 111 m with any other figure.

Remember

Part-whole for a total; comparison for a difference. A longer shadow goes with a lower Sun; a shorter shadow with a higher Sun. Classroom shadow lengths (80 cm and 20 cm) are invented and labeled invented. The stick’s height stays the same.

Watch me

The same invented stick has an 80 cm morning shadow and a 20 cm midday shadow. How much longer is the morning shadow, and which bar is that?

  1. 80 − 20 = 60 cm longer.
  2. A comparison bar: the extra piece is the 60 cm.

Together

Why is the midday shadow shorter, if the stick did not change height?

Hint 1

The Sun is higher in the sky near midday.

Hint 2

A higher light source, for a stick standing on the ground, makes a shorter shadow. This is the observation, not an official NASA length.

Check

These choose a bar, or use the shadow observation with invented lengths labeled.

  1. Invented rockets 63 m and 45 m: find the difference and the stacked whole, and name the bar for each.
  2. Invented shadows 80 cm and 20 cm: find how much shorter the midday shadow is.

Practice

  1. A student adds 80 + 20 and calls that the stick’s height. What was mixed?
  2. Why must you not look at the real Sun to do this observation?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

If a morning shadow is 80 cm and a midday shadow is 20 cm (invented), the morning shadow is 4 times as long as the midday shadow, because 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 80. “Four times as long” is not a new official NASA ratio; it is a reading of these invented lengths.

Remember

Comparison can be a difference or, when one length fits into the other exactly, a ‘how many times’ count. The invented lengths stay labeled invented.

Watch me

How many 20 cm midday shadows, laid end to end, would match the 80 cm morning shadow?

  1. Four, because 4 × 20 = 80, or 20 + 20 + 20 + 20 = 80.

Together

An invented evening shadow is 70 cm. Is the Sun likely higher or lower than at midday, compared with the 20 cm midday shadow, and why?

Hint 1

70 cm is longer than 20 cm.

Hint 2

A longer shadow goes with a lower Sun.

Check

These read the invented shadows or choose a bar.

  1. Find 80 − 20 and 80 + 20. Which one is the comparison difference?
  2. Invented rockets 50 m and 15 m shorter: find the shorter height and the stacked whole.

Practice

  1. Why does “four times as long” not become an official NASA shadow ratio in this lesson?
  2. Find 1,000 − 264 again, as a review fact.

Math circle: a shadow, a stick, and what we may claim

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

You may claim that a longer shadow goes with a lower Sun, from the observation. You may not claim that 80 cm is an official NASA morning shadow, or that every stick on Earth has those two lengths.

Remember

Observation plus labeled invented measurements. Do not turn classroom lengths into official figures.

Watch me

What may you claim about the 80 cm and 20 cm shadows, and what must you not claim?

  1. You may claim that, in this invented classroom trial, the morning shadow was 60 cm longer, and that this fits a lower Sun.
  2. You must not claim that NASA measured 80 cm and 20 cm, or that the Saturn V’s shadow is 80 cm.

Together

Why is adding 80 and 20 a poor way to find the stick’s height?

Hint 1

Those numbers are shadows, not parts of the stick.

Hint 2

The stick’s height did not change; the two shadows are two different times, not two pieces of one bar for the stick.

Check

These keep the claim honest.

  1. What changes from morning to midday: the stick’s height, or the Sun’s height in the sky?
  2. Which bar compares 80 cm and 20 cm?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that a longer shadow goes with a lower Sun, without inventing an official length.
  2. Name one NASA figure and one invented figure from this level, and say how you tell them apart.

Spanish · Es de mañana and a shadow picture

15 minutes · Method: two time-of-day phrases with a shadow drawing. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today adds por la mañana (in the morning) and al mediodía (at midday), used with a long-shadow and a short-shadow drawing. Es de día / es de noche from Level 1 may be reused, not retaught as a first look. Do not give 80 cm or 20 cm as Spanish official lengths.

The guide says por la mañana, la sombra es más larga only if those words have been heard with actions first: point at the long shadow, say más larga (longer, feminine, with sombra). If larga is new, teach it as “long” by pointing, then use it. Instruction stays in English.

Instructions are in English. Hear clips use es-MX. Greetings and numbers 1–10 from Level 1 may be reused, not retaught as a first look. If a student began at Level 2, the guide may first use Level 1 Days 1–2 Spanish for hola and uno–diez, then continue here.

Remember

Por la mañana: in the morning. Al mediodía: at midday. A longer shadow picture goes with the morning phrase; a shorter one with midday. Lengths in centimeters stay in English and stay labeled invented.

Watch me

The guide points at the long-shadow drawing and says por la mañana. What should the student do?

  1. Point at the long-shadow drawing, as the morning picture.

Together

What is the English of al mediodía?

Hint 1

At midday.

Check

These check the two time phrases with the pictures.

  1. Give the English of por la mañana and al mediodía.
  2. Which drawing goes with a shorter shadow?

Exercises

  1. Say the Spanish for “in the morning.”
  2. Why are 80 cm and 20 cm not written as official Spanish facts today?

Latin · Umbra longa and umbra brevis, side by side

15 minutes · Method: two captions already taught, now used as a pair.

Learn

Today is a pair, not a new noun: a morning-style long shadow and a midday-style short shadow.

  • Umbra longa est.
  • Umbra brevis est.

The reason (a lower or higher Sun) stays in English in the mathematics block.

All Latin here is original. Explanations are in English. The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome. Vowels are pure; c like k; v like English w.

Remember

Umbra longa est: the shadow is long. Umbra brevis est: the shadow is short. The science reason stays in English.

Watch me

A short-shadow drawing is shown. Which caption matches?

  1. Umbra brevis est.

Together

What is the English of Umbra longa est?

Hint 1

The shadow is long.

Check

These reuse the two captions as a pair.

  1. Give the English of both captions.
  2. What does umbra mean?

Exercises

  1. Write both original captions.
  2. Why is the lower-Sun reason not written as a Latin paragraph?

Notebook: shadows through the day, with honest labels

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: the same stick at two times, a long morning shadow and a short midday shadow, with invented lengths 80 cm and 20 cm labeled invented. Label stick, Sun (higher / lower), shadow, morning, midday. Never look at the real Sun. Then write from stems.

Word bank: shadow, stick, morning, midday, longer, shorter, invented, higher, lower.

Because / but / so: 1. The midday shadow is shorter because … 2. The stick is the same height, but … 3. The morning shadow is 80 cm, so …

End with 80 − 20 = 60 cm (invented), the NASA-free observation about higher and lower Sun, and one “what next” question.

Safety: never look at the real Sun with unprotected eyes.

Remember

Draw both shadows first. Because: the Sun is higher. But: the shadows are different lengths. So: the morning one is 60 cm longer, invented. Do not call 80 cm a NASA length.

Watch me

Finish: “The midday shadow is shorter because …”

  1. A model: “… because the Sun is higher in the sky.” Other complete finishes accepted.

Together

Finish: “The stick is the same height, but …”

Hint 1

The shadows are not the same length.

Hint 2

The Sun’s height in the sky has changed.

Check

These check the observation and the labels.

  1. What two lengths must stay labeled invented?
  2. What must you never do with the real Sun?

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question about a later time of day.
