Unit 9 · Day 44 · Chance, and measuring with error

Today's big question: What is the chance of a fair outcome, and how do we write a measurement that is not exact?

Core lesson: chance, and measuring with error

40 minutes · Method: fair outcomes as “out of” and as a fraction, then “about” and a range for a measurement that is not exact

Learn

When every outcome of a trial is equally likely, we call the trial fair. The chance of one of those outcomes is “1 out of how many outcomes there are,” which we can also write as a fraction.

A fair coin has two faces, heads and tails. The chance of heads is 1 out of 2, or 1/2. A fair six-sided die has six faces. The chance of rolling a 5 is 1 out of 6, or 1/6. The chance of rolling an even number (2, 4, or 6) is 3 out of 6, which is the same amount as 1/2.

The spinner on the left has four equal parts, with one part shaded. The chance of landing on the shaded part is 1 out of 4, or 1/4. The spinner on the right has four parts that are not equal, and the shaded part is the small one. You cannot honestly say “1 out of 4” for that shaded part, because the parts are not equal chances.

Expected means what the fair chance points to in the long run. In 10 fair coin tosses, you would expect about 5 heads. You will not get exactly 5 heads every time. A short run of heads does not, by itself, prove that a coin is unfair. You would need a much longer, careful record before that claim was even worth asking.

A measurement can be honest without being exact to a made-up extra digit. The ruler above is marked in centimeters. The rod ends a little past 8 cm and does not reach 9 cm. An honest reading is about 8 cm, or between 8 and 9 cm. Writing 8.27 cm would claim a finer scale than the ruler has.

A yearly mean, such as the SILSO sunspot means, is an average of many daily totals, so one day’s look at the Sun can be different from that year’s mean. A classroom meteor count can also miss a meteor if someone looks away, so two people can disagree by a little. In those cases, “about,” or a short range, is the honest wording.

Remember

When outcomes are equally likely, chance is “1 out of n,” which can be written as a fraction. Unequal parts are not a fair “1 out of 4.” Expected means about, not exactly, in a short run. A short run of heads does not by itself prove a coin is unfair. A measurement is written only as finely as the tool allows: “about” or a range, not a fake extra digit.

Watch me

A fair six-sided die is rolled. What is the chance of a 3, as “out of” and as a fraction?

  1. There are six equal faces, and one of them is 3.
  2. The chance is 1 out of 6, or 1/6.

Together

The centimeter ruler shows a rod that ends a little past 8 and does not reach 9. What is an honest length, and why is 8.27 cm a miss?

Hint 1: The marks

The marks are in whole centimeters.

Hint 2: Honest wording

About 8 cm, or between 8 and 9 cm. 8.27 cm claims tenths and hundredths the ruler does not show.

Check

These use fair chance, or “about” and a range.

  1. A fair coin is tossed. What is the chance of tails?
  2. In 10 fair coin tosses, about how many heads would you expect, and must you get exactly that many?

Practice

  1. On the unequal spinner, why is “1 out of 4” the wrong chance for the small shaded part?
  2. A length is clearly past 12 cm and not yet to 13 cm on a centimeter ruler. Write an honest reading.

Challenge problems: expected versus observed, and a fair reading

25 minutes · harder work with the same chance and measurement ideas

Learn

Expected is what the fair chance points to. Observed is what actually happened in a short record. A tool’s marks set how fine a measurement may honestly be.

Remember

Expected and observed can differ in a short run. “About” and a range stay honest. Unequal parts are not a fair “out of.”

Watch me

A fair die is rolled 6 times and the number 1 never appears. Does that, by itself, prove the die is unfair?

  1. No.
  2. In a short run, a fair outcome can easily be missing. That record is not, by itself, enough to call the die unfair.

Together

A labeled invented spinner is half red, one quarter blue, and one quarter yellow. What is the chance of red, and what is the chance of blue?

Hint 1: Red

Red is half of the spinner: 1 out of 2, or 1/2.

Hint 2: Blue

Blue is one quarter: 1 out of 4, or 1/4.

Check

These stay with chance and honest measurement.

  1. A fair coin is tossed 10 times and comes up heads 7 times. What was expected, and what was observed?
  2. Why does a SILSO yearly mean not have to match one afternoon’s look at the Sun?

Practice

  1. A rod ends right on the 10 cm mark of a centimeter ruler. What length do you write, and why is “about 10 cm” still fair to say?

Math circle: a short run is not a proof, and “about” is not a dodge

20 minutes · a reason about expected versus observed, and honest precision

Learn

A short record can surprise you even when a trial is fair. “About” is the honest wording when a tool, or an average, does not support a finer claim.

Remember

Expected is not a promise of an exact short-run count. A measurement is written only as finely as the marks or the source allow.

Watch me

A fair coin comes up heads five times in a row. Why is that not, by itself, a proof that the coin is unfair?

  1. A fair coin can make a short run of heads.
  2. Five tosses is a short record, not a long, careful one.
  3. Calling the coin unfair would be a new claim that this short run does not prove.

Together

Why is writing 8.27 cm from a centimeter ruler a different kind of miss than writing “about 8 cm”?

Hint 1: The extra digits

8.27 cm claims tenths and hundredths the ruler does not show.

Hint 2: “About”

“About 8 cm,” or “between 8 and 9 cm,” stays inside what the marks support.

Check

These check the reason.

  1. Give one reason “expected about 5 heads in 10 tosses” uses the word about.
  2. Why is an unequal spinner a poor model for teaching “1 out of 4”?

Practice

  1. If two people count the same invented meteor watch and get 14 and 16, what honest wording can they share, and what should they not do?

Spanish · la moneda, el dado, and la cara, with igual again

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la moneda lah moh-NEH-dah the coin
el dado el DAH-doh the die (the cube you roll)
la cara lah KAH-rah the face (of a coin or a die)
igual ee-GWAHL equal (already known; used again)

Today’s new words name a coin, a die, and a face. igual is already known from Day 31 and is used again, not taught as new. Instruction stays in English. “1 out of n,” fractions, and expected counts stay in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: Hay una moneda. Hay un dado. Hay una cara. La cara es igual.

For the guide: point to a drawn coin, then to a die, then to one face, as you say the words. This is present-tense description, not a chance lesson in Spanish. lado is already known and may be used in pointing, but it is not a new word today.

Remember

la moneda; el dado; la cara. igual is used again, not new. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No “1 out of n” work in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “There is a coin.”

  1. Hay una moneda.
  2. Hay introduces it. Una moneda is the coin.

Together

How do you say “There is a die”?

Hint 1: The die

el dado

Hint 2: The sentence

Hay un dado.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “There is a face.”
  2. Write: “The face is equal.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *El moneda, if someone used the wrong article.
  2. Hear moneda, dado, and cara, then use each.
  3. Why does the Spanish not say “1 out of 6”?
  4. A pointing retell: a coin, a die, a face, equal.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Notebook: a fair trial and an honest length, and a because-sentence

about 1 hour · a coin or die, a ruler reading, then because / but / so

Learn

Draw a fair coin or a fair die, and the centimeter rod that ends a little past 8, then write a because / but / so sentence about expected versus observed, and about “about.”

Remember

The page shows a fair trial and an honest measurement. The stems explain that a short run can differ from what is expected, and that a ruler is read only as finely as its marks.

Watch me

Start: I expect about 5 heads in 10 fair tosses because ________.

  1. The because restates the fair chance: 1 out of 2.
  2. A sound because: …heads is 1 out of 2, so ten tosses point to about half…

Together

Finish: I expect about 5 heads in 10 fair tosses because ________, but a short run can come out differently, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…I might see 7 heads, or 3, in only ten tosses…

Hint 2: The so

…I will not call the coin unfair from a short run, and I will write a length as about 8 cm, not 8.27 cm, on a centimeter ruler.

Check

The page should show the drawings and complete stems.

  1. Draw a fair coin or die, and the rod on the centimeter ruler, with an honest reading written beside it.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about expected versus observed, and about “about.”

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: fair, chance, expected, about, ruler.
  2. Write the chance of heads as 1 out of 2, or 1/2, on the page.
  3. The page is a record, not a score.
  4. File the page. It is not a reward.
