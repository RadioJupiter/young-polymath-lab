Unit 2 · Day 8 · Years on other worlds

Today's big question: A year on Mars lasts 687 Earth days. How many weeks is that? How many full Moons fit in an Earth year? Division answers "how many groups?" and "how many in each group?"

For the guide: division has two meanings, and both should be acted out with counters: sharing (12 counters shared among 3 people: how many each?) and grouping (12 counters in groups of 3: how many groups?). Remainders must be interpreted in context: sometimes you round up, sometimes you drop them, sometimes they are the answer.

Mental math warm-up

10 minutes · use multiplication facts backwards

Learn

Division is the inverse of multiplication, and also sharing equally or grouping. 72 ÷ 8 asks '8 rows make 72: how long is a row?' or 'how many 8s in 72?' A useful mental path is to split the dividend: 100 ÷ 4 = (80 ÷ 4) + (20 ÷ 4).

Remember

If a × b = c, then c ÷ b = a. Split a convenient dividend into parts that you can share.

Watch me

100 ÷ 4 by a split.

  1. 100 = 80 + 20.
  2. 80 ÷ 4 = 20 and 20 ÷ 4 = 5, so 100 ÷ 4 = 25.

Together

360 ÷ 6. Split 360 as 300 + 60.

Hint 1: Parts

300 ÷ 6 = 50 and 60 ÷ 6 = 10.

Hint 2: Whole

50 + 10 = 60.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. 72 ÷ 8.
  2. 1,000 ÷ 8 (try 800 + 200).

Practice

  1. 72 ÷ 8
  2. 56 ÷ 7
  3. 100 ÷ 4
  4. 360 ÷ 6
  5. 1,000 ÷ 8
  6. 84 ÷ 4
  7. 96 ÷ 3
  8. 4,200 ÷ 7

Core lesson: division and remainders

45 minutes

Learn

The facts. Mars takes about 687 Earth days to go once around the Sun. The Moon goes through its full cycle of phases (new moon to new moon) in about 29.5 days.

1. Concrete. Take 23 counters. Put them in groups of 7. How many full groups? How many left over? Write it: 23 ÷ 7 = 3 remainder 2. Check: 3 × 7 + 2 = 23.

2. Pictorial. For 687 ÷ 7, think in chunks: 7 × 90 = 630 (take away 630, leaving 57); 7 × 8 = 56 (leaving 1). So 90 + 8 = 98 groups with 1 left over.

3. Abstract: short division. Divide place by place, from the left, carrying remainders to the right:

   0 9 8  r 1
7 ) 6 8 7

7 into 6 does not go (0), carry 6 to make 68. 7 into 68 is 9 (63), remainder 5, carry to make 57. 7 into 57 is 8 (56), remainder 1. 687 days = 98 weeks and 1 day.

Check every division by multiplying back: 98 × 7 + 1 = 687. ✓

Remember

Short division: share each place in turn, from the left. What will not share equally is written as a remainder on the next place (or recorded at the end). Check by multiplying the quotient by the divisor and adding any remainder.

Watch me

687 ÷ 7, as in the worked example on the page.

  1. 7 into 6 will not go; take 68. 7 × 9 = 63, remainder 5.
  2. Bring down 7, making 57. 7 × 8 = 56, remainder 1.
  3. 687 ÷ 7 = 98 remainder 1. Check: 7 × 98 + 1 = 686 + 1 = 687.

Together

Share 952 ÷ 7, place by place from the left.

Hint 1: Hundreds and tens

7 into 9 is 1, remainder 2; 25 tens: 7 × 3 = 21, remainder 4.

Hint 2: Ones

42 ones: 7 × 6 = 42. The quotient is 136, with no remainder. Check: 7 × 136 = 952.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What is 48 ÷ 4? Check by multiplying.
  2. 84 ÷ 6, by short division.

Practice

  1. 365 ÷ 7: how many weeks and days are in a 365-day year?
  2. Mercury's year is 88 Earth days. How many Mercury years pass in 352 Earth days?
  3. 1,000 ÷ 8
  4. A mission packs 240 meals for 8 days. How many meals per day?
  5. 50 people are going to a rocket launch in vans that seat 8. How many vans are needed? (Careful with the remainder.)
  6. Roughly how many full Moons happen in a 365-day year? Use 30 days for the cycle, then think about whether the true 29.5 changes the answer.

Challenge problems

25 minutes

Learn

A non-leap year has 365 days. 365 ÷ 7 = 52 weeks remainder 1, so the same date next year (not a leap year) moves forward one weekday. To find a number that leaves remainder 1 with several divisors, notice that N − 1 is a common multiple of those divisors.

Remember

365 = 52 × 7 + 1. If N leaves remainder 1 when divided by a, then a divides N − 1.

Watch me

A date is a Monday. Next year is not a leap year. What day is the same date next year?

  1. 365 ÷ 7 leaves remainder 1.
  2. The date moves forward one weekday: Tuesday.

Together

Find the smallest whole number greater than 1 that leaves remainder 1 when divided by 2 and by 3.

Hint 1: N − 1

N − 1 is a multiple of both 2 and 3, so of 6.

Hint 2: Smallest > 1

N − 1 = 6, so N = 7. Check: 7 = 3 × 2 + 1 = 2 × 3 + 1.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. A date is a Friday this year. Next year is not a leap year. What day is the same date next year?

Practice

  1. This year, a date falls on a Tuesday. Next year is not a leap year. On what day will the same date fall next year? Explain using division.
  2. Find the smallest whole number greater than 1 that leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by 2, by 3, by 4, by 5 and by 6.
  3. A year on Saturn lasts about 10,747 Earth days. About how many Earth years is that? (Use 365 days in a year; first estimate, then calculate to one decimal place or as "a bit more than ___".)

Math circle: secret tests for dividing

20 minutes

Learn

Divisibility tests let you decide without a long division. A number divides by 2 if its last digit is even; by 5 if the last digit is 0 or 5; by 4 if the last two digits form a number that divides by 4; by 3 (and by 9) if the sum of the digits divides by 3 (or by 9). These work because 10, 100, 1,000, … leave known remainders with those divisors.

Remember

Last digit even ↔ ÷2. Last digit 0 or 5 ↔ ÷5. Last two digits ÷4 ↔ the number ÷4. Digit sum ÷3 ↔ the number ÷3.

Watch me

Does 348 divide by 2, by 3, and by 4?

  1. Last digit 8 is even: yes, ÷2.
  2. 3 + 4 + 8 = 15, and 15 ÷ 3 = 5: yes, ÷3.
  3. Last two digits 48, and 48 ÷ 4 = 12: yes, ÷4.

Together

Does 1,350 divide by 2, by 3, and by 5?

Hint 1: Last digit

0 is even, and 0 is 0 or 5, so yes ÷2 and ÷5.

Hint 2: Digit sum

1 + 3 + 5 + 0 = 9, which divides by 3, so yes ÷3.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. Does 7,215 divide by 3? By 5?
  2. Does 1,116 divide by 4?

Practice

Mathematicians have quick tests to see whether a number divides exactly, without dividing: - by 2: the last digit is even; - by 5: the last digit is 0 or 5; - by 4: the number formed by the last two digits divides by 4; - by 3: the sum of the digits divides by 3; - by 9: the sum of the digits divides by 9.

  1. Test 384,400 for division by 2, 3, 4, 5 and 9.
  2. Test 1,391,400 (the Sun's diameter in km) for 3 and 9.
  3. Investigate: pick any three-digit number, and subtract the sum of its digits from it. Try five numbers. What do you notice about the answers? Can you explain why?

Spanish · Tiene y quiere

15 minutes · Method: comprehensible input (Krashen) and Total Physical Response (Asher). Listen and act first; speak when ready. Instructions to the guide are in English.

Learn

Tiene means 'has'. Quiere means 'wants'. Un and una mean 'a' (un with many el words, una with many la words).

Word How to say it Meaning
tiene TYEH-neh has
quiere KYEH-reh wants
un perro oon PEH-rroh a dog
una casa OO-nah KAH-sah a house
un gato oon GAH-toh a cat

Learn

For the guide: hold a real object (Ana tiene un lápiz). Then mime wanting water. Keep perro, gato, luna in play: El perro quiere agua.

Ana with a cat. Ana tiene un gato.

Look and listen.

  • Ana tiene un gato. Yo tengo un lápiz.
  • El gato quiere leche. El perro quiere agua.
  • ¿Qué tiene Ana? Un gato.

Act. Toca el lápiz. Tienes el lápiz. (put the pencil in the student's hand) ¿Tienes un lápiz? —Sí.

Remember

Ana tiene un perro — Ana has a dog. Ana quiere un gato — Ana wants a cat.

Watch me

Ana tiene una casa. Ana has a house. Una is used because casa takes la (la casa).

Together

Say in Spanish: 'Ana has a dog.'

Hint 1: Words

Ana, tiene, un perro.

Hint 2: Sentence

Ana tiene un perro.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does quiere mean?
  2. What does tiene mean?

Exercises

  1. Point and say: Ana tiene un gato.
  2. Answer: ¿Qué tiene Ana?
  3. Copy: El perro quiere agua.

Latin · Sōl and lūna

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin reading (in the spirit of Ørberg and of Minimus). All text here is original; those books are not copied.

Learn

Sky words in Latin. Sōl is the sun, lūna the moon, stēlla a star, caelum the sky.

Word How to say it Meaning
sōl sohl sun
lūna LOO-nah moon
stēlla STELL-lah star
caelum KAI-lum sky
spectat SPEK-tat looks at, watches

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Learn

For the guide: spectat is "watches" or "looks at"; videt is "sees". Both take the same ending on the thing looked at (lūnam). Review Gaia lūnam videt from Day 6.

The sun, the moon, and Gaia looking at the moon.

Read.

  • Sōl est magnus.
  • Lūna est in caelō.
  • Gaia lūnam spectat.
  • Aulus sōlem non spectat. (Aulus does not look at the Sun. Never look at the Sun.)

Remember

Gaia lūnam spectat — Gaia looks at the moon. Lūnam is lūna in the 'being looked at' form, like canem yesterday.

Watch me

Sōl est in caelō. The sun is in the sky. Gaia lūnam spectat. Gaia looks at the moon.

Together

What does Gaia lūnam spectat mean?

Hint 1: Words

Gaia, lūnam (the moon, being looked at), spectat (looks at).

Hint 2: Meaning

Gaia looks at the moon.

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. What does sōl mean?
  2. What does stēlla mean?

Exercises

  1. Point: sōl, lūna, caelum.
  2. Read the four lines.
  3. Why must Aulus not look at the Sun? Answer in English, then read the Latin line again.

Notebook: predicting the full Moon

60 minutes · draw, label, because / but / so · How the notebook works · Review sheet

Learn

The time from one full Moon to the next is about 29 or 30 days (the synodic month is about 29.5 days). If you have a date when the Moon looked full, add 29 or 30 days to predict the next one. The reason the shape repeats is that the Moon goes once around Earth, and we see the sunlit part from a changing angle.

Remember

Next full Moon ≈ this full Moon's date + 29 or 30 days. The drawing should show Sun, Earth, and Moon, and which part of the Moon is lit.

Watch me

If a full Moon was seen on the 3rd, the next is about the 3rd + 29 or 30 days, so around the 2nd or 3rd of the following month (depending on the length of the month). Write the predicted date, then test it against the sky or an almanac.

Together

A full Moon is recorded on 10 March. About which dates do you predict for the next one?

Hint 1: Add

10 + 29 = 39, and 10 + 30 = 40.

Hint 2: Calendar

That is about 8 or 9 April (March has 31 days: 21 days remain after the 10th, then 8 or 9 more in April).

Check

These questions use the same skill as the example. Write the answers, then look at the key.

  1. About how many days is it from one full Moon to the next?

Practice

How the guide helps today: the Sun–Earth–Moon drawing is the hard thinking. Help the student shade the half of the Moon that faces away from the Sun in each position, then ask "from Earth, which of those looks full?" Do not answer first. Use because / but / so on "A full Moon happens".

Look back at your Moon journal. Mark any day the Moon looked full, or nearly full. If you have one, add 29 or 30 days to predict the next full Moon, and write your prediction with a date. If you have not seen a full Moon yet, find the date of the last one from an almanac or NASA's Moon pages and do the same.

Then explain with a drawing: the Moon makes no light of its own; it reflects sunlight. Draw the Sun on one side of the page, Earth in the middle, and the Moon in four positions around Earth. Shade the half of the Moon that faces away from the Sun.

1. Draw that four-position diagram, large.

2. Label Sun, Earth, Moon, full, new, and the two quarter positions if you know them.

Word bank: Sun · Earth · Moon · full · new · quarter · reflects · sunlight · shade · prediction · opposite

3. Write.

  • A full Moon happens when ________.
  • My predicted date for the next full Moon is ________.

Then:

  • A full Moon happens because ________.
  • A full Moon happens but ________.
  • A full Moon happens so ________.

4. Which position gives a full Moon seen from Earth? Mark it on the drawing.
