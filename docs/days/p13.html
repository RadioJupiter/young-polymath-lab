Core lesson: dividing into equal groups, and 54 ÷ 6

45 minutes · concrete → pictorial → abstract

Learn

Division shares a whole into equal groups, or asks how many equal groups fit. 54 ÷ 6 can mean: 54 in 6 equal groups, how many in each group? Or: how many 6s fit into 54?

Concrete: 54 counters shared into 6 piles (invented classroom counters, labeled invented). Each pile has 9. Pictorial: an array of 6 rows of 9 is 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9, and 54 ÷ 9 = 6.

Abstract: 6 × 9 = 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9 and 54 ÷ 9 = 6. These four facts are a fact family: 6 × 9, 9 × 6, 54 ÷ 6, 54 ÷ 9.

Check a division by multiplying: 6 × 9 = 54, so 54 ÷ 6 = 9 is right.

Count in 6s to 54: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54 — nine 6s.

The length of the month used in this level is from the NASA Moon fact sheet: the Moon’s synodic period is 29.53 days, taught here as about 29½ days. https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html This level does not invent a finer official length, and does not begin the sidereal month or Moon phases as fractions (those belong later). Classroom bag, rock, and array counts are invented and labeled invented. They are not official NASA counts. A week of 7 days is a calendar fact used here, not a NASA orbital period.

Remember

54 ÷ 6 = 9, because 6 × 9 = 54. Division is the other side of multiplication. Check by multiplying. Classroom shares are invented and labeled invented.

Watch me

Find 54 ÷ 6, and show a multiplication that checks it.

  1. 9.
  2. 6 × 9 = 54, or 9 × 6 = 54.

Together

What is 54 ÷ 9, and why does it belong in the same family?

Hint 1

If 6 groups of 9 make 54, then 9 groups of 6 also make 54.

Hint 2

54 ÷ 9 = 6.

Check

These divide, or use the fact family.

  1. Find 54 ÷ 6.
  2. Find 36 ÷ 9.

Practice

  1. Write the fact family for 6, 9, and 54.
  2. A student writes 54 ÷ 6 = 48 because 54 − 6 = 48. What was mixed?

Challenge problems

25 minutes · try every one; being stuck for a while is part of the work

Learn

If 54 ÷ 6 = 9, then 60 ÷ 6 is one more 6 in the whole: 9 + 1 = 10. 48 ÷ 6 is one 6 less in the whole: 8.

Remember

A nearby division can use a known fact by adding or taking one equal group in the whole.

Watch me

Using 54 ÷ 6 = 9, what is 60 ÷ 6?

  1. 60 is one more 6 than 54, so 10 groups of 6: 10.

Together

What is 48 ÷ 6?

Hint 1

48 is one 6 less than 54.

Hint 2

8.

Check

These stretch 54 ÷ 6.

  1. Find 72 ÷ 6 (you may think of 6 × 10 = 60, then two more 6s).
  2. Find 54 ÷ 9.

Practice

  1. Why is 54 ÷ 6 not 9 remainder 0 written as a new kind of number today — and what is the exact quotient?
  2. Find 35 ÷ 7 (days in five weeks, if it helps).

Math circle: why a multiplication checks a division

20 minutes · one rich problem, solved and then explained

Learn

If you share 54 into 6 equal groups of 9, putting the groups back together must make 54 again. That is why 6 × 9 = 54 checks 54 ÷ 6 = 9.

Remember

A division and its multiplication are two views of the same equal groups. The check is the groups put back as a whole.

Watch me

Why does 6 × 9 = 54 show that 54 ÷ 6 = 9 is right?

  1. 6 groups of 9 make the whole 54.
  2. If the groups make the whole, each group of the share was 9.

Together

A student checks 54 ÷ 6 by computing 54 − 6 and getting 48, then stopping. Why is that not a complete check?

Hint 1

Subtracting 6 once is not the same as making 6 equal groups.

Hint 2

A complete check multiplies: 6 × 9 = 54.

Check

These defend the check.

  1. What multiplication checks 36 ÷ 4 = 9?
  2. What division belongs with 7 × 8 = 56, besides 56 ÷ 7?

Practice

  1. Write a short argument that 54 ÷ 6 = 9, with a check.
  2. Why are the 54 counters labeled invented?

Spanish · Igual and cada, for equal groups

15 minutes · Method: share a small picture into equal piles. Instruction in English.

Learn

Today names igual and cada, used while sharing an invented small set of marks into equal groups. ¿Cuántos? may be reused from Level 2. Do not give 54 ÷ 6 in Spanish as a new official fact.

The guide shares 6 marks into 3 equal piles and says grupos iguales and cada grupo (each group), only after igual and cada have been heard with pointing.

Instructions are in English. Hear clips use es-MX. Words from Levels 1 and 2 may be reused, not retaught as a first look. If a student began at Level 3, the guide may first use earlier foundation Spanish, then continue here.

Remember

Igual: equal. Cada: each. ¿Cuántos?: how many? Point and share first. The 54 ÷ 6 fact stays in English.

Watch me

The guide makes two equal piles and says grupos iguales. What should the student do?

  1. Point at the equal piles, or show that the piles match.

Together

What is the English of cada grupo, as used today?

Hint 1

Each group.

Check

These check igual and cada.

  1. Give the English of igual, cada, and ¿cuántos?.
  2. Hear grupos and igual. Give the English of each.

Exercises

  1. Say the Spanish for “equal” and for “each.”
  2. Why is 54 ÷ 6 not taught as a Spanish official fact today?

Notebook: 54 ÷ 6, and a fact family

60 minutes · draw first, then label, then write · How the notebook works

Learn

Draw first: 54 invented marks shared into 6 equal groups of 9, or a 6-by-9 array, and the four facts of the family. Then write from stems.

Word bank: divide, equal, groups, check, fifty-four, nine, invented.

Stems: 1. 54 ÷ 6 is 9 because … 2. I could have written 54 − 6 = 48 as the answer, but … 3. I check with 6 × 9, so …

End with the fact family and one “what next” question.

Remember

Draw the share or array first. The but-stem should refuse a single subtraction of 6 as the division answer.

Watch me

Finish: “54 ÷ 6 is 9 because …”

  1. A model: “… because 6 groups of 9 make 54, or 6 × 9 = 54.” Other complete finishes accepted.

Together

Finish the but-stem about 54 − 6 = 48.

Hint 1

48 is taking 6 away once, not sharing into 6 equal groups.

Check

These check the family in writing.

  1. What four facts belong in the family?
  2. What check multiplication should appear?

Practice

  1. Finish all three stems in writing.
  2. Write one “what next” question about 54 ÷ 9.
