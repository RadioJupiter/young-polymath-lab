Mental math warm-up: totals and groups of five

10 minutes · say the answer, then explain how you got it

Learn

A total is the sum of the parts you have counted. Groups of five make a count quicker to read.

Remember

Five and five make ten. A total is the sum of the counts.

Watch me

You counted 5, then 5, then 3. What is the total?

  1. 5 + 5 = 10.
  2. 10 + 3 = 13.

Together

Four groups of five, and two left over. What is the total?

Hint 1: The groups

Four groups of five make 20.

Hint 2: The leftover

20 + 2 = 22.

Check

These are short totals.

  1. 7 + 8 + 5.
  2. Three groups of five, and one left over.

Practice

  1. 12 + 9.
  2. Six groups of five.

Core lesson: tallies and tables, and a meteor watch

40 minutes · Method: a tally for each count, then a table of the same numbers

Learn

A tally is a mark for each thing you count. The first four marks in a group are upright lines. The fifth mark crosses the group, so you can read the count in fives and leftovers.

A table is a way to write the same information in rows and columns, with a heading for each column. The table does not add new numbers; it organizes the counts you already have.

The marks and the table above are an invented classroom meteor watch: five one-hour watches, not a named meteor shower and not an official rate. The counts are 4, 8, 2, 10, and 6.

Watch 4 has two full groups of five, which is 10. Watch 1 has four marks and no complete group, which is 4. The total of the five invented watches is 4 + 8 + 2 + 10 + 6 = 30.

Remember

A tally uses one mark for each thing counted, grouped in fives. A table organizes the same counts under clear headings. An invented classroom watch stays labeled invented; it is not an official meteor rate.

Watch me

How many meteors are in Watch 2 of the invented watch, and how does the tally show it?

  1. Watch 2 has one group of five and three leftover marks.
  2. 5 + 3 = 8, which matches the table.

Together

What is the total of Watches 1, 3, and 5 in the invented table, and how do you know?

Hint 1: The three counts

Watch 1 is 4, Watch 3 is 2, and Watch 5 is 6.

Hint 2: The sum

4 + 2 + 6 = 12.

Check

These use the invented watch above, or a new labeled invented list.

  1. Which invented watch has the most meteors, and how many is that?
  2. Write a tally for an invented count of 13, and say the number in groups of five and leftovers.

Practice

  1. A new labeled invented watch has counts 5, 9, and 6. Make a two-column table and find the total.
  2. Why must this lesson not call 30 an official rate for a named meteor shower?

Challenge problems: reading a table carefully

25 minutes · harder work with the same tally and table skills

Learn

The numbers below are labeled invented classroom counts. Read them as they are written, then compare, total, or complete a missing cell.

Remember

A table organizes counts. A missing cell is filled from the total or from the other parts. Invented counts stay labeled invented.

Watch me

A labeled invented table of three watches has a total of 24. Watch A is 7 and Watch B is 9. What is Watch C?

  1. The two known parts are 7 + 9 = 16.
  2. Watch C is the missing part of 24, so 24 − 16 = 8.

Together

A labeled invented four-watch list is 3, 11, 3, and 8. Which two watches have the same count, and how much greater is the largest than the smallest?

Hint 1: The match

The first and third watches are both 3.

Hint 2: The difference

The largest is 11 and the smallest is 3, so 11 − 3 = 8.

Check

These stay with tallies, tables, and labeled invented counts.

  1. A labeled invented table has watches 6, 6, 6, and n, and the total is 25. Find n.
  2. Write a tally for 17, then say why a table still needs a heading, even when the tallies are already clear.

Practice

  1. A five-watch invented list is 4, 0, 5, 12, and 4. What is the total, and why is a zero still worth a row?

Math circle: why tallies group in fives

20 minutes · a reason, not a new counting trick

Learn

Grouping tallies in fives is a choice that makes a count easier to read. It does not change the total.

Remember

Five-groups let you skip-count. The leftover marks are the ones that do not make a full five. The total stays the same.

Watch me

Why is a tally of 12 easier to read as two groups of five and two leftovers than as twelve separate marks in a line?

  1. Two groups of five can be skip-counted: 5, 10.
  2. The two leftover marks make 12.
  3. Twelve separate marks have to be counted one by one, and it is easier to lose your place.

Together

Does grouping in fives change a count of 12 into a different number? Why or why not?

Hint 1: The marks

Every mark still stands for one thing counted.

Hint 2: The total

2 × 5 + 2 = 12, which is the same total.

Check

These check the reason.

  1. A tally could, in theory, group in tens instead. Give one reason fives are a handy group for a quick count.
  2. Why is a messy scatter of marks a weaker record than grouped tallies?

Practice

  1. If two people tally the same invented watch and write different totals, what should they do before trusting either total?

Spanish · la tabla, la noche, el meteoro, muchos, and pocos

15 minutes · Instruction in English; Hear for Spanish words · present tense only

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
la tabla lah TAH-blah the table (a chart of numbers)
la noche lah NOH-cheh the night
el meteoro el meh-teh-OH-roh the meteor
muchos MOO-chohs many
pocos POH-kohs few

Today’s words name a table, a night, a meteor, “many,” and “few.” Instruction stays in English. Invented meteor counts and tally arithmetic stay in the English mathematics.

Original sentences: Hay una tabla. Hay una noche. Veo un meteoro. Hay muchos meteoros. Hay pocos meteoros.

For the guide: point to the day’s table, then to a drawn night sky with a streak, as you say the words. This is present-tense description, not a tally lesson in Spanish. estrella and cielo are already known from earlier days and may be used in pointing, but they are not today’s new words.

Remember

la tabla; la noche; el meteoro; muchos; pocos. Instruction in English. Hear for Spanish words. No tallies or invented totals in the Spanish.

Watch me

Say: “There is a table.”

  1. Hay una tabla.
  2. Hay introduces it. Una tabla is the table.
  3. This is a present-tense pattern, not a past narrative.

Together

How do you say “There are few meteors”?

Hint 1: Few

pocos

Hint 2: The sentence

Hay pocos meteoros.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “There is a night.”
  2. Write: “I see a meteor.”

Exercises

  1. Correct: *El tabla, if someone used the wrong article.
  2. Hear tabla, noche, meteoro, muchos, and pocos, then use each.
  3. Why does the Spanish not add 4 + 8 + 2?
  4. A pointing retell: a table, a night, a meteor, many, few.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Latin · summa and nota

15 minutes · Method: picture-based Latin; original sentences. Instruction in English.

Learn

Word How to say it Meaning
summa SUM-mah a total (nominative, feminine)
Summa magna est SUM-mah MAG-nah est The total is large
nota NOH-tah a mark (nominative, feminine)
Nota adest NOH-tah AH-dest A mark is here

The Hear-word button uses an Italian voice as an approximation of Latin, not the speech of ancient Rome.

Original sentences, not from a textbook: Summa magna est. Summa nōn magna est. Nota adest. Nota nōn adest.

summa and nota are feminine, so magna matches summa. Invented counts and tally arithmetic stay in the English mathematics; no tables of added totals in the Latin.

For the guide: the student reads as much as possible. Point to a written total, then to a single tally mark. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation.

Remember

summa; Summa magna est; nota; Nota adest. Instruction in English. Italian-voice Hear, labeled as an approximation. No tally arithmetic in the Latin.

Watch me

Read: “The total is large.”

  1. Summa magna est.
  2. summa is the total. magna matches because summa is feminine.

Together

How do you say “A mark is here”?

Hint 1: The mark

nota

Hint 2: The sentence

Nota adest.

Check

These use only today’s phrases.

  1. Write: “The total is not large.”
  2. Write: “A mark is not here.”

Exercises

  1. Why is *Summa magnus est a miss?
  2. Hear summa and nota, then use each in a sentence.
  3. Why does the Latin not add the invented watch?
  4. A pointing retell: a total, a mark.
  5. Copy one model sentence into the notebook.

Notebook: a tally and a table, and a because-sentence

about 1 hour · a tally, a table, then because / but / so

Learn

Copy the invented classroom watch as tallies and as a table, then write a because / but / so sentence about why the marks group in fives.

Remember

The page shows the same invented counts two ways. The stems explain the habit: fives make the count easier to read, and the watch stays labeled invented.

Watch me

Start: The tallies group in fives because ________.

  1. The because restates that groups of five are easy to skip-count.
  2. A sound because: …I can count the groups of five at a glance…

Together

Finish: The tallies group in fives because ________, but grouping does not change the total, so ________.

Hint 1: The but

…each mark still stands for one meteor in the invented watch…

Hint 2: The so

…the table and the tallies must show the same numbers, and I will label the watch invented.

Check

The page should show the tally, the table, and complete stems.

  1. Draw the tallies and the two-column table for the invented watch 4, 8, 2, 10, and 6, and write the total 30.
  2. Write a three-stem explanation (because / but / so) about grouping in fives and labeling the watch invented.

Practice

  1. Tick the word-bank words you used: tally, table, group, total, invented.
  2. Write one heading on the table so a later reader knows what the numbers count.
  3. The page is a record, not a score.
  4. File the page. It is not a reward.
