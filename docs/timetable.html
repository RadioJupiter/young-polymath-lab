Daily plan

The five parts of the day

Part Time What happens Why
Mental math 10 min Quick calculations done in the head, explained aloud Fluent facts and strategies free attention for real problems
Core lesson 45 min New idea, taught concrete → pictorial → abstract, then practice Understanding first, then the efficient written method
Challenge problems 25 min Non-routine problems that need thought Gifted students need problems they cannot do instantly
Math circle 20 min One deep puzzle in the Russian math-circle tradition Logic, proof and creative reasoning
Notebook and project 60 min Observing, measuring, sketching, inventing Mathematics used on the real world, in the spirit of Leonardo's notebooks

Focused mathematics: about 1 hour 40 minutes a day, which can stretch toward 2½ hours when a problem is going well. Notebook and project time: about an hour. Stop while attention is still good; stamina grows over weeks.

Ground rules

  • Short movement break after the core lesson.
  • Challenge and circle problems are not races. When the block ends, an unsolved problem can be continued tomorrow.
  • Hints come in steps: first "What do you know?", then "Can you draw it?", then "Try a smaller case", and only then a direct hint.
  • No points, badges or streaks. Progress is shown by what you can now do.
